# The four lenses

Short summaries of published frameworks, in our words, adapted from the MIT-licensed founder-skill pack
(github.com/Jakeschincariol/founder-skill, fd9385b). A reviewer applies ONE lens. It never speaks as the author,
never quotes them, and never says they would back anything: write "the Offers lens", never a person's name.

## Offers lens (from Alex Hormozi, $100M Offers)
- Market first: is the pain big, do these buyers have money, can we reach them, is the market growing? A weak market
  sinks the best offer.
- Value: the buyer's dream outcome and their belief it will happen push value up; time to the result and effort
  push it down. Score all four, 1 to 10. Most offers fail on belief (no proof) or effort.
- Never win on cheapness alone. What would make saying no feel silly: more outcome, faster, less effort, a guarantee
  we can afford?
- The stack: every obstacle between the buyer and the result, each turned into a fix; keep the fixes cheap for us and
  valuable to them. Urgency and scarcity only when true (our 3 seat cap is true; a countdown would not be).
- Asks: who exactly has this pain and money? Why would they believe it works? How fast is the first result?

## Monopoly lens (from Peter Thiel with Blake Masters, Zero to One)
- Competition eats margin. Find the small market we can own first.
- Lasting edges: something about ten times better, network effects, scale, brand. Which one can this build?
- Seven questions: breakthrough or small step, why now, a big share of a small market, the right team, a way to reach
  buyers, still holding in ten years, the secret we know that others do not.
- Distribution is half the business: how do the first 10 buyers arrive, and what does each cost us?
- Asks: who else sells this to the same buyer? What stops a copycat in month six?

## Product lens (from Walter Isaacson, Steve Jobs, and Apple's public record)
- Focus is saying no. What do we cut until one thing is left that we do brilliantly?
- Start from the buyer's first five minutes with it and work back.
- Every touchpoint is the product: the email, the page, the checkout, the receipt, the first list, the follow-up.
  Hand-offs between tools are where it breaks.
- Craft in the parts nobody sees. Simple is hard: if the pitch needs a paragraph, the product is not clear yet.
- Asks: what is the one thing? What would you cut? Where does the buyer's path cross between systems?

## Numbers lens (the CFO; tools/stress_numbers.py does every sum)
- What one unit leaves after the platform fee and direct costs; fixed costs; break-even units a month against any cap.
- What the $1,000 net month (coo/VENTURE.md) needs from this product alone, and whether the cap allows it.
- The one assumption that moves the result most, shown with a what-if, never guessed.
- Asks: what is the real ramp, month by month, from what we have actually seen (sends, replies, visits)?
