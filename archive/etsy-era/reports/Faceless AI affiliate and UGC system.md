# Skip the avatars and sell the math

ProofNotFluff should build this as an add-on to the system it already runs, not as a new faceless brand or an AI avatar studio. The one model with published payouts that passes every house rule is disclosed software referrals placed beside the free calculators and in the weekly long-form walkthroughs. **Shopify pays $150 per US referral, Gusto $300 and FreshBooks up to $200, and eRank pays 30% of each subscription payment for up to a year**, so one referral matches the gross of 50 to 100 sales at $2.99. The platform-native routes (TikTok Shop, YouTube Shopping, Amazon's social route, Instagram's new Reels tags) sit behind follower counts and real-footage rules that a zero-follower faceless account cannot meet, and on physical goods they pay a few percent. Selling AI UGC to brands has real buyers, from a formal Fiverr category to agency list prices of €363 to €500 a video, but no verified operator income, a $5 marketplace floor, a buyer pool tilted toward skincare and wellness, and tests in which AI characters lost every finance comparison; it needs Todd's sales time, so it belongs at most in a phase-three test with no synthetic people. The AI persona plus keyword-DM model that creators such as Jianna Capri teach has three reusable mechanics (scene-by-scene generation, a hook-loop-CTA script and comment-keyword delivery of a free resource), but its core, an invented character claiming lived results, is the fake-testimonial pattern the FTC bans, and by Capri's own account her character's Facebook account was removed when Meta asked to verify the identity behind it. The legal line is sharp: the FTC treats AI-generated testimonials as fake reviews at up to $53,088 per violation, disclosure has to sit inside the content, New York has required synthetic-performer labels in ads since June 9, 2026, and the platforms now punish templated sameness harder than they punish AI. LTX-2.5 is a genuine free alternative to Higgsfield for b-roll (free under $10 million in revenue, about 2 to 4 cents of rented GPU time per 10-second clip), but it needs an NVIDIA card with 16 GB or more, which neither the cloud agents nor the Arc PC have, while Higgsfield's unlimited plans forbid the automation and resale an agent crew implies. Production is no longer the constraint; reach is, so the 90-day plan below costs $0 to start and is judged on outbound clicks and first referrals.

## Of five business models, only disclosed software referrals fit today

Money is verifiably being made around AI UGC in three places, and none of them is a faceless affiliate account. Tool vendors are the clearest case: when Arcads raised a $16 million seed round in December 2025, it reported more than 6,000 clients, 100,000 assets a month and profitability with a team of seven, though those figures are self-reported ([Arcads](https://www.arcads.ai/blog/arcads-raises-16m-seed)). Agencies sell batches to large advertisers; Admiral Media lists €10,000 for 20 ads, €16,000 for 40 and €29,000 for 80, aimed at clients spending $50,000 or more a month on media ([Admiral Media](https://admiral.media/ai-ugc-agency/)). Teaching is the third: The Clueless runs a Skool community of 5,200 members at $9 to $97 a month under the line "Want to build a €10,000/month AI agency? Start here," with no published member outcomes ([Skool](https://www.skool.com/ai-avatar-society-9744/about)). By contrast, **no independently verified faceless or AI-made affiliate account with documented commissions turned up on any channel**, and the only payment-verified numbers for small AI UGC software are tiny: UGC Copilot shows $332 in monthly recurring revenue from 8 subscriptions, AutoUGC has zero active subscriptions after $2,479 in all-time revenue, and LightUGC shows $38 a month ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/ugc-copilot?metric=mrr); [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/autougc); [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/lightugc)). ### Selling AI UGC to brands has buyers but no proven sellers

Demand is real. Fiverr now lists "AI UGC" as one of two types in its UGC Videos category, defined as content made with digital presenters or synthetic narration and no human on camera, and it requires sellers to name their tools, hold likeness permissions and never promise CTR or ROAS ([Fiverr](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/37333410852625-UGC-video-service-guidelines)). A one-day Upwork snapshot showed a skincare brand and a women's wellness brand each hiring a full-time AI video editor for about $1,500, next to a $150 Meta ad edit and a $50 high-volume job ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Expert-UGC-creator-needed_~022052334852707357326/)). Brands buy batches, not single videos: one concept cut into five hooks, three aspect ratios and captions, with paid-usage rights and whitelisting sold as add-ons. One freelancer rate card puts solo work at $150 to $350 per finished ad, five-hook batches at $1,250 and retainers at $2,500 to $8,000 a month, without citing any sales data ([Motionbox](https://motionbox.io/blog/how-much-to-charge-clients-for-ai-ugc-ads)).

The economics underneath are harsh. **Generation costs a few dollars or less per video**: Creatify's app plans run $12 to $156 a month ([Creatify](https://www.creatify.ai/pricing)), HeyGen's Creator plan works out to about $0.97 per avatar minute ([Arcade](https://www.arcade.software/post/heygen-pricing)), and Arcads to about $11 per video ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/en/blog/arcads-ai-pricing)). One live Fiverr gig sells a 30-second AI UGC video, with script, captions and three months of usage rights, for **$5** ([Fiverr gig](https://fiverr.com/paw_mik/make-ai-ugc-or-ai-dtc-video)). MakeUGC sells its own managed service on its Enterprise tier ([MakeUGC](https://makeugc.ai/pricing)) and TikTok gives its sellers a built-in AI video maker ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-adds-more-ai-powered-tools-to-assist-tiktok-shop-merchants/809789/)), so rendering itself has no pricing power; clients pay for hook writing, test design and iteration. The time-to-revenue evidence is weak. The most detailed playbook tells readers to expect 60 to 90 days of unpaid outbound before a first client, and its "5-10% per outreach" conversion figure has no source ([Vantaige](https://vantaige.io/blog/how-to-make-money-selling-ai-ugc-content-brands-2026)).

The performance data cuts against ProofNotFluff's own category. In Admiral Media's equal-budget tests across 13 accounts, AI characters matched or beat human UGC on conversion in 9, but **human UGC won every finance test** ([Admiral Media](https://admiral.media/ai-characters-vs-ugc-performance-ads/)). One agency's pooled data shows the gap closing to within 5% on products under $30, while real creators convert about 31% better on products above $75 ([Moburst](https://intercept.moburst.com/ai-avatar-ads-vs-real-creators-which-wins-on-meta/)). The only multi-tool spend test found, $115,000 across five platforms for a single client, was won by Captions' Mirage Studio, which requires a $399-a-month plan ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/115k-ai-ugc-platform-test-findings); [Captions](https://help.captions.ai/mirage/mirage-billing)). Consumers are cooling: in the IAB's survey, 82% of ad executives thought young consumers felt positive about AI ads, but only 45% did, and 39% of Gen Z felt negative ([IAB](https://www.iab.com/insights/the-ai-ad-gap-widens/)). Brands are drawing lines too. SharkNinja voids affiliate commissions for AI-generated content, and Rare Beauty says it does not work with AI creators ([Newswav/The Independent](https://newswav.com/article/brands-urge-tiktok-shop-creators-to-ditch-ai-fakes-report-says-A2607_sFmfB6)).

For ProofNotFluff this model fails three house tests at once. What the market calls AI UGC is an AI person on camera, which collides with the no-invented-people rule. The visible buyers skew to skincare and wellness, the edge of the excluded supplement niche and the categories where even a vendor says AI lags ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/ai-ugc-vs-human-ugc-meta-ads-roas-2026)). And every source treats discovery calls and spec ads as part of the sale, which makes it Todd's time, not the crew's. A version that would pass the rules does exist: a **faceless creative-variant service** that builds hook and format variants from a client's real product footage or app screen captures, with synthetic narration and motion graphics, labeled as AI-assisted and priced per testing batch, for transactional e-commerce or software brands outside health, wellness and finance. It belongs in the plan only as a gated phase-three test. Web searches found no page by or about Jianna Capri or Devin Seymour. Capri's method is covered in the next subsection from a tutorial transcript Todd supplied, but her "$100k in 90 days" and 7,000-member community claims remain unverified, as do Seymour's LTX-2.5 versus Higgsfield comparisons. A Skool community's About page shows its member count without a login, so the community claim can be checked quickly once its URL is known ([Skool](https://www.skool.com/ai-avatar-society-9744/about)).

### The AI persona and keyword-DM model: keep the mechanics, drop the persona

Todd supplied a transcript of Jianna Capri's AI UGC tutorial, and it is the only source for her method here; every figure in it is self-reported and unverified. Call it the **AI persona plus keyword-DM digital product** model. Capri runs an AI-generated grandmother character named Rose, whose content she says made "over $700 in just a few days." Her process runs in five steps. She follows AI characters in her niche and copies what goes viral ("don't hate the player, hate the game"). She rewrites the viral video's script into a hook that opens a loop, a body that closes it and a call to action. She splits the script into scenes, four in her example, and generates one still per scene from a reference photo plus a prompt, using Higgsfield and an image model she describes as OpenAI's and calls "GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst," chosen because it is cheap. She animates each still at 9:16 in Higgsfield with a model she calls "SeaDance 2.5," then stitches the clips in Edits or CapCut. The call to action asks viewers to follow and comment a keyword, which automatically sends them her digital product by direct message; her own tutorial ends by asking viewers to comment "VALUE" for a free guide with her prompts. Neither model name could be verified: Higgsfield's own lineup lists ByteDance's Seedance 2.0, not a 2.5 ([Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/higgsfield-all-unlimited-explained)), and no source here confirms the image model. Her example video has Rose explaining how she got "pearly white, natural teeth" growing up, "never professionally whitened."

Her Day 79 update, also self-reported, shows how the model can end. Rose reached almost 30,000 Instagram followers and more than 45,000 on Facebook, with over $1,000 in 14 days and more than 6 million views in 12 days. Then Meta asked to verify the identity behind Rose's Facebook account, which Capri had set up as a personal profile for the fictional character and labeled AI-generated. She could not verify it, the account was removed, and she says there was no human to appeal to. Her own personal account now shows would-be followers an "Are you sure you want to follow this person?" warning, and she ended the effort with 11 days left in her challenge. Taken at face value the numbers are also thin: about $1,000 against more than 6 million views is **under 17 cents per thousand views**, so a single $150 Shopify referral would equal the revenue from roughly 900,000 such views.

| Part of the method | Keep it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Scene-by-scene production: script split into scenes, one still per scene, image-to-video per scene, clips stitched | Keep | A production technique, not a claim; open models do it, since LTX-2.5 and Wan 2.2 both run image-to-video and LTX-2.5 ships first-and-last-frame templates ([ComfyUI Wiki](https://comfyui-wiki.com/fr/news/2026-08-11-ltx-2-5-open-weights-release)) |
| Hook that opens a loop, body that closes it, call to action | Keep | A script shape, not anyone's words; one organic test saw a 37-fold view gap from the opening alone ([LinkedIn](https://ca.linkedin.com/in/ailis)) |
| Comment a keyword, receive a free resource by DM | Keep, on the official route | Meta's private-replies API supports it for professional accounts ([Meta for Developers: private replies](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/messenger-platform/instagram/features/private-replies/)) |
| Studying what performs in the niche | Keep, as analysis | Structure and pacing are fair to study; wording and footage are not |
| Copying and lightly rewriting viral scripts | Drop | Meta cuts distribution for content that reuses another creator's work without credit, TikTok's 2024 guidelines told creators to post only their own work, and copied wording can also draw a copyright claim ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-meta-cracks-down-on-unoriginal-facebook-content/); [TikTok Guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)) |
| An invented character with a name, an age and a life story | Drop | The house rule bars invented people and stories, and YouTube reportedly now names AI personas discussing health, finance or legal topics as non-monetizable ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-cant-tell-a-directed-ai-film-from-a-bot-farm)) |
| First-person lived-experience claims ("never professionally whitened") | Drop | A testimonial from someone who does not exist about an experience that never happened is what 16 CFR 465.2(a) bans, and testimonials cannot substantiate a cosmetic or health result under § 255.2(a) ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465); [Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-07-26/html/2023-14795.htm)) |
| A fictional character run as a personal Facebook profile | Drop | It failed Meta's identity check in Capri's own case; characters and brands belong on Pages or professional accounts owned by a verified person |

Capri's AI label does not cure the core problem. A label tells viewers the image is synthetic; it does not tell them the experience never happened, and a teeth story implies that some method produces a cosmetic result, which needs evidence a testimonial cannot supply. Whether a label alone would satisfy the FTC is untested and a question for counsel, but the house rule against invented people, stories and testimonials settles it for ProofNotFluff regardless.

The compliant ProofNotFluff version keeps all four reusable mechanics and replaces the persona with proof. An openly labeled AI narrator, or at most a labeled AI character Todd approves under the presenter rule in the compliance section, walks through a real calculator with real numbers and never claims a backstory, a personal experience or a result. Production runs scene by scene on the $0 stack: screen captures of the calculator for the proof scenes, open-model image-to-video for any scene-setting shot, Kokoro narration, and ffmpeg or Remotion to stitch. The script keeps Capri's shape. The hook states the question the calculator answers ("What should it cost to pressure-wash a 2,400-square-foot driveway?"), the body closes the loop with the computed answer, and the call to action asks viewers to comment a keyword. Delivery runs through Meta's own private-replies API, which lets an Instagram professional account send one message to a commenter within seven days of a comment on a post, reel or ad; the reply lands in the inbox only if the commenter follows the account, which explains the "follow and comment" ask ([Meta for Developers: private replies](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/messenger-platform/instagram/features/private-replies/)). That single message carries the link to the free calculator, or to a lead magnet that feeds the email list once it exists, and says it is an automated reply. The API needs a linked Facebook Page and an app with Advanced Access, so in practice it runs through a messaging tool built on that API, which Todd connects. Instagram's AI label goes on for any realistic AI visual or voice ([Meta letter to Sen. Warner](https://www.warner.senate.gov/public/_cache/files/7/1/71cdcf73-5cce-49c4-8713-0102c072b9cf/A5A52F05BD96683C10F08525788CBB67.meta-response.pdf)). Because human creators beat AI characters in every finance test Admiral Media ran, the narrator-over-calculator version is the stronger default for a finance brand ([Admiral Media](https://admiral.media/ai-characters-vs-ugc-performance-ads/)).

### Platform affiliate programs lock out a zero-follower faceless account

Every platform-native program is gated by audience size or footage rules that ProofNotFluff, with Shorts drawing tens to hundreds of views and no Instagram following yet, does not meet.

**TikTok Shop.** The self-applied affiliate path starts at **1,000 followers**, and accounts under 5,000 followers sit in a 30-day pilot capped at about three shoppable videos a day (another guide says five) ([Creators Agency](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/tiktok-shop-affiliate-requirements-2026); [Short Form Nation](https://www.shortformnation.com/blog/tiktok-shop-creator-program-how-it-works-requirements-and-how-to-earn-2026-guide)). TikTok Shop's May 23, 2026 quality rules require at least three seconds of dynamic content, ban still-image videos that show neither the creator's face nor the physical product, and ban product-page screenshots; one trade outlet reads the rules as requiring real-world footage with both the creator's face and the product ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shops-quality-rules-ban-ai-voices-and-still-images-from-lives/); [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/tiktok-shop-bans-ai-voices-prerecorded-audio-from-promotional-livestreams/)). TikTok pays commission on disclosed AI content ([World Journal](https://www.worldjournal.com/wj/amp/story/121172/9636854)), but an AI-only pipeline cannot honestly show a product it has never held, and TikTok separately reduces the reach of content that sends US users to buy off-platform ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/15/tiktoks-new-guidelines-add-subtle-changes-for-live-creators-ai-content-and-more/)). The market is also lopsided: more than half of 803,500 registered US TikTok Shop stores have recorded no sales ([SmartScout](https://www.smartscout.com/blog/tiktok-shop-statistics-2026)).

**YouTube Shopping.** It requires Partner Program membership, which at the lower tier means 500 subscribers plus either 3,000 watch hours in a year or **3 million Shorts views in 90 days** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13376398?hl=en); [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/are-you-ready-to-make-money-on-youtube)).

**Amazon.** Amazon reviews an Associates application only after **three qualified sales within 180 days**, wants about 500 followers from a social-only applicant or a site with about 10 recent posts, and does not reassess rejections ([Amazon Associates](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/G8TW5AE9XL2VX9VM)). Its rates are thin and falling. Onsite content earns 0.5% to 4% under the current table ([Amazon](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/G4ARBJC7Z2NK48CA)), offsite rates run 3% to 4.5% in most mainstream categories ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/amazon-influencer-program/)), negotiated publisher rates were cut by up to 50% without announcement in 2026 ([eMarketer](https://emarketer.com/content/amazon-cuts-affiliate-commissions-by-up-50--raising-pressure-on-publishers)), and since April 14, 2026 any purchase referred through a paid or boosted ad earns nothing ([Amazon](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/compare)). At 3%, a $25 home item pays 75 cents.

**Instagram, Pinterest and Etsy.** Instagram's Reels affiliate links, live in the US since April 2026, need products in a Meta commerce catalog and have no published eligibility rules ([Affiverse](https://www.affiversemedia.com/instagram-now-lets-creators-add-affiliate-links-to-reels/)). When Pinterest launched product tags, it limited them to approved partners: Amazon, ShopStyle, LTK and Rakuten ([Pinterest](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/announcing-product-tagging-and-affiliate-partnerships-on-pinterest/563)). Etsy's own affiliate program now lets sellers earn on their own shop, but it bans AI-generated images in affiliate content, requires AI disclosure and sets rates case by case ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/affiliates/terms)); its Creator Collective typically pays about 4% ([Make Influence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/etsys-creator-collective-and-affiliate-program-how-creators-actually-earn-from-etsy)), which is about 12 cents on a $2.99 listing.

The success stories behind these channels do not hold up. A claim that 180 faceless TikTok accounts produced $38 million in GMV in 30 days comes from an agency that gives no method ([Commerce Social](https://commercesocial.co/blog/180-faceless-accounts-made-38m-tiktok-shop-30-days)). The best-documented Amazon creator earns about $10,000 a month from filmed product reviews, and the article itself calls him an outlier against an average posting creator near $300 a month ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/blue-collar-worker-says-makes-123000364.html)). For ProofNotFluff these channels are traffic sources that point at its own pages, not places to drop affiliate links.

### Software bounties of $150 to $300 match the calculator audience

The programs that approve on site quality rather than follower counts pay far more per conversion than anything above. Shopify pays **$150** for each US referral who pays full price for at least one month after the trial ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/affiliates/earnings)). Gusto lists **$300** for each new customer who runs payroll ([PartnerStack](https://market.partnerstack.com/page/gusto)), and FreshBooks lists $10 per qualified trial and up to $200 per paid plan ([PartnerStack](https://market.partnerstack.com/page/freshbooksusa)). eRank pays **30% recurring for up to 12 months**, rising to 50% after 100 referrals ([eRank](https://help.erank.com/faq/does-erank-have-an-affiliate-program/)). Canva pays 80% of the first two monthly payments or 25% of an annual plan, and its own funnel averages (25% of visitors sign up, 23% of those start a trial and 56% of trials convert) imply that about 3% of clicked-through visitors become paying users ([Canva](https://www.canva.com/affiliates/hubpage/how-tos)). QuickBooks runs a PartnerStack program but does not publish its amounts ([QuickBooks](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/partners/qbbusinessaffiliates/)), and Notion's program is closed to new affiliates ([Notion](https://notion.com/affiliates)). The arithmetic makes the case: **one Shopify referral equals the gross of about 50 sales at $2.99, and one Gusto referral about 100.**

These programs fit the assets ProofNotFluff already has. The free calculator site targets home-service pricing (contractor hourly rates, pressure washing, junk removal), e-commerce finance and Etsy selling, which map onto invoicing and payroll software, store platforms and accounting, and Etsy research tools. Shopify expects an active website, an established audience and original content before it approves an affiliate ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/affiliates/about)), so the site's growth is what gets ProofNotFluff approved. Links belong in four places: a disclosed "next step" block on the matching calculator page, the descriptions of the weekly long-form walkthroughs (ordinary affiliate links, separate from YouTube Shopping), pins that point to those pages rather than carrying links themselves, and email once a list exists. They do not belong inside the $2.99 downloads or the $79 workbook. Amazon bans its links in any e-book or printed material ([Amazon Program Policies](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/policies)), and other programs' rules on links inside files were not checked.

One FTC rule shapes which programs to pick. An endorser "must have been a bona fide user" of the product ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-07-26/html/2023-14795.htm)), so ProofNotFluff can describe experience only with tools its crew actually uses. eRank qualifies, because the R&D lab already runs it for keyword research. Payroll and invoicing software do not, so those links must be framed as factual options with sourced prices and features, never as "what we use."

### Selling the math to the AI UGC economy is the untested model

The people chasing AI UGC and affiliate income are a market with exactly ProofNotFluff's kind of problem. TikTok's affiliate program has nearly 1 million US creators ([Newswav/The Independent](https://newswav.com/article/brands-urge-tiktok-shop-creators-to-ditch-ai-fakes-report-says-A2607_sFmfB6)), and its payout rules invite a calculator. Commission is computed on revenue minus refunds, a full refund zeroes it, and payment typically arrives 15 days after delivery, sometimes 31 ([Dashboardly](https://www.dashboardly.io/post/tiktok-shop-affiliate-commissions-2026-payouts-clawbacks-profit-math)). Sellers set rates anywhere from 1% to 80%, and rate changes take 30 days to apply on open plans ([Dashboardly](https://www.dashboardly.io/post/tiktok-shop-affiliate-commissions-2026-payouts-clawbacks-profit-math); [Syncly](https://syncly.app/blog/tiktok-shop-affiliate-roi-in-what-the-data-actually-shows)). AI UGC freelancers price batches with usage-rights add-ons of 30% to 100% and whitelisting at $500 to $2,000 per asset ([Motionbox](https://motionbox.io/blog/how-much-to-charge-clients-for-ai-ugc-ads)). That is pricing math, the shop's core category, and creator deal economics sit inside the e-commerce finance work Todd has done. Three candidates stand out: a UGC rate-card and usage-rights calculator, a TikTok Shop affiliate payout and clawback tracker, and a premium creator-program budget workbook for small brands. No search-demand data exists yet for these phrases, so they go through the R&D lab's demand floor like any other idea once the current freeze on new builds lifts on November 1. They should stay financial tools rather than contract templates, which drift toward legal documents.

## LTX-2.5 can replace Higgsfield, but only on an NVIDIA card

The house rules settle most of the tool question before price does. A crew that never shows a synthetic person needs four things: screen-recorded demos of its own calculators, motion graphics, a synthetic narrator and short b-roll. Almost all of that runs free on CPUs. Video models matter only for b-roll and product motion, and that is where LTX-2.5 and Higgsfield compete.

### Higgsfield sells model access that agents may not automate

Higgsfield is a subscription storefront for other companies' models, including Seedance 2.0, Kling 3.0, Wan 2.7, Gemini Omni Flash and Eleven v3. Its own July 2026 explainer limits the "All Unlimited" plans to **"personal, human use"** and prohibits automation, scripting, credential sharing and reselling; MCP and CLI access run on credits, and unlimited video is capped at 720p to 1080p and clips of about 8 seconds ([Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/higgsfield-all-unlimited-explained)). An agent crew would therefore pay per credit, and a service reselling output would be barred from the unlimited tiers. Even the credit price is unclear: the Plus plan appears at $34, $39 or $49 a month depending on the source and billing period ([Higgsfield](https://geo.higgsfield.ai/task/blog/understanding-higgsfield-ai-pricing-plans); [Creatify](https://creatify.ai/blog/higgsfield-pricing-%282026%29-plans-and-what-you-ll-actually-pay)), and estimates of credits per premium video differ about tenfold.

LTX-2.5 is the opposite kind of product: one model you run yourself. Lightricks released the 22-billion-parameter open weights on **August 11, 2026**, with joint audio and video generation, image-to-video, native multishot that holds a character, setting and voice across cuts, and ComfyUI templates on day one ([ComfyUI Wiki](https://comfyui-wiki.com/fr/news/2026-08-11-ltx-2-5-open-weights-release)). The license is free for organizations under **$10 million in annual revenue**, with no usage caps or seat fees ([LTX](https://ltx.io/model/license)). One report says the license also forbids removing synthetic-media transparency features ([NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/ltx-2-5-adds-native-multi-shot-video-and-a-diffusion-decoder/)); the full text was not verified, and ProofNotFluff labels AI content anyway. The cost per clip is low. A rented RTX 5090 costs $1.19 an hour ([RunPod](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-cloud/pricing)), and community timings put an 8-second 720p clip at about 80 seconds on that card ([OpenClawDC](https://openclawdc.com/blog/can-i-run-ltx-2-5-locally/)). That works out to **about $0.02 to $0.04 of warm GPU time per 10-second clip**, against $1.30 to $1.70 on LTX's own API ([AIReiter](https://aireiter.com/blog/ltx-2-5-api-pricing-guide)); doubling the self-hosted figure covers cold starts and failed takes.

So the answer to Todd's question is yes, with limits. LTX-2.5 replaces the b-roll and product-motion part of Higgsfield at a small fraction of the cost and with permission to automate. It does not replace Higgsfield's menu of models, its avatar and lip-sync studios, or its one-click interface, and users of the earlier LTX-2.3 reported subjects drifting in image-to-video and eyes degrading in multi-person scenes ([Magic Hour](https://magichour.ai/blog/ltx-2-3-vs-wan-2-2)). Where a product's label or logo must stay exact, Wan 2.2 (Apache 2.0) holds identity better but has no audio, and in one hosted test it took about four minutes for a 5-second 720p clip that LTX-2.3 finished in under 45 seconds ([Magic Hour](https://magichour.ai/blog/ltx-2-3-vs-wan-2-2)). Neither is frontier quality. The September 2, 2026 top ten of the Artificial Analysis video arena is led by Gemini Omni Flash, Wan 3.0 and MiniMax H3 Max, and LTX-2.5 is not on the list ([BenchmarkList](https://benchmarklist.com/arenas/artificial_analysis_text_to_video/)). Wan 3.0 has no public weights, and MiniMax H3's open weights exclude US use ([HowAIWorks](https://howaiworks.ai/blog/alibaba-wan-open-weights-stopped-at-2-2); [Creative AI News](https://www.creativeainews.com/articles/best-open-source-ai-video-models-2026/)).

## Compliance turns on who speaks, what they claim and where the disclosure sits

The rules converge on one pattern: never fabricate a person or an experience, disclose the money inside the content, and label realistic AI. This section is planning information, not legal advice, and the items flagged for counsel should be confirmed before launch.

### An AI customer is a fake testimonial under federal law

The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule took effect **October 21, 2024**, and its Statement of Basis and Purpose says plainly that "AI-generated reviews are covered by the final rule" ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-22/html/2024-18519.htm)). Section 465.2(a) makes it a violation to "write, create, or sell" a review or testimonial that misrepresents that the testimonialist exists or used the product, and the rule defines a testimonialist as the person "giving or purportedly giving" the testimonial ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465)). Knowing violators face civil penalties of up to **$53,088 per violation**, the 2025 level, which still applies because the 2026 inflation adjustment was cancelled ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/87476); [Federal Register](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-18853.pdf)). An operator who deliberately builds AI personas has that knowledge by design. Enforcement is active: Operation AI Comply targeted AI "passive income" storefront schemes in September 2024 ([Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/ftc-cracks-down-on-deceptive-ai-practices/)), and the FTC sent Consumer Review Rule warning letters to 10 companies in December 2025 ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/consumer-products-and-retail-navigator/2026/01/ftc-warning-letters-over-consumer-review-rule)). That same month the FTC set aside its order against Rytr, an AI tool that could bulk-generate reviews, but that decision concerned a tool provider's liability, and counsel read it as no retreat from review enforcement ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/327399); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/in-rare-move-ftc-sets-aside-rytr-order-for-burdening-ai-innovation-and-failing-to-plead-violations)). The FTC draws its line between a fake customer and an obvious dramatization: paid actors in a clearly fictional ad are not covered unless the testimonials themselves are false ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-22/html/2024-18519.htm)). That is why a clearly labeled brand voice is a different legal object from an AI "customer."

### Affiliate disclosure has to live inside the video

The Endorsement Guides require disclosures that are "difficult to miss," "unavoidable" on social media and, in video, made in both the audio and the visuals, and they give the example of an influencer who "relies solely upon a social media platform's built-in disclosure tool" as one that fails ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-07-26/html/2023-14795.htm)). The FTC's FAQ gets specific about wording. It suggests "I get commissions for purchases made through links in this post," calls "paid link" beside a link adequate, warns that consumers may not understand "affiliate link" and that "commissionable link" is probably unclear, and notes that viewers easily miss disclosures placed in a video description ([FTC FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)). The endorser must be a bona fide user, the advertiser is liable for a deceptive endorsement even when the endorser is not, and testimonials never substitute for evidence that a claim is true ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-07-26/html/2023-14795.htm)). Platforms are automating enforcement. In September 2026 YouTube began rolling out automated systems to catch undeclared promotions ([YouTube changelog](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10008196?hl=en#zippy=%2Ccommunity-guidelines)), and TikTok flags posts with links, codes or product recommendations and gives the creator 24 hours to switch on the commercial-content setting before the video loses For You eligibility ([TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-the-content-disclosure-setting-for-creators)).

### New York, the platforms, TikTok Shop and Amazon each add a layer

**New York.** The state's synthetic performer law took effect **June 9, 2026**. It requires anyone who produces an ad, knowing it contains a human-looking AI performer, to disclose that conspicuously, with penalties of $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 after; audio-only ads are exempt ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-29-new-york-enacts-synthetic-performer-disclosure-law-for-advertisements-including-those-using-generative-ai); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/new-york-enacts-law-regulating-the-use-of-ai-generated-synthetic-performers-in-advertising)). The December 2025 federal executive order targeting state AI laws does not suspend it ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/new-york-mandates-ai-disclosure-in-ads-as-white-house-moves-to-block-state-ai-laws/)). Whether an affiliate promoting someone else's product is covered is unsettled and a question for counsel. With no synthetic people on screen, ProofNotFluff stays outside the definition, and a voice-only narrator over real screen footage likely does too, though that is untested.

**The platforms.** TikTok requires labels when AI realistically depicts people or scenes ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/tiktok/tiktok-community-guidelines/ai-generated-content-labeling-requirement/)) and lets users turn AI content down in their feeds ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-content?lang=en)). YouTube requires its "AI use" disclosure for photorealistic altered or generated content but not for script help or a creator's own cloned voice; it says disclosure itself does not limit reach, and it may penalize creators who consistently fail to disclose ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). Meta requires self-disclosure of photorealistic AI video and realistic AI audio and may penalize accounts that don't ([Meta letter to Sen. Warner](https://www.warner.senate.gov/public/_cache/files/7/1/71cdcf73-5cce-49c4-8713-0102c072b9cf/A5A52F05BD96683C10F08525788CBB67.meta-response.pdf)). Pinterest labels AI images automatically and lets users see fewer AI pins in categories including home decor and beauty ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/30/pinterest-launches-new-tools-to-fight-ai-slop); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/pinterest-ai/)).

**TikTok Shop.** The May 23, 2026 rules ban AI-generated voices, recorded audio and dominant animated figures in shopping LIVEs ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-bans-ai-generated-voices-in-shopping-livestreams/822977/)).

**Amazon.** Amazon requires the sentence "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases" and a disclosure at each link. It bans cloaked links and any use of its reviews, ratings, prices or cached images outside its APIs, and it requires any AI agent that touches its content to identify itself. Violations can bring immediate termination and permanent loss of commissions ([Amazon Operating Agreement](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/agreement); [Amazon Program Policies](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/policies)).

For ProofNotFluff the table collapses into one house standard the legal desk can check mechanically. No synthetic person appears on screen by default; the narrator speaks as the brand, never as a customer, and makes no first-person experience claims except about tools the crew really uses. Every number on screen traces to a primary-source URL or to the calculator's own output. Any piece that recommends a third-party product says so in its first seconds, aloud and on screen ("Ad: we earn a commission if you sign up through our link"), starts its caption with "Ad:" and puts "(paid link)" beside every link, with the platform toggles switched on as well, never instead. Realistic AI b-roll carries the platform AI label and never depicts a result. Posts carrying Amazon links are never boosted, and no affiliate link sits inside a downloadable file. Shorts and Reels promote the free calculator, while the recommendation and its disclosure live on the page beside the link, which is where the FTC says a single disclosure works best ([FTC FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)).

Several rules here rest on trade press rather than the platform's own page. TikTok Shop's May 2026 rules, Meta's branded-content and AI ad policies, Meta's terms on personal accounts and Pinterest's affiliate rules were not read at the source, and no state law besides New York was verified. Two operational questions are also open: whether Buffer passes TikTok's AI-generated and commercial-content flags through its API, and whether TikTok's toggle applies to pure affiliate posts. Until the legal desk confirms these at primary sources, the in-video and caption disclosures carry the load. A one-time review of the disclosure standard by counsel would settle the New York question and the open question of how the FTC counts violations; whether to pay for it is Todd's call.

## Extend the crew with a registry, a gate and a job ledger

Public AI UGC pipelines are generation chains: a trigger, a vision model describing the product, a script model, an image model, a video model, then delivery, with no quality, compliance or analytics stage. A popular n8n template claims under $1 a video and 5 to 10 minutes per video but reports no QA step and no outcome data ([GrowwStacks](https://growwstacks.com/blog/ai-ugc-video-ads-veo-n8n/)). ProofNotFluff already runs the stages those templates lack (a legal desk, an optimizer, a scoreboard), so the add-on is mostly new duties for existing agents plus one scout.

| COO | COO | Weekly read of the scoreboard; turns gate decisions into one-tap quests; keeps the account recovery plan current | Budget tier and the service test |

**Video jobs should never be awaited inside one scheduled run.** Generation takes minutes, a naive polling loop can "run forever, lose the task state, or hide failed generations," and the fix n8n users converged on is bounded polling with job state stored outside the run ([n8n Community](https://community.n8n.io/t/how-to-build-bounded-async-polling-for-seedance-video-generation-in-n8n/303711?tl=en); [n8n](https://n8n.io/workflows/12684-async-video-polling-engine-background-job-handler-for-ai-video-generation/)). In the crew's terms, the producer submits a job and writes a row to the scoreboard (job ID, model, prompt hash, attempt count, deadline and cost), and the next run polls it, retries within a cap or marks it failed.

**Failures and tooling need logging and gates.** Model APIs fail opaquely: Veo users saw "internal server issue" errors for days while the status page showed green ([Google AI Developers Forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/video-generation-failed-due-to-an-internal-server-issue/116167)). Logging every attempt's cost matters because published per-video costs ignore failed generations. Two production tools need specific gates. Remotion's agent skills let Claude build templated motion graphics, but a successful preview does not guarantee a successful render, so the gate checks the render ([Tella](https://www.tella.com/blog/how-to-use-remotion-agent-skills-with-claude-code)). Comfy's official MCP has been in public beta since June 29, 2026 with unpublished pricing, so it stays a trial tool ([MCP.Directory](https://mcp.directory/blog/comfy-mcp)). Third-party agent skills never reach agents that hold publishing credentials without review in a sandbox; one public skill registry reportedly carried about 900 malicious skills ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/openclaw-clawd-bot-youtube-creators-2026/)).

**Human gates should be few and sharp.** The FTC says a company "is ultimately responsible for what others do on your behalf" ([FTC FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)), and an agent crew counts as "others." That argues for a handful of human gates rather than a review of every post. Todd handles accounts, sign-ups, applications and money, as the house rules already require. He approves the first published piece of every new format and decides on any synthetic person (off by default) and any client deliverable, and any platform warning pauses the affected pipeline automatically and goes to him. Everything else is checked by a separate reviewer agent so that no model grades its own work, following Meta's published design for comparison tests, in which a separate judge sees all versions side by side ([GitHub](https://github.com/facebook/astryx/wiki/Designing-Vibe-Tests)).

**Accounts follow identity rules.** Capri's lost account turns into four operating rules. Personal profiles belong only to the real, verifiable owner, never to a character or a brand. Any character or brand runs as a Facebook Page or an Instagram professional account owned by that verified person, which is also the structure Meta's messaging API assumes: a professional account linked to a Page ([Meta for Developers: private replies](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/messenger-platform/instagram/features/private-replies/)). Ownership records are in order before growth arrives, because a viral spike appears to be what drew Capri's identity check. And Meta enforcement is treated as automated with weak appeal routes: Meta removed about 10 million impersonating profiles in the first half of 2025 alone ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-meta-cracks-down-on-unoriginal-facebook-content/)), and Capri reports no human to appeal to. The COO keeps a written recovery plan in the repo that lists every account, its owner of record, its recovery email and two-factor method, its latest content export, and the owned channel that takes over its traffic if it disappears.

## Judge on clicks and referrals, and never kill before a data floor

Revenue arrives too late to steer by. YouTube Shopping pays 60 to 120 days after a purchase ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13376398?hl=en)), Shopify pays only after the referred merchant's first full-price month ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/affiliates/earnings)), FreshBooks holds commissions through a 30-day window ([PartnerStack](https://market.partnerstack.com/page/freshbooksusa)), and Notion claws back commissions on refunds and downgrades within two months ([Notion](https://notion.com/affiliates)). Outbound clicks are therefore the leading metric, and the sign-ups each network reports confirm them.

Attribution has to be designed in, because the networks will not do it for you. Amazon allows 100 tracking IDs per account and reports only totals by ID ([Amazon Associates Help](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GJDYPQZK6E37RLPU)), and it rarely passes sub-IDs through, so click-level attribution is usually impossible ([wecantrack](https://wecantrack.com/amazon-integration/)). TikTok Shop does report GMV per video, with a 24 to 48-hour lag ([Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/track-tiktok-shop-affiliate-performance-across-multiple-creators); [Archive](https://archive.com/blog/track-tiktok-shop-seeding-affiliate)). Sub-ID support on Impact (Shopify, Canva) and PartnerStack (Gusto, FreshBooks, QuickBooks) was not researched and should be checked at application; where a network has none, one link per page cluster keeps the data readable. Every published piece gets one scoreboard row carrying a concept ID, hook family, format, platform, landing page and attribution key, so outcomes can be joined later.

Hit rates set how patient to be. In Motion's dataset of 578,750 Meta creatives, about 5% became winners, and only 3.8% to 4.0% for accounts spending under $10,000 a month; testing four ads a week yields about one winner every five weeks ([Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/winners-are-rare)). Common Thread Collective found that 3.5% of ads drew 66% of spend and that the median outlier took 39 days to surface ([Common Thread](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/the-ecommerce-creative-testing-framework-from-gambling-to-math)). These are paid-ad numbers and organic rates may differ, but they imply the crew should measure 20 to 25 distinct concepts before judging whether the system works. In the one organic test found, the hook mattered more than anything else: the same video with two different five-second openings drew about 12,000 and about 450,000 views ([LinkedIn](https://ca.linkedin.com/in/ailis)).

| What is judged | Data floor before judging | Kill or scale rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| A hook family (organic) | At least 3 posts per version, posted 24 to 48 hours apart, read after 48 to 72 hours | Retire it after losing 3 of 3 or 3 of 4; a 2-2 split means the hook does not matter | Practitioner heuristic ([TT Calculator](https://ttcalculator.net/guides/growth/tiktok-ab-testing-content/)) |
| A format | Its first 10 pieces | Stop it after two originality failures or any platform notice | YouTube and Meta enforcement above |
| An affiliate program | 100 outbound clicks | Zero sign-ups at 100 clicks points to wrong placement or audience; move it once, then drop it | Canva's own funnel implies about 3 paying users per 100 clicks ([Canva](https://www.canva.com/affiliates/hubpage/how-tos)) |
| A paid boost (own products only) | 5,000 impressions, 2,000 impressions, or $50 of spend, by rule | Kill if hook rate is under 15% at 24 hours, CTR is under 0.5% at 48 hours, or cost per sale is above twice target at 3 days | Published agent skill, heuristic ([skills.sh](https://www.skills.sh/synter-media-ai/free-skills/kill-scale-rules)) |
| A b-roll model or tool | A fixed set of 10 test prompts | Adopt only if it beats the current tool on judged quality and on cost per usable clip | Meta's comparison-test method ([GitHub](https://github.com/facebook/astryx/wiki/Designing-Vibe-Tests)) |
| The whole content system | 20 to 25 distinct concepts | Do not judge the system on fewer | About 4% to 5% of creatives win ([Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/winners-are-rare)) |
| The AI UGC service test | 30 tailored spec pitches | Zero paid batches means stop | Even the unverified 5% to 10% pitch claim predicts 1.5 to 3 clients ([Vantaige](https://vantaige.io/blog/how-to-make-money-selling-ai-ugc-content-brands-2026)) |
| The add-on at day 90 | 90 days | Go with at least one confirmed referral; under 100 clicks is a traffic problem; 100 or more clicks with no sign-ups is a placement problem | Synthesis of the rows above |

## A scout that promotes tools only after they ship

The scout's job is to keep the system current without letting unproven tools into production, and the video leaderboard shows why it should re-test monthly. Seedance 2.0 led the Artificial Analysis video arena in April 2026 ([Magic Hour](https://magichour.ai/model-leaderboard/text-to-video)); by September 2, Google's Gemini Omni Flash, Alibaba's Wan 3.0 and MiniMax H3 Max sat within three points of each other at the top ([BenchmarkList](https://benchmarklist.com/arenas/artificial_analysis_text_to_video/)). Some sources are machine-readable at no cost. The Hugging Face Hub serves trending models, datasets and spaces as JSON with no key ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/scrapemint/the-hugging-face-hub-is-a-free-json-api-rank-trending-ai-models-without-a-key-39cb)), and Google's Trends API, still an alpha that requires an application, returns consistently scaled values across five years and across regions ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/apis/trends)). Others are not: TikTok's Creative Center has no API and must be read through a browser ([AdCreate](https://adcreate.com/blog/tiktok-creative-center-find-trending-ads)), and the TikTok Shop data tools are dashboards whose accuracy claims are unverified ([Virlo](https://virlo.ai/compare/kalodata-vs-fastmoss)).

| Cadence | What the scout reads | What it produces |
|---|---|---|
| Daily | Hugging Face trending models through the keyless API; arena rank mirrors; the crew's own analytics | A short change file: new models, rank moves, metric outliers |
| Weekly | Official policy pages (YouTube changelog, a TikTok guidelines tracker, FTC press releases, Amazon's change page, Etsy affiliate terms); registry pages due for re-reading; trend sources | A digest for the COO with anything that changes a rule, a price or a candidate |
| Monthly | A fixed set of test prompts run against the current b-roll and voice tools | Ring moves (Assess, Trial, Adopt, Hold) with cost per usable clip |
| Quarterly | The ring list as a whole | Drop anything unchanged for two cycles; revive at most one dropped item |

Promotion follows an evidence bar borrowed from the Thoughtworks Technology Radar, which places items in "Trial" only after seeing them deployed in production and lets an item fade after two editions with no change of opinion ([Thoughtworks](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-gb/insights/blog/how-we-create-technology-radar)). For the crew, Assess means the scout has read about a tool, Trial means it produced at least one published piece that passed the legal desk, Adopt means it beat the current tool on the fixed test set and on cost per usable clip, and Hold means it failed or a policy blocks it. The monthly bake-off follows Meta's published method for comparison tests: 5 to 15 prompts that describe the real task, 2 to 5 conditions that each change one variable, every run isolated, objective checks scripted in advance, and harder-to-measure quality judged by a separate agent that sees all versions side by side ([GitHub](https://github.com/facebook/astryx/wiki/Designing-Vibe-Tests)).

The scout also hunts for angles, not only tools. Its first month's open questions are affiliate programs for field-service software (for the home-services pages) and for short-term-rental software, Gumroad's own affiliate feature (which could recruit other people to sell the $79 workbook), Instagram's eligibility rules for Reels affiliate links, and any new primary-source evidence on AI versus human creative. Income claims, including those of creators like Capri, enter the ledger only with payment-verified evidence, such as a TrustMRR dashboard, or a primary record.

## Reach, sameness and term drift outrank account bans

**Reach is the most likely failure.** Affiliate income scales with visits to the pages that carry links, and nothing in this plan creates visits faster than the site's search growth and the existing posting schedule. The big AI reach stories are warnings, not models. Kapwing modeled the ten top-earning AI-slop YouTube channels at a combined $33.6 million a year ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/one-in-five-youtube-shorts-shown-to-new-users-is-ai-generated-slop-study-finds/)), and the largest by subscribers was later among 16 channels removed in a January 2026 enforcement wave, 11 terminated and 5 wiped ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-cant-tell-a-directed-ai-film-from-a-bot-farm)). The defense is owned search traffic, with clicks as the score.

**Sameness is the most likely penalty.** YouTube reviews monetization at the channel level, looking at the main theme, the most-viewed and newest videos, and the metadata ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)), and it terminated two AI movie-trailer channels with over a billion combined views after they slid back into violations ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/tech/youtube-terminates-screen-culture-kh-studio-over-ai-fake-movie-trailers/)). In July 2026 YouTube reportedly named AI personas discussing health, finance or legal topics as one of three categories that cannot earn money ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-cant-tell-a-directed-ai-film-from-a-bot-farm)); ProofNotFluff is not in the Partner Program, but a synthetic narrator on money topics sits close to that line if it ever applies. TikTok and Pinterest let users turn AI content down ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-content?lang=en); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/pinterest-ai/)). The defense is a distinct worked example in every piece, plus the originality check.

**One account can end a business built on it.** Capri's Rose is the clearest case in this research of how a fast-growing AI account dies: roughly 75,000 followers across two platforms, then removal at a single identity check, with a warning now attached to the owner's personal account (all self-reported). Viral growth can draw an identity check, as it apparently did here, and Meta's moderation should be treated as automated with weak appeal routes, so the account rules in the crew section apply before growth, not after. The structural defense is revenue spread across owned channels, meaning the site, the email list, Etsy and Gumroad, so that losing any one social account costs reach, not the business, together with the written recovery plan. One question is Todd's to decide, not the crew's: any personal-profile alias the business runs, such as a LinkedIn personal profile under a name that is not the owner's own, carries the same identity-verification risk, because LinkedIn's user agreement requires real names on personal profiles. LinkedIn's agreement page blocked automated reading in this pass, so the exact clause should be confirmed at the source ([LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)).

**Legal exposure is unlikely but expensive.** The fines run up to $53,088 per FTC violation and $1,000 to $5,000 per New York ad, Amazon can terminate an account and keep its commissions, and brands such as SharkNinja claw back commissions for AI content even where the platform allows it ([Newswav/The Independent](https://newswav.com/article/brands-urge-tiktok-shop-creators-to-ditch-ai-fakes-report-says-A2607_sFmfB6)). The house standard, the registry's record of each brand's AI policy, and one counsel review contain this.

**Program terms drift without notice.** Amazon cut negotiated publisher rates by up to 50% with no announcement in 2026 ([eMarketer](https://emarketer.com/content/amazon-cuts-affiliate-commissions-by-up-50--raising-pressure-on-publishers)), and its onsite table now pays less than its 2023 table did ([Amazon](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GWSMACYG6EJMNW74)). Notion has closed its program to new affiliates. Etsy's October 9, 2026 terms reversed its old ban on sellers earning from their own shops ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/affiliates/terms); [Awin](https://www.awin.com/us/news-and-events/awin-news/etsys-affiliate-program-now-open-to-sellers)), and YouTube's 100% commission incentive is temporary ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13376398?hl=en)). The registry's 30-day re-read exists for exactly this.

**Vendors and models fail in expensive ways.** Higgsfield's fair-use rules forbid automation. Under Arcads' terms, if an actor withdraws, the client must delete the videos everywhere or pay €100 a day, and Arcads may train on client inputs ([Arcads terms](https://www.arcads.ai/terms)). Credit-based tools charge for unusable generations ([Superscale](https://superscale.ai/alternatives/reelfarm/review)). Open models, the job ledger and a cost log per attempt contain this.

**The niche boundary needs active screening.** Health, a category that includes supplements, has drawn $4.0 billion in lifetime US TikTok Shop sales by one tracker's count ([SmartScout](https://www.smartscout.com/blog/tiktok-shop-statistics-2026)), and the full-time AI video roles in the Upwork snapshot came from skincare and wellness brands. Every client list and program screen excludes supplements, longevity and wellness products through late 2027. Products built on Todd's e-commerce finance experience should draw on public program terms and his general know-how, never on a former employer's documents or data.

**Opportunity cost is real but small.** The shop's first sales remain the priority, and the add-on rides on pages and walkthroughs that already exist. Each calculator page keeps its paid product as the first call to action, with the affiliate block after the result as the next step. If affiliate blocks pull clicks away from listings without producing sign-ups, they move lower on the page.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. AI generation is now a commodity priced from cents to about $11 a video, so the scarce inputs are trust, originality, verifiable identity and owned traffic, which are exactly what the FTC, New York and every major platform have moved to protect over the past two years. ProofNotFluff's constraints (no invented people, sourced claims, a faceless brand voice, a real owner behind every account) look like handicaps next to avatar-led competitors. In October 2026 they are the parts of the system that regulators and recommendation systems reward. The formats they allow, real calculators running real numbers, sit where AI production holds up best: cheap, demonstrable products with no fake human whose credibility can collapse.

The verified money in this market went to tool vendors and to agencies with large clients. The faceless fortunes stayed unverified, and the one persona case examined here lost its main account at its first identity check. A finance-tools brand built on real e-commerce finance work does not need to compete in that crowd. It can sell the math instead: referral links beside its own calculators, and calculators for the people pricing AI UGC and chasing TikTok commissions. Evidence, not enthusiasm, should change this verdict. That could be a payment-verified faceless operator, a calculator page that converts at scale, or a policy shift that makes synthetic presenters safe for a finance brand. The scout exists to notice any of the three.
