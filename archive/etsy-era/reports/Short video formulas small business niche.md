# Trade the template for sendable proof

ProofNotFluff's short videos are failing because of sameness and missing stakes, not because of volume, voice or the lack of a face. All three platforms now score the first three seconds and the share of viewers who stay. The account has no followers, so on Instagram every view comes from "unconnected" recommendation, and there **sends per reach and watch time count most** ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)). A daily "$X an hour looks fine, break-even is $Y" clip with the same numbers and the same voice gives a stranger no new reason to watch past second two, and no reason to send it to anyone. The formulas that work in this niche show a **visible real-world stake** (a job, an order, a paycheck) and resolve it with a specific number, the way trades channels turn a messy yard into a clean one ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/ar/inspiration/yard-maintenance-geelong)). Agents can render, voice, post and measure four such formulas at $0 using code (HyperFrames or the current Playwright pipeline, Kokoro TTS, the Instagram Graph API, the YouTube Data API and Buffer). A short list of human steps remains: checking Instagram Account Status, setting the Shorts related-video link in YouTube Studio, accepting Collabs, and optionally recording a human voice for an A/B test. The owner should **not** adopt a realistic AI avatar. It is not feasible on a CPU-only $0 stack, it triggers Instagram's AI-person profile label and reach limits, and disclosure measurably lowers engagement, all for a brand whose name promises proof. Short video should be judged on credibility, follows and visits to the free calculators. Sales of a $2.99 template will come mostly from search channels (Etsy, Pinterest), with video feeding trust.

## Zero likes on 21 Reels is a distribution failure first

The owner's data describes two distinct problems. On Instagram, 21 Reels in 12 days earned 0 likes, 0 comments and 0 followers. That is unusual even for a new account, because Reels normally get some non-follower test impressions. The first step is diagnostic, not creative. Instagram confirms that content which breaks its Recommendation Guidelines can still reach followers but is kept from non-followers, and **Settings > Account Status** is where that restriction shows ([Instagram for Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/tips-for-improving-your-reach)). With 0 followers, an ineligible account gets near-zero views. Since Apr 30, 2026, accounts that "primarily" post unoriginal content also lose recommendation eligibility. ProofNotFluff's self-rendered demos should count as "content you designed", but the flag is visible in Account Status and appealable there ([Instagram for Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram/)). If the account is eligible and the Reels drew non-follower views, the problem is the hook and the format. Instagram's **Skip Rate**, added in August 2025, is the share of viewers who scroll past within the first 3 seconds, and agencies treat anything over about 50% as a failing hook (industry estimates, not official) ([BabbleBoxx](https://www.babbleboxx.com/post/instagram-adds-reels-retention-skip-rate-what-influencer-marketers-should-do-next)).

YouTube's drop from 700 to 1,000 views on Oct 3 and 4 to under 10 since Oct 12 fits how Shorts are distributed. YouTube's Shorts lead has described Shorts going first to a small "seed audience" before wider promotion ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). Since Mar 31, 2025, any start or replay counts as a "view", so the early 700 to 1,000 says little about real watching. The useful numbers are engaged views and **Viewed vs. swiped away** ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/)). The likeliest reading, which is an inference and not a YouTube statement, is that the first template Shorts were tested, swipe and retention data came back weak, and later Shorts in the same template got smaller tests. Comparing Viewed vs. swiped on the Oct 3 and 4 Shorts against the later ones will confirm or rule this out. YouTube's own policy language names this pattern. Its July 2025 "inauthentic content" update lists "slideshows that all have the same narration" and stories with "only superficial differences" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)), and the policy page flags "content that looks like it's made with a template" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). Officially this is a monetization rule, and no YouTube source says it suppresses reach. A viewer who sees the same structure twice still swipes, though, and swipes are what the seed test measures.

Volume is not the bottleneck. Buffer's 2.1M-post Instagram dataset found that reach per post *rises* with frequency, with the gains flattening past 3 to 5 posts a week ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)). At about 1.75 Reels a day, ProofNotFluff already posts above Buffer's top band. Supply is the bigger headwind. Metricool found short-form posting volume grew 70% in 2025 while Instagram and YouTube reach fell ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/social-media-short-video-report-2025/)), which makes a near-duplicate daily post easy to skip. Cross-posting one file is not penalized in any rule found. The risk is a visible watermark: TikTok makes clips carrying another platform's logo ineligible for the For You feed ([ContentIQ summary of TikTok rules](https://contentiq.media/rules/tiktok)), and Instagram is "less likely" to recommend watermarked content ([Instagram for Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/tips-for-improving-your-reach)). The bigger cost of one shared file is that it cannot fit every platform. Instagram's best-performing length bucket is twice what works on TikTok, and Instagram's UI covers a larger share of the bottom of the frame.

## Winners in adjacent niches sell a visible stake, not a spreadsheet

No 2024 to 2026 source documents a faceless spreadsheet or pricing-calculator account growing on short video with real numbers. That gap is itself a finding. The best-documented case, Miss Excel (Kat Norton), is face-on. She posted one 14-second tip a day over a green screen with dance trends, her 4th video hit 100K views, her first corporate-training lead arrived after video 6, and the free videos fed $44 to $997 courses ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/miss-excel-breakdown)). Her lesson for a faceless brand is that **the format is the brand**. One instantly recognisable device repeated daily, with a different tip inside it every time, made the account followable.

The closest faceless analogues come from trades. SB Mowing built a reported **40M followers** from time-lapse cleanups of neglected yards. The writer's thesis is "give people what they want to see rather than what you want to tell them" ([Why Is This Interesting](https://whyisthisinteresting.substack.com/p/the-viral-yard-cleanup-edition)). Yard Maintenance Geelong reached 51K TikTok followers and 1.6M views on its top video with yard transformations, voiceover explaining technique, and location tags, and that brought local leads and brand deals ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/ar/inspiration/yard-maintenance-geelong)). Structurally, a transformation and a number reveal are the same thing: tension (a mess, or a price that looks fine) resolved on screen. ProofNotFluff's template already has the reveal. It lacks the *job*: a named person, a specific driveway or order, a cost the viewer can feel. The large Excel accounts on Favikon's 2025 list share a second pattern. Quick free tips lead to a free lead magnet (an eBook, guide or newsletter), and that leads to a paid product ([Favikon](https://www.favikon.com/blog/top-excel-office-productivity-influencers-worldwide)). ProofNotFluff's free web calculators already fill that middle slot.

The sales evidence argues for patience about what video can do. Spreadsheet sellers with documented revenue credit search, not virality. ChartSheets reports $40,000 a month and calls Pinterest "a game-changer" ([Niall Doherty](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/25460887-40-000-per-month-selling-spreadsheet-templates-via-pinterest)), and Emily McDermott credits Etsy SEO alone for $280K in under two years ([Medium](https://emily-mcdermott.medium.com/how-ive-made-280k-selling-spreadsheets-on-etsy-51b0759a9465)). Omni Calculator's paid TikTok push produced 5.48M impressions, a 3.77% CTR and 48K followers, yet TikTok still drove only **0.91% of site sessions** ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/inspiration/omni-calculator-tiktok-success-story)). At a 1 to 3% Etsy conversion rate ([Gelato](https://www.gelato.com/blog/etsy-conversion-rate)), one $2.99 sale needs roughly 33 to 100 listing visits. If even 1% of viewers click, that is 3,300 to 10,000 views per sale. Video's realistic job is to earn trust, follows and free-calculator visits, which feed email and later purchases. The Preceden case adds a caution: free tools that draw traffic convert poorly unless the paid product does something the free one doesn't ([MicroConf](https://microconf.com/latest/engineering-as-marketing)). The $2.99 template therefore needs a visible upgrade over the free calculator, such as saved jobs, multi-job tracking or a printable quote.

Link mechanics decide where those visits can come from. Shorts description and comment links have not been clickable since Aug 31, 2023 ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-adds-shorts-links-limits-links-elsewhere/495463)), which matches the owner's finding. The only in-player link is a "related video" to the channel's own content ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en)). YouTube's July 2026 guidance is to pair a spoken CTA with a visual pointer in the last 5 seconds and to make the linked video address the topic within 5 to 10 seconds ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)). On Instagram, the free official route is the Private Replies API: one DM per commenter, within 7 days, holding the link itself ([Meta](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/docs/instagram/messaging-api/private-replies)). The alternative, ManyChat's free plan, caps at 25 active contacts a month ([ManyChat](https://help.manychat.com/hc/en-us/articles/25800197498652-Free-plan)).

## Four formulas a code renderer can make well

The four formulas below rank by fit to the signals that matter at 0 followers (sends and watch time from strangers), by what the niche evidence supports, and by how fully agents can automate them. No 2025 to 2026 study compares hook types or educational structures with published numbers. Viral statistics such as "71% decide in 3 seconds" trace to misread ad studies ([Revid](https://www.revid.ai/short-form-video-statistics)). The craft rules therefore combine platform guidance with documented niche mechanics, and the plan tests them against the account's own metrics instead of assuming they work.

| Rank | Formula | What it borrows | Signal it targets | Audience rotation | Conversion role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Job Ticket**: a named job priced line by line until the true take-home appears | Trades transformation (mess → clean) | Watch time, sends | Cleaner, groomer, pressure washer, photographer, freelancer, one Etsy order, one household paycheck | Free calculator, "price your own job" |
| 2 | **Spot the Mistake**: a quote, price tag or budget with one hidden error, then a pause before the reveal | Quiz and rewatch | Watch time, rewatches, comments | Same rotation | Soft brand CTA |
| 3 | **Myth vs Math**: one belief everyone repeats, broken by one line of arithmetic | Person-to-person forwarding | Sends per reach | Etsy fees, "$50/hr is plenty", "a 20% tax set-aside is enough", sinking funds | Send prompt, no shop CTA |
| 4 | **Run Your Numbers**: the free calculator filled in live, with a formula card the viewer saves | Miss Excel-style single tip and free sample | Saves, site visits | Rotates by calculator | Primary link CTA (comment keyword or bio) |

A **numbered series name** on every video (for example "Price Check #14") works as the persona device that Miss Excel's dancing provided. It gives viewers a reason to follow and gives the account the consistent topic metadata that Instagram's user-editable "Your Algorithm" interest topics match against ([about.instagram.com](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/reels-algorithm-control)). YouTube explicitly allows "same intro and outro … but the bulk of your content is different", and allows series where "each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). The brand sting and series card can stay constant, but the formula, persona, numbers and structure must change from one day to the next. The old "$X looks fine, break-even is $Y" line survives only as one hook option inside the Job Ticket.

### Craft rules that apply to every formula

The first frame must already carry the tension: the big number, a visible change and the spoken line landing together. TikTok's guidance is to introduce the proposition in the first 3 seconds and use "suspense, surprise, or other emotion" ([TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)). Bytecap's hook synthesis adds that the voice and the headline should state the same promise, and warns against "a reveal that gives no reason to keep watching" ([Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-hooks-for-youtube-shorts)). In practice: "This $180 driveway paid $61. Here's where $119 went" leaves the *why* open. "$40 looks fine, break-even is $74.65" gives the payoff away. The narration starts at frame 0, never after a silent title. Opening on an empty sheet that "types" rows is the classic Skip Rate failure.

The middle needs a visible change every 1.5 to 3 seconds. That interval is a creator convention, not a measured threshold. Use punch-in zooms on the active cell, highlight and dim, rows slamming in with a tick sound, and a counter that rolls from the "looks fine" number to the real one. Multimedia-learning meta-analyses find that visual signaling (highlighting, zooming to the relevant part) has a strong effect on what viewers remember, with no reported downside. That effect is on remembering, not on watch time ([QIC-WD summary](https://www.qic-wd.org/sites/default/files/Umbrella%20Summary%20-%20Signaling%20in%20Training%20111021.pdf)). Never show the full grid at a size nobody can read. Put the reveal at about 40% of runtime, the fix at about 70%, and end on a frame that matches frame 1 so an autoplay restart reads as continuous. Captions are burned in, synced word by word, kept within TikTok's 5 to 10 words per second, and placed inside the safe band of a 1080x1920 frame (roughly y 270 to 1250 on Reels, since Instagram's UI covers about the bottom 35%) ([House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/guide-to-safe-zones-tiktok-facebook-instagram-stories-reels/)). Instagram says over 75% of Reels views are sound-on ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2024/11/07/unlock-the-power-of-reel-ads/)), so audio quality matters more than captions alone suggest. Master at about -14 LUFS, YouTube's playback reference, which does not raise quiet audio ([MeterPlugs](https://www.meterplugs.com/blog/2019/09/18/youtube-changes-loudness-reference-to-14-lufs.html)). If the masters are still at the -24 LUFS noted earlier, they play noticeably quieter than neighboring Shorts. Duck the music at least 10 LU under the voice ([University of Salford](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1371938/background-ducking-to-produce-esthetically-pleasing-audio-for-tv-with-clear-speech)).

Length should differ by platform. In Socialinsider's 6M-Reel 2026 dataset, Reels under 30 seconds had the lowest median views (4,700) and 45 to 60 seconds the highest (10,374) ([Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/)). On TikTok, 16 to 30 seconds led a 2.39M-video index ([Revid](https://www.revid.ai/short-form-video-statistics)), and 53% of surveyed Shorts creators say under 30 seconds performs best ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study)). All of this is correlational, so one script should produce two cuts: **40 to 55 seconds for Reels**, with a second worked example or the 12-month cost, and **20 to 30 seconds for TikTok and Shorts**. Every number on screen must come from the product files and a published rate (the IRS mileage rate, Etsy's fee schedule) with the source shown. That is the faceless equivalent of Omni Calculator's credibility play: transparent formulas, an editorial policy and citations ([Omni Calculator](https://www.omnicalculator.com/omni-by-the-numbers)).

### Formula-specific rules

**The Job Ticket** should open on the job and the take-home number together, add each cost as its own beat (supplies, fuel at the IRS rate, gear wear, card fees, tax set-aside, unpaid quoting and travel time), convert the gap into a felt cost ("one unpaid Saturday a month"), then show the price that fixes it. It is the formula most likely to be sent, so the closing line names who to send it to: "send this to the friend who just started washing driveways." Rotate the persona every post. Include the other three audiences (an Etsy order eaten by fees and shipping, a freelancer's real hourly rate, a household paycheck waterfall) so the account's topic reads as "small-business and household money", not one trade.

**Spot the Mistake** shows the artifact for 2 to 3 seconds with an on-screen "one number here is wrong", holds a short countdown, then circles the error at about 40% and shows its yearly cost at about 70%. The pause invites a rewatch, which raises average watch time. Avoid "comment below" prompts. Meta's engagement-bait guideline covers requests for comments that serve no specific purpose, though the guideline text is written for Facebook and its application to Instagram is not stated ([Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/engagement-bait/)).

**Myth vs Math** is the shortest formula: 15 to 25 seconds on TikTok and Shorts, under 40 on Reels. It states a myth in the viewer's own words, shows one line of arithmetic, and ends on the corrected number plus a send prompt. It carries no shop CTA, because its job is unconnected reach. Mosseri's 2025 guidance is that sends matter "slightly more" for content shown to non-followers ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)).

**Run Your Numbers** is the conversion formula. It fills the real free calculator with three inputs, holds the formula card on screen for 1.5 to 2 seconds as a reason to save, and puts the one CTA on screen *during* the fix, not on an end card at the drop-off point. Use one offer per video, never "free calculator and the $2.99 workbook". On Instagram the CTA is a comment keyword answered by a private reply holding a tracked calculator link. On Shorts it is the spoken and visual "link right here" pointing at the related-video walkthrough. It should run about one post in four, so the account does not read as an ad feed.

## Agents can automate the render and the post, not the judgment

Almost the whole pipeline runs at $0 in a CPU-only sandbox. **HyperFrames** (Apache 2.0, open-sourced by HeyGen) renders HTML, CSS and GSAP or Lottie animation to deterministic MP4 through headless Chrome and FFmpeg. It is the same approach as the current Playwright pipeline, but it adds kinetic captions and audio mixing ([GitHub](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes)). Remotion is also free for a for-profit with up to 3 employees, though its license changes at v5.0 ([Remotion license](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). For voice, Kokoro (Apache 2.0) remains the safe CPU default. Qwen3-TTS (Apache 2.0) and Chatterbox (MIT, watermarked output) are more expressive but GPU-oriented ([BentoML](https://www.bentoml.com/blog/exploring-the-world-of-open-source-text-to-speech-models); [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jan/22/qwen3-tts)). XTTS-v2 and Fish Audio S2 Pro are off-limits for commercial use.

The TTS evidence is mixed rather than damning. In a vendor study of 768 instructional-video viewers, a high-quality AI voice matched or beat human voices on comprehension and "professional" ratings ([TechSmith](https://techsmith.com/blog/ai-voices-avatars-in-training-videos)). In a blind test of 1,326 listeners, 55% of those who heard the AI voice judged it human, with no significant gaps on credibility or likability ([Radio Ink](https://radioink.com/2026/07/07/radio-listeners-cant-detect-ai-voice-but-dont-trust-it-either/)). Yet 58% of consumers say they would trust a brand less if its ads used AI voices ([Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/ai-audio-consumer-trust)). The voice is therefore not the main cause of zero engagement, but it is a credibility risk worth one cheap test. Tune Kokoro's speed and pausing, rotate two or three voices across formulas, and A/B it against a human read.

Publishing and measurement are API-reachable. The Instagram Graph API publishes Reels, carousels, up to 3 collaborators and Trial Reels (`trial_params`, added Dec 3, 2025), with a cap of 50 API posts per rolling 24 hours. Reels insights include `reels_skip_rate`, `ig_reels_avg_watch_time`, `shares` and `saved` ([Meta: Content Publishing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/content-publishing); [Meta: Media Insights](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/reference/instagram-media/insights/)). YouTube uploads now cost 1 unit from a separate 100-a-day bucket, with `publishAt` scheduling and a `containsSyntheticMedia` flag ([YouTube Data API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos/insert)). TikTok's own API keeps unaudited apps private-only, and an audit requires serving a wide audience ([TikTok for Developers](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-sharing-guidelines)). Buffer's free API key (3 channels, 10 queued posts each) is therefore the realistic TikTok route ([Zernio](https://zernio.com/blog/buffer-api)). Trial Reels have one caveat: secondary sources say they expanded in July 2025 only to public accounts with 1,000+ followers ([SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/es/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm)). The agent should try `trial_params` once, and if it is refused, test hooks on the main feed instead.

| Step | Automated by agents | Needs a human |
|---|---|---|
| Scenario bank and scripts | Generate per-video personas and numbers from the product files and published rates. Enforce "no formula twice in a row, no repeated numbers". | Weekly 10-minute skim of the coming scenarios for realism. Owner supplies real customer questions when available. |

Clean masters only, never re-downloaded files. | Set each Short's related video in YouTube Studio (no API field). Accept or send Collab invites. TikTok trending sounds only in-app, and businesses are limited to the Commercial Music Library ([TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/commercial-music-library)). |

Log calculator visits per tracked link. | Check Instagram Account Status once now and monthly. Read Viewed vs. swiped in Studio if no export path works. |

Meta's rule text covers "realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created" ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/)). |

The zero-budget growth lever with the most leverage is human, not code. Instagram Collab posts appear to every co-author's followers, need no follower minimum, and can be added after posting ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-collab-post/)). A trades coach, groomer or Etsy-seller educator gets a free branded Job Ticket about their niche, and ProofNotFluff gets their audience. The owner, or the agent with the owner's approval, has to make that ask.

## A realistic AI avatar would cost the trust the brand name promises

The answer to the owner's question is **no realistic AI avatar**, for five reasons that point the same way. First, it is not feasible at $0. Usable open avatar models (MuseTalk, MagiHuman, Duix-Avatar) need GPUs, and HeyGen's free tier is about three one-minute videos ([Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/self-host-ai-avatar-generator-heygen-alternative-2026/)). Second, it triggers labels and reach limits. Since Aug 31, 2026, Instagram limits the reach of profiles featuring an AI-generated person that don't carry its new label, while creators who "simply use AI tools" need no label ([Instagram for Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/ai-generated-profile-label/)). YouTube requires disclosure of realistic synthetic people and gives finance topics a more prominent label ([Google blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-africa/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/how-were-helping-creators-disclose-altered-or-synthetic-content/)). Third, the label itself costs engagement. A Marketing Science Institute study of TikTok found the "mere presence" of AI disclosures reduces engagement and identification with creators ([MSI](https://msi.org/working-paper/made-with-ai-consumer-engagement-with-media-containing-ai-disclosures)), and 63% of Gen X and Boomers say they would avoid AI-influencer content ([Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/youtube-tests-ai-creator-avatars-as-consumers-remain-wary-of-virtual-influencers-2/)). Those are the older service-business owners this shop sells to. Fourth, platform direction runs against it. Mosseri's year-end memo says "the professional look becomes the tell" and "rawness … is proof" ([Om Malik](https://om.co/2026/01/01/what-is-instagrams-adam-mosseri-really-saying-in-his-year-end-memo/)). In July 2026, YouTube named AI personas of real people discussing finance, legal or health topics as a non-monetizable category ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)). That category would cover an avatar cloned from the owner's own face. Fifth, the FTC does not ban avatars, but an avatar voicing customer experiences that aren't real, attributable testimonials crosses its fake-review rule ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)).

Faceless trades channels already show what works instead: the *work* as the visual plus an explaining voice ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/ar/inspiration/yard-maintenance-geelong)). A stylized, clearly non-realistic device (a cursor character, an illustrated "price inspector" stamp, a consistent pair of animated hands) gives the persona without the label duty. If the owner ever wants a human element, the cheapest and most credible option is the owner's real voice on hooks and CTAs, or real hands at a keyboard filmed on a phone.

## The prioritized plan

**Priority 1, this week: diagnose before creating.** The owner checks Instagram Account Status for recommendation eligibility and confirms the account is public and professional. The agent pulls Skip Rate and non-follower reach for the 21 Reels, and Viewed vs. swiped for the Oct 3 and 4 Shorts against the later ones. If Account Status shows a restriction, fix or appeal it before posting anything else, because no formula can beat ineligibility.

**Priority 2, weeks 1 to 2: replace the template with the four-formula rotation**, about one post a day per platform, with no formula on consecutive days. The weekly mix is roughly 2 Job Tickets, 2 Myth vs Math, 2 Spot the Mistake and 1 Run Your Numbers. Every script gets fresh personas and numbers, two length cuts and clean per-platform masters. Captions carry plain keywords ("pricing calculator", "Etsy fees", "freelance rate"), because public professional Instagram content is indexed by Google since July 2025 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/instagram-content-becomes-searchable-on-google-starting-july-10/)), and use at most 5 hashtags under Instagram's December 2025 cap ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)).

**Priority 3, weeks 2 to 4: wire the conversion paths and the human levers.** These are the comment-keyword private reply on Reels, the related-video link in YouTube Studio to a 60 to 120 second horizontal walkthrough per calculator, two Collab invitations a week, and the optional human-voice A/B.

| Stop | Why |
|---|---|
| The single "$X looks fine, break-even is $Y" template (18 of 21 videos) | It matches YouTube's "same narration" and "superficial differences" examples and gives viewers no new reason to stay |
| Reusing the same example numbers | Every video must teach a distinct point. Numbers come from a scenario bank. |
| One identical file for all three platforms | Reels need 40 to 55 s and a higher safe zone. TikTok and Shorts need 20 to 30 s. Watermarked files lose distribution. |
| Shorts description links and "search ProofNotFluff" end cards | Shorts links don't click, and a three-step search CTA lands at the drop-off point. Use the related video and a mid-video CTA. |
| Silent title openings and slow typing animations | Frame 1 must carry the number, the motion and the voice together |
| Judging Shorts by raw views | Views count any start. Use engaged views and Viewed vs. swiped. |
| Two offers in one CTA | One CTA per video, and only in Run Your Numbers |

| Metric | Where | Working threshold | Decision rule after about 8 posts per formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skip Rate | IG insights / `reels_skip_rate` | Under about 40% healthy, over 50% failing (agency estimates) | Rework the hook of any formula averaging over 50% |
| Viewed vs. swiped | YouTube Studio | 70 to 79% working, 80%+ strong (creator-reported) ([1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/)) | Compare formulas against each other, not against benchmarks |
| Average watch time / % viewed | IG `ig_reels_avg_watch_time`, YT APV | Under 35 s: 80 to 90% APV working (creator-reported) | A flat retention line through the 40% reveal means the structure works |
| Sends per reach | IG `shares` ÷ `reach` | No published benchmark | The top formula on sends gets an extra weekly slot |
| Saves per reach | IG `saved` ÷ `reach` | No published benchmark | Validates Run Your Numbers' formula card |
| Follows | IG account insights, YT subscribers by Short | No published benchmark | Credit the series wrapper. Zero follows after 30 varied posts means rethinking the persona device. |
| Site visits | Tracked link per video to the free calculator, Etsy referral domains | 1 to 3% of Etsy visits convert ([Gelato](https://www.gelato.com/blog/etsy-conversion-rate)) | Keep any channel that sends calculator visits, whatever its view count |

With samples this small, no test will reach statistical significance. The rule is directional: retire the formula that loses on both its leading metric and Skip Rate after about 8 posts, and replace it with a new candidate, such as a ranked "3 costs you forgot" list.

## Conclusion

The core change is from treating video as a product ad to treating it as proof a stranger would forward. At 0 followers, ProofNotFluff is competing for the unconnected audience Instagram ranks on sends, inside a feed where supply grew 70% in a year. A reveal of a number the viewer has already seen loses that contest. A named job whose real price surprises someone wins it. The evidence gap cuts both ways: nobody has published proof that a faceless calculator account can grow on short video, and the closest analogues (trades transformations, Omni's calculator TikToks) grew on visible work and varied topics, not on avatars or polish.

That shifts what success should mean for the next 60 days. The realistic payoff from short video is credibility, a following and free-calculator visits feeding email. The $2.99 sales will mostly come through Etsy search and Pinterest. If the four formulas, now judged on Skip Rate, sends and calculator visits, still show nothing after Account Status is confirmed clean and about 30 varied posts, the right response is to move agent time toward search channels and Collabs, not toward a realistic AI avatar.
