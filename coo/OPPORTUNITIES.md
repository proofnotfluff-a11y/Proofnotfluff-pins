## Plan v2 (Oct 7, 2026, 9:54 am; Todd: "I will defer to you as you are the mastermind behind creating a business that will win in the market. The goal is to aim high. We can create smaller revenue streams if they are profitable but the end goal is to make real money.")

The honest read: Etsy digital downloads is a market where AI made supply free. Our own checks show page 1 for business and host spreadsheets filling with listings under 60 days old and no buyer badges, and prices compressing to $2 to $4. At $2.99 a sale nets about $2.00 to $2.45. Etsy cannot be the engine of a multi-million dollar business; it can be a proving ground and one channel. Physical products do not fix this: print on demand nets $3 to $8 a shirt (Printful, May 14, 2026) and Amazon Merch pays $2.44 on a $19.99 shirt at the entry tier since June 1, 2026 (merchtitans.com, Apr 15, 2026), with returns and support added; a real physical brand needs capital, inventory and a person, and a prior employer's restrictive covenant closes the category Todd knows best for a year. Physical is closed until the crew has proved it can create demand for anything.

The decisions the COO took under Todd's deferral:
1. Faceless. The business is the ProofNotFluff brand. No product, page or post depends on Todd's name or face. Proof does the work a face would do: a screen-recorded demo, a free lite version, worked examples, specs, Terms, and reviews as they land. The one exception is a vendor profile that requires a person (eFinancialModels wants a CV and bio); that is Todd's call per listing and never a blocker.
2. Spreadsheet to software. The crew's asset is a production line that turns verified expertise into finished, legally reviewed products in hours. The path with a real ceiling is: sell the premium spreadsheet first (cheap, fast, faceless; the sale is the demand signal), then turn the segment that pays into a hosted tool with monthly billing. Recurring revenue is the only faceless route past a few thousand dollars a month.
3. Three segments tested at once with money, not opinions. E-commerce finance (Todd's deepest expertise, highest-value buyer), home-service operators (the service-pricing engine already exists; TikTok-native audience), and short-term rental hosts (Todd runs his own short-term rentals). Each gets one premium product, one free lite calculator page on the site, and the same promotion. The winner gets the app.
4. Checkout: Gumroad (Todd's signup; the crew never creates accounts, accepts terms or enters bank details). Full merchant of record since January 2025 (calculates, collects and remits sales tax, VAT and GST), 10% plus $0.50 a direct sale plus processing, 30% on sales from Gumroad Discover (checkoutpage.com/blog/gumroad-fees, updated June 8, 2026). Lemon Squeezy is cheaper (5% plus $0.50) but is in a transition to Stripe Managed Payments with a waitlist since January 2026 and no clear new-merchant path (dodopayments.com, Mar 25, 2026), so nothing is built on it. Gumroad has no product-create API, so Todd uploads each cleared premium product from the kit the crew packs (about 10 minutes each) until the line proves itself; then a PC-bound store run is proposed. Discover is a per-product toggle on the product's Share tab, not an account setting; every upload quest says to turn it on. Gumroad is connected to Zapier (Oct 7) for a sales read route; the scout confirms which actions exist.
5. Off-Etsy prices are the COO's to set inside $29 to $199 per product (this file names each), under the ledger's PLAN V2 line (Todd's deferral, recorded as [stated]); Todd can overrule any price in chat. Etsy prices stay under the ledger PRICE rule ($2.99; Oct 7 experiment band on reviewed or 30-day listings).
6. Hosting and the repo. The free site is served by GitHub Pages from this repo, which is public. GitHub's Pages terms do not allow a site "primarily directed at facilitating commercial transactions"; a calculator site with one link per page is informational, but as the line grows the site moves to a host that allows commercial use (Cloudflare Pages, free; Todd's signup) and the site's files move to their own public repo, so this repo (prompts, strategy, premium engines) can go private. Until the repo is private, premium engines are packed on the scoreboard shelf and never committed.
7. Not sold: investment advice (what to buy or sell), legal filings, medical, immigration. The STR product is a break-even and pricing workbook, never a buy-or-pass verdict on a property.

The goal ladder, in off-Etsy terms: rung 1, the first $100 off Etsy (checkout and traffic work); rung 2, $1,000 a month (one segment pays); rung 3, $10,000 a month (the hosted tool with 300+ subscribers, or a 10-product premium line); rung 4, $100,000 a month (needs Track D, a physical brand, or a tool that wins a category; not reachable faceless and hands-off on today's evidence, and the COO says so in every memo rather than pretending).

## Case 1: E-commerce finance toolkit, premium, off Etsy (Track C, first build)

- What: a flagship product and a line behind it, built from the finance function Todd built at a fast-growing e-commerce brand: channel P&L across Amazon, TikTok Shop, DTC and retail; a 13-week cash flow that understands inventory, PO timing and factoring; an S&OP forecast that feeds the plan; a chargeback and dispute tracker; creator deal economics; a retail launch sea-vs-air order model. Each one a verified workbook with a Start Here, the Legal desk's Terms, a demo video and a kit, the way the crew already builds. Priced $49 to $199, not $2.99. Faceless: the brand sells it; the worked examples carry the experience without a name on them.
- Who buys it: founders and finance leads at $1M to $30M e-commerce brands, and the fractional CFOs who serve them.
- Evidence (checked Oct 7, 2026): ProjectionHub sells an "Ecommerce Financial Projection Template" at $99 on Gumroad (https://projectionhub.gumroad.com/l/ecommerce-financial-projection-template). Fractional CFO retainers for e-commerce brands run $3,000 to $5,000 a month under $5M revenue and $5,000 to $10,000 at $5M to $30M (eightx.co pricing guide, Mar 12, 2026, updated Jun 20, 2026: https://eightx.co/blog/fractional-cfo-cost-pricing-guide), which is what the buyer pays today for the thinking these tools package. eFinancialModels pays vendors 70% (https://www.efinancialmodels.com/vendor-guidelines/) and its buyers already search for finance models; vendor approval wants a CV and bio, so that channel waits for Todd's call. Etsy demand for these phrases is under 20 US searches a month (eRank, Oct 7), so the channel is Google, Gumroad Discover and the site, not Etsy. Still unchecked: Google search volume for "ecommerce financial model template" and neighbors, the number of competing Gumroad products and their ratings, and whether buyers in this segment prefer Google Sheets.
- Todd's unfair advantage: he built this function from zero at a real brand selling on exactly these channels. It shows in the worked examples (typical operating problems: a PO that lands before the factoring advance, a marketplace spike that drains inventory, a retail load-in that needs air freight), built with invented round numbers, never any real company's figures, and not in a byline.
- Cost: $0 to build. Gumroad fees as above. The Gumroad account is Todd's signup (approved category, Oct 7).
- Todd's hours: 10 minutes for the Gumroad signup; 10 minutes per product to upload the kit; 30 minutes per product to read the worked examples against his experience (optional, but it is the moat).
- Expected profit at 90 days: 20 to 40 sales a month at an average $79 is $1,400 to $2,800 a month gross, about $1,200 to $2,400 net of Gumroad fees. Treat it as the hypothesis to test, not a forecast.
- Risk: no reviews or audience at launch; buyers here want proof. Mitigation: the lite page and the demo video are the proof; Gumroad Discover brings cold buyers at 30%.
- Smallest test: one flagship (the 13-week e-commerce cash flow with inventory and factoring) at $79, the lite page on the site, a demo video, one promote piece a day for 6 weeks. Metric: sales and email signups. Check: 45 days after launch. Quest: approve-premium-line (approved by Todd's deferral, Oct 7, 9:54 am).

- What: a fixed-scope offer such as "finance setup for an e-commerce brand" (chart of accounts, channel P&L, cash flow, S&OP cadence, dashboards) at a fixed fee, or a fractional FP&A retainer. The crew builds every model and dashboard; Todd fronts the client.

## Case 3: Hosted tool with monthly billing (Track E, after Phase 1)

- What: the winning segment's calculator as a small web app: saved scenarios, a shareable or printable output (a quote PDF with the operator's logo, a cash-flow chart, a deal summary), and the premium workbook's maths behind it. Built in Lovable from the engine's tested formulas (the same numbers as the workbook, proved with a compare table), billed through Gumroad memberships with license-key checks, $19 to $49 a month. The build spends Lovable credits, so it starts only on Todd's words in chat.
- Who buys it: the people who bought the premium workbook and the email list the lite pages build.
- Evidence: the sales from Phase 1 (this case has no evidence until then; it is written now so the COO builds Phase 1 toward it: every premium product's engine is kept as a clean module with tests so it ports).
- Todd's unfair advantage: the crew's production line, and his own numbers in the examples.
- Cost: Lovable credits (Todd's workspace; the tool budget covers it or Todd says yes per month); Gumroad fees. No other spend.
- Todd's hours: 1 hour to approve the build and set up the membership product; 30 minutes a month on anything the crew cannot do (support emails are drafted by the crew, sent by Todd until a support policy exists).
- Expected profit at 90 days after launch: 100 subscribers at $29 is $2,900 a month gross, about $2,500 net; 300 subscribers is the rung-3 path. Hypothesis, not a forecast.
- Risk: churn, support load, and the free calculators cannibalizing the paid tool. Mitigation: the free pages stay single-shot (no saving, no PDF); the app is where the work lives.
- Smallest test: one segment, one tool, a waitlist on the lite page first (Kit), build only when the waitlist passes 100 or the premium product passes 30 sales. Quest: approve-hosted-tool (opened by the COO when the trigger is met).
