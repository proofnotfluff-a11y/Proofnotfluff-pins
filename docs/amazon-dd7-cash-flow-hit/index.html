E-commerce cash flow

How do you handle the cash flow hit from Amazon DD+7?

By ProofNotFluff, run by a finance executive · Worked example from the free Amazon DD+7 payout calculator. Checked Oct 26, 2026.

The short answer

Size it in dollars first, then shrink it. Under DD+7, Amazon holds each order's money until 7 days after the delivery date it uses, then pays it on your payout schedule. The cash tied up is about your daily payout times the days from order to bank. Have that much ready before it bites, request your payout every day, and keep valid tracking on orders you ship yourself.

In the worked example below, a store paid $1,000 a day after fees has up to $25,000 held on the 14-day payout and runs $6,600 short on day 28. Requesting the payout daily cuts the most held to $12,000, and cash never drops below $1,600.

How to handle it

  1. See what Amazon is holding now

    In New Seller Central, Manage Transactions filtered to Deferred lists each order still held and the day Amazon expects to release it. In Legacy Seller Central it is Payments, Transaction View, filtered to Deferred transactions.

  2. Put a dollar figure on the hit

    Multiply what Amazon pays you per day, after referral and FBA fees, by the days from order to bank. In the example that is $1,000 a day for up to 25 days, or $25,000. Fees in an order are held with it, but ad costs are charged when billed, so ads keep going out while sales wait.

  3. Have the cash ready before the low point

    The low point is the most you need on hand at once. In the example it is $6,600, on day 28. In the Seller Forums DD+7 thread, one seller says they started building a reserve the day the first notice arrived.

  4. Request your payout every day

    Disburse on Demand lets you request your available balance once every 24 hours. It skips the wait for the next 14-day payout day, not the 7-day reserve. In the example it cuts the most held from $25,000 to $12,000 and the cash needed from $6,600 to $0.

  5. Keep valid tracking on orders you ship

    With valid tracking from an integrated carrier, Amazon uses the actual delivery date. Without it, Amazon waits for the latest estimated delivery date, which can be later. In the example, merchant orders with no valid tracking need $9,600 on the 14-day payout, against $6,600 for FBA.

  6. Keep Express Payout on

    Express Payout sends payouts to an eligible US bank account within 24 hours and is on by default for eligible sellers; otherwise a payout takes 3 to 5 business days. With a daily request and 5 days to the bank, the example needs $1,200 instead of $0.

  7. Plan stock and ads around the weeks money lands

    Put each payout in the week it reaches your bank and each supplier payment and ad bill in the week it leaves, then check the lowest week before you place a bigger order. Asking a supplier to take the balance a week later costs nothing if they agree; the Shopify cash gap page shows how much one week can move the low point.

When Amazon holds more than DD+7

The 7-day reserve is not the only hold. Amazon can extend the reserve period after a review of risk and past performance, and an account level reserve for open A-to-z claims, chargebacks or an account review can hold more on top. In a Seller Central thread read Oct 26, 2026, a seller reports three orders held until delivery plus 22 days, with Amazon pointing to an extended reserve period.

Neither shows up in the example above, so if your Deferred list shows release dates well past delivery plus 7, plan with your own dates, not the rule.

If the cash needed is more than you have

You need that much cash, credit or a slower spend by the day of the low point. A daily payout request and valid tracking cost nothing, so test those first. Any loan or credit line has a cost, so compare its terms against your own numbers before you use it. This page is not financial advice.

Questions

How much cash does Amazon DD+7 tie up?

About what Amazon pays you per day after fees, times the days from order to bank. In the example, $1,000 a day on the 14-day payout ties up $12,000 to $25,000 at any time.

Is the DD+7 cash flow hit a one-time hit?

The drop in deposits comes once, when orders first wait the longer hold, and again whenever sales jump. The money held stays tied up for as long as you sell at that rate.

How much cash reserve do I need for DD+7?

Enough to cover your low point, the most you are short at once before payouts catch up. In the example that is $6,600 on the 14-day payout and $0 with a payout requested every day.

Does DD+7 hit merchant fulfilled orders harder than FBA?

The 7-day rule is the same. The date it counts from is not: orders you ship without valid tracking from an integrated carrier wait for the latest estimated delivery date. In the example that lifts the most held from $25,000 to $29,000.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a planning estimate from the numbers you enter and Amazon's published rules.
