The Amazon rules this calculator uses

  • Funds are reserved until delivery plus a 7-day reserve period. Amazon's example: sold January 1, delivered January 6, available January 14. The period can be extended after a review of risk and past performance. Source: Payments based on delivery date.
  • With an integrated carrier, Amazon uses the actual delivery date. Without valid tracking, it uses the latest estimated delivery date. Source: Payments based on delivery date.
  • Fees that are part of an order, such as referral and FBA fees, are held with the order and charged when it is released. Ad costs are charged when billed. Source: Deferred transactions for accounting and payment reconciliation.
  • The scheduled payout period is 14 days. Source: Why didn't I get paid?.
  • Disburse on Demand lets you request your available balance once every 24 hours. Delivery date reserves still apply. Source: Disburse on Demand.
  • Express Payout sends payouts to an eligible US bank account within 24 hours and is on by default for eligible sellers. Otherwise a payout takes 3 to 5 business days. Source: Express Payout frequently asked questions.
  • An account level reserve for open A-to-z claims, chargebacks or an account review can hold more on top. Source: What is account level reserve?.

All sources are Amazon's US Seller Central help pages, read Oct 24, 2026. Amazon can change them, so open a link to check today's wording.

Where to see your own numbers in Seller Central

  • Each order still held, and the day Amazon expects to release it: in New Seller Central, Manage Transactions filtered to Deferred; in Legacy Seller Central, Payments, Transaction View filtered to Deferred transactions. Source: Deferred transactions for accounting and payment reconciliation.
  • Your next payout date: Manage Payouts in New Seller Central, or the Payments dashboard in Legacy Seller Central. Source: Why didn't I get paid?.

How it works

Amazon's help page on payments based on delivery date says funds are reserved until delivery plus 7 days. Its own example: sold January 1, delivered January 6, available for payout starting January 14. So the calculator makes each day's sales available 8 days after the delivery date it uses.

Which delivery date: with valid tracking from an integrated carrier, Amazon uses the actual delivery date; without it, the latest estimated delivery date. Amazon's page does not say how it dates FBA deliveries, so for FBA orders the calculator uses the days to delivery you type.

Then the payout. On the scheduled payout every 14 days, money that becomes available waits for the next payout day, so an order can wait up to 13 more days. If you request a payout every day, it goes out the day it becomes available. Then add the days your bank takes.

Money held is everything sold but not yet in your bank, once sales have run long enough to settle into the cycle. The low point starts from a standing start: sales at this rate begin today, nothing is held yet, and your daily spend goes out from day 1. That is the squeeze a new store, a new product or a jump in sales feels. To test a jump, type only the extra daily sales and the extra daily spend.

Not included: refunds, A-to-z claims and chargebacks that Amazon can hold as an account level reserve, a reserve period extended after a risk review, and charges billed outside the order such as ads.

A worked example

Say a brand sells $1,000 a day after Amazon fees through FBA, orders arrive in 3 days, it is on the 14-day payout with the next one 14 days out, and Express Payout lands it the next day. Each order becomes available 11 days after it was placed and reaches the bank 12 to 25 days after. Amazon holds between $12,000 and $25,000 at any time, up to 25 days of sales. Starting with $10,000 and paying out $700 a day, the first $3,000 lands on day 15, the next payout not until day 29, and cash bottoms at -$6,600 on day 28. Requesting a payout every day instead cuts order to bank to 12 days and the most held to $12,000, and the low point is $1,600 on day 12, with no extra cash needed.

Questions

What does DD+7 mean on Amazon?

Delivery date plus 7 days. Amazon reserves the money from each order until 7 days after it is delivered. In Amazon's own example, an order delivered January 6 is available for payout starting January 14.

How long does Amazon take to pay sellers after an order?

The days to delivery, plus 8 in Amazon's example, plus the wait for your payout day, plus your bank's time. In the example, with 3-day delivery and the 14-day payout, that is 12 to 25 days.

Is DD+7 different for FBA and merchant fulfilled orders?

Amazon's page gives one rule for every shipment: delivery plus 7 days. What can change is the delivery date it uses: the actual one with valid tracking from an integrated carrier, or the latest estimated delivery date without it, which can be later. The page does not single out FBA.

Does requesting a payout every day get around DD+7?

No. Disburse on Demand pays the balance that is already available, once every 24 hours. It skips the wait for the 14-day payout day, not the reserve.

Why is Amazon holding more than this page shows?

An account level reserve for open A-to-z claims, chargebacks or an account review can hold more on top, and Amazon can extend the reserve period after a review. This page leaves both out.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a planning estimate from the numbers you type and Amazon's published rules.
