How to do it

  1. Find the builder on the permit

    Look up the project in the City of Austin's Issued Construction Permits data on data.austintexas.gov. Each permit row names a contractor company and its trade; General Contractor is the builder.

  2. Start with builders who have several jobs

    On this Monday's list, Turner Construction, Swinerton Builders Inc., Rogers-O'Brien Construction Co. Ltd, OHT Construction LLC and Skybeck Construction, LLC each have 5 active projects. Getting on one builder's vendor list may put you in front of more than one project.

  3. Find the right person first

    Call the builder's main office, name the project and the permit number, and ask who buys cleaning for it. Then send that person a one-page sheet: what you clean, crew size, insurance, equipment and two references.

  4. Ask to meet

    A short meeting at the office with your sheet and photos of past jobs puts a face to your name.

  5. Watch the stage

    This week's count by stage: permit just issued 25, work under way 132, near finish 1 (some of the project's building permits already finaled). Get in touch early, before the final clean is booked.

Questions

Where do I find the general contractor for an Austin permit?

In the City of Austin's Issued Construction Permits dataset on data.austintexas.gov. Each row names a contractor company and its trade, and the trade General Contractor marks the builder.

Why is the valuation so big on some projects?

Austin records a development's valuation on each of its building permits, so a project's valuation can cover several buildings or phases. Treat it as a size signal, not a budget or a contract value.

Does a project on the list need a cleaner?

Not necessarily. A permit shows that work is planned or under way and who the builder is. It does not say who cleans the job or whether that work is already taken. Ask the builder.

Do you contact builders for me?

No. You make your own calls and emails. We never contact anyone on your behalf, and no business listed has endorsed or works with ProofNotFluff.
