How to do it

  1. Find the builder on the permit

    Look up the project in the City of Austin's Issued Construction Permits data on data.austintexas.gov. Each permit row names a contractor company and its trade; General Contractor is the builder.

  2. Start with builders who have several jobs

    On this Monday's list, Turner Construction, Rogers-O'Brien Construction Co. Ltd and Skybeck Construction, LLC each have 5 active projects. Getting on one builder's bidders list may put you in front of more than one project.

  3. Find the right person first

    Call the builder's main office, name the project and the permit number, and ask who buys cleaning for it. Then send that person a one-page sheet: what you clean, crew size, insurance, equipment and two references. Ask for the project's final cleaning section too: builders quote from the project's own spec, which usually includes one. Our Chicago guide shows one public spec line by line.

  4. Line up insurance before you bid

    Builders usually won't let an uninsured vendor on site, so have proof of insurance ready. Once you have employees you will usually need workers' compensation too, and some clients ask for a surety bond, per ZenBusiness. Commercial clients may pay 30 or 60 days after the invoice, so budget for the wait.

  5. Ask to get on the bidders list

    Write to the main office and ask to be added to its bidders list for cleaning, with your insurance, licenses, references and examples of past work, as Cleaning & Maintenance Management advises.

  6. Watch the stage

    This week's count by stage: permit just issued 24, work under way 131, near finish 1 (some of the project's building permits already finaled). Get in touch early, before the final clean is booked.

  7. Visit, but keep it short

    Walk onto a site only to introduce yourself. Find the trailer, ask for the superintendent or project manager, hand over a card and a one-page sheet, and leave, with a hard hat and vest on. The Cleaning & Maintenance Management article says the same.

  8. Bill by phase

    Clean in units or phases and have the superintendent sign each one off as done. It keeps invoicing clean and gives you a basis to bill a re-clean if other trades come back through.

Questions

Where do I find the general contractor for an Austin permit?

In the City of Austin's Issued Construction Permits dataset on data.austintexas.gov. Each row names a contractor company and its trade, and the trade General Contractor marks the builder.

Who do I contact for post-construction cleanup work?

The general contractor's office. Ask who buys cleaning for a named project, usually a project manager or superintendent, and ask to get on the bidders list.

What does post-construction cleaning include?

Whatever the project's spec says. Ask the builder for the final cleaning section; it usually lists debris removal, floors, glass, fixtures and the air system's filters and grilles.

Why is the valuation so big on some projects?

Austin records a development's valuation on each of its building permits, so a project's valuation can cover several buildings or phases. Treat it as a size signal, not a budget or a contract value.

Does a project on the list need a cleaner?

Not necessarily. A permit shows that work is planned or under way and who the builder is. It does not say who cleans the job or whether that work is already taken. Ask the builder.

Do you contact builders for me?

No. You make your own calls and emails. We never contact anyone on your behalf, and no business listed has endorsed or works with ProofNotFluff.
