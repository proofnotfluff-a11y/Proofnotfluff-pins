Lead cost

My carpet cleaning leads from Thumbtack and Local Services Ads are mostly price shoppers. Which channels and numbers actually work?

By ProofNotFluff, run by a finance executive · Worked example from the free cost per lead calculator. New license counts from the City of Chicago's Business Licenses data, list of Nov 2, 2026. Google's Local Services Ads help page read Nov 8, 2026.

The short answer

The channel that works is the one with the lowest lead cost per booked job, and you only find it by counting. For 30 days, write down for each source what you paid, how many leads came in, how many you quoted and how many booked. Divide the spend by the jobs booked and set that against what one job leaves after labor and supplies.

Price shoppers hurt because they ask several companies at once, so fewer of your quotes book. In the free calculator's example, $1,200 of leads brings 18 quotes; at 6 jobs booked (33% of quotes) each job carries $200 of lead cost while a job leaves $170, so the month loses $180. It takes 8 bookings (44% of quotes) to break even. Referrals, repeat customers and your own outreach have no per lead bill, which is why they are worth counting too.

How to measure your channels in 30 days

  1. Give every source its own count

    Ask every caller how they found you and log it, and keep each paid channel's own lead report: Thumbtack, Local Services Ads, Facebook, referrals, repeat customers and your own outreach each get a line.

  2. Count four numbers per source

    What you paid, leads, quotes sent and jobs booked. A source with no bill, such as a referral, still gets counted, so you can see what it books.

  3. Divide spend by jobs booked

    That is the lead cost per booked job. In the example, $1,200 and 6 jobs is $200, more than the $170 a job leaves.

  4. Check what you control

    One reply in the thread says to check how many calls you answer, to return calls within 2 hours and to compare the close rate by source before paying another agency. A missed call from a paid lead can still be billed: Google charges for a missed call in business hours when the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds.

  5. Move money to the lowest cost per booked job

    Keep the channels where a booked job costs less than it leaves, cut the rest, and ask any marketing company for these same four numbers each month.

What the thread says

The question was posted Jul 26, 2026 on The Cleaning Standard forum by a carpet cleaner in the Fort Lauderdale and Hollywood, Florida area, almost a year in business. The owner had hired several marketing companies, including ones that specialize in carpet cleaning, and used Thumbtack and Google Local Services Ads, but said most inquiries were price shoppers contacting several companies at the same time and the phone was still barely ringing. The owner asked for an honest diagnosis and a lead system that can be measured, including what numbers to ask a marketing company for.

A forum admin answered that it is a conversion or positioning problem, not a channel problem, and listed checks: Google Business Profile click rate (a concern below 5 to 10% in a competitive market), the share of calls answered (below 80% is a concern), returning calls within 2 hours, the close rate by source and the repeat rate (below 30% is a concern), tracked for 30 days before hiring another agency. Another reply said referrals and repeat work depend on whether customers find the owner pleasant to have in the home. These are advice from the thread, not measured results.

Why shared leads close less

A lead that goes to several companies is a race on price and speed. When fewer of your quotes book, the bill stays the same and each booked job carries more of it: in the example, the cost per booked job runs from $200 at 6 bookings up to $600 at 2. Google's help page says it charges for each valid lead, such as a call you answer, a text or a voicemail, and that the price of a message lead takes into account, among other things, whether the customer contacted other advertisers.

So the first fix is often on your side of the phone: answer or call back fast, quote clearly, and ask every customer for the review and the next visit.

The numbers to ask any marketing company for

Per channel, every month: what you spent, leads, quotes, jobs booked and lead cost per booked job, with the leads you could not serve counted separately. A report of clicks, impressions or followers alone can't tell you if the money came back. If a company can't give you jobs booked per channel, keep your own count from your booking records.

Channels without a per lead bill

Referrals and repeat customers cost no new lead, so every job they book lowers your average. A repeat visit costs no new lead: one return visit per customer halves the lead cost per visit in the example, to $100.

Questions

Are Thumbtack and Google Local Services Ads leads worth it for carpet cleaning?

Only if the lead cost per booked job is less than what a job leaves you. In the calculator's example, $1,200 of leads booked 6 jobs, $200 each, while a job left $170.

How do I stop getting price shoppers?

You can't stop a shared lead from shopping. You can answer and quote faster, track which sources book, and grow the channels with no per lead bill: referrals, repeat customers and your own outreach.

What close rate do I need on paid leads?

Enough bookings that the bill divided by jobs booked is less than a job leaves. In the example, 8 of 18 quotes (44%) is the break even.

What should a marketing company report to me?

Per channel each month: spend, leads, quotes, jobs booked and lead cost per booked job. Clicks and impressions alone don't show if the money came back.
