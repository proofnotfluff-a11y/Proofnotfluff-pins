How to read these numbers

  • Awards, not open bidsThese are contracts the City has already approved. To bid on new work, use the City's bid lists below.
  • Award totals are not unit pricesAn award is the most the City may spend over the whole contract, often years and many sites or items. It does not say what one job or one hour pays.
  • $0 means orders as neededThe City lists some blanket contracts at $0. The City orders as it needs, and no amount is promised. They are counted, but not in totals or medians.
  • Changes are counted apartA new award is revision 0. Later changes (more money, less money, more time) are modifications, shown on their own. Money taken out shows as a negative amount and is never subtracted from award totals.
  • End dates cover blanket contractsThe City records an end date only for blanket contracts (repeat purchases). Standard contracts, most construction, end when the work is accepted, so they never show as ending.
  • Sorted by the contract's own wordsA contract joins a trade when its description uses that trade's words (the map is below). One contract can count in two trades, and work described in other words is missed.

Matching ignores capitals, and most words also find their longer forms (roof finds roofing). Left out of every trade: purchases of goods or equipment (supplies, parts, vehicles) unless the description also names work such as service, repair, maintenance or removal; upkeep of the City's own machines and fleet; design, engineering and consulting; software; art; grants to agencies; leases; and redevelopment deals. A few more words keep look-alikes out (rock salt from snow removal, lead paint removal from painting, elevators from HVAC).

How this page is made

Every morning we read the City of Chicago's public Contracts dataset, which lists the contracts and modifications the City has awarded since 1993 and is updated daily by the City.

We keep the awards and changes approved in the last 36 months, plus every contract whose latest end date falls in the next 12 months, and sort them into trades by the words in each contract's description (the trade map above).

Revision 0 is the original award; every later revision is a change and is counted apart. A $0 award on a blanket contract means the City orders as needed. Totals and medians use new awards with a dollar amount.

We never pull the vendor's address. Vendor names are business names as the City lists them; a name that does not read as a business shows as Name withheld.

Questions

Can I bid on these contracts here?

No. These are contracts the City has already awarded. Open City bids are on the City's eProcurement bid opportunities list and the Department of Procurement Services Current Bid Opportunities page, linked on this page.

Does an award total tell me what the City pays per job?

No. An award is the most the City may spend over the whole contract, often several years and many sites or items. Read the contract PDF for its prices and terms.

Why do some contracts show $0?

The City lists some blanket contracts at $0 because their total depends on requirements: the City places orders as it needs them and promises no amount. We count them, but leave them out of totals and medians.

What does ending in the next 12 months mean?

It is the latest end date the City has recorded for the contract, after any time extensions. When a contract ends, the City may bid the work again, so it is a good time to watch the bid lists. Only blanket contracts carry an end date.

How often does this page change?

Every morning. The City updates its Contracts dataset daily, and this page and its CSV files are rebuilt from it each day.
