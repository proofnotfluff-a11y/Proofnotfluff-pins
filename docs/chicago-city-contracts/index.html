How to read these numbers

  • Awards, not open bidsThis tracker lists contracts the City has already approved. To bid on new work, use the City's bid lists on this page.
  • Award totals are not unit pricesAn award is the most the City may spend over the whole contract, often years and many sites or items. It does not say what one job or one hour pays.
  • Changes are counted separatelyThe first entry for a contract is the award. Later changes (more money, less money, more time) are shown on their own and are never counted in award totals. On the trade pages and in the CSVs, money taken out shows as a negative amount.
  • End dates are mostly on blanket contractsThe City records end dates mostly on blanket contracts (repeat purchases). Many standard contracts, such as most construction, end when the work is accepted, so they do not show as ending.
  • Sorted by the contract's own wordsA contract joins a trade when its description uses that trade's words. One contract can count in two trades, and work described in other words is missed.

What every trade leaves out

Matching ignores capital letters, and most words also find their longer forms (roof finds roofing). Left out of every trade: anything the City buys rather than hires, meaning a contract described as a purchase, and lists of supplies, parts, products or vehicles that name no work such as service, repair, maintenance or removal; upkeep of the City's own machines and fleet; design, engineering and consulting; software; art; grants to agencies; leases; redevelopment deals; deals with other public bodies, such as the Board of Education and other intergovernmental agreements; and airport terminal projects the City pays airlines or airline groups to run (the vendor is the airline). A few more words keep look-alikes out (rock salt from snow removal, lead paint removal from painting, elevators from HVAC, holiday trees and tree inventories from landscaping). Contracts only for inspection or construction supervision are left out too.

How this page is made

Every morning we read the City of Chicago's public Contracts dataset, which lists the contracts the City has awarded since 1993 and the changes to them, and is updated daily by the City.

We keep the awards and changes approved in the last 36 months, plus every contract whose latest end date falls in the next 12 months, and sort them into trades by the words in each contract's description (the trade map above).

The first entry for a contract is the award. Every later entry is a change and is counted separately, including one that only gives the contract a new number. Totals and medians use new awards only.

We never pull the vendor's address. Vendor names are business names as the City lists them, and a name that may be a person's is not shown.

Questions

Can I bid on these contracts here?

No. These are contracts the City has already awarded. Open City bids are on the City's eProcurement bid opportunities list and the Department of Procurement Services current bid opportunities page, linked on this page.

Does an award total tell me what the City pays per job?

No. An award is the most the City may spend over the whole contract, often several years and many sites or items. Read the contract PDF for its prices and terms.

What does ending in the next 12 months mean?

It is the latest end date the City has recorded for the contract, after any time extensions. When a contract ends, the City may bid the work again, so it is a good time to watch the bid lists. Most contracts with an end date are blanket contracts.

How often does this page change?

Every morning. The City updates its Contracts dataset daily, and this page and its CSV files are rebuilt from it each day.
