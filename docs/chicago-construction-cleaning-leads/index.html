How to use this list

Start with the builders, not the buildings. A general contractor with several big jobs open buys subcontracted work on each, so one builder's vendor list can lead to more than one project.

Call the builder's main office and ask who buys cleaning for the project you name. Have the permit number and the address ready.

Send a one-page capability sheet the same day: what you clean, crew size, insurance, equipment and two references.

Watch the stage. Permit issued is early, inspections under way means work is moving, and partial occupancy approved means the City has already cleared parts of the building for use.

Questions

Where does this data come from?

The City of Chicago Data Portal's Building Permits dataset. We pull it every Monday and keep permits issued in the last 24 months for apartment buildings of 4 or more homes and commercial work with a reported cost of $500,000 or more that are still open and list a business as general contractor.

Why are some big projects missing?

Permits with no business listed as general contractor, owner-builder permits, houses and 2 to 3 flats, single condo units, permits marked complete, suspended or cancelled, and work that never needs a construction clean under its own permit (foundation, caisson, demolition, roof, mechanical or site work only) are left out. This free page shows a sample; the weekly list covers every permit that passes the filter.

Is the reported cost the contract value?

No. It is the cost the applicant filed with the City for the permit. Treat it as a size signal, not a budget.

Do you contact the builders for me?

No. You make your own calls and emails. We never contact builders or anyone else on your behalf.

Does any builder here work with ProofNotFluff?

No. Builders appear because their names are on public City permits. None has endorsed or works with ProofNotFluff.

Will you cover other cities?

Chicago and Austin now. More cities with open permit data are next.
