How long a big job takes

Big jobs take a long time. Of the new buildings permitted in 2024, 64% have their certificate of occupancy today; of those permitted in 2025, 22%. Recent renovations get theirs sooner: 55% of the 2025 renovations have theirs. So most new buildings permitted in the last year do not have their certificate yet. The City records cannot say how close any one job is.

What the stages mean

Inspections, certificate of occupancy required
The City record puts the job in its inspection stage. The job needs a certificate of occupancy before anyone can use the space.Most open jobs are here, some with permits issued in the last 6 months and some more than 2 years ago.
Temporary or partial occupancy approved
The City has approved temporary or partial use of the building while the certificate is still pending.Usually late in the job.
Certificate of occupancy pending
The City record shows the certificate as pending, with no partial occupancy approved.Counted with the open jobs.
Certificate of occupancy issued
The building can be used. These jobs have their certificate and are not counted as open.They appear only in the bars under How long a big job takes.

How this page is made

Every Monday we pull the City of Chicago's public Building Permits dataset and keep new construction and renovation permits issued since January 1, 2021 with a reported cost of $500,000 or more. A job counts here when its City milestone puts it on the certificate of occupancy path: inspections with a certificate required, certificate pending, partial occupancy approved, or certificate issued.

Open jobs are the ones without a certificate yet. Permits the City lists as suspended, expired, cancelled or revoked are left out of the open counts. We count permits, not projects, so a large project built under several phased permits can count more than once. Each open job is placed in a community area by the area the City gives for it and grouped by days since the permit was issued: under 6 months, 6 to 12 months, 1 to 2 years and over 2 years.

How long a big job takes compares permit years: for each year, the share of jobs on the certificate path that have the certificate today. The City's records do not say on what day a certificate was issued, so the page does not give a number of weeks to move-in for any one job.

Only counts are published. We never request contact, address, map point or description fields. To count the jobs that change stage from one week to the next, last week's permit numbers and stages are stored encrypted and are never shown, so the page names no building, address or business.

Questions

How many big construction projects are still open in Chicago?

This page counts new construction and renovation permits in Chicago with a reported cost of $500,000 or more, issued since January 1, 2021, whose City milestone shows they still need a certificate of occupancy. Permits the City lists as suspended, expired, cancelled or revoked are left out. The count updates every Monday, and the table splits it by all 77 community areas.

How long does it take to get a certificate of occupancy in Chicago?

It depends on the job. The bars on this page show, for each permit year, how many big new buildings and renovations have their certificate today. Recent renovations usually get theirs sooner than new buildings.

What does partial occupancy mean?

The City has approved temporary or partial use of the building while the certificate of occupancy is still pending. On this page it usually means a job is well along, not that it is done.

Can I see the addresses?

Not on this free page, which shows counts only. Our paid Chicago construction cleaning leads list gives the address, the builder and the stage of open apartment and commercial jobs of $500,000 or more permitted in the last 24 months.
