Roofer Pay in Chicago

Half of the Chicago metro's roofers earn more than $39.47 an hour, and the middle half earn $28.73 to $50.61 (BLS, May 2025). A 2-person crew for an 8-hour day at the median costs $715.92 with employer Social Security, Medicare and Chicago paid leave counted.

Who is covered, and Chicago paid leave

  • Chicago: domestic workers and youth workers must receive at least the $17.05 minimum.
  • Cook County: covers employees over 18 who work in Cook County at least 2 hours in any two-week period, for an employer with 4 or more employees (or as a domestic worker), when the employer has a business facility or license in the County.
  • A town can opt out of the County rate or set its own minimum wage. Chicago sets its own, shown in the City of Chicago box.
  • Paid leave for any reason: 1 hour for every 35 hours worked, up to 40 hours in a 12-month period; employees can start using it by the 90th day after they start the job. Up to 16 hours carry over if the employer did not give all 40 hours up front.
  • Paid sick leave: 1 hour for every 35 hours worked, up to 40 hours in a 12-month period; employees can start using it by the 30th day after they start the job. Up to 80 hours carry over.
  • Both apply to employees who work at least 80 hours within any 120-day period. The ordinance has exceptions; check with the City's Office of Labor Standards.
  • When a job ends, employers with 51 or more employees pay out up to 56 hours of unused paid leave; employers with 1 to 50 do not.

The Chicago rules are in Chicago Municipal Code 6-105 (minimum wage) and 6-130 (paid leave).

Questions

How much does a roofer make an hour in Chicago?

In the Chicago-Naperville-Elgin metro the median is $39.47 an hour, from BLS's May 2025 survey of employers. The 10th percentile is $23.45 and the 90th is $51.34.

What is the Cook County prevailing wage for roofers?

The Cook County schedule posted October 19, 2026 lists Roofer at $53.75 an hour base plus $32.28 of fringes, $86.03 in all, for public works. Which line applies depends on the work; check with the Illinois Department of Labor.

Are these the rates I should charge customers?

No. These are wages paid to employees. The BLS figures are straight-time pay before overtime and premium pay, without benefits, and leave out the self-employed. A rate you bill also has to cover your crew's unpaid time, your vehicle, insurance, overhead and profit.
