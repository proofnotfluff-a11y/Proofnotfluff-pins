How to do it

  1. Start with places about to open

    The City's Retail Food License Readiness Checklist says an applicant should pay for the license only when it is ready for inspection, with construction complete, and that the health department then schedules an on-site inspection. A place with a fresh license inspection is often close to opening and may be choosing its first vendors.

  2. Then the hood and exhaust citations

    A hood or exhaust violation is a public record with a date. Get in touch within a few days, name the record and its date, leave any judgment out of it, and offer a service visit.

  3. Go in person

    Visit in a quiet hour, such as between lunch and dinner, and ask for the kitchen manager. Bring a one-page sheet: what you clean, how often you can come, insurance and two references.

  4. Offer a schedule from the first job

    Before you leave the first job, book the next visit and offer a simple recurring plan, so saying yes again is simple.

Questions

What counts as a hood or exhaust violation here?

Our reading of the City's violation codes: violation 56 when it names a hood, filter, exhaust or grease. The members' list shows the City's code next to our category.

Is a license inspection the same as opening?

No. It is the health inspection the City does after a business applies and pays for a food license. Per the City's checklist, an applicant should pay for the license only when construction is complete and the place is ready to operate, so many open soon after, but some take longer.

Why doesn't this page name places?

On our public pages we never name a business with a failed or conditional result. Members get the full list, licensed to their own business for its own sales calls.
