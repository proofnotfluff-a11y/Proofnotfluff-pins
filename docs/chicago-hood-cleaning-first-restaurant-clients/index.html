How often a hood system should be inspected

NFPA 96, the NFPA standard for commercial cooking ventilation and fire protection, sets how often a system must be inspected for grease buildup, by the kind of cooking. It is the easiest way to explain why a restaurant needs you on a schedule.

Kind of cookingHow often
Solid fuel cookingMonthly
High volume, such as 24-hour cooking, charbroiling or wok cookingQuarterly
Moderate volumeEvery six months
Low volume, such as churches, day camps, seasonal businesses or senior centersYearly

Source: 2017 NFPA 96, Table 11.4, as quoted by the Village of New Paltz, NY. Later editions renumber the table; the frequencies here are from the 2017 text as quoted by the village. Check with the Chicago Fire Department for the rules that apply to a specific kitchen; a system found contaminated with grease is cleaned, not just inspected.

How to do it

  1. Start with places about to open

    The City's Retail Food License Readiness Checklist says an applicant should pay for the license only when it is ready for inspection, with construction complete, and that the health department then schedules an on-site inspection. A place with a fresh license inspection is often close to opening and may be choosing its first vendors.

  2. Then the hood and exhaust citations

    A hood or exhaust violation is a public record with a date. Get in touch within a few days, name the record and its date, leave any judgment out of it, and offer a service visit.

  3. Go in person

    Visit in person and ask for the kitchen manager. Bring a one-page sheet: what you clean, how often you can come, insurance and two references.

  4. Lead with the schedule

    NFPA 96 sets an inspection schedule for the exhaust system by the kind of cooking. Show the table above, ask what they cook and how much, and offer the visit frequency that fits.

  5. Book the next visit before you leave

    Before you leave the first job, book the next visit and offer a simple recurring plan, so saying yes again is simple. A reply in the Jobber Community thread that asked this question gives similar advice: lock in a service schedule from day one and offer a simple recurring plan.

  6. Keep a client after the first job

    Show up when you said you would, send photos of the hood, filters and fan after each visit, and remind the manager before the next one is due. Reliability and clear communication make the manager's job easier, which keeps a recurring account.

Questions

How do I get hood cleaning jobs with no clients yet?

Start where the need is dated and public: restaurants that just had a license inspection, and restaurants with a fresh hood or exhaust violation in the City's food inspection records. Visit in person and offer a recurring schedule.

How often does a restaurant hood need cleaning?

NFPA 96 sets inspection frequency by the kind of cooking: monthly for solid fuel, quarterly for high volume such as 24-hour cooking, charbroiling or wok, every six months for moderate volume and yearly for low volume. A system found with grease buildup gets cleaned.

What counts as a hood or exhaust violation here?

Our reading of the City's violation codes: violation 56 when it names a hood, filter, exhaust or grease. The members' list shows the City's code next to our category.

Is a license inspection the same as opening?

No. It is the health inspection the City does after a business applies and pays for a food license. Per the City's checklist, an applicant should pay for the license only when construction is complete and the place is ready to operate, so many open soon after, but some take longer.
