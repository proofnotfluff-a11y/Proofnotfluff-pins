How to use this list

Who it is for: commercial cleaning and pest control companies first, and any vendor a new business needs, such as POS and payments, signs, insurance, bookkeeping, waste and supplies.

Pick the trades that buy from you. A new restaurant and a new salon need different vendors, so the list is sorted by what each business does.

Reach out in the first weeks. The license date tells you how recently the City licensed the business at that site, so you can reach out early.

Visit or call the business at its licensed address, name the license and its date, and say in one line what you do for businesses like it.

If you also have Chicago Restaurant Inspection Leads: food businesses usually get a City food license inspection before the license is issued, and that product lists those inspections as Opening soon. So you may already have seen most of the food businesses here. Each food row with that inspection is marked in the members' list, so you can hide them. Nearly all businesses in the other trades are only here.

Questions

Where does this data come from?

The City of Chicago Data Portal's Business Licenses dataset, and its Food Inspections dataset for the food license inspection on food rows. The list is refreshed every Monday from the City's latest records. It keeps new licenses (application type Issue) the City issued in the last 30 days at a Chicago business site.

Is every license a brand-new business?

Most are: about 6 in 10 on recent lists are a first license, and each row says which. A business that opens another site, adds a new license type at a site it already has, or licenses a site again (a new owner, or the same business after an older license ended) also gets a new license. The members' list reads the City's earlier licenses for each business and marks which is which: first license, new site, added license or relicensed.

Who is left out?

Renewals and changes, cancelled or revoked licenses, licenses for one person's own work, for work away from a fixed business site, or for an event or season (such as peddlers, special events, pop-ups, tow trucks, home-sharing, bed-and-breakfasts, shared kitchen users, flea market vendors and market stalls), home and mail-drop addresses (home-based businesses, apartments, private mailboxes and virtual offices), sites outside Chicago, and any business whose shown name could be, or starts with, a person's full name. A business with two licenses at one site is listed once.

Does it overlap with Chicago Restaurant Inspection Leads?

Partly, and only for food businesses. That product lists the City's food license inspection, which usually comes before the food license this list shows. The members' list marks every food row with that inspection in the 120 days before its license, so a member of both can hide them. Nearly all shops, salons, venues, auto shops and day care centers are only here.

Is every business on the list open?

Not necessarily. A license shows the City issued it on that date. Some businesses open the same week, some later.

Do you contact these businesses for me?

No. You make your own calls and visits. We never contact anyone on your behalf, and no business listed has endorsed or works with ProofNotFluff.
