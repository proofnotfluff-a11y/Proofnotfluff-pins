    Degradation fee on moratorium streets (page 11)

    Fee = P times (length divided by 200) times $1,000.00, where P is the multiplier for the pavement's age in the table below. For a cut the length is set to 200 feet; for a trench the schedule says a minimum of 200 foot increments, which we read as at least 200 feet, prorated above that. The schedule says residential homeowners are exempt on repairs to existing sewer and water services. Moratorium streets are on CDOT's ChiStreetWork map.

    How the calculator reads the schedule

    Every amount comes from CDOT's 2026 Permit Fee Schedule, effective January 1, 2026. The tables it uses are on this page, with the schedule's page numbers, and the calculator only adds and multiplies them.

    How each permit type is priced

    Dumpsters: the application fee depends on the street (CBD, or a residential or arterial street outside it), and the usage fee on the size class, inside or outside the CBD, for 1 to 3 days or per month. The schedule gives no rate between 3 days and a month, so the calculator asks you to pick one.

    Sidewalk and street closures: the schedule issues these permits in 30-day increments and sets a per-foot rate and a minimum monthly fee that both rise the longer the closure stays. Our reading: each 30-day block is charged at the rate for the days it falls in (days 181 to 210 at the 181 to 360 day rate, for example), as the per-foot rate times the length or the monthly minimum, whichever is more, then halved outside the CBD. CDOT may instead price the whole permit at one rate. Past 720 days, the schedule sets sidewalk and parkway closures at the previous monthly fee plus $4.00 or $8.00 times the length; we read this as adding again each month. Ask CDOT how it prices yours.

    Openings on a moratorium street: the opening fee doubles and the degradation fee from page 11 is added, using the schedule's own formula and multipliers. We show it for each cut, and charge a trench on at least 200 feet; both are our reading.

    Some items in the schedule are priced per foot without a time period, and some charges, like metered spaces your closure makes unusable, have no amount in that part of the schedule. The calculator says so where it applies. CDOT sets the fee on each permit.

    Questions

    How much is a dumpster permit in Chicago in 2026?

    Outside the Central Business District, a Class A dumpster (30 cubic yards or less) is $50.00 for 1 to 3 days or $100.00 a month, plus an application fee of $22.60 on a residential street or $56.51 on an arterial street. Inside the CBD the usage fees double and the application fee is $92.63.

    Does the size of the dumpster change the fee?

    Yes. Class B dumpsters, more than 30 cubic yards, cost twice Class A: $100.00 for 1 to 3 days or $200.00 a month outside the CBD, and $200.00 or $400.00 inside it.

    What is the Central Business District?

    It is the district whose boundaries the schedule points to in Municipal Code section 9-4-010. Many CDOT fees are higher there, and the schedule says closure fees outside it are one-half of the CBD amounts.

    What does a moratorium street add to an opening permit?

    The schedule says opening fees double on a moratorium street, which includes streets paved less than five years ago, and a degradation fee is added that is higher the newer the pavement. Its own example: one cut in a street paved 6 months ago carries a $20,000 degradation fee. The schedule says CDOT approves openings on these streets case by case.

    Will these fees change?

    CDOT says permit fees may change year to year. The schedule says opening fees rise 5% a year, and several other fees can rise with the Consumer Price Index. Confirm the fee with CDOT before you apply.
