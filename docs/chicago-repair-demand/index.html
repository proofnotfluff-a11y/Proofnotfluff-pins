How violations are sorted into trades

Hand mapping by ProofNotFluff, Oct 27, 2026, from the City's own violation codes and descriptions. A violation code named in our mapping goes to its trade; any other code in the City's EV (elevator), BR (boiler), VT (ventilation), RF (refrigeration), PL (plumbing) or EL (electrical) code series goes to the matching trade; everything else is not counted by trade.

How this page is made

Every day we count the violations in the City of Chicago's public Building Violations dataset, which holds the violations the Department of Buildings has issued since 2006, by the month of each violation's date.

Each violation is sorted into a trade by its violation code with our own published mapping: a code we list goes to its trade, any other code in the City's elevator, boiler, ventilation, refrigeration, plumbing or electrical code series goes to the matching trade, and everything else is not counted by trade. Codes for paperwork, permits, plans, records and arranging inspections are left out on purpose.

The dataset has no named community area column, so each violation's map point is placed inside the City's own community area outlines. Addresses and map points are used only for this step and are never stored or shown.

12 months means the last 12 whole months, and the change compares them with the 12 months before. One inspection can cite several violations, and a violation counts in the month it was cited whether or not it has since been fixed.

Only counts are kept. No address, location, property, owner, inspector or comment is stored or shown, so the page cannot point to any building.

Questions

What repairs does Chicago cite most?

Each trade's panel lists the violations the City cited most for it in the last 12 whole months, in the City's own words, and the tiles at the top compare the six trades.

Which Chicago neighborhoods get the most masonry, porch or HVAC violations?

Pick a trade in the panel or on the map to rank all 77 community areas by new violations for it. A higher count can also mean more older buildings, or more inspections there.

Does this count tuckpointing?

In the City's records from Nov 2025 to Oct 2026, no violation is worded as tuckpointing, so this page counts the masonry items the City does name: exterior walls, parapets and coping, chimneys, lintels, sills, cornices and foundations.

Does a violation mean the work is still needed?

Not necessarily. A violation is one item an inspector cited on the date shown. The City's records include cases where the respondent was later found not liable, and this page does not track whether an item was fixed. It names no building or owner.

Can I use this to check a property I am buying?

No. The City says these records are historical and should not be relied on for real estate transactions, and this page holds only neighborhood counts. For a closing, the City points to the title commitment and the court and hearings records.

Which community area is my neighborhood in?

The City counts by its 77 community areas, which hold many neighborhoods: Wicker Park is in West Town, Bucktown is in Logan Square, Wrigleyville is in Lake View, and Pilsen is in the Lower West Side.

Can I use these numbers?

Yes. The area by month download is a free CSV of the counts, and the trade mapping is free to download too. Please name the City of Chicago Building Violations dataset as the source.
