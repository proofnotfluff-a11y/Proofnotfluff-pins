What counts on this page

A plowable day is one day at the weather station with new snow at or over your trigger; a winter runs July 1 to June 30.

Every definition on this page
  • Plowable day: one day at the weather station with new snowfall at or over the trigger depth (1, 2, 3 or 6 inches). Two days in a row that each reach the trigger count as two. A storm that drops 3 inches split 1.5 and 1.5 over two days does not count at a 2 inch trigger, because neither day alone reached 2 inches.
  • Winter: July 1 to June 30, so a winter holds every snow from fall to spring.
  • Light, middle and heavy winter: line up the complete winters from fewest days to most. Light is the winter one fifth of the way up. Middle is the one in the middle, or halfway between the two middle winters when they differ. Heavy is the winter four fifths of the way up. Each tile says how many winters came in at that count or lighter (light) or at that count or heavier (heavy).
  • Incomplete winter: more than 3 days between October and May with no snowfall value in NOAA's file. It is left out of every count, because a missing day could hide a storm. A complete winter can still miss up to 3 days; those days count as no snow.
  • This winter so far: the plowable days since the winter began on July 1, set against the past winters' counts by the same date.
  • Days with snow on the ground: days NOAA measured 1 inch or more of snow depth. It shows how long snow stayed on the ground.

How this page is made

Every count comes from NOAA's daily snowfall and snow depth records for two stations, refreshed each morning from October to April.

Sources, stations and limits

Every count here comes from NOAA's daily records: the Global Historical Climatology Network daily (GHCN-Daily), read through the NCEI Access Data Service. It holds each station's new snowfall and snow depth, one value a day; this page reads them in inches. Station IDs come straight from NOAA's station list, and any value NOAA's quality checks flagged counts as missing.

  • O'Hare: NOAA station USW00094846, Chicago O'Hare International Airport. Its day runs midnight to midnight, local standard time. Snowfall is reported on more than 98.3% of October to May days in the 30 winters.
  • Midway: NOAA station USC00111577, Chicago Midway Airport 3 SW. It is a NOAA cooperative observer station. Its name puts it about 3 miles southwest of the airport, and it has the fullest snowfall record of the NOAA stations named for Midway. Its day is the 24 hours before the observer's daily reading, so it may not run midnight to midnight. NOAA's automated station at the airport itself reports snowfall on too few days to use. Snowfall is reported on more than 99.9% of October to May days in the 30 winters.

The last 30 finished winters are 1996-97 to 2025-26. The page refreshes every morning from October to April; NOAA's file usually runs a few days behind today, and NOAA can revise recent daily values after its quality checks, so this winter's counts can change.

Two weather stations cannot tell you the snow on every street: snowfall can differ across Chicago in a single storm. Use these counts as a starting point next to your own route records. This page counts past winters and does not forecast the coming one.

Questions

How many times do you plow in a Chicago winter?

It depends on your trigger. At O'Hare, the middle winter of the last 28 complete ones had 6 days with 2 inches or more of new snow; a light winter had 4 or fewer and a heavy one 8 or more. Pick your trigger and station at the top of this page for your counts.

What counts as a plowable day here?

One day at the weather station with new snowfall at or over your trigger depth. Two days in a row that each reach the trigger count as two. A storm that splits its snow across two days counts only on a day that alone reached the trigger.

Is this a forecast for this winter?

No. It counts past winters only. Nobody can tell you this winter's count ahead of time, so price a seasonal contract with a cushion for a heavy winter.

Does my neighborhood get the same snow?

Not always. Even these two stations can differ a lot: in 2000-01, O'Hare had 39.2 inches and Midway 54.5. Check your own storm records too.

Why are some winters marked incomplete?

NOAA's daily file has more than 3 days with no snowfall value between October and May in those winters. A missing day could hide a storm, so those winters are left out of every count on this page.
