Chicago general contractor license

The City of Chicago licenses general contractors in five classes, A to E, set by the largest single project value and the demolition allowed. Here is each class, its fee and insurance, and what the State adds.

City of Chicago

The Department of Buildings requires a general contractor license for most building construction, alteration, repair and demolition work in Chicago. Licensed plumbing, electrical and mason contractors working within their own license, and subcontractors working under a licensed general contractor, do not need one.

Five classes: A has no project limit, B goes to $10 million, C to $5 million, D to $2 million and E to $500,000 for the largest single project. A and B may do ordinary or complex demolition, C and D ordinary only, E none.

An individual working on their own primary residence needs no general contractor license if the building has three or fewer stories above grade, six or fewer dwelling units and no nonresidential use. Excavation and demolition still need one. Only one property a calendar year qualifies.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

State of Illinois

The State's professional regulation list has no general contractor entry. No State agency page we read says the State licenses general contractors.

Source: Regulated Professions and Industries, Illinois Financial and Professional Regulation, read Oct 27, 2026

The City says licenses issued by the State of Illinois may also be needed for some kinds of construction, demolition, repair and maintenance work.

Source: Trade License Information Center, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Fee

License and yearly renewal: Class A $2,000, Class B $1,000, Class C $750, Class D $500, Class E $300.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Insurance

Class A $5 million and Class B $3 million per occurrence (primary or umbrella); Classes C and D $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate; Class E $1 million per occurrence. The insurer must be rated B+ or better, and the license goes inactive if the policy lapses.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

State law: a business doing home repair and remodeling carries liability insurance of $100,000 per person and $300,000 per occurrence for bodily injury and $50,000 per occurrence for property damage, plus $10,000 per occurrence for work not done to code, unless its net worth is at least $1,000,000.

Source: Home Repair and Remodeling Act, Section 25, Illinois General Assembly, read Oct 27, 2026

Bond

The City page lists no bond.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Exam

The City page lists no exam. Continental Testing Services takes applications by mail for the Department of Buildings, with a statement of financial solvency, proof of insurance, proof of authority to do business in Illinois (not for sole proprietors) and photo ID.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Renewal

A license is valid for one year from issuance. Renewal goes to Continental Testing Services by mail with proof of current insurance.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Suburb licenses

Not stated. The City page does not say whether a license from a suburb or another town counts in Chicago.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Federal

Firms whose work disturbs lead-based paint in homes, child care facilities and kindergartens built before 1978 must be certified by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) or an authorized state and use certified renovators.

Source: Lead Renovation, Repair and Painting Program Rules, U.S. EPA, read Oct 27, 2026

Which class do you need?

The City sets the class by the largest single project you take on and the demolition in your work. Enter both to see the smallest class that covers them, with its fee and insurance.

The City counts the materials and labor of every contractor and subcontractor on all concurrent and consecutive regulated work at the same site.
