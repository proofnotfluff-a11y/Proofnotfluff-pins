City of Chicago

The City permit page we read names no separate HVAC or refrigeration contractor license. An Express Permit to replace HVAC equipment, add a heat pump or air conditioning, or install refrigeration equipment needs a licensed general contractor, Class A to E by project cost. In some cases an owner who lives at the address may act as contractor with Form 401.

Source: Mechanical work, Express Permit Program, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Home heating and cooling work such as central air, central heating, boilers, furnaces and hot water heaters falls under the City home repair license. If the work needs a building permit, a general contractor license is needed instead.

Source: Home Repair License, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

Operating a steam boiler over 50 boiler horsepower or over 10 pounds per square inch, or a hot water boiler over 160 pounds per square inch or 250 degrees Fahrenheit, needs a City stationary engineer license.

Source: Stationary Engineer License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

State of Illinois

The State's list of licensed professions has no HVAC, heating, refrigeration or mechanical entry. No State agency page we read says the State licenses this work.

Source: Illinois Licensed Professions, Illinois Financial and Professional Regulation, read Oct 27, 2026

Fee

Where a City general contractor license is needed: $300 a year for Class E up to $2,000 a year for Class A, the same to renew.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Home repair license: $250, valid two years, and $250 to renew. Fees change, so check with the Department of Business Affairs and Consumer Protection (BACP) for the current fee.

Source: Home Repair License, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

Stationary engineer: $70 per exam, $30 for the 2 year license and $30 to renew.

Source: Stationary Engineer License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Insurance

Where a City general contractor license is needed: $1 million per occurrence for Class E up to $5 million per occurrence for Class A.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Home repair license: commercial general liability of at least $300,000 per occurrence.

Source: Home Repair License, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

Bond

The general contractor page lists no bond.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Exam

The general contractor page lists no exam.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Stationary engineers pass a written exam given by Continental Testing Services, are at least 20 and have 2 years of practical experience.

Source: Stationary Engineer License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Renewal

A general contractor license runs one year from issuance.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

A stationary engineer license runs two years.

Source: Stationary Engineer License, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Suburb licenses

Not stated. The City trade license pages do not say whether a license from a suburb or another town counts in Chicago.

Source: Trade License Information Center, City of Chicago Buildings, read Oct 27, 2026

Federal

Technicians who maintain, service, repair or dispose of equipment that could release refrigerant need U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Section 608 certification (Type I, II, III or Universal) from an EPA approved certifying organization. The certification does not expire.

Source: Section 608 Technician Certification Requirements, U.S. EPA, read Oct 27, 2026

Check with the Chicago Department of Buildings, the Chicago Department of Business Affairs and Consumer Protection and the U.S. EPA before you apply or bid. Fees and rules change, and this page is not legal advice.
