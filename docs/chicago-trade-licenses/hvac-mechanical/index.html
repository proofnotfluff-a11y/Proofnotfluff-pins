Chicago HVAC and mechanical licensing

No separate City HVAC license. Permitted HVAC work needs a City general contractor; refrigerant work needs EPA Section 608 certification.

Every line checked on Oct 27, 2026 on the agency's own page, linked beside it.

HVAC and mechanical

The City pages name no separate HVAC or refrigeration contractor license. Here is which City license covers HVAC work with and without a permit, the boiler operator license, and the federal refrigerant rule.

City of Chicago

The City has no separate HVAC or refrigeration contractor license on its permit pages. A permit to replace HVAC equipment, add a heat pump or air conditioning, or install refrigeration equipment needs a licensed general contractor, Class A to E by project cost. An owner who lives at the address may act as contractor with Form 401.

Source: Mechanical (HVAC) work permits, City of Chicago Buildings, checked Oct 27, 2026

Home heating and cooling work that needs no building permit, such as central air, furnaces, boilers and water heaters, falls under the City home repair license. Work that needs a permit needs a general contractor license, and home repair licensees may not pull building permits.

Source: Home Repair License, City of Chicago, checked Oct 27, 2026

Running most steam and hot water boilers needs a City stationary engineer license for the person who operates them.

Source: Stationary Engineer License, City of Chicago Buildings, checked Oct 27, 2026

State of Illinois

The State's list of licensed professions has no HVAC, heating, refrigeration or mechanical entry. No State agency page we opened says the State licenses this work.

Source: Illinois Licensed Professions, Illinois IDFPR, checked Oct 27, 2026

Fee

Where a City general contractor license is needed: $300 a year for Class E up to $2,000 a year for Class A, the same to renew.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, checked Oct 27, 2026

Home repair license: $250, valid two years, and $250 to renew.

Source: Home Repair License, City of Chicago, checked Oct 27, 2026

Stationary engineer: $70 per exam, $30 for the 2 year license and $30 to renew.

Source: Stationary Engineer License, City of Chicago Buildings, checked Oct 27, 2026

Insurance

Where a City general contractor license is needed: $1 million per occurrence for Class E up to $5 million per occurrence for Class A.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, checked Oct 27, 2026

Home repair license: commercial general liability of at least $300,000 per occurrence.

Source: Home Repair License, City of Chicago, checked Oct 27, 2026

Bond

The general contractor page lists no bond.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, checked Oct 27, 2026

Exam

No exam for the general contractor license. Stationary engineers pass a written exam given by Continental Testing Services, are at least 20 and have 2 years of practical experience.

Source: Stationary Engineer License, City of Chicago Buildings, checked Oct 27, 2026

Renewal

A general contractor license runs one year from issuance.

Source: General Contractor License, City of Chicago Buildings, checked Oct 27, 2026

A stationary engineer license runs two years.

Source: Stationary Engineer License, City of Chicago Buildings, checked Oct 27, 2026

Suburb licenses

Not stated. The City trade license pages do not say whether a license from a suburb or another town counts in Chicago.

Source: Trade License Information Center, City of Chicago Buildings, checked Oct 27, 2026

Federal

Technicians who maintain, service, repair or dispose of equipment that could release refrigerant need EPA Section 608 certification (Type I, II, III or Universal) from an EPA approved certifying organization. The certification does not expire.

Source: Section 608 Technician Certification Requirements, U.S. EPA, checked Oct 27, 2026

Check with the Chicago Department of Buildings and U.S. EPA before you apply or bid. Fees and rules change, and this page is not legal advice.
