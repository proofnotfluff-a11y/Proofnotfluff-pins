Chicago junk removal and hauling licensing

Chicago licenses refuse haulers as private scavengers and junk collectors as junk peddlers. Here is what each City page says, the recycling rules for haulers, and the State special waste permit.

City of Chicago

The license table of the City's Department of Business Affairs and Consumer Protection (BACP) lists a Private Scavenger license and a Junk Peddler license, both regulated business licenses. No City page we read says which one a home junk removal company needs.

Source: Business Licensing, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

The company that collects from a commercial refuse container (dumpster), on private or public property, gets a Chicago Department of Transportation permit, and to get one it holds a valid private scavenger license.

Source: Refuse Container Permit System, City of Chicago Transportation, read Oct 27, 2026

BACP's junk peddler fact sheet says the license covers anyone who uses a vehicle to collect, dispose of or transport junk, including junk bought from people who want it hauled away.

Source: Junk Peddlers fact sheet, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

Under the City recycling ordinance, every private hauler offers recycling, takes recyclables to a permitted facility, keeps a working GPS (satellite location) tracker on each vehicle and files a yearly report by February 28. A private scavenger license is not renewed if that report is late or incomplete.

Source: Chicago Recycling Ordinance, with 2023 updates, City of Chicago, read Oct 27, 2026

On covered construction and demolition projects, at least 50% of the debris is recycled, and the waste hauler certifies that it was.

Source: Construction and Demolition Recycling Ordinance overview, City of Chicago Public Health, read Oct 27, 2026

State of Illinois

The Illinois Environmental Protection Agency (Illinois EPA) requires a special waste hauling permit to haul special waste.

Source: Special waste transportation permits, Illinois EPA, read Oct 27, 2026

The State rule's definition of industrial process waste leaves out household waste, landscape waste and construction or demolition debris. The State pages we read list no license for hauling ordinary household junk.

Source: Part 809, Nonhazardous Special Waste Hauling, Illinois Pollution Control Board, read Oct 27, 2026

Fee

Private Scavenger $250 and Junk Peddler $250 for two years each, in the BACP license table read on Oct 27, 2026. Fees change, so check with BACP for the current fee.

Source: Business Licensing, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

The older junk peddler fact sheet prints a $150 application fee, which differs from the license table.

Source: Junk Peddlers fact sheet, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

Insurance

The junk peddler fact sheet asks for proof of vehicle insurance for each junk vehicle, with no amount. No City page we read prints an amount for a private scavenger license.

Source: Junk Peddlers fact sheet, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

Bond

The junk peddler fact sheet lists no bond.

Source: Junk Peddlers fact sheet, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

Exam

The BACP license table lists no exam for either license.

Source: Business Licensing, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

Renewal

A junk peddler license renews every two years.

Source: Junk Peddlers fact sheet, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

Suburb licenses

Not stated. The BACP pages do not say whether a license from a suburb counts in Chicago.

Source: Applying for a Business License, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

Check with the Chicago Department of Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, the Chicago Department of Transportation and the Illinois EPA before you apply or bid. Fees and rules change, and this page is not legal advice.
