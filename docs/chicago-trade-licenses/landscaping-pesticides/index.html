Chicago landscaping and pesticide licensing

Landscapers who apply pesticides for hire are licensed by the Illinois Department of Agriculture. Here is that license, its insurance and exams, and what the City says.

Landscaping and pesticide use: City and State requirements

City of Chicago

The City home repair page says landscaping does not need a home repair license.

Source: Home Repair, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, checked Oct 27, 2026

No City page we read names a license for this trade. The license table of the Department of Business Affairs and Consumer Protection (BACP) lists a Limited Business license for general retail sales and services, says a business may need more than one license, and gives 312-74-GOBIZ as the number to call to find out which license applies.

Source: Business Licensing, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, checked Oct 27, 2026

BACP says it decides the license type from the business's activities.

Source: Business License Types, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, checked Oct 27, 2026

BACP says most business activities in Chicago require a City business license.

Source: Applying for a Business License, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, checked Oct 27, 2026

BACP's home business fact sheet lists a landscaping business that stores goods and materials at home as a use not allowed as a home occupation.

Source: Home-Based Businesses in Chicago fact sheet, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, checked Oct 27, 2026

State of Illinois

The Illinois Department of Agriculture requires a Commercial Applicator or Commercial Operator license for anyone who buys, uses or supervises the use of general or restricted use pesticides for hire. Each business has at least one licensed applicator at each location, and its license categories cover all its pesticide work, such as turf or ornamental pest control.

Source: Pesticide certification and licensing, Illinois Agriculture, checked Oct 27, 2026

Fee

Licenses run 3 years. For hire: Commercial Applicator $300, Commercial Operator $240. Not for hire: applicator $180, operator $150.

Source: Pesticide certification and licensing, Illinois Agriculture, checked Oct 27, 2026

Limited Business License: $250 for two years, in the BACP license table read on Oct 27, 2026. Fees change, so check with BACP for the current fee.

Source: Business Licensing, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, checked Oct 27, 2026

Insurance

For hire applicators file a certificate of liability insurance or a surety bond: at least $50,000 per person and $100,000 per occurrence for bodily injury with a $500,000 aggregate, and $50,000 per occurrence for property damage; or a $100,000 combined single limit with a $500,000 aggregate.

Source: Pesticide certification and licensing, Illinois Agriculture, checked Oct 27, 2026

Bond

A surety bond is accepted in place of the insurance certificate; the page prints no separate bond amount.

Source: Pesticide certification and licensing, Illinois Agriculture, checked Oct 27, 2026

Exam

Applicators and operators pass the 100-question General Standards exam with 70 or more right; applicators also pass at least one 50-question category exam with 35 or more right.

Source: Pesticide certification and licensing, Illinois Agriculture, checked Oct 27, 2026

The Department gives pesticide exams in person and online.

Source: Training and testing for pesticide applicators and operators, Illinois Agriculture, checked Oct 27, 2026

Renewal

Licenses run 3 years, and passed exams stay valid for 3 years if the license does not lapse. Applicators show insurance or a bond again at renewal.

Source: Pesticide certification and licensing, Illinois Agriculture, checked Oct 27, 2026

Suburb licenses

Not stated. The Agriculture pages do not say whether a license from a suburb counts in Chicago. Someone currently licensed for the same type of pesticide application in their home state, on a closed book exam taken in the past three years, can apply for a one calendar year Illinois license.

Source: Pesticide certification and licensing, Illinois Agriculture, checked Oct 27, 2026

Check with the Illinois Department of Agriculture and the Chicago Department of Business Affairs and Consumer Protection before you apply or bid. Fees and rules change, and this page is not legal advice.
