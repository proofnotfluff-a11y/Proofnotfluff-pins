Chicago pest control license

Pest control businesses are licensed by the Illinois Department of Public Health and must employ a certified technician. Here is the State license, exams, insurance and renewal, and what the City says.

City of Chicago

No City page we read names a license for this trade. The license table of the Department of Business Affairs and Consumer Protection (BACP) lists a Limited Business license for general retail sales and services, says a business may need more than one license, and gives 312-74-GOBIZ as the number to call to find out which license applies.

Source: Business Licensing, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

BACP says it decides the license type from the business's activities.

Source: Business License Types, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

BACP says most business activities in Chicago require a City business license.

Source: Applying for a Business License, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

A business run from the owner's Chicago home uses the Home Occupation license, which allows no more than one non-resident employee to work in the home.

Source: Home Based Business, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

State of Illinois

The Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH) requires a Commercial Structural Pest Control Business License, and the business employs at least one IDPH certified technician.

Source: Structural Pest Control, Illinois Public Health, read Oct 27, 2026

Fee

State business license: $250 application fee.

Source: Structural Pest Control business license application, Illinois Public Health, read Oct 27, 2026

Technician exams: $125 for General Use and any restricted use subcategories, $50 to add a subcategory to a current certification.

Source: Technician certification application, restricted use, Illinois Public Health, read Oct 27, 2026

Limited Business License: $250 for two years, in the BACP license table read on Oct 27, 2026. Fees change, so check with BACP for the current fee.

Source: Business Licensing, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

Insurance

The IDPH certificate of insurance form, signed by the insurer's authorized representative, lists $100,000 per person or $300,000 per occurrence for bodily injury and $50,000 per occurrence for property damage, or $350,000 per occurrence combined.

Source: Structural Pest Control certificate of insurance form, Illinois Public Health, read Oct 27, 2026

Bond

The State business license application lists no bond.

Source: Structural Pest Control business license application, Illinois Public Health, read Oct 27, 2026

Exam

IDPH gives the technician exams, and 70 percent passes each one.

Source: Structural Pest Control, Illinois Public Health, read Oct 27, 2026

General use work needs the General Standards exam; restricted use work adds one or more subcategory exams, such as termites, fumigation, or insects and rodents.

Source: Technician certification application, restricted use, Illinois Public Health, read Oct 27, 2026

Renewal

State business licenses expire December 31 each year. Technician certificate renewal needs nine credit hours of approved training.

Source: Structural Pest Control FAQ, Illinois Public Health, read Oct 27, 2026

Technician certification is renewed by December 1 every 3 years.

Source: Structural Pest Control, Illinois Public Health, read Oct 27, 2026

City business licenses run one or two years and renew online through Chicago Business Direct after BACP sends a notice.

Source: Renewing a Business License, City of Chicago Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, read Oct 27, 2026

Suburb licenses

Not stated. The IDPH program page and its questions page do not address licenses from other towns.

Source: Structural Pest Control FAQ, Illinois Public Health, read Oct 27, 2026

Check with the Illinois Department of Public Health and the Chicago Department of Business Affairs and Consumer Protection before you apply or bid. Fees and rules change, and this page is not legal advice.
