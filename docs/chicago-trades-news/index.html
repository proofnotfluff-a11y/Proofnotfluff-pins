Tax and payProposedReported Oct 21, 2026

Budget plan would raise Chicago's tax on equipment rentals and cloud software to 20%

Mayor Johnson's proposed 2027 budget raises the City's personal property lease transaction tax from 15% to 20%, worth about $234 million in 2027. That tax covers rented equipment and cloud software; car leasing would stay at 15%.

What it means for you: If it passes, rented lifts and tools and software subscriptions such as scheduling, invoicing and office apps could cost more. Check which of your bills already carry the Chicago lease tax so you know what would change.

  • All trades

Sources:WBEZ, head tax returns in the budget planWTTW, Johnson's proposed 2027 budget

Jobs and projectsProposedReported Oct 21, 2026

Budget plan adds 28 City rat crews and 20 vacant lot cleanup crews

The proposed 2027 budget adds 28 rodent control crews and 20 crews to clear trash from vacant lots, and trims 15,000 more trees a year. It follows a July Inspector General audit that faulted the City for reacting to 311 rat calls instead of preventing them.

What it means for you: Pest control firms may hear more from customers about sealing and sanitation, not only bait. The weekly 311 rat counts for every community area are on our free rat complaints page.

Tax and payProposedReported Oct 21, 2026

Chicago's 2027 budget plan brings back a per-worker tax on large employers

Mayor Johnson's proposed 2027 budget includes a tax of $25 a month for each employee at companies with more than 125 workers, which the City expects to raise about $100 million a year. A larger version was voted down last year, and the City Council still has to vote on this one.

What it means for you: Most small trade shops are under 125 workers, so check your headcount if you are near the line. Larger contractors and cleaning or facility service firms should follow the budget hearings and the vote before year end.

Jobs and projectsStarts Dec 1, 2026Reported Oct 16, 2026

Winter leans mild with more sleet and freezing rain; parking ban starts December 1

NOAA's December to February outlook leans warmer than normal for the Chicago area with even odds on precipitation, as El Nino strengthens, and a local forecaster expects more mixed sleet, snow and freezing rain days. The City's overnight parking ban on 107 miles of main streets runs 3 am to 7 am from December 1 to April 1.

What it means for you: Snow contractors may see fewer big plowable storms but more salting calls, so price ice runs carefully and stock salt early. Plan overnight truck and trailer parking around the December 1 ban.

Social Security tax will apply to the first $192,300 of pay in 2027

The Social Security Administration raised the maximum earnings taxed for Social Security from $184,500 in 2026 to $192,300 in 2027. Medicare tax still has no earnings cap.

What it means for you: Owners who pay themselves a salary, and top earners on your crew, will owe Social Security tax on more pay next year. Make sure your payroll service and your 2027 estimated tax payments use the new number.

Rules and permitsStarts Dec 1, 2026Reported Oct 9, 2026

Chicago's updated construction codes are required for permits filed from December 1

The City Council passed ordinance SO2026-0024930 on June 17, 2026, updating Chicago's mechanical, fuel gas, energy and interim fire prevention codes to current model codes, with targeted changes to the building and rehab codes. The new codes have been optional since October 1 and are required for projects submitted from December 1, 2026, counted from the day the permit fee is paid.

What it means for you: Design any job whose permit fee will not be paid before December 1 to the new codes. HVAC contractors should note that homes now need ducted make up air, and the energy code moves to the 2024 IECC.

  • HVAC

SafetyStarts Jan 1, 2027Reported Oct 2, 2026

Illinois genetic privacy law expands to biomarker tests; drug testing questions raised

Public Act 104-0642 extends the Genetic Information Privacy Act to biomarker testing, so employers may not require genetic or biomarker tests as a condition of work or act on the results. A law firm reports the change starts January 1, 2027 and that some employers worry the wording could reach workplace drug tests.

What it means for you: If you run pre hire, random or post accident drug tests, ask your testing vendor or lawyer to review the program before January. The law does not mention drug testing by name.

  • All trades

Sources:Illinois General Assembly, Public Act 104-0642Amundsen Davis, October 2026 updates

1099s for 2026 payments start at $2,000, not $600

Under the 2025 federal tax law, businesses send Form 1099-NEC or 1099-MISC only for payments of $2,000 or more made from January 1, 2026, up from $600, with inflation adjustments from 2027. Payment apps file Form 1099-K only above $20,000 and 200 payments.

What it means for you: When you send 1099s to subcontractors early next year, the cutoff for 2026 payments is $2,000. Keep collecting a W-9 from every sub.

City contractors and their subs face new conduct certifications; rules due November 24

An executive order signed August 26 directs the City's chief procurement officer to write rules, due by November 24, 2026, requiring firms seeking City contracts over $250,000 to certify no public integrity, discrimination or wage theft violation in the past five years. Primes must pass matching terms down to their subcontractors.

What it means for you: If you work as a sub on City jobs, expect new certification forms from your prime. Make sure your wage and hour records are in order now, since wage theft counts.

Rules and permitsStarts Jan 1, 2027Reported Aug 19, 2026

From 2027, job ads can ask for a driver's license only when driving is essential

A change to the Illinois Job Opportunities for Qualified Applicants Act, HB 4758, says that from January 1, 2027 a job posting may require a valid driver's license only when driving is an essential part of the job and a business necessity, and the posting must say why. It covers employers with 15 or more employees and employment agencies.

What it means for you: Service techs who drive to calls will usually qualify, but the ad now has to say so. Drop the license line from postings for shop, warehouse or office jobs where driving is not part of the work.

  • All trades

Sources:Saul Ewing, Illinois employment law updateHinshaw, Illinois employment law changes for 2027

New City review for data centers; a 12 month pause is proposed

An executive order signed August 11 sends any data center that needs a new Planned Development through a review of power, water, backup generators, air, noise and neighborhood impact, and orders new air permit and noise rules. A 12 month pause on new or bigger data centers has been proposed to the City Council but not passed.

What it means for you: Electrical and mechanical contractors chasing data center work in the city should expect slower approvals, and possibly a pause on new projects. Projects the zoning already allows may still move.

Tax and payIn effect since Jan 1, 2026Reported Aug 6, 2026

Overtime on 2026 W-2s goes in Box 12, Code TT, for the federal overtime deduction

The IRS updated its guidance on the federal overtime deduction, worth up to $12,500 a year or $25,000 on a joint return, with a phase out at higher incomes. For 2026, employers report qualified overtime in Box 12 of the W-2 with Code TT, and the 2025 relief is not extended.

What it means for you: If you pay hourly crews overtime, set up payroll now to track the federally required overtime premium so January W-2s are right. Only overtime the federal rules require counts, not extra premiums from a union contract or company policy.

  • All trades

Sources:EY Tax News, IRS 2026 overtime guidanceJournal of Accountancy, IRS overtime FAQs

Rules and permitsStarts Jan 1, 2027Reported Aug 5, 2026

Illinois Attorney General gets new powers to investigate employers from January 1

HB 4725, signed July 31, 2026, lets the Attorney General's Workplace Rights Bureau issue subpoenas, require sworn written answers, inspect non public parts of a business, copy records on site and interview workers, starting January 1, 2027. Knowingly hiding or destroying records under subpoena can be a Class A misdemeanor.

What it means for you: Wage, hour and worker classification complaints can turn into on site state investigations. Get timesheets, pay records and your 1099 or W-2 decisions organized before January.

  • All trades

Sources:Amundsen Davis, Attorney General investigation powersSaul Ewing, Illinois employment law update

Tax and payStarts Jan 1, 2027Reported Jul 31, 2026

Illinois employers with more than 25 workers must pay for jury duty from 2027

Governor Pritzker signed HB 4844 on July 31, 2026, so employers must pay workers their regular rate for time on jury duty starting January 1, 2027. Employers with 25 or fewer employees still only have to give unpaid leave.

What it means for you: If you have more than 25 employees, add paid jury duty to your handbook and payroll before January. Smaller shops keep giving unpaid time off for jury service.

Sources:Illinois General Assembly, Public Act 104-0683Hinshaw, Illinois employment law changes for 2027

Chicago's minimum wage is $17.05 an hour

Chicago's minimum wage rose from $16.60 to $17.05 an hour on July 1, 2026, for employers with four or more workers, and the tipped wage rose to $12.96. Suburban Cook County is $15.40 and the Illinois minimum stays at $15.

What it means for you: Check that helpers, cleaners and entry level crew working in the city get at least $17.05 an hour. Jobs in the suburbs follow the county or state rate.

How this page is made

Each Tuesday we read the City of Chicago, Cook County, State of Illinois and federal pages that publish new rules, taxes, wage rates and licenses, plus Chicago's main news outlets, and pick the items that change how a trade business works or what it pays.

Every item is written in our own words and links the page it comes from. We prefer the government page itself; when an item rests on news reports, the source line says so. A second reviewer checks every number and date against its source before the brief is published.

Proposed means not passed: budget items, ordinances and bills can change or fail. Items stay on the page for about four months, or until their start date passes.

This is news, not legal, tax or financial advice. Before you change a price, a payroll setting or a filing, check the source and ask your accountant, lawyer or licensing office.
