Christmas light pricing

What do installers charge per foot to hang Christmas lights?

By ProofNotFluff, run by a finance executive · Worked example from the free Christmas light installation pricing calculator. Checked Oct 27, 2026.

The short answer

Installers who post their prices land around $4 to $8 a foot of roofline, some with a job minimum, and one homeowner guide puts labor on the customer's own lights lower, at $2 to $5 a foot. Service Autopilot calls $5 to $8 a linear foot the most common way to price. In two installer forum threads, one charged $4 to $5 a foot with the lights lent, and another set $7 a foot as the minimum for C9 lights with an $850 job minimum.

Treat those as a check, not your price. Work your rate out from your crew's pace, drive and lights. In the worked example below, a 150-foot roofline with 4 bushes quotes $915, or $6.10 a foot, and the same crew's floor is $4.26 a foot.

How to set your own rate a foot

  1. Measure the roofline and count the extras

    Price the feet of roofline you will hang, then add each bush or tree by the item. Big trees are priced on their own: in the Pressure Washing Resource thread, installers price trees by height and how full they are, and raise the rate as trees get taller.

  2. Time your crew

    Your pace in feet an hour drives most of the labor. In the example, going from 25 to 60 feet an hour each moves the quote from $1,130 to $795. Time a few houses this season and use the real number next year.

  3. Count takedown and the drive

    Takedown is a second trip and more hours. In the example the two trips add $150.40 for the crew's time and the miles, which is why short rooflines cost more a foot.

  4. Decide who owns the lights

    Lights you lend each year are spread over the seasons they last plus storage. Lights the customer buys are charged this year, so the example quotes $1,220 now and $710 next season. On the customer's own lights the same house quotes $710.

  5. Add your margin and a job minimum

    The quote divides the floor by one less your margin. A job minimum covers the small houses where the setup and trips eat the profit: in the example an 80-foot roofline quotes $505, $6.31 a foot.

  6. Check it against the going rates

    If your rate is well under the published ranges below, you may be leaving money on the table; well over, check your pace and costs before you cut it.

What installers and guides publish

In the Pressure Washing Resource thread this page answers (November 2021), the asker charged $7 Canadian a foot for C9 lights. One reply set $7 a foot as the minimum for C9 with an $850 job minimum. In a Window Cleaning Resource thread from November 2020, a window cleaner charged $4 to $5 a foot with the lights lent, and said they expected the first season to pay about $30 to $50 an hour because the lights were bought that year, then about $70 to $100 an hour after. The same installer said in a later post that other companies have customers buy the lights, charge for labor only, and add $100 a year for storage.

Guides written for homeowners run lower or wider. ThePricer lists $2 to $5 a foot for labor on the customer's own lights, $5 to $10 a foot with lights supplied, and $50 to $150 a year for storage. Angi's Washington, D.C. guide puts installation labor at $3.10 to $7.80 a linear foot, not counting the lights. These are other people's numbers, read Oct 27, 2026, not ours.

Lend the lights or sell them?

Lending keeps the customer coming back each year and spreads the lights over the seasons they last, but you store, test and fix them. Selling them charges the lights up front and lowers next season's quote. The calculator shows both for the same house.

Questions

How much do installers charge per foot to hang Christmas lights?

Installer prices in public posts and guides run about $4 to $8 a foot of roofline, and labor on the customer's own lights runs lower. In the worked example, a 150-foot roofline with 4 bushes quotes $6.10 a foot.

Do Christmas light installers charge a minimum?

Some do. One installer in a 2021 forum thread set an $850 job minimum. In the example, an 80-foot roofline quotes $505, which is $6.31 a foot, because the trips and setup are spread over fewer feet.

Is takedown included in the price per foot?

Often. ThePricer and Angi both say removal is often part of the install price, so say in your quote whether yours is. The worked example counts takedown at 50% of the install hours and its own trip.

What should I charge to hang a customer's own lights?

Less, since you do not supply or store the lights. In the example the same house quotes $710 on the customer's own lights, $4.73 a foot, against $915 with your lights lent.
