Check your quote against published ranges

  • Service Autopilot puts per-foot pricing at $5 to $8 a linear foot and calls it the most common way to price. Source: Service Autopilot, How to Bid Christmas Light Installation.
  • ThePricer, a homeowner cost guide, lists $2 to $5 a foot for labor on the customer's own lights and $5 to $10 a foot with lights supplied, with storage at $50 to $150 a year. Source: ThePricer, How Much Does Christmas Light Installation Cost.
  • Jobber divides labor, materials and overhead by one less your target margin; this calculator divides the same way. Source: Jobber Academy, How to Price Christmas Light Installation.

These are other companies' published estimates, checked Oct 26, 2026, not our data. None of them gives a pace in feet per hour, so the calculator uses yours. Time a few houses this season and use your real number next year.

How it works

Install hours are the feet of roofline divided by the feet one person hangs an hour, plus each bush or tree at your minutes. Takedown adds a share of those hours. All of it costs your labor rate per person-hour, whether you pay a crew or price your own time.

Every trip, install and takedown plus any service calls you expect, carries the whole crew's drive time at the same rate and the vehicle's miles.

Lights you own and lend each year are spread over the seasons they last, plus storage and handling. Lights the customer buys are charged in full this year; next season the same house quotes without them.

The price floor is labor, drive, lights and storage. The quote divides the floor by one less your margin and rounds up to the next $5. Price per foot is the quote over the feet of roofline.

A worked example

A 150-foot roofline and 4 bushes, a 2-person crew that hangs 40 feet an hour each and wraps a bush in 20 minutes, at $45 a person-hour: 5.08 hours of install work, 2.54 hours on site for the crew, and takedown at half the install hours: 7.63 hours of work, $343.13 of labor. Two trips of 40 minutes and 20 miles add $150.40. Lights you own at $1.60 a foot and $30 a bush cost $360, spread over 3 seasons that is $120, plus $25 of storage. The floor is $638.53 and a 30% margin quotes $915, $6.10 a foot with $276.47 of profit. If the customer buys the lights instead, this year quotes $1,220 and next season $710.

Questions

How much should I charge to hang Christmas lights?

Price from your own costs: your crew's time for install and takedown, the drive, the lights and your margin. With the example numbers a 150-foot roofline and 4 bushes quotes $915, or $6.10 a foot. Type your own numbers to see yours.

Should I charge per foot or per house?

Most installers quote per foot of roofline and add trees and bushes by the item. This calculator gives both: the house total and the price per foot it works out to.

Should I sell the lights or lease them?

Owning and lending them each year spreads their cost over the seasons they last and keeps the customer coming back, but you store and test them. Selling them charges the lights up front and makes next season's quote lower. Switch the lights option to compare.

Is takedown included?

Yes. Takedown hours are a share of install hours that you set, and the takedown trip is counted. Change the share if your takedowns run faster or slower.
