If you ask a platform for a credit

Each platform sets its own rules for lead credits and decides each request. If you plan to ask, these records are worth keeping for every lead you dispute:

  • The date and time the lead arrived and the amount you were charged for it.
  • The request exactly as the platform sent it: the service, the location and the job details.
  • Every call, text and message you sent the customer, with the time.
  • What did not match: a wrong or disconnected number, a duplicate, a job outside your service area, or a service you don't offer.
  • Screenshots of the lead and the charge, taken the day it arrived.
  • The platform's credit rule as it read that day, with the date you copied it.

Good records don't mean a credit will be given. Check the platform's current rules before you ask.

How it works

Cost per lead is what you paid for leads divided by the leads you were charged for. Cost per won job divides the same spend by the jobs you won, so it carries the leads that went nowhere. That second number is the one to set against what a job leaves you. The math is the same for any trade that buys leads.

Profit left after lead spend is the revenue from the jobs you won, less their direct cost (labor you pay, supplies or chemicals, gas) and less the month's lead bill. Your own time and fixed bills such as insurance and the truck payment stay out, so this is what the leads left you to cover them.

The most a won job can cost is what one job leaves you before the lead: average job revenue less its direct cost. Pay more than that in leads for each job you win and the jobs lose money.

The rates come from your own counts: leads that became quotes, quotes that became jobs. The page uses nobody else's close rates. The break-even against our lists uses only our prices, your job profit and your own guess of jobs.

A worked example

Say a house cleaner paid $1,200 for 40 leads in a month, sent 18 quotes and won 6 jobs at $280 each, with $110 of labor, supplies and gas per job. That is $30.00 a lead, $66.67 a quote and $200.00 in leads for each job won. The 6 jobs brought in $1,680 and cost $660 to do, leaving $1,020, so after the $1,200 lead bill the month lost $180. Each job left $170.00 before its lead cost and lost $30.00 after it; covering that lead bill took 8 won jobs, not 6. The most a won job could cost was $170.00, so each one ran $30.00 over. At that job profit, one won job a month covers a $39 or a $29 list; if a list won 1 job a month, the guess in the example, it would leave $131 after a $39 price.

Questions

How do I calculate cost per lead?

Divide what you paid for leads by the number of leads you were charged for. In the example, $1,200 for 40 leads is $30.00 a lead.

What is cost per won job?

Your lead spend divided by the jobs you won from those leads. In the example, $1,200 and 6 won jobs is $200.00 a job, though each lead cost $30.00.

Are Angi or Thumbtack leads worth it?

Only your own numbers can say. If what a job leaves you after its direct cost is more than your lead cost per won job, the leads made money that month. In the example a job leaves $170.00 and the leads cost $200.00 per won job, so they lost $30.00 a job.

What counts as the direct cost of a job?

What you spend only because you did that job: labor you pay for it, supplies or chemicals, gas and parking. Leave out the lead itself and the fixed bills you pay anyway.

Will a platform give me a credit for a bad lead?

Each platform has its own rules and decides each request, so this page can't tell you. The checklist above lists records worth keeping if you ask.

Do your lists cost less per won job than Angi or Thumbtack?

We can't say. Nobody on our lists has asked for a quote, and we have no data on how many records become jobs. The page shows break-even instead: the won jobs a month each list needs to cover its price, at your own job profit.

How are your City-record lists different from platform leads?

A platform lead is a person who asked for a quote. Our lists are City records, such as food inspections, new business licenses and building permits, sorted by trade. Nobody on them has asked for a quote, so you make the first contact.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is arithmetic on the numbers you type.
