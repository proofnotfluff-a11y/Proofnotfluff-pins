Compared, with prices

Ways to find post-construction cleaning leads in Chicago

The ways cleaning companies find builder work in Chicago, with what each costs and what you actually get. Prices were read from each company's own page on Oct 25, 2026 unless marked as a third-party estimate. One of these is ours, and it is labeled.

Updated Oct 25, 2026. Prices change; each row links the page it came from.

City of Chicago Data Portal: Building Permits

The city's open dataset of building permits issued since 2006, updated often (last refreshed Oct 24, 2026).

Price
Free (no fee shown; reuse governed by the portal's terms of use)
What you get
Raw permit rows: address, work description, fees, and contact columns with each contact's role (general contractor, owner, architect) and name, plus city, state and ZIP. No phone or email.
The catch
Hard to use as is: it mixes small and large jobs, and you must filter it and find the builder's phone or email yourself.

Source: the City's dataset page, read Oct 25, 2026.

ConstructConnect (Project Intelligence)

National commercial construction project database and bid platform.

Price
Starter $129/mo per market per seat (10% off annual); Professional from $199/mo per market per seat, annual contract; Premium from $10,000/yr
What you get
Bidding project leads with key contacts (Starter); Professional adds planning-stage projects, full phone, email and LinkedIn contacts, and plans and specs.
The catch
Built for trades bidding plans; full contact details need the annual Professional contract, and cost scales per market and per seat.

Source: the company's own page, read Oct 25, 2026.

Dodge Construction Network (Dodge One)

Commercial construction project data and market intelligence covering North America.

Price
Price on request (Dodge says it does not publish a standard price list)
What you get
Project reports from planning through construction start, contact intelligence on decision makers, plan room, saved searches and alerts.
The catch
Quote only: pricing comes after a demo with a product specialist and depends on region, users and contract length, so small cleaning firms cannot compare costs up front.

Source: the company's own page, read Oct 25, 2026.

BuildZoom (for contractors)

Marketplace that matches homeowner projects to contractors.

Price
Not published: a flat connection fee per project once you connect, plus a referral percentage if hired; amounts appear only on each project
What you get
Homeowner project requests, each checked with the homeowner and matched to the work you do.
The catch
Projects come from homeowners, not general contractors, so it is not built for builder post-construction cleanup; fee amounts only appear project by project.

Source: the company's own page, read Oct 25, 2026.

Angi Leads (Angi's pay-per-lead program for pros)

Pay-per-lead homeowner marketplace for home service pros, including cleaning.

Price
Not published by Angi. Third-party estimate: about $15 to $85 per lead plus about $300 a year membership
What you get
Homeowner service requests, charged as soon as each lead is sent, whether or not you win the job.
The catch
Leads are often shared with several pros and you pay even if the homeowner never answers; homeowner cleaning, not builder cleanup.

Source: a third-party estimate, read Oct 25, 2026.

Thumbtack (pay per lead)

Local services marketplace where pros pay for customer leads.

Price
Not published by Thumbtack; no subscription. Third-party summary: pros report about $10 to over $100 per lead
What you get
Homeowner requests matched automatically or chosen by you; you pay per lead and can cap weekly spend.
The catch
No refunds when a customer goes quiet or hires someone else, and the price per lead varies by market and job.

Source: a third-party estimate, read Oct 25, 2026.

GC bidder lists and local networking (example: BOMA/Chicago)

Asking general contractors to add you to their subcontractor or vendor list, plus joining a local building-industry group to meet buyers.

Price
GC lists: free to ask. BOMA/Chicago Introductory Affiliate: $1,500 for the calendar year (prorated to $375 from Oct 1); full Affiliate dues not published
What you get
Direct relationships with GCs and property managers; BOMA gives member-rate events and an Affiliate Directory listing, but not the Building Member Directory.
The catch
Slow and relationship driven; you still need to know which GCs are active. BOMA connects you with commercial building owners and managers rather than builders.

Source: the company's own page, read Oct 25, 2026.

PlanHub (bid board)

Construction bid platform where GCs post projects and invite subcontractors to bid.

Price
Subcontractor Premier plans $799 to $3,499 a year, priced by mileage radius (25 miles to unlimited), after a free trial
What you get
GC bid invitations and project listings within your radius; paid plans add full plan room access, the full GC bid list, and the awarded GC's name and contact.
The catch
Built around bidding from plans, so a cleanup scope competes with every trade's invites; billed yearly, and a wider radius costs more.

Source: the company's own page, read Oct 25, 2026.

ProofNotFluff Chicago Construction Cleaning Leads (ours)

A weekly list of open Chicago apartment and commercial permits of $500,000 or more, built from the same City dataset, with the general contractor named on each and the projects with temporary or partial occupancy approved marked.

Price
$29 a month after a 7-day free trial, no lock-in
What you get
Every kept permit, the builder on each, near-finish projects first, a members' web app and an Excel file every Monday.
The catch
Chicago only and weekly, not daily; it names the builder but not a phone or email, so you still make the first call; it is our own product.

Source: our page, read Oct 25, 2026.
