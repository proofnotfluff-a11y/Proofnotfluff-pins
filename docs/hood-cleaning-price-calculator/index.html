The schedule and what others charge

  • NFPA 96 sets how often a kitchen exhaust system is inspected: monthly for solid fuel, every 3 months for high volume (24-hour cooking, charbroiling or wok), every 6 months for moderate volume and yearly for low volume. This summary from the Massachusetts fire marshal cites the 2011 edition. Source: Massachusetts Department of Fire Services, Cleaning or Inspection of Commercial Cooking Exhaust Systems.
  • The schedule is for inspection. NFPA 96 calls for cleaning when the inspection finds grease deposits, so a kitchen whose inspection finds none can go longer, and one inspected more often can need cleaning sooner. This calculator prices one cleaning per scheduled visit. Source: Rhode Island State Fire Marshal, Bulletin 2025-01.
  • Chicago adopts the International Fire Code, 2024 edition, in part, but not its Chapter 6, where the IFC puts kitchen hood inspection and cleaning. We found no City hood cleaning schedule of its own; the Chicago Fire Department's Fire Prevention Bureau is the authority to ask. Source: Chicago Municipal Code 14F-6-600.
  • Angi's homeowner guide puts a hood cleaning session at about $450 on average, with most from $200 to $1,500. Source: Angi, How to Hire a Kitchen Hood Cleaning Professional.
  • A hood cleaning company's guide (2017, updated 2019) lists $10 to $20 per linear foot of hood, $40 to $60 a fan and minimums around $400 to $450. Source: Halo Restoration Services, What Does an Average Hood Cleaning Cost.

The price ranges are other companies' published estimates, checked Oct 26, 2026, not our data. This page is a pricing tool, not fire code advice.

How it works

Visits a year follow the NFPA 96 inspection frequency for the kind of cooking you pick: 12, 4, 2 or 1. Change the pick if a kitchen's inspections find it needs cleaning on a different schedule.

A visit costs the crew hours for the hoods (hoods times hours per hood) and for the duct, fan and grease containment, at your labor rate plus any night premium. Add the crew's drive time at the same labor rate, without the night premium, the vehicle's miles and your chemicals and supplies.

The price per visit divides that cost by one less your margin and rounds up to the next $5. The yearly contract is the price per visit times the visits a year.

A worked example

A charbroil kitchen with 2 hoods, 2.5 crew hours per hood and 1 hour for duct and fan is 6 crew hours. At $40 an hour plus a 10% night premium that is $264. Two people driving 50 minutes round trip and 25 miles add $85.67, and supplies $45, so a visit costs $394.67. A 30% margin prices it at $565, $282.50 a hood. High volume means a visit every 3 months, so the yearly contract is $2,260, about $188 a month, with $681.33 of profit a year.

Questions

How often does a restaurant hood need to be cleaned?

NFPA 96 sets how often the exhaust system is inspected: monthly for solid fuel, every 3 months for high volume such as charbroiling or wok cooking, every 6 months for moderate volume and yearly for low volume. It calls for cleaning when the inspection finds grease deposits. Ask the local fire authority what it enforces.

How much should I charge to clean a restaurant hood?

Price from your crew hours, your rates and your costs. With the example numbers a 2-hood charbroil kitchen prices at $565 a visit and $2,260 a year. Type your own numbers to see yours.

Should I price per hood, per foot or per hour?

Customers compare per visit, so this calculator gives the visit price and what it works out to per hood. It builds the price from your hours, so it holds whether you quote per hood, per foot or per visit.

Does Chicago have its own hood cleaning schedule?

We found none. Chicago adopts the 2024 International Fire Code in part, but not the chapter where it puts kitchen hood cleaning. Check with the Chicago Fire Department's Fire Prevention Bureau for what it enforces.
