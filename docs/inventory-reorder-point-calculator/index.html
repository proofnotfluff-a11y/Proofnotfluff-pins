How it works

Reorder point = units sold per day x supplier lead time in days x (1 + safety stock). It is the stock that covers the sales you expect while an order is on its way, plus a cushion.

Order up to = units sold per day x (lead time + days between your orders) x (1 + safety stock). When you order, you order enough to get back up to this level: order up to minus on hand minus on order, rounded up to a whole unit.

The status reads Order now when on hand plus on order is at or under the reorder point, or when on hand runs out before an order could land and nothing is on order. Days until you reach the reorder point = (on hand + on order - reorder point) / units sold per day. Over 120 days of stock reads as overstocked, the workbook's default.

A worked example

Take the goat milk soap bar in the workbook's sample shop: it sells 6 a day, the supplier takes 7 days, safety stock is 20% and the shop orders every 14 days. The reorder point is 6 x 7 x 1.2 = 50.4 bars, and with 46 on hand and none on order it is time to order. Order up to is 6 x 21 x 1.2 = 151.2, so the order is 106 bars, $275.60 at $2.60 each. The shop's speckled mug sells 1.4 a day with a 21-day lead time: its reorder point is 35.28 mugs, so 48 on hand reaches it in about 9.1 days, and the next order is 24 mugs, $156. Questions

How do you calculate a reorder point?

Multiply the units you sell per day by your supplier's lead time in days, then add a safety stock cushion. Selling 6 a day with a 7-day lead time and 20% safety stock gives 6 x 7 x 1.2 = 50.4 units.

What is safety stock?

A cushion on top of the sales you expect while an order is on its way, written here as a percent of those sales. Raise it for a supplier that often runs late or a product whose sales jump around.

How many units should I order?

Enough to get back up to your order-up-to level: the sales you expect over the lead time plus the days until your next order, with the same cushion, minus what you have on hand and on order.

How do I find units sold per day?

Count the units sold in the last 30 days and divide by 30. A product that sold 42 units last month sells 1.4 a day.

Does this work for products with seasonal sales?

Use your recent daily sales and check again before your busy season. The workbook adds a trend adjustment that raises or lowers daily sales for the season ahead.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a planning estimate based on the numbers you enter. Check minimum order quantities, price breaks and lead times with your supplier.
