Rats in Chicago gardens

How do I keep rats out of my garden in Chicago, and where are they worst?

By ProofNotFluff, run by a finance executive · City 311 counts through Oct 26, 2026, pulled Oct 27, 2026. Garden advice checked Oct 28, 2026. Refreshed weekly.

The short answer

Take away what draws rats to a garden: food, water and shelter. Keep meat, fish, eggs, dairy, grains and cooked food out of the compost, keep it in a closed bin, pick up fallen fruit, and keep garbage cart lids shut. Clear woodpiles, lumber and clutter where rats burrow. UC IPM's rule for buildings is to seal any opening larger than 1/4 inch and never to rely on chicken wire; applied to a raised bed, a mesh floor needs galvanized wire with openings of 1/4 inch or less. Report rats to 311: the City's Bureau of Rodent Control investigates every reported sighting and places rodenticide in the burrows.

Rats are a citywide problem, not one block's. Chicago 311 took 4,554 rat complaints from Sep 27, 2026 to Oct 26, 2026, +10% on the same 30 days a year earlier. Lake View had the most, 239, and West Englewood had the most per resident, 3.70 per 1,000.

How to keep rats out of a garden or raised bed

  1. Look for signs first

    The Chicago Community Gardeners Association lists droppings, fresh gnawing, tracks, runways and burrows near buildings, fences, under raised beds and under plants. It warns that squirrels, opossums, raccoons and rabbits leave similar damage, so check before you treat for rats.

  2. Take the food away

    Keep compost to raw fruit and vegetable scraps, coffee and tea grounds and plenty of leaves, in an enclosed bin or a pile you turn often. UC IPM says rats burrow under compost piles, particularly when meat, fish, eggs or eggshells go in, and that they can gnaw through plastic. Pick up fallen fruit, and if you feed birds, clear what falls to the ground each day.

  3. Shut the garbage cart

    The City's rodent page says exposed garbage is the main reason rats multiply and asks residents to keep carts closed. It says alley conditions are investigated and damaged carts are replaced; the gardeners' association suggests asking your alderperson for a new cart when rats have gnawed yours.

  4. Remove the shelter

    Norway rats burrow along foundations, under rubbish and woodpiles, and around gardens, UC IPM says. Move lumber, bricks, pallets and stored supplies, keep the garden from overgrowing, and collapse old burrows, as the gardeners' association advises.

  5. Build the bed to keep them out

    UC IPM says to seal openings larger than 1/4 inch with steel wool, wire screen or sheet metal, and never to use lightweight chicken wire, which rats pass through. For a bed like the one in the question, the same rule points to a floor of galvanized wire mesh with openings of 1/4 inch or less fixed to the frame; the top stays open, so the food and shelter steps still matter.

  6. Report it, and take care with bait

    Call 311 or file online for rodent baiting; the City investigates every reported sighting and puts rodenticide in burrows. If you bait yourself, UC IPM says all rodenticides are toxic to pets, people and other animals, and that even a single dead rodent might be enough to poison a pet. Where bait may be placed is regulated and differs by state, so follow the product label. The gardeners' association says never to bait near food crops.

A Chicago gardener wrote on Houzz about 14 years ago that rats had burrowed into all the soil and that they planned a raised bed kit surrounded by wire mesh for vegetables, and asked: will the rats get in anyway? The steps above answer it from extension and City guidance, and the tables show where in Chicago rats are reported most right now.

What the numbers can and cannot tell you

The counts are 311 requests, so they rise where people call more, not only where there are more rats. In the 12 months from Oct 1, 2025 to Sep 30, 2026, Chicago 311 took 42,621 rat complaints, -3% on the 12 months before (44,099). Per resident, West Englewood had the most that year, 36.96 per 1,000, then Fuller Park and Clearing.

Questions

Should I report rats in my garden to Chicago 311?
