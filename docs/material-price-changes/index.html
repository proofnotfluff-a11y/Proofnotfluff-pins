Read these numbers right

Index numbers, not Chicago prices

Each index tracks what U.S. producers get for that material, across the country, or across the Midwest region for the regional ready-mix concrete index. It shows how far prices moved, not what a Chicago supplier charges you.

The last 4 months can still change

BLS revises each month for up to 4 months after it first comes out. Those months carry a P. Run a quote again after a revision and the result can move a little.

Compare the changes, not the index numbers

Each material has its own starting point, so a lumber index of 282 and a steel mill products index of 389 say nothing about which costs more. The percent changes compare fine.

When it updates

BLS publishes the October 2026 numbers on Nov 13, 2026, and this page is set to update that day.

How this page is made

Every number comes from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics producer price index by commodity, not seasonally adjusted, pulled from BLS's Public Data API every weekday, so a new release or revision usually shows up the same day.

The materials are BLS series for what trade owners commonly buy: wood, concrete and masonry, steel, aluminum, copper wire, roofing, drywall, insulation, paint, pipe, fixtures, heating equipment and diesel. Each series ID and title is BLS's own.

A change is the later index divided by the earlier one, less one, shown as a percent rounded to a tenth. The calculator multiplies your amount by the same ratio, which is how BLS's Producer Price Index (PPI) Guide for Price Adjustment shows a price following an index.

Index values are shown as BLS publishes them. The percent changes and calculator results are ProofNotFluff's arithmetic on those values. They are not a price quote and do not say what any contract allows; your contract does. For questions about an index, check with BLS.

Questions

How do I work out how much a material went up since my quote?

Divide the price index for this month by the index for the month you quoted, then multiply your material cost by that number. Copper wire and cable was 464.522 in Sep 2025 and 607.705 in Sep 2026, so $10,000 of it then is $13,082.37 at the Sep 2026 index, up 30.8%. The calculator on this page does the same math for every material here.

Are these Chicago prices?

No. These are producer price indexes from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics: what U.S. producers get for each material, measured across the country. Ready-mixed concrete is also shown for the Midwest region. Your supplier's price in Chicago can be higher or lower, and can move at a different speed. Use the index to see the size of a move.

Why did a number change after I used it?

BLS revises each month for up to 4 months after it first comes out, as late reports arrive. Months still open to revision carry a P on this page. Right now Jun 2026 to Sep 2026 are preliminary.

Which index should I use for a material I buy?

If your contract names an index, use that one. Each material's page shows its BLS series ID, so anyone can look up the same numbers on bls.gov; for questions about an index, check with BLS.

Are these seasonally adjusted?

No. These are the raw monthly numbers, with no smoothing for the seasons. That is the kind BLS's Producer Price Index (PPI) Guide for Price Adjustment says to use when a price follows an index.

When does this page update?

BLS releases the producer price index once a month. The next release, for Oct 2026, is on Nov 13, 2026, and the page is set to update that day, along with any revisions to the 4 months before it.

Can I download the numbers?

Yes. The CSV on this page has every month since January 2017 for every material, with BLS's series ID and a column that says which months are preliminary. Each material's page has its own CSV too.
