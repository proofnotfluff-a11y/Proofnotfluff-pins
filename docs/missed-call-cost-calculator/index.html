How it works

Only new-customer calls count here: people calling for a quote or a booking. A client who misses you usually calls back, so those calls stay out.

Missed calls a month are your weekly new-customer calls times the share nobody answers, times 52 weeks over 12 months. Some of those callers try again; the share who never call back is your own guess, and the page never assumes every missed caller is lost.

The jobs lost are those callers times the share of your answered new-customer calls that book. Revenue lost is jobs lost times your average job price, times the jobs a new customer books in their first year, so a repeat-service customer counts a full first year. Profit lost uses your gross margin: what a job leaves after labor, supplies and gas.

An answering option pays for itself when it would save more profit a month than it costs. The customers needed to cover it are its monthly cost divided by the profit one new customer brings in their first year, rounded up. The page names and ranks no vendor; type the price you were quoted.

A worked example

Say a cleaning company gets 20 new-customer calls a week and misses 25% of them while on jobs. That is about 21.7 missed calls a month. If half of those callers never call back and 40% of answered new-customer calls book, about 4.3 jobs a month go elsewhere. At $250 a job that is $1,083 of revenue, and at a 45% gross margin $488 of profit a month, or $5,856 a year. A new customer who books once leaves $112.50, so a $300 a month answering service pays for itself if it saves 3 new customers a month, and would leave $188 a month if it saved every one.

Questions

How much does a missed call cost a small business?

It depends on how many new-customer calls you miss, how many of those callers go elsewhere and what a job leaves you. With the example numbers, 25% of 20 calls a week missed costs about $488 of profit a month. Type your own numbers to see yours.

How do I find my missed call rate?

Most phone plans and business phone apps show missed and unanswered calls in the call log. Count a normal week of new-customer calls and the ones nobody picked up.

Is an answering service or AI receptionist worth it?

Only if it saves more profit than it costs. Type the monthly price you were quoted: the page shows the jobs it must save each month to pay for itself. It never assumes it saves every call.

Why count only new-customer calls?

A regular client who misses you usually calls back or texts. A new caller often calls the next company on the list, so those calls carry the cost.
