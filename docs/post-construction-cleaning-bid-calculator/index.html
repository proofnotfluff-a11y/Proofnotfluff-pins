How it works

Each clean is priced on its own, from your crew's speed. Cleaner-hours are the square feet divided by the square feet one cleaner does in an hour on that clean. Labor is those hours times your labor cost per cleaner-hour.

Days on site are the cleaner-hours divided by your crew's day (cleaners times hours in a day), rounded up, and each day on site is one trip at your travel and parking cost. Supplies are your cost per 1,000 square feet for each clean. A touch-up repeats for each visit.

Cost is labor plus supplies plus travel. A margin prices it at cost divided by one less the margin; a markup at cost times one plus the markup. Each price rounds up to the next $5, and the bid is their sum. A flat touch-up price is used as you type it, and the page tells you when it is under what the visits cost.

For apartments, the size is the units times their average size, plus the common areas. The proposal lists only the cleans you picked, at your prices.

A worked example

Say a 12,000 sq ft office build-out needs all three cleans. Each cleaner does 600 sq ft an hour on the rough clean, 200 on the final and 1,000 on a touch-up. Labor costs you $30 a cleaner-hour, supplies $15 per 1,000 sq ft for each clean, and travel and parking $40 a day, with a crew of 4, 8-hour days and a 40% margin. The rough clean is 20 cleaner-hours in 1 day: $600 of labor, $180 of supplies and $40 of travel, $820 of cost, priced at $1,370. The final clean is 60 cleaner-hours over 2 days, $2,060 of cost, priced at $3,435. One touch-up visit is 12 cleaner-hours, $580 of cost, priced at $970. The bid is $5,775, about 48 cents a square foot, with $2,315 of profit, 40.1% of the price.

Questions

How do I price a post-construction cleaning bid?

Price each clean on its own: the hours your crew needs for that clean at that size, times your labor cost, plus supplies and travel, then your margin. With the example numbers, a 12,000 sq ft job is $1,370 for the rough clean, $3,435 for the final and $970 for one touch-up visit, $5,775 in all.

What is the difference between a rough, final and touch-up clean?

A rough clean comes after the main trades finish: debris out, floors swept, dust wiped. A final clean comes before turnover: windows, fixtures, cabinets, labels and floors. A touch-up is a return visit before the walkthrough or move-in, for dust and marks left by punch-list work. Builders don't all use the same words, so the proposal spells out the scope of each.

What do post-construction cleaners charge per square foot?

Two cleaning business blogs published ranges of $0.10 to $0.30 a sq ft for a rough clean and $0.30 to $0.75 for a final clean in October 2026. Those are their published ranges, not a market rate and not what you should charge. Your crew's speed and your costs set your price, and the calculator shows yours per square foot.

How do I bid an apartment building by the unit?

Pick Apartment units, then type the units, their average size and the common areas. The calculator turns that into square feet, prices each clean and shows the bid per unit.

Should touch-up visits be a flat fee?

That is your call. Pick Flat price a visit to use your own number. The page still works out the hours and cost of each visit at your rates and tells you when the flat price is under that cost.

Is the proposal a contract?
