How it works

A handyman's day is many small jobs, so a big share of it goes to things no customer pays for: estimates, driving between jobs, hardware store runs and invoicing. The van, insurance and tools cost money whether you work that week or not.

This calculator grosses your take-home up for the tax share you set aside, adds a year of business expenses, and spreads that revenue over the hours a customer actually pays for: your working hours times your billable share. That is your minimum hourly rate; the rate card rounds it up to the next $5.

To test a rate, it multiplies the rate by your billable hours, takes out expenses and your tax set-aside on any profit, and divides what is left by every hour you work. A job quote is its billable hours at your rate-card rate, plus materials with your markup.

A worked example

A handyman who wants $55,000 a year take-home, sets aside 25% for tax and spends $9,000 a year on the van, insurance and tools needs $82,333 of revenue. At 48 weeks of 45 hours with 55% billable, that is 1,188 billable hours out of 2,160 worked, so the minimum rate is $69.30 (rate card $70). Charging $50 an hour brings in $59,400 and leaves $37,800 after expenses and tax: $17.50 for every hour worked, $17,200 short of the goal. A 3-hour job at $70 is $210 of labor; $85 of parts with a 15% markup adds $97.75, for a $307.75 quote. Billing 80% of your hours instead of 55% would bring the minimum rate down to $47.65.

Questions

How much should a handyman charge per hour?

Start from the take-home you want, add the tax you set aside and a year of business expenses, then divide by the hours customers actually pay for. With the example numbers that is $82,333 over 1,188 billable hours, or $69.30 an hour. Enter your own numbers to see yours.

Why does drive time matter so much for a handyman?

Small jobs mean more driving, estimates and store runs for each paid hour. In the example only 55% of a 45-hour week is billed, so every paid hour has to cover almost two hours of work.

Should I charge a minimum for small jobs?

A minimum charge is one way to make sure a 30-minute fix still covers the drive. One way to pick one is to price your shortest normal job with this calculator, drive time included in the billable hours.

Should I mark up parts and materials?

That is your call. A markup pays for the time spent buying, hauling and returning parts; set it to 0 to pass parts through at cost. The example uses 15%.

What tax set-aside rate should I use?

Ask your tax preparer. A set-aside rate is a savings habit you choose, not a tax calculation, and this calculator does not tell you which rate to use.

Is this tax or financial advice?

No. It is a planning estimate based on the numbers you enter.
