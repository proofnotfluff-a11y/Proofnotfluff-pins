How it works

Your cost per job after the rise is today's cost times one plus the rise. Keeping your profit per job adds the cost rise to the price, dollar for dollar. Keeping your margin keeps profit as the same share of a bigger price: the new cost divided by one less today's margin.

The two differ: keeping the margin raises the price more, because your profit grows with the price. The page rounds the new price up to the next dollar.

What waiting costs is the cost rise times your jobs a month at today's price. After the raise, monthly profit is the jobs you keep times the new price less the new cost. Clients you expect to lose are your own guess.

The most clients you can lose is one less today's monthly profit divided by the raised profit on every job you do today. Lose fewer than that and the raise leaves you ahead.

A worked example

Say a cleaning job is $150 and costs you $90 in labor, supplies and gas, a 40% margin. Your costs rise 8%, to $97.20 a job. Keeping your $60 profit per job means $157.20, rounded to $158. Keeping your 40% margin means $162, a $12 or 8% raise. At 60 jobs a month, waiting costs $432 a month. At $162, even losing 5% of clients leaves $3,694 a month against $3,600 today, $94 ahead, and you could lose up to 7.4% of clients before earning less than today.

Questions

How much should I raise my prices?

At least enough to cover what your costs went up. Keeping your profit per job adds the cost rise to the price. Keeping your margin raises it a little more, so your profit keeps up as prices rise. The calculator shows both.

How many clients can I lose after a price increase?

Enough that the jobs you keep earn as much as all your jobs earn today. With the example, a raise to $162 can lose up to 7.4% of clients before monthly profit drops below today's.

What does it cost to wait on a price increase?

The cost rise on every job you do at the old price. In the example, a $7.20 rise on 60 jobs a month is $432 a month.

Is profit per job or margin the right thing to keep?

Margin keeps your profit growing with your prices; profit per job keeps the raise as small as possible. Pick the one you would rather explain to clients, and check the client loss each can take.
