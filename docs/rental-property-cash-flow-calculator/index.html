How it works

Profit before depreciation is the rent and other rental income you collected, less operating expenses and mortgage interest. Those are the expense lines on IRS Schedule E.

Cash flow takes that profit and also subtracts mortgage principal and capital improvements. IRS Publication 527 (2025) says you can't deduct mortgage principal payments and must capitalize improvements, so neither lowers profit, but both still leave your bank account.

The monthly average is cash flow divided by the months you entered. Depreciation is left out because it is not cash; your tax preparer works it out. All figures are estimates from the numbers you type.

A worked example

The Rental Property Spreadsheet's sample single-family house, Oak Street House, is fictional. From January to September 2026 its log shows $14,850 of rent (nine months at $1,650), $5,200 of operating expenses ($3,720 property tax, $1,140 insurance, $240 repairs, $62 supplies and $38 of travel), $4,806 of mortgage interest, $2,844 of mortgage principal and a $1,450 capital improvement. Profit before depreciation is $14,850 less $5,200 less $4,806, or $4,844. Take away the $2,844 of principal and the $1,450 improvement and the cash kept is $550, about $61 a month. A $4,844 profit and $550 kept: that gap is why the workbook shows both.

Questions

What is rental property cash flow?

The money a rental leaves you with after every payment it needs: operating expenses, mortgage interest, mortgage principal and improvements. It is what you kept, not what you earned on paper.

Why is my cash flow lower than my profit?

Mortgage principal and capital improvements are paid in cash but are not expenses on Schedule E. IRS Publication 527 (2025) says you can't deduct principal payments and must capitalize improvements. So they lower cash flow without lowering profit.

Should I include security deposits?

Leave out a deposit you plan to return; it isn't income when you receive it. IRS Publication 527 (2025) says a deposit meant as the tenant's last month of rent is advance rent and counts as income when you receive it, so include that one.

Is depreciation included?

No. Depreciation lowers taxable profit but is not money leaving your account, so it is left out of both numbers here. Your tax preparer works it out.

Is this tax advice?

No. It is planning arithmetic on the numbers you type. Check how each item is treated with a tax professional.
