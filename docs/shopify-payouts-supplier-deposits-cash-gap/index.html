E-commerce cash flow

How do you plan the cash gap between Shopify payouts and supplier deposits?

By ProofNotFluff, run by a finance executive · Worked example from the 13-Week E-commerce Cash Flow workbook. Checked Oct 23, 2026.

The short answer

Plan it week by week, in cash, not sales. Put each supplier payment in the week the money leaves (the deposit, then the balance, then freight) and each payout in the week it reaches your bank. The lowest ending week is your gap.

In the worked example below, a brand with $20,000 in the bank and $21,700 a week left over still drops to -$10,200 in week 4, when a $117,000 balance is due. It needs $10,200 by then, or a later due date. Moving the balance one week later keeps every week above zero.

A payout that slips into the next week does not shrink sales, but it can turn a small gap into a large one in the week a supplier is paid.

How to plan it

  1. Start from cash in the bank today

    Use the balance you can spend this week, not your revenue or your profit.

  2. Use payouts, in the week they land

    Count the money that reaches your bank after fees and refunds, in the week it arrives. Shopify Payments lets you choose a daily, weekly or monthly payout schedule, and Shopify's help center says banks typically take 1 to 3 business days to process a deposit. Check your own payout schedule in your Shopify admin.

  3. Give each supplier payment its own week

    Put the deposit in the week you confirm the order, the balance in the week your supplier's terms say it is due, and freight and duties in the week you pay them. A single total for the order hides the week that hurts.

  4. Take out the costs that never stop

    Payroll, rent, apps, ads and loan payments go out every week whether or not stock has arrived.

  5. Find the lowest week and the cash it needs

    The lowest ending cash across the 13 weeks is your low point. If it is below zero, that amount is what you need by that week.

  6. Test the timing before you place the order

    Move the balance a week later, split the order in two, or switch to a shorter payout schedule, and see which change lifts the lowest week. In the example, moving the balance one week lifts the low point from -$10,200 to $11,500.

  7. Update it every week

    Replace last week's estimate with what really landed and roll the plan forward one week.

Why a profitable store still runs short

Sales are recorded when they happen, but payouts arrive later and stock is paid for before it sells. A bigger order makes the gap wider, because the deposit and balance leave before the new stock earns anything.

That is why the example brand, which adds $21,700 a week, still needs $10,200 in week 4: the money is coming, just not by the week the supplier is paid.

If the lowest week is below zero

You need that much cash, credit or a later payment date by that week. A later due date is often the first lever to test, because it costs nothing if the supplier agrees. Any credit or financing has a cost, so compare its terms against your own numbers before you use it. This page is not financial advice.

Questions

How long do Shopify payouts take?

Shopify Payments lets you pick a daily, weekly or monthly payout schedule, and Shopify's help center says banks typically take 1 to 3 business days to process a deposit to your account. Your own payout schedule is in your Shopify admin.

Should I plan with sales or payouts?

Payouts: the money that reaches your bank after fees and refunds, in the week it arrives.

How much cash do I need before a bigger stock order?

Enough to keep your lowest week at zero once the deposit, balance and freight are each in their own week. In the worked example that is $10,200, in week 4.

What if my lowest week is below zero?

You need that much cash, credit or a later payment date by that week. Test a later due date first, since it costs nothing if the supplier agrees, then a split order.
