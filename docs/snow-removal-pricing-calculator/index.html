How it works

A push costs your time on site plus the drive to this stop from the one before it. Every minute carries the crew's labor rate and the truck, plow or blower's cost per hour, then any salt or ice melt you put down on that visit.

The price divides that cost by one less your margin and rounds up to the next $5. A deep storm uses its own minutes on site, so its price follows the extra time.

A seasonal contract is the per-push price times the pushes you expect, plus your cushion for a heavy winter, rounded up to the next $5. It pays the same whether it snows or not.

The pushes it covers are the contract divided by the cost of one push, rounded down. Past that number the season costs you more than the contract pays.

A worked example

One driveway on a route: 25 minutes on site, 10 minutes from the stop before, one person at $40 an hour, a truck and plow at $35 an hour and $15 of salt. A push costs $58.75 and a 25% margin prices it at $80. A deep storm at 45 minutes costs $83.75 and prices at $115. At 8 expected pushes and a 15% cushion, the seasonal contract is $740, or $148 a month over 5 months, with $270 of profit at 8 pushes. It covers 12 pushes before the season costs more than it pays.

Questions

How much should I charge per push?

Your minutes on site and the drive to the stop, at your labor and equipment cost per hour, plus salt, divided by one less your margin. With the example numbers a 25-minute driveway prices at $80 a push. Type your own numbers to see yours.

Is a seasonal contract better than per push?

A seasonal contract pays the same in a light or heavy winter, so you carry the risk of a heavy one. The calculator shows how many pushes the contract covers before it loses money, so you can set the cushion.

How many times will I plow in a Chicago winter?

It depends on your trigger depth and the winter. O'Hare averages 38.4 inches of snow a season, but a push needs a storm over your trigger. Count your own pushes from past seasons and use that number.

How do I price a big storm?

Type your minutes on site for a deep storm. Its price follows the extra time, so a 45-minute push prices higher than a 25-minute one at the same rates.
