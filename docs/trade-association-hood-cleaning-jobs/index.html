The short answer

Not on its own. None of the three membership pages we read on Nov 2, 2026 promises a number of jobs: IKECA's membership rules list a directory listing and the logo, PWNA's contractor page a national listing and training, and PWRA's signup page a members map and an RFP service. How to decide before you pay dues

  1. Price one account first

    Put your own hoods, crew hours, rate and margin into the free hood cleaning calculator. The profit a visit is the number every membership has to beat.

  2. Turn the dues into visits

    Divide the first year cost by your profit a visit. In the example IKECA's $1,245 is 8 visits, the Introductory $675 is 4, PWNA's $350 is 3 and PWRA's $199 is 2.

  3. Ask what work it has sent members near you

    The 2014 reply in the thread said the association's bid postings may not be concentrated enough in one area to bring work right away. Before you join, ask how many postings or directory requests came from your city last year.

  4. Join when a buyer or a city asks for it

    A kitchen or its city may ask for a certified cleaner. Boston, for one, requires certification under a 2008 city ordinance. If a buyer you want asks for it, the dues buy that job; if no one asks, they buy a listing.

  5. Spend the first months on dated needs

    A fresh hood or exhaust citation or a license inspection is a public record with a date. Call or visit soon after, name the record, and offer a visit schedule.

What the thread says

The question was posted Nov 26, 2014 on Pressure Washing Resource by someone who had read that the site's association could get members work, and asked whether joining would bring kitchen exhaust cleaning jobs.

The one answer that evening said the association regularly posts jobs that are put out for bid, one of several member benefits, but warned the postings may not be concentrated enough in a given area to produce work right away. It suggested a marketing firm that books on-site appointments to talk about bidding on hood cleaning instead. The asker replied that this sealed the deal and planned to join.

What a membership can do for you

A listing helps when buyers search a directory. IKECA lists Active members in its directory and lets them use the logo; branch listings cost $150 each. Active North American members must show at least $2,000,000 of liability and completed operations insurance, workers compensation, and at least one CECS or CESI certified employee, so the listing tells a buyer something.

IKECA's rules also limit what you can claim: PECT is a designation, not a certification, an Introductory member may not use the logo, and the logo comes off every website, card and sticker when membership ends. PWNA's contractor page lists safety training and a national listing, with certification courses at extra cost.

When certification is what buyers ask for

IKECA's Boston handbook, revised October 2026, quotes a Boston City Council ordinance passed in 2008: anyone cleaning commercial cooking hood systems there must be certified. The Boston cleaning exam costs $315. Where a city or an insurer asks for it, certification is part of the price of the job.

Rules differ by city. Before you pay for an exam, ask the fire department in the city you work in, and the kitchens you want, what they require.

Questions

Does joining IKECA get you hood cleaning jobs?

Not directly. IKECA's membership rules list a directory listing and logo use for Active members, not leads or referrals. The listing can help when a buyer asks for a member or a certified cleaner.

How much does IKECA membership cost?

Per its Membership Policies and Procedures, amended Aug 27, 2026: $995 a year plus a $250 application fee for Active North American members, and a one time Introductory year at $575 plus a $100 fee.

Is a cheaper association worth it for a new hood cleaner?

Count it in visits. In the calculator's example a visit leaves $170.33, so PWNA's $350 a year is 3 visits and PWRA's $199 is 2. Ask what work it sent members in your city before you pay.

Where do new hood cleaners find restaurant jobs?

Start with dated public records. In the 30 days through Oct 29, 2026, 29 Chicago food businesses failed or passed with conditions with a hood or exhaust violation and 138 had a license inspection.
