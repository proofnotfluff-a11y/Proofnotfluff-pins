What a clean has to cost
so it pays you

Most cleaners divide the pay they want by the hours they work. That number leaves out unpaid hours, monthly costs and self-employment tax. Work these four steps once, then price every job from your own break-even rate.

Your four numbers

StepWorked exampleYours
1Profit you need before tax. Gross up your take-home for self-employment tax (15.3% on 92.35% of profit) and your income tax estimate.$52,000 take-home, 12% income tax
= $70,394
2Revenue you need. Add a year of business costs: insurance, phone, apps, marketing, equipment, fees.+ $6,540 ($545 a month)
= $76,934
3Hours customers pay for. Weeks x hours x the share that is paid. Driving, quoting and invoicing are not.48 x 45 x 65%
= 1,404 hours
4Break-even rate. Step 2 divided by step 3. Add your margin for the target rate.$54.80 an hour
$64.47 with 15%
In this example, pay divided by all hours says $24.07 an hour. The real break-even is $54.80.

Price floor for any job

(Hours on site + drive hours) x break-even rate + miles x vehicle cost + supplies. That is the price at zero profit. Add your margin and round up to the next $5 for the quote.

Sources and terms

Where the numbers come from

Self-employment tax: 15.3% on 92.35% of net earnings (IRS Topic 554 and Schedule SE). Vehicle: IRS business standard mileage rate of 76 cents a mile for July 1 to December 31, 2026 (IRS news release IR-2026-29). Checked October 7, 2026. Income tax is your own estimate; your real rate depends on your whole return. The example uses made-up inputs.
