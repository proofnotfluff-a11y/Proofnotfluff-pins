The number most service owners get wrong

With the example numbers in the file ($52,000 take-home, 45 hours a week, 48 weeks), dividing pay by hours says you need $24 an hour. Once you count unpaid hours, monthly costs and self-employment tax, the real numbers are:

$24.07The rate you would guess
$54.80Break-even per paid hour
$64.47Target per paid hour, with 15% profit

Your numbers will differ. That is the point: tab 1 shows your own.

Set up once

  1. Open 1 Your Numbers and fill the yellow cells with the pay you want, weeks and hours you really work, the share of hours you are paid for, and your monthly business costs.
  2. Open 2 Trade Presets and keep the rows for your trade and change hours, people, supplies and miles to your real jobs. Add your own job types in the empty rows.

Every quote

  1. Open 3 Quote Builder and pick the job from the dropdown. Type in an override cell only if this customer is different (bigger house, longer drive).
  2. Read your quote, the deposit to collect, and the profit inside it. Enter your current price to see whether it covers your pay goal and costs.

Green cells are formulas and peach cells are your key results. Leave both alone; they update on their own.

Two tips

  1. Type percentages with the % sign: 25%, not 25.
  2. Not sure what share of your hours is paid? Start at 60% to 70% and adjust after a month of tracking.

Two costs quotes usually leave out

Drive time. An hour in the car is an hour you are not paid for unless the price covers it. The Quote Builder turns round-trip miles into drive time at your average speed and charges it at your break-even rate.

Vehicle cost. Fuel, wear, insurance and depreciation. The default is the IRS business standard mileage rate of 76 cents a mile (from July 1, 2026). If your vehicle payment is already in your monthly costs, use your real cost per mile instead so it is not counted twice.
