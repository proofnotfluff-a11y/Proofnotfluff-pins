## Description (paste as plain paragraphs; no links)
You're about to hire employee number one. You know nothing about employer payroll, and you're a little afraid of the IRS. The fear is reasonable. The fix is a sequence, not a lawyer.

This guide is that sequence, written down by someone who set up payroll and benefits from zero at a fast-growing e-commerce company and kept running them as the team grew. Six steps in the order they have to happen, each ending with a checkpoint: "You're done with this step when..." Four worksheets, each filled in first for a fictional 3-person cleaning business and then blank for you to write in.

WHAT'S INSIDE
The 30-day sequence: EIN, state accounts, workers' comp, payroll provider, new-hire paperwork, first payroll run.
W-2 or 1099: the ten-question decision checklist and what misclassification costs, in plain words.
Choosing a payroll provider: Gusto, Rippling, QuickBooks Payroll, ADP Run and Patriot compared, with September 2026 pricing from their own pages where they publish one, and a "pick this if" line for each.
Registering as an employer in your state: the lookup method, a checklist you fill in per state (two copies), and the out-of-state trap.
ICHRA and QSEHRA explained for teams under ten, with the 2026 limits and the cost worked out.
Your first payroll run, line by line: every pay-stub line defined, what an employee costs on top of wages, and the true hourly cost of a $20-an-hour employee ($22.52 before software or benefits).
The filing calendar: what gets filed when, with a check box for each deadline.
Five first-timer mistakes (composites, not real people): the pattern, what it cost, the one fix for each.
The whole thing on one page: a checklist to tick off in order, plus notes pages.

WHO IT'S FOR
The owner of a one-person business about to make the first hire: a cleaning company, a shop, a trade, a studio, a small office. If you have an EIN and a payroll provider already, skip ahead; the sequence still tells you what you might have missed.

WHY THIS ONE
Every federal rate, limit and deadline names its source and the date it was checked, on the page where it is used and again in the sources list at the back. Written by a finance executive who also ran HR.

Made with AI assistance and reviewed and tested by the shop owner.
