# Legal desk review: advertisement label on cold emails (Oct 24, 2026)

**Scope:** the fixed footer the sender appends under every first email and follow-up (tools/pnf_rules.py FOOTER, email/SEND_RULES.md step 4). Raised by the Kimi and Argon audits, Oct 24. Todd, Oct 24, 11:13 am, live chat: "Please research this yourself and come up with what you think is best."

**Verdict: FIXED.** This is a risk checklist written by an AI, not legal advice. The question stays on Todd's attorney list as a check, not a blocker.

1. Commercial message. Our first emails offer a free sample of a paid list, so their primary purpose is to promote a product: commercial under CAN-SPAM, whatever the ask's wording. The FTC says the law has no exception for business-to-business email.
2. Advertisement label. A commercial email must clearly and conspicuously identify itself as an advertisement or solicitation unless the recipient gave prior affirmative consent (15 U.S.C. 7704(a)(5)(A)(i) and (B)). Cold recipients gave none. The FTC says the law leaves "a lot of leeway" in how. The old footer had no label; the older outreach files (A1, R1, U1) had one, so this restores it.
3. Wording chosen: "This is a sales email." It is plain, conspicuous in a four-line plain-text footer, and reads as a person wrote it. Kimi's "Ad ·" line and longer forms were not taken: the shorter sentence says the same thing without reading like spam.
4. Already compliant, unchanged: an honest From line and subject; our postal address, a private mailbox at a commercial mail receiving agency, which the FTC accepts when registered under Postal Service rules; an opt-out by reply that works for at least 30 days after sending; opt-outs honored at once (DoNotEmail), inside the 10 business days the law allows.
5. Not affected: the Monday email and Kit broadcasts go only to people who asked for them; the trial offer email already carries its own label ("This one is an offer", legal/reviews/2026-10-22-monday-email-and-trial.md).

Sources: FTC, CAN-SPAM Act compliance guide for business, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business (read Oct 24, 2026).
