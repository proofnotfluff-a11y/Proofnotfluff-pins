# Show the product, then route the click

ProofNotFluff's videos need two fixes before any new tool: put the real product on screen in the first two seconds, and give every platform a link path that actually works. The current Shorts open on abstract text and show nothing a buyer can use until the end, which is the reverse of what TikTok's own creative research found in its highest click-through ads ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance)). They also sit inside a platform where description and comment links stopped being clickable in August 2023 ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-adds-shorts-links-limits-links-elsewhere/495463)), which explains 1,280 views and 0 Etsy visits better than any hook problem. The documented winners in this category sell through product-in-use visuals and one channel worked steadily. They did not get there with viral faceless clips: the best-documented Etsy spreadsheet shop makes about $14K a month from listing videos that show multiple spreadsheet pages ([Insights by Jess](https://insightsbyjess.com/168k-store-case-study-digital-products-to-sell-on-etsy)). The crew's existing HTML, Playwright and ffmpeg renderer can already make screen-recorded demo videos at $0, so no paid render, avatar or clipping tool earns its cost. The only spend worth testing is a $5 a month voiceover plan, and only as a two-week A/B test. Pinterest stays the backbone for clicks because every pin carries a working link. Measurement has to come from the crew's own layer: Etsy Stats referring domains, per-pin outbound clicks and per-video links. Etsy itself documents no campaign tracking. One thing to check gently: Pinterest's help center says Etsy shops can't be claimed, so the "claimed" status the owner saw is worth a one-minute look. Nothing breaks either way.

## The slideshow fails at frame one and at the link

Hard, public data on which short-form formats turn views into template sales does not exist. No platform or reputable study breaks purchase conversion down by demo, slideshow, POV or UGC format, and the "demos convert 3x" claims on creator blogs have no data behind them. The closest primary evidence is TikTok's own creative analysis: **over 63% of the highest click-through videos showed the key message or product within the first 3 seconds**, and 40% of top view-through ads used text overlays. That study dates from 2020 and covers paid ads, so treat it as direction, not proof ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance)). The sellers with disclosed numbers point the same way. Jren, an Etsy spreadsheet shop with 34 listings at about $10 to $12 each, makes roughly **$14K a month**. It credits listing videos that show multiple spreadsheet pages, "How It Works" and "Is This Product for You?" images, and Etsy SEO, not a social video channel ([Insights by Jess](https://insightsbyjess.com/168k-store-case-study-digital-products-to-sell-on-etsy)). Easlo made **$239K in a year** selling Notion templates by posting on X two or three times a day and giving away free templates first ([Yahoo Finance](https://money.yahoo.com/made-239-000-selling-budgeting-120626735.html)). Neither won through viral faceless video. Both won by making the product obvious and working one channel without letup.

Today's Shorts are five text slides at 5.33 seconds each with generated music, and the product shows up only as a URL at the end. The fix is a new primary format: a **screen-recorded demo with a result reveal**. Numbers get typed into the actual calculator, and the profit or hourly-rate cell flips from a guess to the true figure. The first frame should name the audience, show a number and show the product UI together, for example "Cleaners: your $40 an hour nets $20" over the cleaning kit's pricing tab. Three supporting formats come from the same footage: a "wrong math vs right math" split screen on the real product, a problem-agitate-solve cut where the solve beat is the product, and a POV text hook over the demo. Pure text slides can still find reach, since the best one got 1,010 views, but they carry no proof that the tool exists or works. The demo format also fits two standing ledger rules for free: every number must come from the product's own files, and the shop name promises proof.

Length should move out of the middle. A secondary analysis of 35 billion Shorts views found Shorts performance is bimodal: **13 to 15 seconds for loops and about 60 seconds for substance, with 30 to 45 seconds underperforming**. Reels peak in average views at 60 to 90 seconds ([Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/video-length-sweet-spots-tiktok-reels-shorts)). The primary dataset isn't linked, so the crew should treat this as a hypothesis and run two lanes: an 8 to 15 second loop (hook, reveal, end card) and a 35 to 60 second walkthrough. The current 27 seconds sits close to the weak band. Judging by views is also unreliable now. Since March 2025, YouTube counts a Short view on any start or replay with no minimum watch time. The metrics that show attention are **"Chose to view"** (the share who watched instead of swiping away), engaged views and average percentage viewed. YouTube publishes no universal benchmark, so each Short should be compared with the channel's own earlier Shorts ([Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)). That also means the ledger's Short Series trigger (500 views earns three follow-ups) should key on engaged views and Chose-to-view, not raw views.

Voiceover is a real tradeoff, not a default. An Adobe Express survey of 850 consumers found that **only 31% find AI voices acceptable in short-form video, and 58% would trust a brand less when AI voices appear in ads** ([ContentGrip on Adobe Express](https://www.contentgrip.com/ai-voices-in-marketing-adobe/)). Music-only slides give a muted viewer nothing beyond the text, and a voice adds a second reason to stay. For a brand called ProofNotFluff, though, an obviously synthetic narrator is a credibility risk. So the call is to test it, not adopt it. Run the same demo footage with and without a plain, non-human-sounding voice reading short, number-heavy lines, and burn word-by-word captions into both versions so they work muted. Text on screen is effectively mandatory on every platform, and Pinterest guidance calls captions critical because most video pins play silently ([Tailwind](https://www.tailwindapp.com/blog/video-pins-vs-static-pins)).

Repurposing needs care. Instagram reportedly down-ranks near-duplicate and watermarked reposts ([PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-the-instagram-algorithm-works)). YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" in July 2025 to catch mass-produced uploads ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/07/10/youtube-is-fighting-ai-slop-with-new-monetization-guidelines/)). Pinterest's guidelines ban repetitive content made to earn money ([Pinterest Community Guidelines](https://policy.pinterest.com/en/community-guidelines)). The renderer should produce one clean master per idea, then render each platform's variant with its own first line of text and its own end card, rotating three or four visual templates so the account never reads as one asset posted over and over.

The smaller ask often converts better than a direct $7 link, which makes the planned Kit email list's free mini tool a natural DM payload once it exists.

Pinterest deserves more production time than its share of the conversation suggests, because it is the only channel where every post is clickable. Tailwind's study of more than 1 million pins found **89% of the most viral pins were static images and only 8% were video**, with 2:3 (1000x1500) and light backgrounds dominant. Video pins of 6 to 15 seconds help saves and follows more than clicks ([Tailwind](https://www.tailwindapp.com/blog/video-pins-vs-static-pins)). Static pins built from real product screenshots should carry the click load, with demo loops cut from the Shorts footage as a supplement. Real screenshots also avoid the "AI modified" label, which some users filter out of their feeds.

About the Pinterest claim. The ledger records that the owner "claimed the Etsy website" on Oct 4. What most likely happened is that the profile's website field now shows the Etsy shop, which the agent filled in. That's useful but not the same thing as a claim. It's worth a one-minute look under Pinterest settings, in claimed accounts. Nothing is lost either way, because product rich pins work for Etsy URLs without claiming. The only difference is that the claimed-accounts analytics filter won't separate other people's saves of Etsy pages.

## Count Etsy visits per video, not views

Etsy won't do the attribution for the crew. Etsy Stats groups visits into sources such as Social media and Direct, and shows "the specific page the buyer was on immediately before landing." It doesn't mention UTM tracking ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015774268-How-to-Use-Etsy-Stats-for-Your-Shop)). The GA4 hookup is documented for traffic, not sales ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000337967-How-to-Use-Google-Analytics-for-Your-Etsy-Shop)). The crew needs its own layered system.

The first layer is **Share & Save listing URLs**. Etsy refunds **4% of the order total** on sales from a seller's own links within 30 days, and offers unique URLs per shop, listing and section ([Etsy Share & Save Terms](https://www.etsy.com/legal/policy/etsys-share-save-terms-program-terms/1162874007996)). On a $6.99 founding-price sale, that refund covers well over half the 6.5% transaction fee ([Etsy Fees](https://www.etsy.com/legal/fees/)). The URLs are per listing, not per post, so they mark seller-driven orders without telling videos apart. The second layer is **per-video click counts**. Use a unique short link for each YouTube walkthrough description, each Instagram DM payload and each X or LinkedIn post. On Pinterest, use the API's per-pin outbound clicks instead, which avoids redirects ([Pinterest Developers](https://developers.pinterest.com/docs/api/v5/multi_pins-analytics/)). The third layer is a **weekly reconciliation**: clicks per link, Etsy Social media visits by referring domain (youtube.com, instagram.com, pinterest.com, t.co, linkedin.com), and orders. Two things are unverified and need a one-time test before anyone relies on them: whether a Share & Save URL keeps working with an added query string, and whether it survives a short-link redirect. If either fails, the short link should point at the plain listing URL and Share & Save should be used only where links are pasted directly.

Offsite Ads affect both the fees and the attribution. Etsy charges **15%** (12% after $10K in yearly sales, capped at $100 an order) when a buyer clicked an Etsy-placed offsite ad within 30 days of buying, and participation becomes mandatory for good once a shop passes $10,000 ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000338367-How-Etsy-s-Offsite-Ads-Work)). An order that Etsy attributes to its ad loses the Share & Save refund. Your own social links never trigger the fee. For a shop with zero orders, Offsite Ads are pay-on-sale exposure on Google, Instagram and Pinterest that the crew can't buy any other way, so the case for leaving them on is real. The recommendation is to leave them on for now, have the owner confirm the current setting so the crew knows which orders may be Etsy's, and decide again before revenue nears $10K.

Each platform also has a metric that predicts clicks better than views do. On YouTube that's Chose-to-view and engaged views ([Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)). On Instagram it's sends per reach and comments on keyword CTAs. On Pinterest it's outbound clicks ([Pinterest Help](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pinterest-analytics)). Socialinsider's 2026 data sets realistic expectations: accounts under 5K followers averaged **15,160 views per Short, 625 per Reel and 350 per TikTok**, and comments run about 50 per TikTok, 20 per Reel and 10 per Short ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/tiktok-vs-reels-vs-shorts/)). Those averages are skewed by outliers, but they suggest Shorts reach is easier to get than Reels reach, while comment-driven CTAs work better on Reels. With about 18 Etsy visits in 30 days, no test here will reach statistical significance. The crew should use directional rules: a format must win on its platform's leading metric and produce at least some referral visits from that domain over 20 posts.

## Conclusion

The big lesson is that the promo studio's problem was never production polish. The videos had no proof in them and no door out. A screen recording of the calculator fixes the first, and it costs nothing because the crew already owns a renderer. Platform-specific link paths fix the second: related video to a horizontal walkthrough, keyword DMs, direct-link pins. Once both are in place, voiceover is the one variable worth paying to test, and the test is cheap enough to settle within two weeks.

The shop should also stop treating every channel as equal. At $5 to $15 a sale, Pinterest's clickable pins and the Etsy listing video probably return more visits per hour of agent time than any Short. The Shorts and Reels job is to get viewers to the walkthrough or the DM, not to close the sale. Weekly referral-domain numbers next to each channel's cost will settle which channels deserve more volume. If a channel still sends almost nothing after the link path works, cut it, however good its view counts look.
