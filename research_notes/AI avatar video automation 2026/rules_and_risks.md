# AI Presenter / AI Voice Clone in Promo Videos: US and Platform Rules and Risks (as of Oct 15, 2026)

Research brief, not legal advice. Scope: a one-person US (Chicago) business posting Reels, Shorts and TikToks with a realistic AI presenter (stock avatar or digital twin of the owner) or the owner's cloned voice, to promote its own $2.99 spreadsheets (Etsy) and free calculators. Items dated before 2026 are marked [older]. Many platform findings come from secondary sources (vendor blogs, trade press) because primary help pages could not be fully retrieved within budget; each one is flagged.

## 1. Platform rules in 2026 (Meta/Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Etsy) and penalties for not labeling

### Takeaway
No major platform bans a realistic AI presenter promoting your own product. All of them require disclosure of realistic synthetic people or voices. The 2026 changes that matter most are Instagram's account-level "AI-generated profile" label (announced Aug 31, 2026; unlabeled AI-person profiles lose recommendation eligibility) and YouTube's July 16, 2026 YPP clarification (AI personas discussing finance are non-monetizable). Not labeling leads to platform-applied labels, reduced distribution or loss of recommendation eligibility, and for repeat or misleading cases, removal or monetization loss. None of the sources I found describe a formal strike for a missing label alone.

### Cited Findings
**Instagram / Meta (organic)**
- Instagram announced on Aug 31, 2026 (creators blog) that it renamed the "AI creator label" to "AI-generated profile". It is a profile-level tag for accounts that regularly post AI-generated or modified content, and it appears on the profile, in search, next to content in Feed/Reels/Stories, and beside comments and messages. — [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/instagram-renames-ai-creator-label-and-restricts-unlabeled-ai-generated-profiles/) (cites creators.instagram.com/blog/ai-generated-profile-label and help.instagram.com/1555776438852001)
- Accounts "whose profile features an AI-generated person rather than a human" are "expected to self-identify". Creators who only use AI tools in their process do not need the profile label. — [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/instagram-renames-ai-creator-label-and-restricts-unlabeled-ai-generated-profiles/)
- Instagram will restrict the distribution of profiles that feature an AI-generated person without the label. Detected accounts are notified and can see in Account Status that they are "no longer eligible for recommendations". Adding the label or winning an appeal restores eligibility. Instagram says adding the label proactively does not change reach, and it concedes detection "might not always be perfect". — [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/instagram-renames-ai-creator-label-and-restricts-unlabeled-ai-generated-profiles/); also covered by [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/meta/is-it-ai-instagram-gets-crystal-clear-with-a-reworked-ai-generated-profile-label)
- If a post already carries a post-level AI label, that label is shown instead of the profile label. — [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/instagram-renames-ai-creator-label-and-restricts-unlabeled-ai-generated-profiles/)
- [older] Meta renamed "Made with AI" to "AI info" in July 2024, so minor AI touch-ups no longer trigger it. Meta auto-applies labels when it detects C2PA/IPTC "AI generated" signals. — [CineRads (secondary)](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ugc-facebook-ad-policy); Meta primary posts: [Feb 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/), [Apr 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/), [Transparency Center: labeling AI content](https://transparency.meta.com/governance/tracking-impact/labeling-ai-content)

**Meta advertising**
- AI-generated ad creative, including AI people, is permitted ("about disclosure and context, not prohibition"). Advertisers are expected to use Meta's AI disclosure control for photorealistic video or realistic audio made with tools that don't embed C2PA metadata. A synthetic person presented as a real, named customer is treated as deceptive, while a clearly scripted brand spokesperson is not. Using a real person's likeness without permission is a violation. — [CineRads (secondary, Mar 2026, updated Jul 2026)](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ugc-facebook-ad-policy); primary: [Meta Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/en-gb/policies/ad-standards/), [Authentic identity representation](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/authentic-identity-representation/)
- [older] Meta's mandatory AI disclosure for digitally created or altered ads applies to political and social-issue ads (announced Nov 2023, in force 2024). It does not apply to ordinary product ads. — [TechCrunch, Nov 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/08/meta-will-start-requiring-disclosures-for-political-ads-manipulated-with-ai); [CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ugc-facebook-ad-policy)

**TikTok**
- TikTok requires a label on AI-generated content that depicts realistic people or scenes. Undisclosed deepfakes of real people are prohibited, and synthetic media of private individuals is banned even when labeled. AI-assisted scripts and captions are exempt. — [Storrito (secondary)](https://storrito.com/resources/tiktoks-2026-ai-labeling-rules-and-what-they-signal-for-platform-governance/); see also [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/tiktok-demands-you-label-any-realistic-ai-content/)
- [older] TikTok integrated C2PA Content Credentials (reported Jan 2025, first announced 2024) to auto-label AI content from embedded metadata, even when the creator doesn't disclose. It also uses invisible watermarking and detection models. — [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/tiktoks-2026-ai-labeling-rules-and-what-they-signal-for-platform-governance/); [Social Media Today on in-stream AIGC labels](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-officially-launches-new-in-stream-labels-for-ai-generated-content/694154/)
- Unlabeled AI content that TikTok detects may be auto-labeled, have its distribution reduced, or be removed. Repeat violations hurt account standing and Creator Rewards eligibility. Branded content and paid ads follow the same labeling rules as organic posts. — [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/tiktoks-2026-ai-labeling-rules-and-what-they-signal-for-platform-governance/)
- Count of labeled videos conflicts: Storrito says "over 1.3 billion". Other 2026 sources headline "3 billion" ([Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/guides/tiktok-ai-labeling-at-scale), [ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/ai-generated-content-on-social-media-in-2026-how-tiktok-meta-and-youtube-are-labeling-the-flood)). I did not verify either figure against a TikTok primary source.

**YouTube**
- [older, still in force] Since Mar 18, 2024, creators must disclose in Creator Studio any realistic altered or synthetic content "a viewer could easily mistake for a real person, place, scene, or event". This explicitly includes generating a voice to narrate a video. Scripts, ideas, captions, beauty filters and clearly unrealistic animation are exempt. The label goes in the expanded description, and videos on sensitive topics including **finance** get a more prominent label on the player. YouTube may add the label itself and will consider enforcement against creators who consistently don't disclose. — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content)
- July 2026: YouTube updated its YPP monetization policies on July 16, 2026 to define "inauthentic content". Three categories are non-monetizable: (1) generic, repetitive or template-based content easily made with AI, CGI or templates; (2) off-putting or distressing content; (3) **AI personas discussing sensitive topics, named as finance, legal, healthcare and medical**. A channel with too much content in any category cannot monetize. — [TechCrunch, Jul 20, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/); also [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/off-putting-youtube-tighten-rules-104500810.html)
- TechCrunch describes category 3 as "AI representations of real people". Whether a fictional stock avatar or an owner's own digital twin counts is not stated. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- [older] July 2025: YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" (mass-produced, repetitive videos) in YPP. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)

**Etsy**
- Under Etsy's Creativity Standards (tightened June 2025 [older]), AI-generated items are allowed under "Designed by" when you supply the prompts and shape the result, but AI use must be disclosed in the listing description. Standalone AI prompt bundles are prohibited. Listing photos must be real, original photos of the actual item, and stock images are not allowed (narrow exception for production-partner mockups). — [Listadum (secondary, Jul 2026)](https://www.listadum.com/blog/etsy-creativity-standards)
- I found no Etsy rule that specifically addresses AI avatars or AI voices in listing videos. — [Listadum](https://www.listadum.com/blog/etsy-creativity-standards) (states it does not cover video)

### Inferences
- A brand account whose recurring "face" is an AI presenter most likely meets Instagram's "profile features an AI-generated person" test. Turning on the AI-generated profile label is the low-risk choice, because skipping it puts recommendations (the main reach channel for Reels) at risk.
- On YouTube, finance-adjacent topics (budgets, pricing, business money) delivered by an AI persona carry a monetization risk under the July 2026 rule. This matters only if the channel is in YPP. Disclosure is required either way. ProofNotFluff's goal is product promotion, not ad revenue, so the monetization rule affects it less than the disclosure rule does.
- An AI-voiced narration over screen recordings of the spreadsheet still triggers YouTube's synthetic-voice disclosure if the voice is a realistic clone of a real person (the owner). A clearly synthetic stock TTS voice probably does not, because it is not "a real person" being mistaken. That reading is an inference from YouTube's examples.
- Etsy: keep the AI presenter out of listing *photos*, which must show the real product. A listing video should show the actual spreadsheet. If AI was used to make the product itself, disclose it in the description.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve TikTok's primary Support/Community Guidelines page text on AIGC labels or any 2026 TikTok-specific limits on AI avatars in branded content or Spark Ads. TikTok Symphony avatar terms were not checked.
- Meta's Ads Help text on AI disclosure for non-political ads was not fetched directly. The findings rely on a secondary source.
- No primary Etsy help article on AI disclosure was retrieved. Etsy's video-specific AI rules are unknown.
- Whether YouTube's "AI personas" category covers fictional avatars or self-clones was not clarified in any source found.

## 2. Do platforms reduce reach for labeled AI content?

### Takeaway
Officially, no. Instagram says adding the AI-generated profile label does not change reach. The reach penalties that are documented apply to *unlabeled* AI content: Instagram's loss of recommendation eligibility and TikTok's reduced distribution. I found no reliable, independent evidence that labeled AI content is suppressed.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram: the profile label is meant to increase transparency "without affecting content distribution", and proactively adding it does not change reach. Unlabeled AI-person profiles lose recommendation eligibility. — [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/instagram-renames-ai-creator-label-and-restricts-unlabeled-ai-generated-profiles/)
- TikTok: unlabeled AI content that is detected may see reduced distribution. No statement says labeling itself reduces reach. — [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/tiktoks-2026-ai-labeling-rules-and-what-they-signal-for-platform-governance/)
- YouTube: the 2024 disclosure announcement does not say the label affects reach. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content)
- Several 2026 vendor blogs discuss "reach limits" for AI labels ([Truescho](https://truescho.com/en/blog/instagram-ai-generated-profile-labels-2026), [Creatorflow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-ai-generated-profile-label/), [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/guides/tiktok-ai-labeling-at-scale)). These are marketing sites selling AI tools, and I did not verify any data they present.

### Inferences
- Viewer reaction is a separate issue from algorithmic suppression. A visible AI label may lower trust or engagement, and lower engagement then reduces reach indirectly. This is plausible but no controlled data was found. Since YouTube says viewers find some AI content "off-putting" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)), A/B testing (avatar vs. faceless screen recording) is the honest way to find out.

### Gaps
- No independent study (academic or audited) of reach for labeled vs. unlabeled AI content was found.

## 3. US law: FTC rules, enforcement, state laws (NY, CA, IL), financial-advice content

### Takeaway
Federal law does not ban AI presenters. The FTC rule targets **fake or false testimonials** and deception, so an AI avatar posing as a satisfied customer is the main legal danger. A clearly identified brand presenter is not. New York (effective June 9, 2026) requires a conspicuous disclosure when an ad uses a "synthetic performer", meaning an AI human likeness that is not a real person, and it applies to any advertiser whose ads reach NY consumers. Illinois prohibits distributing unauthorized digital replicas of a real person, which is not an issue when the owner clones himself with consent. California's AI laws mainly bind AI providers and entertainment employers, not small advertisers.

### Cited Findings
**FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465)**
- [older] The final rule banning fake reviews and testimonials was announced Aug 2024 and took effect Oct 21, 2024. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); date per [ZebraTruth](https://www.zebratruth.ai/blog/ftc-consumer-review-rule-enforcement-2026)
- FTC Q&A: "The rule has no blanket prohibition on the use of AI-generated avatars in marketing." AI stock avatars don't give "consumer reviews", but their statements can be "testimonials", which are prohibited only if fake or false. Avatar use can still be deceptive under the FTC Act. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
- FTC Q&A: a testimonial is an advertising message consumers would likely believe reflects a real consumer's or celebrity's experience, including in social media. Actors portraying testimonialists are outside the rule "unless the underlying testimonials were fake or false", but may still be deceptive under the FTC Act. An obviously fictional dramatization involves no testimonial. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
- FTC Q&A: courts can impose civil penalties for knowing violations, and there is no private right of action. The rule's only disclosure requirements concern company insiders (for example employee or family reviews), which must be clear, conspicuous and "unavoidable" (not behind a click or hover). — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
- Penalty: up to $53,088 per violation (inflation-adjusted; verify the current figure). — [ZebraTruth (secondary)](https://www.zebratruth.ai/blog/ftc-consumer-review-rule-enforcement-2026)
- Enforcement: in Dec 2025 the FTC reportedly sent warning letters under the rule to "nearly a dozen" companies (the source relies on a Benesch alert; not checked against ftc.gov). No case involving AI avatars or AI testimonials was identified. — [ZebraTruth](https://www.zebratruth.ai/blog/ftc-consumer-review-rule-enforcement-2026)
- [older] Dec 2024: the FTC finalized an order against Rytr, an AI "testimonial and review" generator. In Dec 2025 the FTC **reopened and set aside** the Rytr order, citing the administration's AI Action Plan. — [FTC Dec 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/12/ftc-approves-final-order-against-rytr-seller-ai-testimonial-review-service-providing-subscribers); [FTC Dec 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-reopens-sets-aside-rytr-final-order-response-trump-administrations-ai-action-plan)
- July 2026: the FTC sought public comment on a policy statement addressing AI accuracy (title only; content not reviewed). — [FTC press release, Jul 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-seeks-public-comment-policy-statement-addressing-ai-accuracy)

**New York synthetic performer ad disclosure**
- S.8420-A/A.8887-B was signed Dec 11, 2025 and took effect June 9, 2026. It applies to any company whose ads reach NY consumers, wherever headquartered. — [Crowell & Moring, Jun 11, 2026](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/synthetic-performers-real-consequences-implications-of-trailblazing-new-york-ai-ad-law)
- A "synthetic performer" is an asset created with generative AI or a software algorithm, meant to emulate a real but **unidentifiable** human. Ads using one must "conspicuously disclose" it. No wording is mandated and "conspicuous" is undefined. Exemptions: expressive works, **audio ads**, and AI used solely to translate a human performer's language. The law doesn't distinguish lead performers from extras or partial body parts. Penalties are $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 for each later one, with no private right of action. — [Kelley Drye, Jun 8, 2026](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ny-synthetic-performer-law-goes-into-effect)
- The law does not apply when AI enhances an ad featuring a real human likeness, or when AI creates ad imagery without human likenesses. — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/synthetic-performers-real-consequences-implications-of-trailblazing-new-york-ai-ad-law); further analysis: [Honigman](https://www.honigman.com/the-matrix/understanding-new-yorks-synthetic-performer-disclosure-law-impacts-businesses-steps-compliant), [Mitchell Silberberg & Knupp](https://www.msk.com/newsroom-alerts-3114), [Falcon Rappaport & Berkman](https://frblaw.com/new-yorks-synthetic-performer-disclosure-law-a-guide-to-the-nations-first-ai-advertising-rule/)

**Illinois (Chicago business)**
- [older] HB 4875 / Public Act 103-0836 amended the Right of Publicity Act. Approved Aug 9, 2024 and effective Jan 1, 2025, it bars distributing, transmitting or making available a sound recording or audiovisual work containing a **digital replica** of an individual when the distributor has actual knowledge that the individual did not authorize it. A digital replica is a newly created electronic representation of a real person's voice, image or likeness in a work they did not actually perform in. Exceptions cover news, commentary, parody and certain other uses. — [ILGA bill status](https://www.ilga.gov/ftp/legislation/103/BillStatus/HTML/10300HB4875.html); [enrolled text](https://ilga.gov/legislation/103/HB/PDF/10300HB4875enr.pdf)

**California**
- [older] SB 942 (California AI Transparency Act) places obligations on "Covered Providers", meaning gen-AI providers with more than 1M monthly users, which must provide free detection tools and offer visible and latent markers. Penalties are $5,000 per violation, enforced by the AG, with no private right of action. AB 2602 makes some digital-replica contract terms unenforceable without informed consent (entertainment performers). AB 1836 covers digital replicas of deceased personalities. — [Pryor Cashman, Dec 2024](https://www.pryorcashman.com/publications/californias-new-ai-laws-what-content-creators-and-ip-owners-need-to-know); [Skadden, Sep 2024](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2024/09/california-enacts-host-of-ai-related-bills)
- SB 942's operative date was reportedly moved to Aug 2, 2026 by AB 853 (signed Oct 2025). This is from search-result titles only; I could not fetch the alert. — [Troutman (title)](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2025/10/california-ai-transparency-act-amendments-signed-into-law/); [CASRAI (title)](https://www.casrai.org/news/california-sb-942-ai-transparency-act-effective-august-2026)

**Financial-advice style content**
- YouTube puts a more prominent AI label on finance-topic videos and demonetizes AI personas discussing finance (see Q1). — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- Meta: performance and outcome claims must be substantiated, regardless of whether AI made the creative. — [CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ugc-facebook-ad-policy)

### Inferences
- The biggest legal risk for ProofNotFluff is **scripting the avatar as a customer** ("I saved $4,000 with this spreadsheet!"). This is a fake testimonial under 16 CFR 465 and deceptive under Section 5. A presenter who speaks for the brand ("I'm ProofNotFluff's AI presenter; here's how the pricing calculator works") is low risk.
- NY law: a **stock avatar** (fictional, unidentifiable human) in an ad reaching NY users is the textbook "synthetic performer" case, so disclose it conspicuously. An organic post may or may not count as an "advertisement". A product promo is plausibly one, so treat all promo videos as ads. An **owner digital twin** depicts a real, identifiable person and appears to fall outside the "unidentifiable human" definition and within the "real human likeness" exemption Crowell describes. Disclosing anyway is cheap. A **voice-only** clone in an audio-only ad is exempt, but a video with an AI voice over screen recordings is not audio-only, and a voice alone is not a "human likeness" in the visual sense. The law's coverage of that case is unclear.
- Illinois: the owner cloning his own face or voice is authorized use, so the HB 4875 prohibition is not triggered. The Illinois risk runs the other way: if someone else clones him, the Act gives him a claim. Get written consent from anyone else whose likeness or voice you use.
- Calculators and budget spreadsheets are tools, not personalized investment advice. Avoid outcome or earnings promises ("this will double your profit") and typical-results claims without substantiation. Add a "general information, not financial/tax advice" line. This is general FTC-substantiation reasoning, not a sourced rule specific to AI.

### Gaps
- No FTC enforcement action specifically about AI avatar ads in 2025–2026 was found. The FTC's Dec 2025 warning letters were not confirmed on ftc.gov.
- Did not verify whether Illinois passed further 2025–2026 digital-replica laws (for example a "Digital Voice and Likeness Protection Act" on contracts). Not confirmed.
- Exact NY statutory text, such as the "actual knowledge" standard and the definition of "advertisement", was not retrieved.
- California AB 853's final dates and any 2026 California advertiser-facing obligations were not confirmed from a fetched source.
- The FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255, revised 2023) were not re-fetched. Their virtual-influencer language is known from training only, so it is not cited here.

## 4. Licensing: stock avatar rights and protections for cloning your own face or voice

### Takeaway
Stock avatars are non-exclusive. The same actor-based avatar can appear for any other brand, including competitors or low-quality advertisers. Vendor terms often restrict paid ads and endorsement-style use. Synthesia, for example, requires written approval to use stock avatars in paid or boosted social ads. A digital twin of the owner avoids both problems and the NY synthetic-performer issue, but it creates a security asset you must protect.

### Cited Findings
- Synthesia AUP (last updated Feb 23, 2024 [older]): stock avatars may not appear in paid, boosted or promoted social media ads without Synthesia's written approval. Custom avatars may be used in such ads only with the individual's consent. — [Synthesia AUP](https://www.synthesia.io/legal/acceptable-use-policy)
- Synthesia AUP: stock avatars may not be used to make viewers think the real actor "personally holds, endorses" your opinions or characteristics (allowed only on enterprise plans). Stock avatars in commentary on sensitive topics need written consent. Violating privacy or publicity rights is prohibited. — [Synthesia AUP](https://www.synthesia.io/legal/acceptable-use-policy)
- Synthesia runs a talent program for the real actors behind its stock avatars, so stock avatars are licensed likenesses of real people. — [Synthesia](https://www.synthesia.io/post/introducing-synthesia-talent-experience-program-for-exceptional-actors); [Stock Avatar Licence](https://help.synthesia.io/en/articles/6341928-video-licence)
- Meta advises using purpose-built avatars over real people's likenesses without permission. — [CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ugc-facebook-ad-policy)
- Illinois Right of Publicity Act as amended (effective Jan 1, 2025) gives an individual rights against unauthorized digital replicas of their voice or likeness. — [ILGA](https://www.ilga.gov/ftp/legislation/103/BillStatus/HTML/10300HB4875.html)

### Inferences
- A stock avatar is someone else's licensed face. It can show up in a scam ad or on a competitor's channel, which damages a brand built on "proof, not fluff". It also may not be allowed in boosted posts on lower-tier plans. Check the vendor's terms (HeyGen, Synthesia, Captions and others) for paid-ad, endorsement and sensitive-topic clauses before boosting.
- With a digital twin, read the vendor's terms on who owns the trained model, whether the vendor can reuse it, and deletion on cancellation. Enable consent and verification locks, and keep a written self-consent and scope record. Voice clones are a fraud vector (impersonation calls to Etsy support, banks), so do not use the cloned voice where it could be mistaken for the owner authorizing something.

### Gaps
- HeyGen, ElevenLabs and other vendor terms on stock-avatar exclusivity, paid-ad use and model ownership were not retrieved.
- The text of Synthesia's stock avatar licence page (help.synthesia.io/6341928) was not fetched.

## 5. Practical: what a compliant setup looks like and what is safest for a small seller

### Takeaway
Safest order: (1) faceless screen recordings with your real voice or a plainly synthetic TTS voice; (2) an owner digital twin or voice clone, disclosed; (3) a stock avatar, which needs a NY-style disclosure, a license check before boosting, and acceptance of non-exclusivity. In all cases, never script the AI as a customer, turn on every platform AI toggle, and say "AI presenter" on screen.

### Cited Findings (rules the checklist rests on)
- The avatar must not deliver fake or false testimonials, and must not imply a real person's endorsement. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
- Stock-avatar ads reaching NY consumers need a conspicuous disclosure (no set wording). — [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ny-synthetic-performer-law-goes-into-effect)
- Instagram expects accounts featuring an AI person to turn on the AI-generated profile label. — [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/instagram-renames-ai-creator-label-and-restricts-unlabeled-ai-generated-profiles/)
- YouTube requires the altered/synthetic toggle for realistic people or cloned voices. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content)
- TikTok requires an AI label for realistic AI people and auto-detects C2PA. — [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/tiktoks-2026-ai-labeling-rules-and-what-they-signal-for-platform-governance/)
- FTC disclosures should be unavoidable, not behind a click. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)

### Inferences (compliance checklist)
1. **Platform toggles on every AI-presenter post:** Instagram/Facebook AI label and the account-level "AI-generated profile" toggle; TikTok "AI-generated content" setting; YouTube Studio "altered or synthetic content" = Yes; Meta Ads AI disclosure when boosting. Keep C2PA metadata intact; don't strip it.
2. **On-screen text in the first seconds, plus the caption:** for example "AI presenter, voice and face generated with AI" (stock avatar) or "AI version of the founder's voice/likeness" (twin). It should be large, high-contrast and on screen long enough to read. Put it in the caption too. This covers NY's "conspicuous" standard and FTC "unavoidable" guidance.
3. **Script rules:** the presenter speaks *as the brand* ("we built this"), never as a buyer. No invented results, names, or "I used this and..." stories. Real customer reviews may be quoted only verbatim, from real buyers, without cherry-picking that misleads, and attributed ("Etsy review, Mar 2026").
4. **Finance hygiene:** describe what the tool calculates, not promised outcomes. Add "general information, not financial or tax advice". On YouTube, expect AI-persona finance videos to be non-monetizable, which is irrelevant if the channel is not in YPP.
5. **Etsy:** use real screenshots and mockups of the actual spreadsheet in listing photos. If you use a listing video, show the real product, and disclose AI use in the description wherever AI made the product or the media.
6. **Licensing file:** save the avatar or voice vendor's plan terms (paid-ad and endorsement clauses), a self-consent memo for the owner twin, and the vendor's model-deletion terms.
7. **Safest single option for a $2.99 product:** screen-recorded demos with real or obviously synthetic narration. An owner twin is next. Avoid stock-avatar "UGC testimonial" formats entirely, since they combine all three risks: FTC fake testimonial, NY synthetic performer, and vendor paid-ad restrictions.
