# AI UGC business models: how AI-generated UGC is sold and delivered (US, as of Oct 13, 2026)

Evidence labels used on every finding below:
- **[A]** Primary and dated: the vendor's own live pricing or terms page, a live marketplace listing, a platform or regulator document, a payment-verified revenue dashboard, or a survey with a disclosed sample.
- **[B]** Credible secondary: established press or law-firm reporting with named sources, or a third-party review that shows its method.
- **[C]** Vendor, agency or competitor marketing, or numbers given without a disclosed method.
- **[D]** Folklore: course-seller or guru claims, hypothetical income models, unsourced statistics.

All pages were read on Oct 13, 2026. Several vendor pages draw their plan-comparison checkmarks with scripts or images the fetch tool could not read. Those cases are noted.

## 1. What brands buy as "AI UGC" and what they pay

### Takeaway
Brands buy batches of short avatar-led or AI-voiced ads (15-60s, several aspect ratios), sold with hook and angle variants and testing support, not single videos. Three price tiers run side by side. There is a commodity marketplace tier ($5-$150 per video), a freelancer/boutique tier ($150-$600 per ad, $1,200-$4,000 packs, $2,000-$8,000/month retainers, mostly guidance rather than verified sales), and an agency tier (€363-€500 per video in batches of 20-80 videos a month, for advertisers spending $50k+/month). Generation itself costs about $2-$30 per video.

### Cited Findings

**What the deliverable is**
- [A] One agency's published AI UGC offer is "AI UGC video ads": synthetic talking-head presenters in creator style at 15s, 30s or 60s, in 9:16, 1:1 and 4:5. Alongside them it sells AI script writing, multilingual localization (scripts, voiceovers, on-screen text), performance creative testing, and AI static and carousel ads. The process runs concept, script, AI production, post-production, then human QA. ([Admiral Media AI UGC agency page, 2026](https://admiral.media/ai-ugc-agency/))
- [C] A freelancer rate card prices the work as single ads, hook-variant batches ("one concept, 5 hook variants") and multi-concept batches ("three concepts, captions, two ratios"). It adds charges for extra hooks, aspect ratios, platform versions, brand caption styling, rush delivery, raw project files, paid usage rights and whitelisting. ([Motionbox rate card, Aug 2026](https://motionbox.io/blog/how-much-to-charge-clients-for-ai-ugc-ads))
- [D] One suggested batch structure is 5 hooks x 3 personas x 3 aspect ratios = 45 assets. The same source recommends 30-60 assets/month for indie operators and cites an agency benchmark of 300+ assets/month per client (inBeat). ([Vantaige, updated Jul 27, 2026](https://vantaige.io/blog/how-to-make-money-selling-ai-ugc-content-brands-2026))
- [A] Fiverr lists "AI UGC" as one of two types in its UGC Videos category. Human UGC is the other. Fiverr defines AI UGC as content made with tools such as digital presenters or synthetic narration, with no human on camera. ([Fiverr UGC video service guidelines, undated, live Oct 2026](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/37333410852625-UGC-video-service-guidelines))
- [B] On TikTok Shop, the WSJ (July 2026) described sellers and affiliates using synthetic avatars and "digital duplicates" of real creators for shoppable product videos. It also described TikTok's own "AI Video Maker," which auto-generates shoppable videos. ([Independent/WSJ via Newswav, Jul 16, 2026](https://newswav.com/article/brands-urge-tiktok-shop-creators-to-ditch-ai-fakes-report-says-A2607_sFmfB6); [AI Weekly summary of WSJ, Jul 15, 2026](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/tiktok-shop-sellers-turn-to-ai-avatars-sidelining-creators))
- [A] Upwork demand snapshot. A job page fetched Oct 13, 2026 showed 10 postings marked "Posted 1 day ago", including these four:
  - "AI Video Editor | Generative AI Creative" at a fixed $1,500 for full-time AI-assisted video work for a skin care brand.
  - "Full-Time AI Video Editor for E-commerce" at $1,500/month for a women's wellness brand.
  - "Video Editor for Meta Ad" at $150 fixed.
  - "High-Volume Short-Form Video Editor" at $50 fixed.
  
  ([Upwork job page snapshot](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Expert-UGC-creator-needed_~022052334852707357326/))

By format: testimonial $150-$350; hook + demo ad $200-$450. Perpetual rights add 100-150%. Fiverr takes 20% (paid by creators) and Upwork 10%. ([Superscale](https://superscale.ai/learn/how-much-does-ugc-cost-real-pricing-breakdown-for-2025/))

- [C] Segwise, citing Hoox (Mar 2026) and PPC.io (2026), puts AI UGC at $5-$30 per video against human UGC at $150-$500 (mid-tier) and $800-$2,000+ (premium). **Operator production cost and time**
- [C] Motionbox says a first ad takes 2-3 hours and later ads 40-60 minutes, with tool subscriptions at $39-$399/month flat. It compares a five-variation human creator campaign ($1,100-$2,950) with AI tool fees for the same campaign ($100-$285), and suggests charging $1,250-$2,000 for an AI five-variation batch. ([Motionbox](https://motionbox.io/blog/how-much-to-charge-clients-for-ai-ugc-ads))
- [D] Vantaige's model tool stack costs about $358/month: Arcads Creator $220, HeyGen Creator $29, ElevenLabs Creator $22, Higgsfield Plus $39, Submagic Pro $40 and n8n hosting about $8. It claims a 96.4% gross margin at 4 clients before labor. Its timing claims: first video 30-45 minutes, 10-15 minutes after 20 videos, and 3-5 minutes of human QA once fully automated. ([Vantaige](https://vantaige.io/blog/how-to-make-money-selling-ai-ugc-content-brands-2026))
- [B] Arcads costs about $11 per video on its Starter plan ($110/month for 10 videos), based on reviewers' screenshots of the in-app paywall. ([eesel, Jun 16, 2026](https://www.eesel.ai/en/blog/arcads-ai-pricing))

### Inferences
- The tiers serve different buyers. The $5 Fiverr buyer and the €500/video agency buyer are not the same customer at different prices. The agency tier bundles strategy, test design and iteration with media budgets above $50k/month.
- Generation costs only $2-$30 per video. That means price is set by hook and angle writing, test volume, iteration, usage rights and accountability, not by rendering. A seller who charges per rendered video competes directly with $12-$399/month tools.
- The Upwork snapshot shows small wellness and skincare brands hiring full-time "AI video editors" for about $1,500/month. For ongoing volume work with small brands, that is a realistic ceiling on what a small operator can charge.
- At the low end, AI UGC is priced below typical human UGC ($150-$300). At the agency end it is priced above it, because the batch includes testing strategy.

### Gaps
- No transaction-level data was found showing what brands actually paid for AI UGC services, as opposed to list prices and guidance. There is also no category-wide average for Fiverr's AI UGC category; only one gig was sampled.
- No pricing specific to AI UGC for TikTok Shop sellers was found.
- Budgets, client spend history, hire rates and proposal counts on individual Upwork AI UGC posts are not visible without logging in.

## 2. How operators get clients (marketplaces, outbound, agencies, communities) and reported conversion

### Takeaway
Faceless sellers can list directly in Fiverr's AI UGC category. Human-UGC marketplaces (Billo, Insense) are not channels for AI UGC; they are what brands compare it against. Higher-ticket work comes from outbound with spec ads, or as an add-on inside existing paid-media agency relationships. No credible public close or response rates exist; every number found is unsourced or secondhand.

### Cited Findings
- [A] **Fiverr**: AI UGC is a formal sub-type in the UGC Videos category. Sellers must say whether production is human or AI and name the tools used. ([Fiverr guidelines](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/37333410852625-UGC-video-service-guidelines)) The single gig sampled shows commodity pricing ($5 per 30s video). ([Fiverr gig](https://fiverr.com/paw_mik/make-ai-ugc-or-ai-dtc-video))
- [A] **Upwork**: skincare and wellness brands are posting AI video editor roles, priced at a fixed $1,500 for full-time work or $1,500/month. ([Upwork snapshot](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Expert-UGC-creator-needed_~022052334852707357326/))
- [A] **Billo** does not offer AI UGC. Its blog presents AI avatar platforms as a separate option that "run the highest compliance risks." Billo sells a vetted-creator hub, CreativeOps and a managed service. ([Billo](https://billo.app/blog/ugc-ads/))
- [B] **Insense** is a marketplace for human creators. Brands pay $500-$800/month plus a 7-20% fee, and AI UGC is not mentioned. ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/insense))
- [D] **Outbound playbook (Vantaige)**:
  - Channel mix: 60% outbound, 40% inbound.
  - Daily volume: 30 personalized LinkedIn connection requests and 30 personalized DMs across LinkedIn and X; 5-10 Upwork bids.
  - Target: 5 discovery calls a week.
  - Inbound: post 5 AI UGC examples a week; inbound DMs "start arriving" after about 60 days.
  - Follow-up cadence: DM, then 5-7 days, then a follow-up, then 10 days, then a "breakup" message.
  
  ([Vantaige](https://vantaige.io/blog/how-to-make-money-selling-ai-ugc-content-brands-2026))
- [D] **Reported conversion figures, all unverified**:
  - The spec-ad approach converts "5-10% per outreach" (no source).
  - Following a brand's launch and using its product first gives "3x higher" conversion than cold outreach alone (attributed to a Popfly guide).
  - "Half your spec-ad targets will at least book a discovery call" (no source).
  - The breakup message "gets the best response rate" (no number).
  
  ([Vantaige](https://vantaige.io/blog/how-to-make-money-selling-ai-ugc-content-brands-2026))
- [D] **Time to first client**: the author's roadmap puts the first paying client at days 31-60, at $1,500-$2,000 with a founder discount. Its FAQ tells readers to expect "60-90 days of unpaid outbound work" before revenue. Both are the author's estimates. ([Vantaige](https://vantaige.io/blog/how-to-make-money-selling-ai-ugc-content-brands-2026))
- [A] **Agency add-on route**: Admiral Media's page reports a Clutch rating of 4.9 from 48 reviews, 150+ brands and €500M+ in managed ad spend. It sells AI UGC to paid-media clients spending $50k+/month. ([Admiral Media](https://admiral.media/ai-ugc-agency/))
- [A] **Vendors sell done-for-you service too**: MakeUGC's Enterprise tier includes a "Managed by MakeUGC" service, which competes directly with operators. ([MakeUGC pricing](https://makeugc.ai/pricing))
- [A] **Communities as a channel and as a product**: The Clueless, creators of AI influencer Aitana López, run a Skool community titled "Free AI Creator Academy" that is actually paid.
  - Tiers: Standard $9/month; Premium $39/month; VIP $97/month (VIP adds avatars, UGC, ads and automation).
  - Size: 5.2k members.
  - Curriculum: AI images, video, avatars and UGC; "Build a portfolio and services to pitch to brands"; creating an offer and landing a first client.
  - Pitch line: "Want to build a €10,000/month AI agency? Start here."
  - Extras: 30% on eligible academy referrals; a price rise planned at 6,000 members.
  
  ([Skool, The Clueless](https://www.skool.com/ai-avatar-society-9744/about))
- [B] **TikTok Shop affiliate route** (earning from AI UGC-driven sales rather than selling a service):
  - TikTok lets creators earn commissions on AI-generated content if they disclose the AI use and avoid misleading claims.
  - Creator Daria Simhony makes at least one fully AI-generated promo video a day and earns commissions plus paid brand deals (for example, Jumiso).
  - SharkNinja's affiliate program enforces a "No AI-Generated Content policy," and affiliates who break it lose commissions.
  - The affiliate program has 11.3M creators globally, nearly 1M of them in the US.
  
  ([Independent/WSJ via Newswav](https://newswav.com/article/brands-urge-tiktok-shop-creators-to-ditch-ai-fakes-report-says-A2607_sFmfB6))
- [A] Tool vendors run affiliate programs ([Arcads affiliate page](https://www.arcads.ai/affiliate); [MakeUGC affiliate page](https://makeugc.ai/affiliate)). Only the pages' existence was confirmed; commission terms were not read.

### Inferences
- For a faceless operator, Fiverr is the easiest entry because it has an explicit no-human-on-camera category. The one listing sampled suggests a race to the bottom there, so the margin has to come from full automation.
- Higher-ticket buyers (DTC brands spending real money on Meta and TikTok) are reached through outbound plus spec ads, or through agencies that already hold the media budget. The sources recommend discovery calls as part of the funnel. Nothing shows whether a fully faceless, async sales process closes at comparable rates.
- Vendors' managed services (MakeUGC) and platform-native generators (TikTok AI Video Maker) both squeeze the low end of the service market.
- Communities and courses about AI UGC are themselves a business model, often more visible than the service businesses they teach.

### Gaps
- No operator-reported, verifiable response, close or churn rates were found.
- No terms were found for agency white-label partnerships (wholesale price per video, revenue share).
- Demand volume for the Fiverr and Upwork AI UGC categories (number of buyers or jobs) could not be retrieved.

## 3. Delivery tools: cost, commercial rights, avatar-actor consent, quality limits

### Takeaway
A working stack costs $12-$79/month at entry level (Creatify, MakeUGC, HeyGen). Arcads runs about $110-$220/month for 10-20 talking-actor videos, and Captions' Mirage Studio, which won one $115k test, needs a $399/month plan. Rights language varies a lot. Creatify and MakeUGC state commercial ownership or usage plainly. Arcads gives only a non-exclusive license that lets actors restrict or withdraw use. HeyGen's public pages are silent outside Enterprise. Higgsfield's "unlimited" mode bans automation and reselling, which matters for an AI-run pipeline.

### Cited Findings

**Arcads**
- [B] Plans per screenshots of the in-app paywall (no public pricing page found):
  - Starter: $110/month for 10 videos, about $11/video.
  - Creator: $220/month for 20 videos.
  - Pro: custom pricing, adds cloned actors.
  
  Videos run up to 120s. Small edits mean regenerating the whole video, which uses another credit. ([eesel, Jun 16, 2026](https://www.eesel.ai/en/blog/arcads-ai-pricing))
- [A] Arcads' help center (as of Oct 2026) describes credit-based billing. Talking-actor models (Arcads 1.0, Audio-Driven, OmniHuman 1.5) cost 800 credits per minute per actor, rounded up. Video models are priced per generation: Veo 3.1 at 800 credits, Kling 3.0 at 176-560, Seedance 2.0 at 96-1,620 depending on resolution. The "Product Showcase" preset costs 800. ([Arcads help: credits](https://help.arcads.ai/en/articles/13725589-how-are-credits-counted))
- [A] The homepage claims "1,000+ Captivating AI Actors," shows no plan prices, and mentions a free trial. Performance tiles such as "REVENUES GENERATED $16K +195%" have no methodology. ([Arcads homepage](https://www.arcads.ai/))
- [A] Terms (operator FRESHR; "Entry into force: 08/10/2026"):
  - **License**: non-exclusive, personal and non-transferable, with commercial use allowed. FRESHR "cannot grant an exclusive license because of the use of artificial intelligence."
  - **Responsibility**: the client is solely responsible for the script and any commercial use, and must not carry out "misleading or deceptive advertising."
  - **Actor controls**: actors ("Creators") may limit their Video Model to certain fields or uses. If a Creator withdraws, FRESHR may require the client to delete the videos "from all supports," with a €100/day penalty for delay.
  - **Training**: client inputs may be used to train FRESHR's models.
  - **Liability**: capped at 12 months of fees.
  - **Consent**: the terms contain no actor consent or compensation provisions.
  
  ([Arcads terms](https://www.arcads.ai/terms))

- [B] LTX (Lightricks) released LTX-2.5 on Aug 11, 2026.

  - Availability: Hugging Face, ComfyUI and the LTX API. API pricing was not given.

**Quality limits**
- [C] Hook-rate (3-second view) benchmarks from CineRads, cited by Segwise: human direct-to-camera 28-38%; high-quality AI avatars 26-36%; "robotic" early-generation avatars 14-20%. ([Segwise $115k test write-up](https://segwise.ai/blog/115k-ai-ugc-platform-test-findings))
- [C] The MisfitMarketing test of five platforms (Arcads, Argil, Creatify, HeyGen, Mirage) found character continuity across scenes a challenge on every tool. It called Argil "too much control" and HeyGen "less experimental." Mirage's realism edge was described as a deliberate trade of volume for quality. ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/115k-ai-ugc-platform-test-findings))
- [C] Segwise says AI is weakest at physical product demonstrations, real-customer testimonials and community-driven niches. ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/ai-ugc-vs-human-ugc-meta-ads-roas-2026))
- [C] One vendor's cost comparison for 50 videos a month: HeyGen $104, Arcads $650. ([SFN AI, Apr 24, 2026](https://www.shortformnation.com/blog/ai-generated-ugc-for-tiktok-shop-what-actually-works-and-what-gets-you-demonetized-2026-guide))

### Inferences
- An operator can deliver competent avatar ads for under $100/month in tools. Premium realism (Mirage Studio) costs about 4-5x more.
- For an AI-run business, Higgsfield's fair-use rule matters. Automated or scripted generation and reselling are excluded from unlimited mode, so an automated pipeline would run on credits (MCP/CLI/API) and "unlimited" plan economics would not apply.
- Arcads' terms create two operational risks a service seller must pass on to clients. First, if a stock actor withdraws, the videos must come down everywhere. Second, the license is non-exclusive, so the same actor may appear in competitors' ads.
- Actor consent and compensation are handled by vendors and mostly not disclosed on public pages. Only Arcads' terms show actors can restrict which uses their likeness allows.
- Open-weights models (LTX-2.5) can cut per-clip cost for businesses under $10M revenue. They are general video models, though, not turnkey UGC avatar tools; lip-sync and talking-head workflows would have to be built on top.

### Gaps
- None of the public pricing pages for Creatify, MakeUGC, HeyGen or Mirage say how many credits a 30-second talking-avatar video uses. That means a true per-video cost cannot be derived from primary sources.
- Arcads' current public plan prices are unknown; its own help center shows a credit system that may have replaced the per-video plans in the June 2026 screenshots.
- Actor consent and compensation terms for HeyGen, Creatify and MakeUGC (ToS and custom-avatar policy pages) were not read.
- Tagshop pricing is missing.
- No independent blind quality test across tools from Q3-Q4 2026 was found.

## 5. Performance evidence (AI UGC vs human UGC) and brand/buyer sentiment

### Takeaway
No independent, controlled test with disclosed spend, sample size and significance was found. Vendor and agency data point the same way: AI avatars hold attention close to human levels at the top of the funnel and win on cost and test volume. They lose on conversion where trust, demonstration or a higher price (above about $75) matters. Consumer sentiment toward AI ads is cooling, especially among Gen Z, while advertisers stay bullish. Some major brands now ban AI content from their affiliate programs.

### Cited Findings
- [C] Segwise (vendor; Apr 27, 2026, updated Oct 12, 2026):
  - AI UGC click-through rate runs 85-110% of well-performing human UGC (Hoox 2026 field data, no sample size given).
  - AI allows 50-200+ variants a month versus 5-15 for human UGC.
  - AI "closes the gap or wins" in transactional verticals (DTC ecommerce, SaaS, mobile games, fast fashion) and lags in skincare, supplements, fitness and premium beauty. No numbers are given for either claim.
  - 80% of brands prefer human UGC for trust and relatability (PPC.io 2026).
  
  ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/ai-ugc-vs-human-ugc-meta-ads-roas-2026))
- [C] Hook rate: human 28-38%, high-quality AI avatars 26-36%, early-generation avatars 14-20% (CineRads, via Segwise). Consumer quality approval: 68% for AI UGC with authenticity signals managed, versus 81% for human UGC (Superscale, via Segwise). ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/115k-ai-ugc-platform-test-findings))
- [C] Moburst (agency; Apr 11, 2026; no sample sizes given):
  - Meta internal case studies, as cited: avatar ads had 12-18% lower CPM than static ads and 1.4x the thumb-stop rate of brand-produced video.
  - Aggregated mid-market DTC data: real creators had about 22% higher watch-through and about 31% higher post-click conversion on products over $75. On products under $30, the gap was within 5%.
  - Recommendation: put 15-20% of budget into head-to-head tests.
  
  ([Moburst Intercept](https://intercept.moburst.com/ai-avatar-ads-vs-real-creators-which-wins-on-meta/))
- [C] Superscale data from Jan 2026, as cited by vendor SFN AI: TikTok engagement 18.5% for AI UGC versus 5.3% for human; authenticity trust 63% versus 81%. SFN's view-count claims contradict each other ("2.8x more views" against "human 35% lower"), so its view figures are unreliable. ([SFN AI](https://www.shortformnation.com/blog/ai-generated-ugc-for-tiktok-shop-what-actually-works-and-what-gets-you-demonetized-2026-guide))
- [A] IAB, "The AI Ad Gap Widens" (Jan 15, 2026; IAB with Sonata Insights; 505 US Gen Z and Millennial consumers and 104 US ad executives; fieldwork Oct 2025-Jan 2026):
  - 82% of executives think young consumers feel positive about AI-generated ads, but only 45% of consumers do. The gap is 37 points, up from 32 in 2024.
  - The share of consumers who feel negative rose 12 points from 2024. Gen Z is 39% negative, Millennials 20%.
  - Consumers are more likely than executives to call AI-ad brands "manipulative" (20% vs 10%) and "unethical" (16% vs 7%).
  - 73% say knowing an ad was AI-made would raise their purchase likelihood or make no difference.
  - Nearly half want AI voices or AI avatars disclosed.
  - 89% of advertisers using generative AI disclose at least sometimes; fewer than half always do.
  - 83% of executives say their company used AI in creative.
  
  ([IAB](https://www.iab.com/insights/the-ai-ad-gap-widens/))
- [B] Per AI Weekly's summary of WSJ reporting, consumer enthusiasm for AI-generated creator content fell from about 60% in 2023 to about 26% by early 2026, and roughly half of consumers worry about brands posting AI content without disclosure. The summary does not name the underlying survey. ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/tiktok-shop-sellers-turn-to-ai-avatars-sidelining-creators))
- [B] Brand sentiment:
  - SharkNinja's affiliate memo bars AI-generated content, and its chief commercial officer said: "We didn't want an AI-generated Shark vacuum cleaning an AI-generated floor."
  - Rare Beauty says it does not work with AI creators or digital duplicates.
  
  ([Independent/WSJ via Newswav](https://newswav.com/article/brands-urge-tiktok-shop-creators-to-ditch-ai-fakes-report-says-A2607_sFmfB6))
- [A] Billo, a human-UGC marketplace, calls AI avatar platforms the option that "run[s] the highest compliance risks." ([Billo](https://billo.app/blog/ugc-ads/))

### Inferences
- For low-priced, demonstrable products (for example, sub-$30 digital tools shown by screen recording), the weak evidence suggests the conversion gap between AI and human creators is smallest. For trust-heavy categories (supplements, skincare) and higher prices, human creators keep an edge. This is directional only.
- The commercial case for AI UGC rests mainly on testing breadth and speed at low cost, not on beating human creators per ad.
- Disclosure is becoming expected. About half of consumers want avatars disclosed, and some brands refuse AI content outright, so undisclosed avatar ads carry reputational as well as platform risk.

### Gaps
- No Meta or TikTok platform-published data on AI avatar ad performance was found.
- Creative-analytics reports specific to AI UGC (Motion, Foreplay, Triple Whale) did not surface in this pass.
- No hook rate, hold rate, ROAS or CPA from a test with disclosed method and a human control was found.

## 7. Risks specific to selling AI UGC (flag level; law and policy covered in depth elsewhere)

### Takeaway
The highest-risk format is the AI "customer testimonial," a synthetic person claiming personal results, because the FTC's 2024 rule bans fake testimonials, including AI-generated ones. Platforms now expect AI labels on realistic synthetic people. TikTok Shop has its own quality rules (AI voices banned in LIVEs), some brands ban AI content outright, and vendor terms put all claim liability on the client.

### Cited Findings
- [B] The FTC's final rule on reviews and testimonials, announced Aug 14, 2024, bans "fake or false reviews and testimonials created by generative AI." It covers creating, selling and buying them, and civil penalties reach about $52,000 per violation for knowing violators. ([Hunton Andrews Kurth, Aug 16, 2024](https://hunton.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/ftc-issues-final-rule-targeting-fake-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials-including-those-generated-by-artificial-intelligence-ai-and-online-bots)) Effective date: Oct 21, 2024. ([CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ad-disclosure-requirements))
- [C] CineRads' FTC reading: AI may voice enthusiasm as a clearly scripted brand spokesperson but may not pose as a real, named customer or verified reviewer. It also cites March 2025 FTC staff guidance that AI-generated or substantially modified ad content should be disclosed; that guidance was not independently verified. ([CineRads, Jul 21, 2026](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ad-disclosure-requirements))
- [C] Platform labels, per CineRads:
  - **Meta**: the "AI info" label is applied automatically when Meta's own generative tools significantly modify an ad or C2PA credentials are present. Manual disclosure is expected for photorealistic synthetic media. ([Meta policy page, as cited](https://transparency.meta.com/governance/tracking-impact/labeling-ai-content/))
  - **TikTok**: realistic AI depictions of people need the AIGC label. Branded content needs both the AIGC label and the commercial-content toggle, and paid ads follow the same rules. ([TikTok policy page, as cited](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content))
  - **YouTube**: realistic synthetic content needs the "altered content" disclosure. ([YouTube policy page, as cited](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491))
  
  ([CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ad-disclosure-requirements))
- [C] A UK agency reports that TikTok has required a disclosure label on realistic AI content in ads "since 21 July," year implied 2026. The rule covers AI voiceovers, AI visuals, photorealistic product imagery and AI spokespeople. The agency describes an escalation of warning, 7-day restriction, 30-day suspension, then permanent ban, but cites no official TikTok source. ([StellarSearch, Aug 12, 2026](https://www.stellarsearch.co.uk/insight/tiktoks-ai-ad-disclosure-rules-are-live-what-brands-running-ai-creative-need-to-do-now))
- [B] TikTok Shop's "Requirements for High-Quality Videos and LIVEs" (US Academy, May 23, 2026):
  - AI-generated voices and pre-recorded narration are prohibited in LIVEs.
  - Shoppable videos need at least 3 seconds of dynamic content.
  - Videos made of still images throughout, without the creator's face or the physical product, are prohibited.
  - Penalties include violation points, content removal, commission restrictions and account restriction, all feeding the Creator Health Rating.
  
  ([PPC Land, Jun 8, 2026](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shops-quality-rules-ban-ai-voices-and-still-images-from-lives/))
- [C] A vendor claims TikTok's Creator Rewards Program excludes AI-generated content and that labeled AI content gets lower For You priority. No official source is cited. ([SFN AI](https://www.shortformnation.com/blog/ai-generated-ugc-for-tiktok-shop-what-actually-works-and-what-gets-you-demonetized-2026-guide))
- [B] At the brand level, SharkNinja's affiliate program enforces a "No AI-Generated Content policy," and affiliates who break it forfeit commissions. TikTok permits commissions on AI content when the AI use is disclosed. ([Independent/WSJ via Newswav](https://newswav.com/article/brands-urge-tiktok-shop-creators-to-ditch-ai-fakes-report-says-A2607_sFmfB6))
- [A] Fiverr's rules for AI UGC sellers:
  - Disclose human vs AI production and name the tools used.
  - Hold tool licenses, voice and avatar permissions, and likeness approvals.
  - Do not promise CTR or ROAS.
  - Do not use props that imply credentials you don't hold (the example given is a lab coat).
  
  ([Fiverr guidelines](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/37333410852625-UGC-video-service-guidelines))
- [A] Arcads' terms put the script and claims entirely on the client, prohibit misleading advertising, and let actors withdraw (deletion everywhere, €100/day for delay). The license is non-exclusive. ([Arcads terms](https://www.arcads.ai/terms))
- [A] Admiral Media describes AI UGC as "scripted advertising, not content from a real customer" that does not show a presenter's personal experience. Clients confirm claims and how synthetic presenters are represented. ([Admiral Media](https://admiral.media/ai-ugc-agency/))
- [C] EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties apply from Aug 2, 2026 (Dec 2, 2026 for systems already on the market). This matters only for EU-facing ads. ([CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ad-disclosure-requirements))
- [B] The LTX-2.x license forbids circumventing synthetic-media transparency features. ([NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/ltx-2-5-adds-native-multi-shot-video-and-a-diffusion-decoder/))
- [A] Nearly half of US Gen Z and Millennial consumers want AI avatars or AI voices disclosed. ([IAB](https://www.iab.com/insights/the-ai-ad-gap-widens/))

### Inferences
- Safer formats: a clearly scripted AI spokesperson or presenter with a disclosure label; product demos built from real screen recordings or real product footage with AI narration; hook variants that make no first-person result claims.
- Risky formats: AI "customers" reporting personal results or "reviews," AI presenters in health, supplement or finance contexts implying credentials, and anything used as a TikTok Shop LIVE voice.
- A service contract should assign claim substantiation and platform labeling to the client, pass down actor-withdrawal takedown duties, and exclude performance guarantees, in line with Fiverr's no-ROAS-promise rule.
- For affiliate content, check each brand's program rules before producing AI videos. Some, like SharkNinja, forfeit commissions for any AI content.

### Gaps
- Official Meta and TikTok ad-policy texts were not read directly; platform rules here come from secondary summaries. Another researcher is covering law and policy in depth.
- No data was found on how often AI-labeled ads are rejected or penalized.
