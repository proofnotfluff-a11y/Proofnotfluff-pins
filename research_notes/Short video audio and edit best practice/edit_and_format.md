# Short-form edit and format best practice for faceless spreadsheet-calculator demos (TikTok, Reels, Shorts, Facebook Reels), as of 13 Oct 2026

Evidence labels: [Platform] = official help page, official blog or named platform staff. [Platform via press] = a platform announcement as reported by trade press or a vendor, not checked on the platform's own page. [Vendor data] = analytics or tool vendor data with a stated sample. [Vendor opinion] = vendor, agency or tool-maker guidance without data. [Academic] = university or peer-reviewed research. [Self-reported] = a creator's or seller's own claims. [OLD] = dated before 13 Oct 2025 (older than 12 months). Several TikTok pages (support.tiktok.com, tiktok.com/community-guidelines) blocked fetching, so TikTok's organic rules are mostly secondhand below.

ProofNotFluff today, for reference: 30 to 37 s videos; hook headline on screen from frame one but not spoken; narration starts about 1.5 s in; sheet in the top half, bottom 40% mostly empty; little picture change except cells filling; same layout and music on every video; AI TTS narrator; end card says to search the shop on Etsy; thousands of Shorts views, almost no clicks.

## 1. Hooks: what happens in the first 1 to 3 seconds

### Takeaway
All the platforms now measure the opening directly. Instagram's Reels "skip rate" counts viewers who leave before 3 seconds, YouTube Studio shows how many people "chose to view" a Short instead of swiping, and TikTok's creative guidance says to land the content proposition in the first 3 seconds and the hook within 6. I found no public, method-stated test of a spoken hook at 0 s against a text-only hook. The defensible rule is to have the voice and the on-screen headline state the same promise from frame one, so viewers with sound on and sound off get the hook in the same 1 to 2 seconds.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram replaced the Reels "View rate" metric with "Skip rate", defined as the share of viewers who skipped the Reel before the first three seconds. It also added a per-Reel retention chart, about which the report notes "the flatter the line, the more engaged your audience is." Announced by Instagram's official account and reported 25 Aug 2025. [Platform via press] [OLD] Source: [SocialSamosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-retention-chart-skip-rate-new-performance-metrics-reels-9730992)
- YouTube Studio reports "how many chose to view" for each Short: the share of feed appearances where the viewer stayed instead of swiping. The source says "YouTube publishes no official benchmarks" for this or any other Shorts metric. Creator-reported ranges are 70 to 79% "working" and 80%+ "strong", and the article says the metric is decided in the first 1 to 2 seconds (its own view). [Vendor opinion, Sept 2026] Source: [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/)
- TikTok's "Creative best practices for performance ads" (last updated June 2025) says to put the hook in the first 6 seconds, using suspense, surprise or other emotions, and to introduce the content proposition in the first 3 seconds "to improve recall and awareness". The structure it recommends is hook, then unique selling points, then a clear CTA. It also says to use captions or text overlays for context, to display 5 to 10 words per second when using text, and that sound or music is a basic requirement. The page cites no data and is written for ads, not organic posts. [Platform] [OLD] Source: [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- The same TikTok page recommends featuring people (creators, employees, customers), a DIY or less polished look, and trends, memes or challenges. A faceless account works against the first of these. [Platform] [OLD] Source: [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Mosseri reportedly named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the top Reels distribution signals in January 2025. Skip rate is not named as a ranking signal. [Platform via press, secondary] [OLD] Source: [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/es/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm)
- "Facebook research": 65% of people who watch the first 3 seconds of a video continue for at least 10 seconds, and 45% watch 30 seconds or more. This is cited thirdhand (Kapwing via Brandefy) with no date or method. [Weak] Source: [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/short-form-video-statistics-tiktok-reels-and-shorts-by-the-numbers-in-2026/)
- A hook taxonomy that labels itself "evidence synthesis" (no dataset) says result-first hooks suit transformations and demos and problem-solution hooks suit tutorials and software. Each type has a failure mode, including "a reveal that gives no reason to keep watching." It says the spoken and on-screen openings should reinforce the same promise, and that on-screen text clarifies the subject for viewers "who haven't committed to listening." It recommends judging a hook by chose-to-view plus the start of the retention curve: a high chose-to-view with weak retention suggests an unfulfilled promise. [Vendor opinion, July 2026] Source: [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-hooks-for-youtube-shorts)
- "85% of Facebook videos are watched without sound" (Verizon and Publicis Media, cited via Kapwing with no date). [Weak, undated] Source: [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/short-form-video-statistics-tiktok-reels-and-shorts-by-the-numbers-in-2026/)
- Instagram Trial Reels, which show a Reel to non-followers first, were expanded in July 2025 to public accounts with 1,000+ followers. Mosseri reportedly called them "a great way to test if your content ranks highly." [Platform via press, secondary] [OLD] Source: [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/es/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm)

### Inferences
Rules the renderer and agent can adopt (my inferences; none has been A/B tested in a public study):
- Start narration at frame 0 and have it speak the hook, word for word or tighter, matching the on-screen headline. The current 1.5 s silent hook spends the most heavily scored second without audio. Basis: TikTok's 3-second proposition rule, Instagram's 3-second skip rate, and Bytecap's "same promise" guidance.
- Keep result-first hooks, since they fit a calculator, but leave one loop open. "$25 an hour looks fine. Break-even is $62.36" gives away the payoff. Something like "$25 an hour looks fine. It's really a $62 job. One line explains it." keeps a reason to stay. Basis: Bytecap's warning about reveals.
- Open mid-action instead of on a static title: the cursor already typing, a number already rolling, or the bad number flashing red at 0.0 s. Basis: TikTok's advice to use motion and graphics to hold view time. Direct evidence on first-frame motion is missing (see Gaps).
- Score hooks per platform on Instagram skip rate, YouTube "chose to view", and early retention. Change one opening element at a time (Bytecap's method) and use Instagram Trial Reels to test variants on non-followers.
- The voice-free first second also costs sound-on viewers. No source I found quantified the sound-on share for TikTok or Shorts, so text and voice should both carry the hook.

### Gaps
- No public, method-stated study compares a spoken hook at 0 s with a text-only hook, or result-first with question-first, on organic TikTok, Reels or Shorts. Vendor posts making such claims (for example, that hooks "drive 90% of retention") show no samples.
- I couldn't fetch TikTok Creator Academy's organic hook guidance, and found no direct Mosseri quote on hooks. No first-party YouTube hook guidance for Shorts was found beyond the "chose to view" metric.
- No evidence was found on first-frame motion as a variable on its own.

## 2. Length and pacing for educational shorts

### Takeaway
There is no single best length. The platforms are pushing longer: Shorts can run 3 minutes, Reels up to 3 minutes can be recommended to non-followers, and TikTok only pays for videos of 1 minute or more. 2026 vendor data on business pages shows average views rising with length on TikTok and Facebook, while Instagram Reels peak at 45 to 60 s. For a one-idea calculator demo, the lever with the best support is retention density (share who choose to view, average percentage viewed), not raw length. Since March 2025, Shorts "views" count every start and replay, so raw Shorts view counts overstate real watching.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube raised the maximum Shorts length to 3 minutes in October 2024. [Vendor report of a platform change] [OLD] Source: [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/short-form-video-statistics-tiktok-reels-and-shorts-by-the-numbers-in-2026/)
- Instagram reportedly confirmed in Feb 2025 that Reels up to 3 minutes can be recommended to non-followers, and raised the maximum Reel length to 20 minutes for eligible accounts in Dec 2025. The vendor's claim that recommendations to non-followers are still capped at 3 minutes is not attributed to Instagram. [Platform via press, secondary] Source: [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/es/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm)
- TikTok Creator Rewards, quoting TikTok Creator Academy: "A video must run at least one minute and clear at least 1,000 For You feed views to count." This is a monetization rule, not a ranking rule. [Platform via vendor, checked Oct 2026] Source: [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-2026-requirements/)
- Socialinsider analyzed 111K videos from business pages, Jan to Jun 2026. Engagement is per follower on Instagram and Facebook and per view on TikTok. [Vendor data, Aug 2026, correlational] Source: [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/short-form-video-statistics/)
  - Instagram Reels: engagement and average views both peak at 45 to 60 s (0.35%, 10,374 views), against 0 to 30 s (0.28%, 4,700) and 30 to 45 s (0.30%, 8,564). Both drop sharply past 180 s (0.15%, 4,428).
  - Facebook Reels: average views rise steadily with length, from 2,825 at 0 to 30 s to 4,220 above 180 s. Engagement peaks at 120 to 180 s (0.50%).
  - TikTok: engagement rate is highest under 30 s (6.0%), but average views peak at 120 to 180 s (12,000, against 1,200 for 0 to 30 s and 2,000 for 30 to 45 s).
  - YouTube Shorts is not covered.
- Metricool analyzed 799,718 videos from 71,177 YouTube accounts, comparing Feb 2025 with Feb 2026: "Shorts views rose 127% YoY, but viewers spent three times less time watching each Short." The Shorts feed generated 61% of YouTube views. [Vendor data, July 2026] Source: [Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-youtube-study-2026/)
- Since 31 March 2025, a Shorts view counts each time a Short starts or replays, with no minimum watch time. The old metric is now "engaged views" under Advanced Mode, and YPP eligibility and earnings still use engaged views. [Platform via press] [OLD] Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)
- Average percentage viewed on Shorts can exceed 100% when viewers loop. Creator-reported (not YouTube) benchmarks:
  - Under 35 s: 80 to 90% is "working"; 90%+ is "strong", looping past 100.
  - 60 s+: 60 to 75% is working; 75%+ is strong.
  [Vendor opinion, Sept 2026] Source: [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/)
- Aggregator claims with no individual source: "21-34 seconds" gives the highest engagement; 15 to 30 s Reels average 5.8% engagement; videos under 90 s keep about 50% of viewers on average. [Weak] Source: [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/short-form-video-statistics-tiktok-reels-and-shorts-by-the-numbers-in-2026/)
- TikTok recommends "transitions, stickers, and graphics to sustain engagement and view time" but gives no frequency. Its current ad best-practices page gives no recommended length. [Platform] [OLD] Source: [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Guo, Kim and Rubin (2014) studied edX MOOC videos and found shorter videos more engaging. They recommend a "duration < 6 minutes", but "for tutorials, length does not matter, support re-watching and skimming." [Academic, different context] [OLD] Source: [NUS course slides summarizing the paper](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~kanmy/courses/6101_2016/cs6101-JeffryHartanto-0303.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Metricool view jump overlaps with the March 2025 counting change, so "thousands of views on Shorts" can include many starts with almost no watch time. The agent should log engaged views, chose-to-view, and average percentage viewed per Short, not views.
- Use two length tiers instead of one. Basis: correlational vendor data plus platform incentives.
  - (a) 18 to 25 s "one number" cut for Shorts and TikTok: hook, 2 to 3 inputs, reveal, CTA. Aim for average percentage viewed above 90%, with loops.
  - (b) 45 to 60 s walkthrough for Instagram and Facebook, where business-page data favors 45 to 60 s and longer. In the Instagram data, 45 to 60 s averaged about 21% more views than 30 to 45 s (10,374 against 8,564).
  - TikTok views rise with length, but so would the risk of losing viewers on a thin script. Test a 60 to 90 s variant only where there is real content (several scenarios, a fix).
- Today the picture barely changes. Add a visible change every 2 to 3 seconds: a zoom step, highlight, counter, caption emphasis, or a scene cut between input and reveal. That interval is a creator convention; I found no rigorous source for it.
- Loop ending: end on a frame that matches frame one (same headline and number), so an auto-replay reads as continuous. Per the 1of10 definition this lifts average percentage viewed past 100%. A causal effect on reach is unproven.

### Gaps
- No platform publishes an optimal length for organic Shorts or TikToks.
- No method-stated data was found on cut frequency ("visual change every N seconds") in short-form.
- No evidence was found that loop endings increase distribution, as opposed to raising average percentage viewed and view counts.
- Socialinsider covers business pages, not faceless tutorial accounts, and excludes YouTube Shorts. Length buckets are confounded with account size and content type.

## 3. Screen-recording techniques (auto-zoom, cursor emphasis, highlight boxes, number counters)

### Takeaway
No platform or vendor I found publishes watch-time retention data on auto-zoom, cursor emphasis or counters specifically. The best evidence comes from multimedia-learning research. Visual signaling (highlighting, color cues, arrows, zooming to the relevant part) reliably helps viewers attend to and remember the right part of the screen, and no downside has been reported. Gimmicky motion such as bouncing or spinning can distract.

### Cited Findings
- Meta-analysis evidence on signaling: [Academic, via a 2021 summary] [OLD] Source: [QIC-WD umbrella summary](https://www.qic-wd.org/sites/default/files/Umbrella%20Summary%20-%20Signaling%20in%20Training%20111021.pdf)
  - Schneider, Beege, Nebel and Rey (2018, Educational Research Review) found signaling "has a strong effect on learners' retention" and a moderate effect on transfer. Eye-tracking shows signaled learners spend more time on the most relevant material.
  - Xie et al. (2017, PLoS ONE) linked larger cognitive-load reductions from cues to better retention and transfer. Alpizar, Adesope and Wong (2020) and Richter, Scheiter and Eitel (2016) are the other meta-analyses cited.
  - No negative effects were reported. Signal type and system-paced versus learner-paced presentation tend not to change the effect.
  - Bouncing or spinning animations can distract (practical guidance, not a tested effect).
  - Note: "retention" in this research means remembering content, not watch-time retention.
- Guo, Kim and Rubin (2014, edX): Khan-style tutorials (step-by-step, voice-led) had a 40% problem-attempt rate, against 31% for other styles. Their recommendations include "Record Khan-style tutorials when possible", "Invest in pre-production effort" and "Bring out natural enthusiasm and speaking fast is okay." [Academic] [OLD] Source: [NUS course slides summarizing the paper](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~kanmy/courses/6101_2016/cs6101-JeffryHartanto-0303.pdf)
- TikTok recommends transitions, stickers and graphics to sustain engagement and view time, and says a DIY or less polished look fits the platform. [Platform] [OLD] Source: [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)

### Inferences
Rules the renderer can adopt. The exact values are design conventions, not tested numbers.
- Punch-in on the active cell. While each input is typed, zoom to about 1.4x to 1.8x so the label and its cell fill most of the frame width. Zoom back out to the full sheet only for the reveal, so the viewer sees the whole model. Use 200 to 300 ms ease in and out, and don't zoom on every keystroke.
- Highlight and dim. Put a colored outline or fill on the cell being typed, and dim non-active cells to about 40 to 60% opacity. This is the "signaling" effect in its simplest form.
- Number counter at the payoff. Roll the result from the "looks fine" figure to the real figure over about 0.6 to 1.0 s, then color-flash it (red for the gap, green for the fix). This adds motion exactly where the hook promised a payoff.
- Cursor. Enlarge it and give it a soft halo or click ripple. Natural cursor movement also delivers the "less polished" feel TikTok recommends.
- Avoid bouncing, spinning or shaking effects. Every motion should point at a number.
- Always include the "fix" step (the optional green result) as a second payoff. It is a reason to stay past the first reveal and supports a varied ending.

### Gaps
- No watch-time retention data on auto-zoom, cursor effects, highlight boxes or animated counters in short-form video. Screen-recording tools promote auto-zoom, but I found no measured results.

## 4. Layout for 9:16 (full frame, text size, captions, safe zones)

### Takeaway
On Instagram and Facebook Reels, roughly the top 14% and bottom 35% of a 1080x1920 frame sit under app UI. TikTok's bottom band is smaller, about 20%. Anything critical (hook, numbers, captions) is safe on every platform if kept between about y=270 and y=1250 px, with about 65 px side margins and extra room on the right for the icon column. The current layout (sheet in the top half, bottom 40% mostly empty) risks the top UI band and leaves the safe middle-lower band unused.

### Cited Findings
- Meta unified the Stories and Reels safe zone in March 2026, per a vendor guide. Its guidance on a 1440x2560 canvas is top 14%, bottom 20 to 35%, and left and right 6% each. Converted to 1080x1920 that is top about 269 px, bottom about 384 to 672 px (treat 672 px as the danger zone), and sides about 65 px. The top covers the profile icon, username and "Sponsored" label. The bottom covers CTA buttons, engagement icons, captions and audio labels, and caption length and device size change how far captions reach. The margins are the article's own guidance, not quoted from Meta. [Vendor opinion, Mar 2026] Source: [billo](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones)
- An agency guide (published Oct 2025, updated Sept 2026) gives these zones for 1080x1920. It says the data were compiled from the Meta Business Help Center and TikTok Creative Center, but it doesn't link specific safe-zone documents and is internally inconsistent in places. [Vendor opinion] Source: [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/guide-to-safe-zones-tiktok-facebook-instagram-stories-reels/)
  - TikTok: top about 10% (about 192 px) and bottom about 20% (about 384 px). The bottom holds captions, the progress bar and the profile icon. The right edge holds the engagement icons.
  - Instagram and Facebook Reels: top 14% (about 269 px), bottom 35% (about 672 px), sides 6% (about 65 px).
  - YouTube Shorts is not covered.
- TikTok says to orient vertically at 9:16, shoot at least 720p, keep content inside the UI safe zone, use captions or text overlays, and show 5 to 10 words per second when using text. [Platform] [OLD] Source: [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Captions: "Captioned videos get about 12% more watch time and are 80% more likely to be watched to completion" (no source given). "85% of Facebook videos are watched without sound" (Verizon and Publicis Media, undated). [Weak] Source: [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/short-form-video-statistics-tiktok-reels-and-shorts-by-the-numbers-in-2026/)

### Inferences
Layout grid for the renderer (1080x1920). This is my inference from the safe-zone figures above; the type sizes are unsourced design conventions.
- y 0 to 270: background and brand color only, nothing critical. The top UI band covers it on Reels and partly on TikTok.
- y 270 to about 460: hook headline, 2 lines maximum, bold, cap height about 70 to 90 px, high contrast on the dark background.
- y about 460 to 1050: the spreadsheet, zoomed to fill the width inside about 65 px left and about 140 px right margins (the right margin clears the TikTok and Shorts icon column; that width is not verified). Use punch-ins from section 3 so the active cell is never small.
- y about 1050 to 1240: burned-in captions (2 lines maximum, about 48 to 60 px type, word-by-word highlight) or the big result number during the reveal. This uses the safe band the current layout leaves empty.
- y 1250 to 1920: decorative only on Reels exports. On TikTok and Shorts exports, y 1250 to about 1530 is visible and can carry secondary elements (a "fix" chip, the shop name), but nothing the video depends on.
- Consider per-platform renders: a Reels and Facebook version with everything above y=1250, and a TikTok and Shorts version that may use down to about y=1530.
- Number legibility: the result figure should be the largest element on screen at the reveal (about 120 px+ cap height). Input numbers should be at least caption size once zoomed.

### Gaps
- No YouTube or reliable secondary source for Shorts safe-zone dimensions was found.
- No platform guidance on minimum text size for phone legibility was found.
- The exact width of the right-side icon column on TikTok and Shorts was not verified.

## 5. Narration (AI vs human voice, speaking pace, labeling rules)

### Takeaway
In blind tests, listeners can no longer reliably tell a good AI voice from a human one and rate them about the same. Stated trust drops, though, when people believe or are told a voice is AI. The platforms' labeling rules focus on "realistic" synthetic media. Meta's rule text covers "realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered". YouTube's exemptions list cloning your own voice but don't explicitly address generic TTS narration. TikTok's rule, seen only through secondary sources, targets realistic depictions of people, places and events. No credible source gives an optimal words-per-minute for short-form narration.

### Cited Findings
- Crowd React Media ran a blind test with 1,326 weekly radio listeners in May to June 2026. The same scripts were read by a human voice actor and by an AI voice. [Industry research, radio context, July 2026] Source: [Radio Ink](https://radioink.com/2026/07/07/radio-listeners-cant-detect-ai-voice-but-dont-trust-it-either/)
  - Detection: 55% of those who heard the AI voice judged it human, and 59% of those who heard the human voice identified it as human.
  - Ratings: there were no statistically significant gaps on professionalism, authenticity, credibility, energy or likability. Overall appeal on the first clip was 60% for the human voice and 61% for the AI voice. The only significant gap was humor (33% vs 26% found it funny).
  - Disclosure: told that a station uses AI, 47% said it would make no difference, 21% would feel more favorable and 33% less favorable. Negative comments were about trust, not sound quality.
- An Adobe Express survey of 850 consumers and 205 marketers (reported Mar 2026, fieldwork dates not stated) found [Vendor survey, secondary report] Source: [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/ai-voices-in-marketing-adobe/):
  - 88% have heard a voice and wondered whether it was AI.
  - 77% trust human voices most.
  - 14% don't trust any voice they believe is AI.
  - 58% would trust a brand less if its ad used AI voices.
- YouTube requires disclosure when AI "meaningfully alter[s] or generate[s] photorealistic content". Examples include content that makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't, or a realistic scene that didn't occur. The rule covers content made with "any AI audio, video, image creation or editing tools", and the page lists "AI generated music" among the items requiring disclosure. Not requiring disclosure:
  - "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs"
  - voice or audio repair
  - "Caption creation"
  - idea generation
  - production help with an outline, script or thumbnail
  
  A generic synthetic narrator that doesn't clone a real person is not addressed either way. [Platform, Help Center page fetched Oct 2026] Source: [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- Meta says it will "require people to use this disclosure and label tool when they post organic content with a photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered", and "may apply penalties if they fail to do so." The page was published Feb 2024 and modified Apr 2025. [Platform] [OLD] Source: [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/)
- TikTok, via a third-party paraphrase (TikTok's own page could not be fetched):
  - AI-generated or significantly altered realistic visuals and audio depicting people, places or events need a label. AI-assisted text (scripts, captions, overlays) is exempt.
  - TikTok uses C2PA Content Credentials, metadata, invisible watermarks and detection models to auto-label. Detected unlabeled content may be auto-labeled, down-ranked or removed.
  - TTS voiceover is not addressed.
  
  [Vendor opinion, 2026] Source: [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/tiktoks-2026-ai-labeling-rules-and-what-they-signal-for-platform-governance)
- A TikTok monetization guide says AI narration "counts as original audio", but cites no TikTok source. [Vendor opinion, unsourced] Source: [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-2026-requirements/)
- Pace: Guo, Kim and Rubin (MOOC videos) recommend "Bring out natural enthusiasm and speaking fast is okay." [Academic] [OLD] Source: [NUS course slides summarizing the paper](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~kanmy/courses/6101_2016/cs6101-JeffryHartanto-0303.pdf)
- TikTok's on-screen text pacing guidance is 5 to 10 words per second. [Platform] [OLD] Source: [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)

### Inferences
- Keeping an AI narrator is defensible, since quality AI voices now pass blind tests. Use one consistent, energetic "brand voice" and make it faster and less flat. A target of about 160 to 190 wpm is my inference and is unsourced for short-form. The MOOC finding favors fast, enthusiastic delivery, and humor was the one place humans beat AI.
- Labels, the conservative path:
  - Meta: the literal rule text covers realistic-sounding synthetic audio, so apply the AI label to Reels with TTS narration.
  - TikTok: labeling is cheap insurance, since C2PA metadata from TTS or music tools can trigger an auto-label anyway.
  - YouTube: a generic TTS narrator isn't listed as needing disclosure. If the background music is AI-generated, check the "AI generated music" example before skipping disclosure.
  - Trade-off: between 33% (radio test) and 58% (Adobe survey) of people say AI disclosure lowers their trust. Put the "proof" (real formulas, real numbers, the downloadable sheet) at the center, which fits the brand name.
- The combination of AI voice, identical template and high volume is exactly what YouTube's monetization policy calls "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production" (see section 6). Vary the voice delivery and script structure, not just the numbers.

### Gaps
- No method-stated data compares AI and human narration on TikTok, Reels or Shorts outcomes (retention, clicks). The available studies are a radio blind test and consumer surveys.
- No reliable source gives optimal narration words-per-minute for short-form. The exact speaking-rate bins in Guo et al. (2014) could not be verified from accessible copies.
- I couldn't read TikTok's own AI-generated content page. No primary source I read settles whether a generic TTS narrator counts as "realistic" audio that needs a label on TikTok or YouTube.

## 6. Variety: does reusing one visual template hurt reach?

### Takeaway
Reusing a template is not banned, but YouTube's monetization policy now explicitly calls out content that "looks like it's made with a template" and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production". It says each video's substance "should be materially varied." That policy governs monetization; YouTube says nothing about reach. Instagram's 2026 originality crackdown targets reposting other people's content, not a creator reusing their own format. TikTok targets duplicated, low-quality and minimally edited content. The safe pattern is consistent branding (the same intro and outro is explicitly allowed) with substance and visible structure that vary from video to video.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube YPP policy, renamed from "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" on 15 July 2025: "Generic or repetitive content includes content that looks like it's made with a template" and "that may feel repetitive to viewers after watching several videos in a row from the same channel." The substance of each video "should be materially varied." The page covers YouTube Partner Program monetization only and does not mention reach, search or recommendations. [Platform, page fetched Oct 2026] Source: [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
  - Allowed: "Same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different", and series content as long as "each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept."
  - Not allowed: "Similar or repetitive content with low educational value, commentary, narratives, or minimal variation."
  - Not allowed: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value."
  - Not allowed: "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production."
- TeamYouTube (community manager Carlos, 15 July 2025) called it "a minor update to our longstanding guideline on repetitious content." His mass-production examples were multiple narrated stories with only superficial differences and multiple slideshow presentations with identical narration. Consequence: "monetization may be removed from your entire channel." The article adds that YouTube "allows creators to use AI tools in content to enhance their storytelling" without affecting eligibility, while realistic synthetic content still needs disclosure. [Platform via press] [OLD] Source: [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/)
- Instagram, Mosseri, reported 30 Apr 2026: accounts that mostly share content they didn't create, or haven't meaningfully transformed, are no longer recommended to non-followers. The rule extends from Reels to photos and carousels, and accounts are judged on roughly a month of posts. His words: "If most of what you post to Instagram is someone else's content, your account is no longer going to be recommendable." Watermarks, minor crops and basic reposts don't count as original. The article does not address an account reusing its own template. [Platform via press] Source: [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- Further reported details of the April 2026 Instagram rule, citing an Instagram post and Social Media Today: a rolling 30-day window, a 70% visual and audio match threshold, penalties of 24 hours to 30 days, and reduced reach to non-followers only. [Platform via press, secondary; specifics unverified] Source: [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/es/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm)
- TikTok, effective 17 May 2024: accounts that repeatedly post content against the For You feed standards can be made temporarily ineligible for FYF recommendation and harder to find in search. [Platform via press] [OLD] Source: [TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/more-tiktok-community-guidelines-to-take-effect-from-may)
- A vendor summary of TikTok's list of content ineligible for recommendation and rewards includes duplicated or reposted content without new creative editing, a watermark from another app, low-quality content (very short clips, static-image slideshows, GIF-only videos), and videos mainly showing a QR code. The vendor's further claim that templates and reused B-roll are what TikTok's originality check targets is its own analysis, based on creator reports of "unoriginal" disqualifications. [Vendor opinion] Source: [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-2026-requirements/)
- TikTok, for ads: "Make creatives with big differences, especially when testing," and refresh creatives when performance declines. [Platform] [OLD] Source: [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- An Excel creator reported that removing TikTok watermarks from Instagram posts helped her grow to 500K Instagram followers in about two months. [Self-reported] [OLD] Source: [Sellfy](https://sellfy.com/blog/stories/corporate-employee-turned-social-media-star-how-emmas-growth-mindset-and-excel-expertise-led-her-to-business-success/)

### Inferences
- ProofNotFluff's current pattern fits the risk profile YouTube describes, even though the numbers change: same layout, same music, AI voice, automated 1 to 2 a day. "Low educational value ... or minimal variation" and "giving the impression of mass production" are the phrases to design against. The direct risk is to YouTube monetization. Viewer fatigue (the "several videos in a row from the same channel" test) plausibly hurts retention and therefore reach, but no platform says so.
- Rules for the agent:
  - Rotate 4 to 6 distinct formats:
    1. "Looks fine vs real number" reveal.
    2. "Fix it" before and after.
    3. "3 numbers you forgot" list.
    4. "Myth vs math."
    5. A named-trade scenario (dog groomer, wedding photographer, cleaner).
    6. A speed build of the whole sheet in 15 s.
  - Rotate at least 6 to 10 music beds and 2 to 3 accent palettes. Vary the hook type (result-first, question, mistake callout) and the end.
  - Keep one constant brand sting and end card. YouTube explicitly allows the same intro and outro.
  - Every video must explain why the number is what it is (commentary and educational value), not just show inputs and outputs.
  - Test for "enough" variety: a viewer who watches 5 in a row should notice a different structure, not just different numbers. This is my reading of YouTube's own framing; no platform quantifies it.
- Instagram's crackdown targets reposts of others' content, so self-rendered originals are low risk there. Always upload clean renders, never files re-downloaded from another platform with its watermark.

### Gaps
- No platform states whether a creator's own consistent template lowers reach, as distinct from monetization, and none quantifies how much variation is enough.
- I couldn't fetch TikTok's own FYF eligibility wording on unoriginal content. Whether Instagram's reported "70% match" logic could flag an account's own near-identical videos is unknown.

## 7. Calls to action that convert for an Etsy or link-out shop

### Takeaway
Organic short-form is a weak direct-click channel. Shorts have no tappable link in the player, and YouTube's one native link in the Shorts player points only to your own YouTube videos. TikTok bio links reportedly need a business account or 1,000 followers. Instagram and Facebook route clicks through the bio. I found no public, method-stated data on click-through from organic Shorts, Reels or TikToks to an external shop. Two things do have support. YouTube's guidance is to pair a spoken CTA with a visual pointer in the last 5 seconds. Etsy's Share & Save gives a 4% refund on sales from your own tracked links, which also makes bio clicks measurable.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube Shorts "related video" link: it lets you showcase "Videos, Shorts, and Live in the Shorts player", chosen "from your channel", and appears as a clickable link below the channel handle. It requires advanced feature access, and the target must be public or unlisted. External links are not covered. [Platform] Source: [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en)
- YouTube Official Blog, 14 July 2026, on Related Videos links. It describes three Shorts audiences, including viewers who only watch Shorts, and gives no click-through data. [Platform] Source: [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)
  - Match viewer intent: link a quick tip to a detailed tutorial, and a product Short to the full review.
  - "Use a clear call-to-action in the last 5 seconds", pairing a spoken prompt ("Get the full step-by-step breakdown by clicking the link right here.") with a visual cue such as a downward pointer or thumbnail overlay.
  - Make the Short work on its own.
  - The linked long-form video should address the Short's topic within its first 5 to 10 seconds, with no slow intros or logos.
- A link-in-bio vendor says "Shorts don't support tappable links in the player". It says Shorts descriptions "support auto-linked URLs" but sit behind "...more", and it doesn't confirm that pinned-comment URLs are clickable. [Vendor opinion, May 2026] Source: [UniLink](https://www.unilink.us/blog/how-to-add-link-youtube-shorts)
- TikTok bio link: personal accounts need at least 1,000 followers, while business accounts have no minimum. This is a vendor claim with no TikTok citation. [Vendor opinion, updated Sept 2026] Source: [Stan](https://stan.store/blog/tiktok-link-bio-requirements-2026-guide/)
- TikTok: end with a strong CTA that tells viewers what to think, feel or do. [Platform] [OLD] Source: [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Etsy Share & Save, announced 6 Sept 2023. [Trade press] [OLD; confirm current terms] Source: [Value Added Resource](https://valueaddedresource.net/etsy-introduces-share-save-marketing-incentive-for-sellers)
  - Etsy refunds 4% of the order total for orders placed within 30 days of a click on the seller's own trackable link.
  - Qualifying links use the yourshopname.etsy.com format: shop, listing, promo-code, section and review links.
  - Qualifying channels include social media and a link in bio.
  - Attribution is last click. An Offsite Ads last click disqualifies the order. Sellers must choose between Share & Save and Creator Co.
  - Some sellers objected that Etsy shows competing listings to traffic they send.
- Sends per reach is one of the three top Reels signals Mosseri reportedly named (Jan 2025). [Platform via press, secondary] [OLD] Source: [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/es/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm)
- Template seller TheDesignTemplateCo drove Etsy traffic from Instagram, Pinterest and TikTok with lead magnets, free tips, free templates, promo codes and content "showing the value of digital downloads." [Self-reported] [OLD] Source: [Alura](https://www.alura.io/interview/thedesigntemplateco)
- An Excel creator selling eBooks said coupon codes "worked well." [Self-reported] [OLD] Source: [Sellfy](https://sellfy.com/blog/stories/corporate-employee-turned-social-media-star-how-emmas-growth-mindset-and-excel-expertise-led-her-to-business-success/)

### Inferences
CTA stack for the agent. This is inference; none of it is backed by click data.
1. Put a soft CTA at the reveal, where attention is highest, not only on the end card: "This calculator is in my Etsy shop." Many viewers swipe before the last seconds.
2. Final 3 to 5 s: say the CTA and show it on screen at the same time, with a visual pointer (YouTube's guidance). Show the exact search phrase or listing name, not just the shop name: "Etsy: ProofNotFluff break-even calculator". A generic phrase pulls up competitors, and sellers report that Etsy shows competing listings even to traffic they send.
3. Bio links on TikTok (switch to a business account if under 1,000 followers), Instagram and Facebook should be Share & Save trackable links, ideally to the listing the video shows. This earns the 4% refund and measures clicks. Rotate the bio link to the product featured that day, or to a shop section.
4. On YouTube, give each Short a related-video link to a 2 to 5 minute walkthrough of the same calculator, uploaded as a regular video. The Etsy link goes in that video's description, which keeps the click path native to YouTube. This is the only in-player link YouTube offers on Shorts. Clickability of long-form description links was not re-verified in this session.
5. On every platform, pin a comment with the exact product name and "link in bio".
6. Put a short promo code on the end card (for example, a 10% code) so sales from viewers who search instead of clicking can be attributed.
7. On Reels, sometimes swap the shop CTA for a share prompt ("send this to a friend who charges $25 an hour"), since sends per reach is a named ranking signal.
8. Consider a free mini-check (for example, "find your real hourly rate") as a lead magnet behind the bio link. Template sellers report free-first funnels working (see section 8).

### Gaps
- No method-stated public data on click-through from organic Shorts, Reels or TikToks to an external store, and no test of "search X on Etsy" against "link in bio".
- Whether Shorts description links and pinned-comment links are clickable in Oct 2026 is contested. One vendor says description URLs auto-link. YouTube was widely reported to have disabled clickable links in Shorts descriptions and comments in 2023, but I could not re-verify that or any later reversal from a primary source in this session.
- Facebook Reels link options were not researched. Etsy Share & Save terms may have changed since 2023.

## 8. Case studies: spreadsheet, Excel-tips, template and finance-tool accounts

### Takeaway
The documented success stories (Miss Excel, an Excel creator called "Emma" in a Sellfy case study, Easlo's Notion templates) are from 2020 to 2021, self-reported, and either face or personality led or driven by text platforms. None is a faceless, auto-generated calculator channel. What they share: one practical tip per video, entertainment layered on real teaching, a high and steady posting cadence, and an owned conversion path (courses, eBooks, coupon codes, email lists), not a "search my shop" CTA.

### Cited Findings
- Miss Excel (Kat Norton). [Self-reported, secondary; underlying events OLD; article Mar 2026, updated July 2026] Source: [Built by Foundry](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/kat-norton-miss-excel-tiktok-creator-business)
  - Started posting in June 2020 while working in corporate sales.
  - Videos ran about 60 seconds, one actionable Excel tip each. The hook was dancing while teaching (for example, pivot tables to a dance trend), with upbeat, on-trend audio. Viewers' comments often said "Wait, I actually learned something."
  - Her first viral video reached millions of views in days. She had about 1.3 million TikTok followers by mid-2021.
  - Business Insider (Dec 2021) reported over $1 million in first-year course revenue. Course bundles cost $197 to $497, plus LinkedIn Learning and brand sponsorships.
- "Emma" (Excel creator, Sellfy case study, apparently 2021). [Self-reported] [OLD] Source: [Sellfy](https://sellfy.com/blog/stories/corporate-employee-turned-social-media-star-how-emmas-growth-mindset-and-excel-expertise-led-her-to-business-success/)
  - Short videos teaching Excel formulas and tricks on TikTok and Instagram, posted "at least three times a week on TikTok and four on Instagram."
  - Almost 2 million TikTok and half a million Instagram followers in about six months.
  - Sold Excel Dictionary eBooks and templates on her Sellfy store. No revenue figures.
  - Growth drivers she named: posting consistently, following TikTok trends and popular songs, sizing videos correctly for Instagram, and removing TikTok watermarks from Instagram posts. She said coupon codes worked well.
- Easlo (Jason Ruiyi Chin), Notion templates including budget trackers. [Press-verified revenue] [OLD] Source: [Yahoo Money / Insider](https://money.yahoo.com/made-239-000-selling-budgeting-120626735.html)
  - $239,000 in template sales in a year, verified by Insider. His $130 Second Brain template brought in over $100,000.
  - Gave templates away free in summer 2021, then started charging in Nov 2021.
  - Channels: Twitter (211,000+ followers), Product Hunt, and email to free-template downloaders. He never paid for ads. No short-form video.
- TheDesignTemplateCo (Etsy marketing templates): $500 to $1,600 a month, 6% conversion rate, 1,500+ sales. Promotion ran on Instagram, Pinterest and TikTok, using free tips and templates and promo codes. [Self-reported] [OLD] Source: [Alura](https://www.alura.io/interview/thedesigntemplateco)

### Inferences
- None of these accounts relied on silent, auto-generated screen captures. Each had a human hook (dance, personality) or a text-platform audience feeding a free-to-paid funnel. A faceless account has to supply the "personality" through:
  - writing: sharp, specific money claims about named trades;
  - visual craft: motion, punch-ins, reveals;
  - a consistent narrator persona.
- A free-first funnel (Easlo, TheDesignTemplateCo) is the documented conversion pattern for template sellers. A free mini-calculator behind a Share & Save bio link plus email capture would mirror it.
- Posting cadence in these cases was 3 to 7 a week per platform, so ProofNotFluff's 1 to 2 a day is already higher. The binding constraints are retention and variety, not volume.

### Gaps
- I found no verified 2025 to 2026 case study of a faceless Excel-tips or spreadsheet-calculator account on Shorts or TikTok with stated formats, lengths and results. Searches surfaced only vendor "faceless channel" guides without data.
- The "$250k selling spreadsheets on Etsy in under 2 years" case study on Side Hustle Nation was behind a bot wall and could not be read.
- No data on what specifically changed results for faceless template sellers.
