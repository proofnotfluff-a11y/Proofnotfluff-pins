# Platform Ranking Signals: How Reels, Shorts and TikTok Pick Videos for Non-Followers (2025 to 2026), and What a 0-Follower Faceless Account Needs

Research date: Oct 15, 2026. Labels used below: [OFFICIAL] means a platform or executive statement, read in a primary source or quoted by reputable press. [3P-DATA] means a dated third-party dataset. [3P-CLAIM] means a secondary source's attribution that I could not check against a primary source. [OLDER] means published before 2025.

## 1. What Instagram and Adam Mosseri officially said in 2025 and 2026 about Reels ranking signals

### Takeaway
Mosseri's official 2025 list of top Reels signals is **watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach**. Sends (DM shares) count slightly more when the viewer does not follow the account, which is exactly ProofNotFluff's situation. Instagram says the first few seconds are "the most important", hashtags have little effect on reach (and are capped at 5 since Dec 2025), and gaps in posting can hurt. Trial Reels are the official way to test content on non-followers only.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Jan 22, 2025, Mosseri ranking video series: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends." He told creators to "pay close attention to average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach." On audiences: "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." Comments were not on his list. — [Social Media Today, Jan 22, 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- [3P-CLAIM] In the same Jan 2025 series, Mosseri reportedly said Reels longer than 3 minutes will not be recommended to non-followers. The page gives no primary link, so this is unverified. — [Hanami Social](https://hanamisocial.com/en/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026/)
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary] Apr 14, 2025: Mosseri named shares (weighted most), watch time and watch rate as the key metrics. He said hashtags now have little effect on reach, and that reach can drop after a long gap in posting because the system lacks recent data on the account. This comes from a translated Taiwanese summary, not the original video. — [Inside.com.tw, Apr 14, 2025](https://www.inside.com.tw/article/38141-instagram-head-adam-mosseri-reveals-three-key-metrics-and-two-pitfalls-for-2025-strategy)
- [OFFICIAL] Jan 29, 2025: Instagram added Reels insights including "View Rate", the share of viewers who keep watching past the first 3 seconds, split between followers and non-followers. Instagram said "the first few seconds of reels are the most important." The article does not use the term "skip rate". — [TechCrunch, Jan 29, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/29/instagram-gives-creators-more-insight-into-their-reels-performance)
- [OFFICIAL] Dec 18, 2025: hashtags are capped at five per post. Instagram's guidance is "Using fewer (up to 5) more targeted hashtags." — [Social Media Today, via Keywords Everywhere tracker](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)
- [OFFICIAL] Jul 10, 2025: public professional-account content became indexable by Google by default. The setting reads "Allow public photos and videos to appear in search engine results." Since Jul 29, 2026, Google Search Console reports Instagram query data. Captions and on-screen keywords therefore now matter for Google search too. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/instagram-content-becomes-searchable-on-google-starting-july-10/); [Google Search Central, Jul 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/07/platform-properties-social-video-guide)
- [OFFICIAL] Apr 8, 2025: Mosseri said in-app search "is not very good on Instagram" and promised investment. — [Keywords Everywhere tracker](https://keywordseverywhere.com/news/instagram-algorithm-updates/)
- [OFFICIAL] Sep 24, 2025: on reaching 3 billion users, Instagram said "almost all of our growth has been driven by DMs, Reels, and recommendations." — [TechCrunch, Sep 24, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/24/instagram-now-has-3-billion-monthly-active-users-will-test-features-to-help-users-control-their-feeds/)
- [OFFICIAL] Dec 10, 2025: "Your Algorithm" lets users view and edit the interest topics behind their Reels recommendations. It expanded to Explore on Apr 16, 2026 and to the main Feed on Jun 10, 2026. Topic and keyword clarity therefore feed user-controlled interest matching. — [about.instagram.com](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/reels-algorithm-control); [TechCrunch, Dec 10, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/10/instagrams-new-your-algorithm-tool-gives-you-more-control-over-the-reels-you-see/); [Social Media Today, Jun 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-extends-your-algorithm-to-the-main-feed/822576/)
- [OFFICIAL, OLDER, Dec 10, 2024] Trial Reels: "Trial reels will be shown to non-followers first." Followers do not see them in Feed or the Reels tab. Metrics (views, likes, comments, shares, comparison with earlier trials) arrive after about 24 hours. A trial can be set to share with followers automatically if it does well on views in the first 72 hours. The page sends readers to the Help Center for eligibility and gives no follower minimum. — [Instagram for Creators, Dec 10, 2024](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels); [TechCrunch, Dec 10, 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/10/instagram-rolls-out-trial-reels-that-arent-shown-to-a-creators-followers)
- [OFFICIAL, OLDER, May 31, 2023] For recommendations, Instagram looks at how many people engage with a post (likes, comments, shares, saves) and how quickly, the viewer's history with the poster, and the viewer's other liked or commented posts. For Search, it advises using relevant keywords in content, captions, bio and hashtags. — [Instagram for Creators, "Tips for improving your reach"](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/tips-for-improving-your-reach)
- [OFFICIAL, undated] Instagram's ranking explainer says each surface has its own ranking system. For Reels it predicts reshares, completion, likes and audio-page visits. Popularity matters "much more" in Explore. — [about.instagram.com "Instagram ranking explained"](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained), as summarized by [Hanami Social](https://hanamisocial.com/en/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026/)
- [OFFICIAL] Sep 15, 2026: Meta One subscribers can add clickable links to organic posts and Reels, capped monthly by plan. No source says whether posts with links rank differently. — [Meta Newsroom, Sep 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-meta-one-subscription-service-more-features-ai/)

### Inferences
- An account with zero followers gets only "unconnected" distribution, so **sends per reach and watch time/completion are the levers that count**. Likes matter less there. A spreadsheet demo needs a reason to DM it to someone ("send this to your friend who undercharges").
- The 3-second View Rate metric shows Instagram measures early abandonment directly. A templated opening that looks the same every day will likely lose viewers quickly once the format is familiar.
- Trial Reels suit a brand-new account well: they are built to test formats on strangers.

### Gaps
- I found no official 2025 or 2026 Instagram statement on **saves** as a Reels ranking signal (as distinct from sends) or on **carousels vs Reels** for non-follower reach. The Jan 2025 Social Media Today piece guesses carousels rank well because they hold attention, but that is the author's guess.
- I could not confirm from a primary source the claim about Reels over 3 minutes. It is irrelevant for short demos anyway.
- I found no official statement on **posting frequency** beyond the reported "consistency/gaps" remark (Apr 2025, secondary).
- I could not confirm Trial Reels eligibility (professional account? follower minimum?) from the Help Center.

## 2. Instagram's originality rules, unoriginal/aggregator demotion, watermarks, cross-posting, templated or AI content, and Meta's AI labels

### Takeaway
Since Apr 30, 2026, accounts that "primarily" post unoriginal content (re-uploads of others' work) **lose recommendation eligibility on photos, carousels and Reels**. They keep reach to followers. Account Status shows whether this has happened. ProofNotFluff renders its own videos, so they should count as original under Instagram's definition. Instagram's long-standing rule is that it is "less likely" to recommend reposts and content with "noticeable watermarks", which matters for any clip carrying a TikTok watermark. I found no Instagram rule that explicitly demotes templated or mass-produced self-made content. Since Aug 31, 2026, an AI label is required only for profiles featuring an **AI-generated person**. Using AI tools in the creative process needs no label.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Apr 30, 2026: "Original content is work that you wholly created or reflects your unique perspective." Examples: footage you shot, content you designed, and third-party content you materially edited. Adding a watermark, border, subtitles or a caption credit does not make content original. Accounts that primarily post unoriginal content "will no longer be shown in places where we recommend content." Accounts become eligible again once most of their posts over a rolling 30 days are original. Creators can remove flagged content or appeal in Account Status. "This update does not impact how we show people content from accounts they follow." — [Instagram for Creators, "Rewarding original creators", Apr 30, 2026](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram/); [TechCrunch, Apr 30, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/instagram-restricts-reach-of-content-aggregators-in-new-crackdown/)
- [OFFICIAL] Apr 30, 2026, Instagram as quoted by Social Media Today: "Content that uses third-party material is fine as long as the poster adds something original." Its test is to ask whether the edits "add real value beyond just simply restating or referencing the third-party content." Publishers with licensing agreements are exempt. — [Social Media Today, Apr 30, 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-algorithm-to-benefit-original-creators/819016/)
- [OFFICIAL, OLDER, May 31, 2023] Instagram is "less likely to recommend reposts of a reel that's already on Instagram" and content with "noticeable watermarks". It suggests Remix as the way to build on others' reels. — [Instagram for Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/tips-for-improving-your-reach)
- [OFFICIAL, undated, via secondary] Instagram lists quality filters that reduce visibility: low resolution, watermarks, borders, muted audio, excessive text and reposted content. — [Hanami Social](https://hanamisocial.com/en/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026/), citing [about.instagram.com ranking explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)
- [OFFICIAL] Dec 31, 2025, Mosseri year-end memo: "authenticity is becoming infinitely reproducible." Also: "in a world where AI can generate flawless imagery, the professional look becomes the tell", "Rawness isn't just aesthetic preference anymore—it's proof", and "The primary way people share now is in DMs." His listed priorities: "Label AI-generated content and verify authentic content", "Surface credibility signals about who's posting", and "Continue to improve ranking for originality." — [Om Malik, Jan 1, 2026](https://om.co/2026/01/01/what-is-instagrams-adam-mosseri-really-saying-in-his-year-end-memo/); [Music Ally, Jan 5, 2026](https://musically.com/2026/01/05/instagram-boss-authenticity-is-becoming-infinitely-reproducible/)
- [OFFICIAL] Aug 31, 2026: Instagram will "limit the reach of profiles that feature an AI-generated person and do not add this label." It also says "Creators who simply use AI tools as part of their creative process don't need to add the label." Post-level AI labels take precedence. Detected profiles are notified and can see recommendation eligibility in Account Status. Synthetic voices are not mentioned. — [Instagram for Creators, Aug 31, 2026](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/ai-generated-profile-label/)
- [OFFICIAL] Aug 6, 2025: the native Reposts feature launched, and "Reposts will be recommended to your friends' and followers' feeds." This is the sanctioned way to share others' content. — [Meta Newsroom, Aug 2025](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/08/new-instagram-features-help-you-connect/)

### Inferences
- ProofNotFluff's code-rendered demos are "content you designed", so the account should not be classed as an aggregator. **Check Settings > Account Status** to confirm, since it is the official place a recommendation restriction would appear.
- Cross-posting the same file is not penalized as such in any Instagram text I found. The risk is **watermarks**: if the API pipeline uploads a TikTok-downloaded file with the TikTok logo, Instagram's long-standing watermark rule applies. Upload clean masters to each platform.
- No text explicitly penalizes "same template daily". Mosseri's Dec 2025 memo signals that polished, synthetic-looking content is losing favor with viewers, and probably with ranking. That is a direction, not a stated rule.
- A TTS voiceover that does not imitate a real person and has no AI-generated human on screen does not trigger the Aug 2026 profile-label rule.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Meta's current post-level "AI info" labeling policy (when synthetic audio or video must be self-disclosed on Reels). I found no 2025 or 2026 primary source stating whether a generic TTS voiceover requires the AI label. Verify in the Instagram Help Center.
- I found no official Instagram statement on templated, repetitive or mass-produced self-created content, nor any statement on posting identical videos to TikTok or Shorts beyond the watermark rule.
- Instagram's "primarily unoriginal" rule has no published numeric threshold. Secondary sources describe it as "most posts over 30 days".

## 3. YouTube Shorts: recommendation mechanics, view counting, the inauthentic content policy, and synthetic disclosure

### Takeaway
Since Mar 31, 2025, any start or replay counts as a Shorts "view". The older, stricter metric was renamed **engaged views**, and monetization and YPP eligibility still run on engaged views. YouTube's Jul 15, 2025 "inauthentic content" update (formerly "repetitious content") is officially a **monetization (YPP) rule**. Its own examples include "slideshows that all have the same narration" and stories with "only superficial differences". I found no official statement that it suppresses distribution. Shorts are tested first on a small "seed audience". YouTube Analytics' "Viewed vs. swiped away" is the main diagnostic.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Announced Mar 26, 2025, effective Mar 31, 2025: Shorts views now count every time a Short starts playing or replays. The previous metric remains in Advanced Mode as "engaged views", showing "how many viewers choose to continue watching your Shorts". YouTube said earnings and YPP eligibility still rest on engaged views. Announced by TeamYouTube in the Help Community. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/); [Digital Music News, Mar 27, 2025](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/03/27/how-does-youtube-shorts-count-views-2025/)
- [OFFICIAL] Jul 2025 policy text: "On July 15, 2025, YouTube is updating our guidelines to better identify mass-produced and repetitious content. This update better reflects what 'inauthentic' content looks like today." — [Search Engine Journal, Jul 2, 2025](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-targets-mass-produced-content-in-monetization-update/550337/)
- [OFFICIAL] Examples in scope: "Channels that upload narrative stories with only superficial differences between them" and "channels that upload slideshows that all have the same narration." YouTube said "channels that use AI in their content remain eligible for monetization" and creators "are required to disclose when their realistic content is altered or synthetic." YouTube Insider called it a "minor update". Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie said re-used content stays monetizable with "significant original commentary, modifications". — [Social Media Today, Jul 13, 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)
- [OFFICIAL, paraphrased] YouTube Shorts product lead Todd Sherman said on Creator Insider (date not given) that Shorts are first shown to a small, targeted "seed audience" before wider promotion, because most Shorts views come from the feed rather than deliberate choice. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/), citing [Creator Insider video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3jsYK_-aRU&t=188s)
- [3P-CLAIM] YouTube Studio > Analytics > Content > Shorts shows a "Viewed vs. swiped away" percentage. vidIQ cites Nate Black's research that channels with 200 or more Shorts tended to see steady view growth. That is not official. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [OFFICIAL, OLDER, Mar 18, 2024] Creators must disclose realistic altered or synthetic content a viewer could mistake for real. Examples include "Generating a person's voice to narrate a video" (in context, a real person's voice), face swaps and altered real events. No disclosure is needed for scripts, ideas, auto captions, clearly unrealistic or animated content, or filters. YouTube may apply labels itself, and sensitive topics including **finance** get a more prominent on-video label. YouTube describes no effect on reach. — [Google blog, Mar 18, 2024](https://blog.google/intl/en-africa/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/how-were-helping-creators-disclose-altered-or-synthetic-content/)

### Inferences
- The early 700 to 1,000 views on Shorts versus fewer than 10 now fits seed-audience testing: early Shorts were tested, swipe-away and retention data came back weak, and later Shorts in the same template got smaller or no tests. This is my inference, not a YouTube statement. The "Viewed vs. swiped away" metric on those early Shorts is the place to check it.
- "Slideshows that all have the same narration" is close to a daily same-template spreadsheet demo with TTS. Officially that affects monetization only. ProofNotFluff is not in YPP, so the direct policy risk is low, but varying script, hook and visual structure is prudent.
- Code-rendered spreadsheet demos with a generic TTS voice are not "realistic synthetic content of a real person", so the disclosure rule likely does not apply. Pricing and budgeting is arguably a finance-adjacent topic.

### Gaps
- I found **no official YouTube statement** that templated or TTS Shorts are suppressed in recommendations, as opposed to demonetized. Any such claim is third-party or folklore.
- I could not find an official date or verbatim quote for the Sherman seed-audience interview.
- I found no official YouTube statement on cross-posted Shorts carrying TikTok watermarks.

## 4. TikTok: For You ranking factors, unoriginal and AI content rules, and how new accounts get first views

### Takeaway
TikTok's official ranking explainer (2020, still its canonical statement) says **follower count and past hits are not direct factors**. Ranking uses user interactions (strong signals such as finishing a video weigh more), video information (captions, sounds, hashtags) and lower-weighted device and settings data. Duplicated content and spam are not recommended. Under the For You feed (FYF) Eligibility Standards (community guidelines effective Sep 13, 2025, per a third-party summary), unoriginal reposts and other-platform watermarks make content FYF-ineligible. Realistic AI content must carry the AIGC label.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, OLDER, Jun 18, 2020] Factors: user interactions (likes, shares, follows, comments, watching to completion); "Video information, which might include details like captions, sounds, and hashtags"; device and account settings, which "receive lower weight". A strong indicator "would receive greater weight than a weak indicator". "neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors." Videos that have "just been uploaded or are under review" may be ineligible. TikTok does not recommend "duplicated content" or spam. — [TikTok Newsroom, Jun 18, 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/)
- [OFFICIAL via 3P summary] Community guidelines effective Sep 13, 2025: reposted content without creative edits is not FYF-eligible. The named example is clips showing another platform's watermark or logo, and the consequence is reduced distribution, not removal. FYF ineligibility can be appealed in the in-app Safety Center. The summary says TikTok has no per-video screen that flags a post as FYF-ineligible. — [ContentIQ summary](https://contentiq.media/rules/tiktok) of [TikTok FYF Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards) and [Enforcement](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/enforcement). TikTok pages could not be fetched (robots), so verify the wording there.
- [OFFICIAL via 3P summary] Realistic AI-generated or significantly edited content showing people or scenes must be labeled, with the AIGC label or a clear caption or sticker. AI audio that mimics a real person's voice requires disclosure. Unlabeled realistic AI content may be removed, restricted or labeled by TikTok. — [ContentIQ](https://contentiq.media/rules/tiktok), citing [TikTok Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- [OFFICIAL via 3P summary] Creator Rewards: "content ineligible for the For You feed will not be eligible for the Creator Rewards Program." Unoriginal content is defined as content "copied from others or has minimal original input or edits." — [ContentIQ](https://contentiq.media/rules/tiktok), citing [TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/creator-rewards-program)
- [OFFICIAL] Nov 18, 2025: TikTok lets users choose how much AI-generated content they see in For You (via Manage Topics). — [TechCrunch, Nov 18, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/18/tiktok-now-lets-you-choose-how-much-ai-generated-content-you-want-to-see/)

### Inferences
- New accounts get first views on merit and are tested on small audiences. The 2020 explainer implies a new account is not penalized for having 0 followers. Uploads must first pass review, and duplicates of content already on TikTok are filtered.
- The same file cross-posted with a watermark from another platform (for example, a Reels or Shorts export) is FYF-ineligible. Each platform should get the clean master render.
- Lowering the AI dial reduces exposure only for content TikTok has labeled as AI. A code-rendered spreadsheet with TTS is likely not "realistic AI" and so not label-required. Unverified.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch TikTok's FYF Standards or Creator Academy pages directly (robots-blocked), so all 2025 TikTok rule wording here comes through a third-party summary.
- I found no 2025 or 2026 official TikTok explanation of how new accounts get their first views, beyond the 2020 explainer.
- I found no official TikTok statement on TTS voiceovers or templated series specifically.

## 5. Posting frequency, consistency and the "niche signal" for new accounts

### Takeaway
Instagram: Buffer's 2025 dataset (about 2.1M posts) finds more frequent posting does **not** lower reach per post, and reach per post rises with frequency, with diminishing returns past 3 to 5 posts a week. Mosseri reportedly warned that gaps in posting hurt. I found no official platform statement on a "niche signal". The closest official mechanisms are interest-topic matching (Instagram's "Your Algorithm" topics) and video metadata (TikTok captions, sounds, hashtags).

### Cited Findings
- [3P-DATA] Aug 13, 2025, Buffer, about 2.1M feed posts from about 102k Instagram accounts. Follower growth per week rises with frequency: 1 to 2 posts +0.12%, 3 to 5 +0.26%, 6 to 9 +0.44%, 10+ +0.66%. Reach per post versus 1 to 2 posts a week: 3 to 5 about +12%, 6 to 9 about +18%, 10+ about +24%. Weeks without posts averaged about 0.08 standard deviations below an account's normal growth. Reels, photos and carousels were not separated. — [Buffer, Aug 13, 2025](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary] Apr 2025: Mosseri said reach may drop after long gaps between posts and that consistency matters. — [Inside.com.tw, Apr 14, 2025](https://www.inside.com.tw/article/38141-instagram-head-adam-mosseri-reveals-three-key-metrics-and-two-pitfalls-for-2025-strategy)
- [OFFICIAL] Dec 2025 to Jun 2026: Instagram's "Your Algorithm" shows users the interest topics behind their recommendations and lets them edit them. Content is matched to topics. — [about.instagram.com](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/reels-algorithm-control)
- [OFFICIAL, OLDER, 2020] TikTok uses captions, sounds and hashtags as "video information" signals. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/)
- [3P-CLAIM] Nate Black: Shorts channels with 200 or more Shorts tended to see steady growth. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)

### Inferences
- ProofNotFluff posted 21 Reels in 12 days, already above Buffer's top band, with 0 engagement. Frequency is not the bottleneck. Per-video watch time, send-worthiness and the opening seconds are.
- The "niche signal" is folklore in the sense that no platform names it. The officially documented equivalent is consistent topic metadata (captions, keywords, on-screen text) matching viewer interest topics.

### Gaps
- I found no official 2025 or 2026 posting-frequency guidance from TikTok or YouTube. I did not retrieve 2025 or 2026 frequency studies from Hootsuite, Later, Socialinsider or Metricool. Buffer's 2026 "creator growth playbook" appeared in search results but was not read.
- I found no official statement on whether a new account is "classified" into a niche after N posts. This is creator folklore.

## 6. Why a new account gets near-zero views: shadow limits, recommendation eligibility and account status checks

### Takeaway
The official diagnostics are **Instagram Settings > Account Status** (shows whether the account or content is ineligible for recommendation to non-followers, with appeal), **TikTok's Safety Center** (FYF-ineligibility appeals), and **YouTube Analytics "Viewed vs. swiped away"**. Instagram has confirmed that ineligible content still reaches followers but not non-followers. For an account with 0 followers, that means near-zero views.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, OLDER, Dec 7, 2022] Instagram launched Account Status checks showing whether an account's content is eligible to be recommended to non-followers. — [TechCrunch, Dec 7, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/07/instagrams-new-transparency-tools-will-tell-you-if-your-content-is-ineligible-to-be-recommended/)
- [OFFICIAL, OLDER, May 31, 2023] Content that goes against the Recommendation Guidelines can still reach followers, but Instagram tries not to recommend it to non-followers. Account Status lets creators see violating content or profile elements, edit or delete them, or request review. — [Instagram for Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/tips-for-improving-your-reach)
- [OFFICIAL] Apr 30, 2026: aggregator restrictions appear in Account Status and can be appealed there. Eligibility returns once most posts over a rolling 30 days are original. — [Instagram for Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram/)
- [OFFICIAL] Aug 31, 2026: detected unlabeled AI-person profiles are notified and lose recommendation eligibility, visible in Account Status. — [Instagram for Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/ai-generated-profile-label/)
- [OFFICIAL via 3P summary] TikTok: FYF ineligibility is an enforcement action that can be appealed in the in-app Safety Center. New uploads "under review" may be ineligible. — [ContentIQ](https://contentiq.media/rules/tiktok); [TikTok Newsroom, 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/)
- [OFFICIAL] Mar 2025: YouTube's engaged views versus views split, plus the "viewed vs. swiped away" analytic, separate auto-plays from real watching. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/)

### Inferences
- **0 likes across 21 Reels is unusual even for a new account**, because Reels with zero followers normally still get some non-follower test impressions. The first step is to check Instagram Account Status > "Content you posted that isn't eligible to be recommended" (or equivalent). Then check Reels insights for non-follower views and the 3-second View Rate. If views are near 0, suspect a recommendation-eligibility issue. If views exist but View Rate and watch time are poor, it is a content and hook problem.
- Likely contributors, inferred from the official rules above: watermarks or cross-platform logos on uploads; sameness of the template, which makes later videos lose viewers early as the platform's tests return weak results; and API-posted content with no follow-up behavior. That last one is unverified: I found no official statement that API-posted Reels rank differently.

### Gaps
- I found no official 2025 or 2026 statement from any platform that content posted through third-party APIs or schedulers is ranked differently. Claims that it is are folklore.
- "Shadowban" is not an official term on any of the three platforms. The official equivalents are recommendation or FYF ineligibility. I found no evidence of hidden limits beyond these.
- I found no official cold-start numbers, such as how many seed impressions a new account's video gets on any platform.
