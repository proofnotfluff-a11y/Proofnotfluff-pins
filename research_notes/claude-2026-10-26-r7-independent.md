## 3. Our read: why $149 does not sell yet
1. Wrong unit of value. Rivals sell a meeting, a walkthrough or a quote sent; we sell a list plus homework. The buyer has to find the contact, pitch a builder cold and wait. Our page even promises we never contact builders.
2. No risk reversal on the seats. Rivals' standard is "you pay only for what qualifies" or "replaced or refunded within N days".
3. No trust. Unknown faceless brand, no identity line, no proof, no sample row with a contact.
4. Thin "why now" on cleaning: 97% of rows are mid-construction, not near-finish.
5. Price is not the blocker. RatRadar's $299 and $110 to $175 per appointment say buyers pay more than $149 when the value is clear.

## 4. Taking it to the next level (proposals; all need Todd, most need the Legal desk)
1. From list to bid desk (the big one). Sell the outcome Construction Clean Partners sells, faceless and by email: each week we write to the builder's office on near-finish projects in a held area and ask whether the final clean is open for quotes; a yes goes to the seat holder as a live bid invitation. Our edge over CCP: we already hold every Chicago job with the builder, weekly, and the builder office contact where its site lists one. Smallest $0 test, before any buyer: a GC-side permission email (cold email rules: named decision-maker, downstream stage, asks permission, no link) to builders on 20 near-finish projects asking if the final clean is open for quotes. If 2 or more say yes, the cleaner email changes from "here is a list" to "a builder is taking final clean quotes on this project; want it?", the strongest true email we can write. Needs: a new template (Todd's yes), the Legal desk, and a change to the "we never contact builders" FAQ line (major). Wording must not imply we already have crews.

3. Show, do not describe: one real near-finish row on the seat section with the builder's office contact as published on its own site (Legal desk check first), and the job math (sq ft range times $0.15 to $0.75 against $149).
