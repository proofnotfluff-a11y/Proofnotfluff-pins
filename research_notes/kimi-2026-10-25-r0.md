## What holds up (sources opened Oct 25)
- What sells to these trades is exclusive, already-interested demand, priced per lead: post-construction and restaurant cleaning leads at $200 each, qualified by a person and exclusive per radius (prospectrdigital.com, two pages); pest control inbound calls at $24.99 (organic) to $65 to $152 (Google Ads) each, 100% exclusive (99calls.com).
- Permit-based construction lead reports already sell from $25 to $67 a month (constructionleadreports.com), so $29 is not cheap against the same model.
- New business lists sell as one-time pulls from $99.75 (leadsplease.com, with a 10% subscribe option) and as emailed feeds with $500 to $1,100 minimums (dmdatabases.com), so the buyer type pays for this data; $29 a month is low against them.
- Shared leads are the common complaint: one lead sold to 3 or 4 companies (community.windowcleaner.com thread, Apr 2025; getjobber.com on Angi).
- Pest firms are reached through NPMA's supplier programs and PestWorld; cleaning firms through ISSA's show and bid sites (npmapestworld.org, issashow.com, getjobber.com).

## What does not hold up
- Two of four changes Kimi proposed (a free trial for the construction list) already exist: the Oct 22 funnel has free Monday emails and a 1-week trial. Our brief left that out; it now says it.
- QuoteIQ Atlas is a business map inside field-service software, not a list of restaurants with hood violations; it is not a direct rival to the restaurant list.
- Buyer voices are thin: one residential window cleaning thread, the rest seller pages. Step 3 (the trade's own words on buying leads) mostly failed.
- Every diagnosis is Kimi's inference at low or medium confidence.
