# Kimi research, rounds R0 to R4 (Oct 25 to 26, 2026): what we learned

Every source was reopened by us before use. R0 kept (15 of 18 held), R1 set aside (7 of 14 did not say what Kimi said), R2 kept (19 of 20 held), R3 kept (5 of 7 held, 2 City queries our fetcher cannot reach; our own license pull agrees with its count). Detail: research_notes/kimi-2026-10-25-r0.md, -r1.md, -r2.md.

1. Buyers pay for leads; they refuse shared ones. Exclusive, already-interested leads sell at $25 to $200 each, $110 per cleaning appointment; the top complaint everywhere is one lead sold to several rivals.
2. Raw public data is a commodity. Permit search from $0 to $599 a month (full search at $29), Chicago new licenses on Apify at $2 per 1,000 rows. Our lists sit in this tier.
3. The one small verified win sold by phone and text, not email: a lead-alert service reached $6,128 a month at about $219 each and sold for $150,000 (microns.io, May 2026), every customer won by cold calls and texts with a 7-day trial.
4. Exclusivity has a working price model: MorningSheet (Washington, live beta, no traction shown) sells new-business openings to vendors at $79 shared, $349 for a 7-day exclusive head start per county and line, and $899 to own a county; its buyer lines include signage and post-construction cleaning.
5. Cook County and Illinois white space is thin: of 12 datasets R3 checked, 11 failed on volume, cadence, terms or an existing rival (Illinois liquor licenses already sell at $129 a state). The one survivor is a slice of our own Chicago new business licenses data: storefront sign leads, one sign company per area. Only 5 areas clear 10 new businesses a month and the Illinois Sign Association lists about 46 Illinois shops, so the ceiling is small (about 5 seats).
6. R4 (Oct 26, records locked in PDFs, agendas and free text): kept, 4 of 5 sources hold. Chicago agendas are thin or mix in private homes; meeting, zoning and contract monitoring is already sold (ZoneWire, Civic IQ, PermitCore). Its one idea, Texas commercial stormwater leads for SWPPP inspectors (about 112 new commercial approvals a month, only Collin and Harris counties clear 10), is parked: the State's search page blocks automated collection in its robots.txt, so it fails our terms rule; the clean route is EPA's national ICIS-NPDES download, which a $5 per 1,000 row scraper already resells; and the ceiling is about 2 seats.
7. Not done: Illinois EPA stormwater notices and state hazmat incidents (no count yet).

## Verdict across R0 to R4
More data products are not where the money is. Every round says the same thing: buyers pay for exclusive, ready-to-act leads, and we sell shared lists by cold email. The next test is the offer, not the dataset.

Proposed (Todd's call, scoreboard decide-exclusive-area): test an exclusive version of a list we already build, on the MorningSheet pattern, before building new data products.
