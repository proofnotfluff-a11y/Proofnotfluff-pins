# Offers lens: done-for-you cash forecast for small online brands

**Score: 6 / 10. Vote: FUND IF.**

## Assumptions the offer rests on
1. Founders at roughly $1M to $10M with no finance lead feel cash pain enough to pay now.
2. Enough of the 15 warm contacts are in that band and not excluded.
3. The owner's record makes buyers believe the forecast will be right, with no client result to show.
4. Clients send clean exports on time, every month.
5. AI holds owner hours to the estimates (to check).
6. "Three decisions" stays clear of the advice the offer excludes.

## Value scores, before fixes (10 = best for the buyer)
- Dream outcome: 7. Seeing the cash low point 13 weeks out is real relief, at far below fractional CFO pricing.
- Belief: 3. Strong record, but zero payers, no case study, no sample, faceless brand.
- Time to result: 8. Five business days is fast and concrete.
- Effort (low effort scores high): 5. The buyer must gather payouts, settlements, balances and payables, the part founders avoid.

## What is strong
- Warm notes beat cold email, where PNF has 0 replies from about 57 sends.
- A specific outcome: a date, a deliverable, a decision list.
- The 5 client cap is true scarcity.
- Existing calculators and kit show the method.

## Top 3 things that would kill it
1. **Belief.** No proof the forecast lands; a buyer cannot see what they get before paying.
2. **Reach.** If many of the 15 sell supplements or wellness, or sit below the band, the test fails on reach, not on the offer.
3. **Buyer effort.** If the monthly data pull falls on the founder, the plan churns after the first forecast.

## Questions I would ask
- How many of the 15 are in band and not excluded?
- Can a buyer see a finished forecast before paying?
- When exports arrive late or messy, who chases?
- Will the owner sell under his own name?
- What guarantee can we afford?

## Conditions for FUND
1. Count qualified contacts first; if too few, widen the list (threshold to check).
2. Build one sample forecast on made-up data and show it in every note.
3. Sell under the owner's name, not the faceless brand.
4. A one-page export checklist so the client's job is one email; on the monthly plan, the owner chases.
5. Only a cheap guarantee, such as on-time delivery or the fee back (terms to check).
6. "Three decisions" are flags for the founder and their accountant, not advice.
7. Price set only by the owner's own words in a live chat.
