# Product memo: done-for-you cash forecast

**Score: 6 of 10. Vote: FUND IF.**

## Assumptions
1. A founder with no finance lead will pay for an accountable person, not a tool.
2. That founder can and will send clean exports from each channel, the bank and payables.
3. The owner's record carries the trust, so the buyer must see who he is.
4. One forecast in 5 business days earns the monthly plan.
5. It fits his hours (to check).

## What is strong
- The first step is one fixed, dated result. Right shape.
- The tool is common; a person accountable for it monthly is not.
- The free calculators show the craft before any sale.

## Top 3 killers
1. **Data intake is the real product and it is undesigned.** The founder must pull payouts, settlement reports, balances and payables from several systems first. If that feels like homework, the 5 days never start.
2. **Too much in the promise.** Channel P&L, forecast, three decisions, a monthly date. It needs a paragraph, so it is not clear yet. The date also hangs on the client's books closing.
3. **Whose name is on it.** Belief comes from his record, but the brand is faceless. A note from him, an invoice from another name and an unsigned file split trust.

## Questions
- What is the one thing? Cut to the 13-week forecast and its decisions; the P&L waits for month two.
- What exactly does the founder send, from where, and how long does a non-finance person take to pull it (to check)?
- What lands on day 5: spreadsheet, one-page summary, walkthrough?
- Which name sends the note, the invoice and the file?
- How do files travel safely with no one logging into their accounts?

## Conditions
1. One offer, one sentence, no P&L in step one.
2. A one-page data checklist per platform, tested on a real export before the first note.
3. Name and billing route fixed before the first note, identical on every touchpoint.
4. A defined day 5 deliverable, with a sample shown up front.

## Buyer's first five minutes
1. Reads a personal note from someone they know. (Crossing: inbox or phone.)
2. Replies yes; gets what it is, the price and what to send. (Crossing: email.)
3. Pays. (Crossing: Gumroad, QuickBooks or bank, undecided. Breaks if payee and sender names differ.)
4. Logs into Shopify, Amazon, TikTok Shop, bank and card portals to export files. (Most crossings; most likely place it dies.)
5. Sends the files. (Crossing: upload route undefined, a trust gap.)

Nothing of value arrives yet. Put a sample forecast and "what happens next" in step 2.
