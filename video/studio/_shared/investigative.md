## The story test (each 0 to 2; keep 8 or more of 10, with a primary document)
- **New:** happened in the last 45 days, or takes effect in the next 120. Old news only with a new fact.
- **Consequential:** moves an owner's money, risk or time this month (a fee, a fine, a lawsuit risk, jobs won or lost).
- **Surprising:** cuts against what owners believe or what a vendor says. The test: would an owner say "wait, what?"
- **Documented:** a primary record opened in this run: a ruling, docket, rule text, regulator page, the vendor's own
  pricing, terms, help page or changelog, an earnings call or SEC filing, a government dataset.
- **Actionable:** one setup or check an owner can finish this week in under 30 minutes, no code (lanes.md).

## Beats (one reporter each)
1. **The records:** regulators and courts. FCC and FTC actions and rules, state AG cases, state AI and call-recording laws,
   CourtListener dockets (AI call recording and analytics suits, robocall and texting suits, fake review cases), licensing
   boards. A lawsuit is what the complaint alleges; a ruling is what the court decided.
2. **The money:** the vendors that sell AI to the trades and the platforms owners pay. ServiceTitan (public: 10-Q, 10-K,
   earnings calls), Jobber, Housecall Pro, Angi, Thumbtack, Google (Local Services Ads, Business Profile), Yelp, Meta, the
   phone and AI receptionist makers. Price and terms changes (the page today against the Wayback Machine), changelogs, what
   a feature really does against what the ad says, who pays when it goes wrong.
3. **The field:** what is happening in real shops. Census Business Trends and Outlook Survey (AI use by industry), BLS,
   trade press (ACHR News, Contractor, PHC News, EC&M, Roofing Contractor, Remodeling), trade associations, owners' own
   published numbers, scams and failures aimed at contractors. Reddit only through Kimi (Todd runs it) or a page we open.

## Verification (the record decides)
- Open every document in this run. A story rests on what the page says, with its date, not on a summary of it.
- Two independent sources for any claim not from a primary record. A press release is the company talking.
- Match the verb to the record: alleges (a complaint), found or ruled (a court or agency), proposed (not passed), says
  (a vendor, a survey sponsor). Say who paid for a survey and how many it asked.
- Ask who benefits from the number. A vendor's survey that says owners need the vendor is said as the vendor's.
- The other side, every time: the vendor's, agency's or defendant's own published words or limits, one beat on screen.
- Names: public companies, agencies, courts, laws and public officials in their role are named. A private person or a
  small business (a plumber who was sued, an owner in a thread) is never named: "an HVAC shop in Ohio", with the record.
- Nothing invented. A reenactment is labeled "Example" on screen. If a fact can't be pinned, the line is cut.

## Script craft (format dispatch, _shared/formats.md)
The voice rules in _shared/voice.md all hold. On top of them, how a great reporter tells it to one owner:
- **The lede (0 to 3 s):** the single most surprising true sentence, 9 to 12 words, with the document or the number on
  screen as it is said (hook pattern receipt; C5: the result by 3 s). A thing, a date, a dollar figure. Never "AI is
  changing", never a question you could answer with "no".
- **The nut (by 8 s):** why it hits this owner's money this month, in one line.
- **The trail (the middle):** two or three receipts, one beat each, each with its source tag on screen while it is said
  (S2). Every 3 to 4 seconds a new fact. Show the page, never a paraphrase of a page we could show.
- **The turn:** the other side or the catch nobody mentions (the fine print, who pays, what the feature does not do).
- **The so-what:** the setup card (C3, lanes.md "Systems, not tricks"): at most 5 steps, held 3 s or more.
- **The kicker:** the last line echoes the lede's key word and lands the rule. Nothing after it.
- Specific over loud: a rule number, a form name, a dollar figure. No "shocking", "insane", "exposed", "they don't want
  you to know". Certainty matches the record. Active verbs, short sentences, one aside.
