# Brief: Six hours, $6,000 (v4 verdict)

- **Format:** verdict
- **Where:** Instagram Reel, 9:16, then TikTok, Facebook, YouTube Shorts.
- **Audience:** anyone who has paid for a job or been paid for one. Owners and crews will pick a side at once; so will homeowners.
- **The one idea:** A crew finished a $6,000 job in under six hours and the customer offered half because it was fast: the price is for the job, not the hours, so agree it in writing before the first shovel.
- **Pull:** conflict yes (the crew lead and the customer); dollars yes ($6,000 agreed, $3,000 offered, $4,000 counter); send yes (the partner who bills by the hour, or the friend who haggles after the work is done). Breadth: a homeowner stops because they have wondered the same thing about a fast job; a tech stops because it has happened to them.
- **Shape:** the fight on frame 0, why it was fast, the counter offer, the question that splits the room.
- **Music:** chase (the make run checks `anim.py tunes` so it is not the tune of the lesson before).
- **Voice:** Todd's clone (`_shared/voice.md`).
- **Source:** The Nerd Stash, "North Carolina Customer Tries to Cut $6,000 Bill in Half After Crew Finishes Job in Six Hours", Jul 4, 2026; We Got This Covered, same story. It is the hardscaping company's own video and account, posted on TikTok. Neither site reports the customer's side beyond what the video shows, and neither knows whether he ever paid. The voice says "the crew lead says". No person or business is named.
- **Read note (Nov 11, 2026):** both pages were read as search excerpts in the research run (one fetch failed, one returned a listing page). The make run opens the pages themselves before the facts gate.

## Beats (a guide for the script, not the script)
1. Frame 0, settled: "$6,000 JOB" over "DONE IN 6 HOURS", the two men close. Spoken hook, 8 words: "Six thousand dollar job. Done in six hours."
2. By 3 s: "So the customer offers half."
3. `[payoff]` by 40%: the crew lead says he brought in extra crews because the customer wanted it done before the holiday. Same job, same result, faster.
4. Held for 70% to 85% (level-up, move 3): he offered to take $4,000. The customer still said $3,000. He told him not to call again and walked. Nobody knows if he was ever paid. Say both numbers aloud.
5. Close: "Pay for the job, or pay for the hours? You decide." Last frame: both sides, each with its case in three or four words ("AGREED PRICE" and "HALF THE TIME").
