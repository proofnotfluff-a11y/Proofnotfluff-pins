# Brief: Owed $4,225, he took a sledgehammer to his own work (v4 case)

Written Nov 12, 2026, the first `case` (Todd, live chat, Nov 12, 2026, 10:03 am: "can we make the necessary changes so we rectify our weaknesses?"; coo/DECISIONS-2026-11-12.md). Rules: `_shared/formats.md` "v4" and "Case"; coo/constitution/video.md. The make run copies it to videos/<slug>/01_voice/brief.md and reads every source again that run.

- **Test:** the case format itself: the result held to the last 40%, 55 to 95 s, a new shot every 6 to 8 s, one acted line a side. Bar: research/TEST-2026-11-23.md "Case test".
- **Format:** case
- **Where:** Instagram Reel, 9:16, then TikTok, Facebook, YouTube Shorts.
- **Audience:** anyone who has hired a contractor or been one. Trades people have all wanted to do this; homeowners fear it.
- **The one idea:** A contractor owed $4,225 smashed the shower he had just built and went to jail for it; small claims court would have heard the whole bill, so get paid on paper, never with a hammer.
- **Pull:** conflict yes (the contractor and the homeowner); dollars yes ($7,555 the job, $3,330 paid, $4,225 held back); send yes (the friend who says "I'd just rip it back out", or anyone about to hold back a last payment). Breadth: a homeowner stops because a stranger is breaking a bathroom; a tech stops because they have been stiffed.
- **Send:** a tech or a homeowner sends it to the one who says "I'd just take it back", because this is what taking it back cost.
- **Shape:** the hammer on frame 0, the deal, the held payment, each side's line, the smash, what followed, the result, the legal route, the question.
- **Result (a case only):** he pleaded guilty to felony criminal mischief and got 15 days in jail and 3 years of supervised probation (KRDO, Oct 13, 2022). Held to 60% or later.
- **Music:** chase or night (the make run checks `anim.py tunes`).
- **Voice:** Todd's clone (`_shared/voice.md`).
- **Source:** KRDO 13 (Colorado Springs), reports from Sept 2021 to Oct 13, 2022; Levelset, Oct 15, 2021. The homeowner's account, the contractor's own statement and words to police, and the court's sentence are all reported there. The company said the story was made up and that it was licensed; the building department said the work had no permit. No person or business is named in the video, the captions or this file.
- **Read note (Nov 12, 2026):** the Oct 13, 2022 KRDO report and the Levelset report were read as pages this run; the other KRDO reports, CBS and Business Insider as search excerpts. The make run opens each page before the facts gate. It also checks Colorado's small claims limit today on the state courts' own site and says "at the time" if it has changed.

## Beats (a guide for the script, not the script; about 150 words, 60 to 70 s)
1. Frame 0, settled: the contractor mid swing in a tiled shower, "OWED $4,225" over "SO HE TOOK IT BACK". Spoken hook, 8 words or fewer: "He was owed $4,225. So he took it back."
2. Wide, the house: a shower rebuild, $7,555.
3. Close, plates: $3,330 paid. $4,225 held back. She says she wanted to look the work over first.
4. Close on him, "Reenactment": he says nobody is paying him, so the shower is his to take back.
5. Cut to the hallway: a roommate had let them in and is now filming.
6. The swing: tile off the wall. He says no contractor in the state will fix it now.
7. New place, a phone: the video goes around the country, millions of views.
8. Other tile setters come and rebuild it for free.
9. The police: warrants for both owners.
10. `[payoff]`, 60% or later, a courtroom: he pleads guilty to a felony. 15 days in jail. 3 years of probation. Stamp each as it is said.
11. The company: closed.
12. The rule: small claims court in Colorado took cases up to $7,500 then. His whole $4,225 fit. So did a lien on the house.
13. Close: "She held back $4,225 on a finished job. He did 15 days. Who was more wrong?" Then the send line: "Send this to the one who says they'd just take it back." Last frame: both sides, "HELD THE LAST PAYMENT" and "TOOK IT BACK".

## Staging notes
- Thirteen beats in about 65 s is one new shot every 5 s. Each beat is its own cue with its own place or framing (`k.cut`, `k.cam`, a room change). No two beats in a row share a frame.
- The hammer never lands on a person and nobody is hurt on screen: tile and wall only. The roommate is shown from behind, holding a phone.
- The faces read at phone size: his anger, her shock, and at the result his face in the dock, not a label.
- The courtroom and the jail door are new set pieces; they appear only from beat 10.
- Nothing before beat 10 says "jail", "court", "guilty" or "sentenced", on screen or in the caption's first line. The caption's dilemma line is the question, not the result.
