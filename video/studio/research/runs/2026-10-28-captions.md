# Captions, keywords, tags and AI labels for ProofNotFluff daily videos

Research date: Oct 28, 2026. Scope: Instagram Reels, TikTok, Facebook Page Reels, YouTube Shorts.

How to read this report:
- **[Official]** means the claim comes from the platform itself (help center, creator blog, newsroom, transparency page, or a named executive quoted by a reputable outlet).
- **[Study]** means a large-sample third-party study.
- **[Secondary]** means a third-party write-up I could not confirm against a platform page.
- **[Best guess]** means I could not verify it and am giving a practical guess.
- Source numbers like [S12] point to the Sources list at the end.

Pages I could not read: TikTok Creator Academy pages and TikTok Support pages (blocked to automated readers or rendered blank), Meta Transparency Center Reels and Search pages (rendered blank), TikTok's commercial disclosure help page (permission timed out). Where those mattered I used secondary sources and labeled them.

---

## Bottom line (read this if nothing else)

1. **Every platform now decides who sees a video mostly from viewer behavior** (watch time, finishing, shares or sends, likes, "not interested"). Text (caption, title, hashtags, spoken words) tells the system *what the video is about* and *which searches it matches*. It does not buy reach by itself. [S1][S5][S14][S20]
2. **Hashtags are capped or near-capped everywhere:** Instagram 5 per post [S2], TikTok 5 per post (in-app prompt, not formally announced) [S9][S10], Facebook "no more than five" per Meta's own guidance [S16], YouTube ignores all hashtags if there are more than 60 and warns against over-tagging [S21].
3. **Keywords in plain language beat hashtags for search** on all four. Put the main keyword in the first line of the caption, say it out loud in the first seconds, and show it as on-screen text. Instagram's own advice is "put keywords and hashtags in the caption, not the comments" [S6].
4. **AI labels (because the voice is a clone of the owner's voice):** turn the AI label ON for Instagram, Facebook and TikTok. Set YouTube "AI use" to **No**, because YouTube explicitly lists "cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs" and fully animated videos as not needing disclosure [S23]. Where platforms say anything about reach, they say labeling does not reduce it [S23][S28][S13].
5. **The keyword that filters for buyers is the word "business"** (or "owner"). "Plumbing tips" pulls homeowners and apprentices; "plumbing business tips" pulls owners. "Speed to lead" pulls marketers and automation builders. Evidence is in section 4.

## 1. How each platform decides who sees a new video (2026)

### Instagram Reels
- **[Official] Top signals.** Adam Mosseri (Jan 22, 2025): "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends," and creators should watch "average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach." [S1]
- **[Official] Reels ranking explained** (Instagram): the most important predictions are how likely you are to "reshare a reel, watch a reel all the way through, like it, and go to the audio page." Signals in rough order: your activity, your history with the creator, information about the reel ("the audio track or visuals in the video, as well as popularity"), information about the creator. Reels can be made less visible if "low-resolution or watermarked," "muted or contain borders," "majority text," or "already been posted on Instagram." [S3]
- **[Official] Hashtags help search, not reach.** Instagram's @creators account said using "fewer (up to 5) more targeted hashtags, rather than many generic ones" can improve performance. Mosseri said in Feb 2025 hashtags "don't work" to increase reach and in May 2025 that they "don't improve visibility on Instagram" (as reported by Social Media Today). [S2]
- **[Official, 2026] Originality.** On Apr 30, 2026 Instagram said accounts that regularly repost others' content without material edits stop being recommended (reported by the Keywords Everywhere tracker citing the Instagram Creators blog and TechCrunch). [S4] Our videos are original, so this is a reason to never upload a file carrying another app's watermark.
- **[Official, 2026] Freshness.** On Meta's Q3 2026 earnings call, nearly 45% of recommended Reels were under a day old, and Reels now ranks sequences of Reels (reported by the tracker). [S4]
- **Caption keywords, on-screen text, spoken words.** Instagram says search text is matched against "usernames, bios, captions, hashtags, and places" [S6]. Mosseri in a July 2026 Q&A said "We try to use all the information we can," describing captions and comments as text signals and embedding models that map videos into the same space as search queries (reported by Kontentino, not verified against a transcript) [S7]. **[Best guess]** Spoken words and on-screen text are likely understood by Meta's video models, but Instagram has not published that they are search signals.
- **Topics.** Instagram tested an "Add Topics" option in the Reels upload flow starting Sep 5, 2022 (up to 3 topics) [S8]. **[Best guess]** It may or may not show on our account; use it if it appears.
- **Location tags.** Search matches "places" [S6]. For a national audience of owners, a location tag adds little. **[Best guess]** Skip it.

### TikTok
- **[Official] Recommendation inputs** (TikTok Newsroom): "user interactions" (likes, shares, follows, comments, content you create), "video information" which "might include details like captions, sounds, and hashtags," and "device and account settings" like language and country, which get "lower weight." Strong signals, like finishing a longer video, count more. "Neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors." [S5]
- **[Official, Apr 2026] Search keywords per video.** TikTok now shows creators keywords it attached to each post: "This post displays keywords automatically added by TikTok based on popular searches. You can manage them at any time." Creators can block a keyword and suggest others. [S11][S12]
- **Spoken words and on-screen text.** TikTok's own advertiser tool (TikTok Content Suite) says it "identifies brand relevance through captions, hashtags, voiceover using speech recognition" and reads "text within a video using optical character recognition" (as quoted by Tagly from TikTok Ads help) [S13b]. That proves TikTok *can* read speech and on-screen text; it does not prove search ranking uses them. **[Best guess]** Treat spoken keyword plus on-screen keyword as likely helpful and free to add.
- **[Official] Hashtags.** Listed as one kind of "video information" [S5]. No hashtag "unlocks" the For You feed; #fyp describes a feed, not a topic (Taskant analysis) [S10].
- **Location.** Country and language are lower-weight inputs [S5]. **[Best guess]** Keep the account and video language set to English and post from a US account; skip location tags.

### Facebook Page Reels
- **[Official] Feed and Reels ranking** (Meta Transparency Center, via FrameOS summary): Feed uses thousands of signals and over 100 prediction models. Reels has roughly ten prediction models; predicted actions include watch duration, completion, hiding a reel and asking to see less. A Jan 14, 2026 Meta engineering post described a model trained on viewers' 1 to 5 ratings of how well a video matches their interests. [S14]
- **[Official] Unconnected reach is large.** Meta's Widely Viewed Content Report: Q2 2026 US Feed views were 49.6% from connected sources and 36.6% from unconnected recommendations (via FrameOS) [S14]. So a Page with few followers can still be shown to strangers.
- **[Official] Original content** (Facebook Creators blog, Jul 14, 2025): accounts that repeatedly reuse others' content get reduced distribution and can lose monetization; duplicates are demoted. Best practices include "captions with no links, minimal use of capital letters, and no more than five hashtags." Stitching clips or adding your watermark "does not qualify as meaningful enhancement." [S16]
- **[Official, Jun 2025] All Facebook videos are now Reels**, with no length or format limits; users are prompted to review audience settings (reported by The Week) [S17].
- **Caption keywords and Facebook search.** I found no current official Meta statement on how Facebook search reads Reels captions. **[Best guess]** Facebook uses the same Meta content-understanding stack as Instagram; write the caption the same way.

### YouTube Shorts
- **[Official] Recommendations** use "viewing behaviour, likes, dislikes, subscriptions and feedback, including from satisfaction surveys" and "the reputation and the quality of a channel." [S19]
- **[Official] Search** weighs relevance, engagement and quality: "how well the title, tags, description, and video content match your search query," "the watch time of a particular video for a particular query," and channels that "demonstrate expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness on a given topic." Note "video content" is listed, so the spoken words likely count. [S20]
- **[Official] Shorts appear** in the Shorts feed, "in search results across YouTube," on the homepage and on the channel page. [S22]
- **[Official-ish] Shorts testing.** Todd Sherman, YouTube Shorts product lead, explained on Creator Insider that Shorts views mostly come from the feed, so the system tests each Short; this ties to the "Viewed vs. swiped away" metric (vidIQ's paraphrase) [S25].
- **[Official, Oct 1, 2026] Originality.** YouTube updated recommendations to prioritize original Shorts and reduce reach for channels "primarily aggregating or re-uploading other creators' clips" (Rene Ritchie post and YouTube Help community post, via OutlierKit) [S26].
- **[Official] Inauthentic content (monetization).** Since Jul 15, 2025 YouTube calls it "inauthentic content." Not monetizable: "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production." Allowed: "a series following a set of characters across episodes" when each has "a distinct storyline, focus, or concept." [S27] Our daily series with one host is fine as long as every lesson is genuinely different.

## 2. Caption or description: length, keyword placement, truncation

| | Hard limit | What shows before "more" | Where keywords go |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram Reels | 2,200 characters [Secondary, S29] | About 125 characters in feed; Reels overlay shows less [Secondary, S29] | First line. Instagram: keywords in the caption, not comments [Official, S6] |
| TikTok | 4,000 characters [Secondary, S10] | Not published. **[Best guess]** about one to two lines on the video | First line, plus spoken and on-screen |
| Facebook Reels | Not verified | Not published. **[Best guess]** one to two lines | First line. No links, few capitals, max 5 hashtags [Official, S16] |
| YouTube Shorts | Title 100, description 5,000 [Official, S24] | Title shows on the Short; description sits behind a tap [Official, S23 says AI labels for animated content "may appear in the expanded description"] | Title first, then description first line [Official, S20] |

Practical rules (all platforms):
- **First 100 characters do the work.** Lead with the plain-language keyword phrase plus who it is for ("for plumbers, HVAC and roofing business owners"). That line both matches search and screens out homeowners.
- **Then the proof line** (the number and its source). That fits the brand and gives people a reason to send it.
- **Then the steps or a short summary**, then one call to action that drives sends or saves (Mosseri lists sends among the top three signals [S1]).
- **Hashtags last.**
- Instagram and Facebook: no link in caption. Meta's own guidance [S16]; Meta's Q2 2026 data puts posts with an external link at 1.1% of Feed views [S14].

## 3. Hashtags and tags

### Instagram
- **[Official] Limit: 5** hashtags per post or reel. @creators: "Instagram will gradually update the number of hashtags that you can include in a caption for a reel or post to five" (Social Media Today, Dec 18, 2025) [S2].
- **[Official] Broad vs niche:** "fewer (up to 5) more targeted hashtags, rather than many generic ones" [S2]. Instagram's Threads post warned that spamming irrelevant or generic hashtags could hurt performance (Digital Trends) [S2b].
- **[Study] Do they help?** Metricool 2026 (24,364,803 posts, 375,118 accounts, Jan to Feb 2026 vs 2025): posts with at least one hashtag had 31.70% fewer views and 33.89% fewer interactions than average. This is correlation, not cause. [S30]
- **Practical:** use 3 to 5, all specific. They are labels for search, not reach.

### TikTok
- **Limit: 5** per post. In-app prompt: "Maximum 5 hashtags. Use accurate hashtags to get more views." Reported Aug 17, 2025 (Social Media Today) and by MediaPost; TikTok has not issued a formal statement. [S9][S9b]
- **[Study] Do they help?** Metricool 2026 TikTok study (2,314,756 posts, 92,000+ accounts): posts with at least one hashtag got about 5% more views and over 9% more interactions; hashtag-driven traffic rose 114% year over year. Correlation only. [S31]
- **#fyp, #foryou, #viral:** no evidence they help; they describe a feed, not a topic [S10].
- **Practical:** 3 to 5 accurate tags; mix 1 audience tag, 1 to 2 trade tags, 1 topic tag.

### Facebook
- **[Official]** "no more than five hashtags" (Facebook Creators blog, Jul 14, 2025) [S16].
- **Practical:** 2 to 3 is enough. **[Best guess]** Facebook hashtags carry little discovery weight; they mostly avoid looking spammy.

### YouTube Shorts
- **[Official] Hashtags:** can go in the title or description and are clickable. "Up to three hashtags that are considered most engaging will appear by your video title." "If a video or playlist has more than 60 hashtags, we'll ignore each hashtag." "Over-tagging may result in the removal of your video from your uploads or from search." "Don't add hashtags that are not directly related." [S21]
- **#Shorts:** YouTube's current Shorts help pages do not say #Shorts is needed; Shorts are defined by format (vertical, up to 3 minutes) [S22][S10c]. **[Best guess]** Harmless but unnecessary; skip it to save space.
- **[Official] Tags field:** "tags play a minimal role in your video's discovery"; useful "if the content of your video is commonly misspelled." Title, thumbnail and description matter more. [S24b]. Tag field character limit: not stated on YouTube's help pages I read; 500 characters is widely reported **[Secondary, not verified]**.
- **[Official] Title rules:** max 100 characters, no invalid characters [S24]. Misleading metadata breaks spam and deceptive practices rules [S21].
- **[Official] Description keywords:** matched in search [S20]. "Ordinary descriptive tags or repetitive sentences in the description" (keyword stuffing) are prohibited [S21].

## 4. Search and keyword research

### How search reads our videos
- **Instagram [Official]:** "The text you enter in the search bar is Instagram's most important ranking signal." Matched against usernames, bios, captions, hashtags and places; then your activity; then engagement. Creator tips: put your known name in username or profile name, "include relevant keywords and locations in your bio," "put keywords and hashtags in the caption, not the comments." [S6] Public posts from professional accounts became indexable by Google from Jul 10, 2025, with an opt-out (tracker citing PPC Land) and Google Search Console added Instagram reporting in Jul 2026 (tracker citing Google Search Central blog) [S4]. So the caption is also a Google snippet.
- **Alt text:** Instagram offers alt text on images [Secondary, S29]. I could not verify alt text for Reels. **[Best guess]** If the Reels advanced settings show an alt text field, paste the keyword sentence there.
- **TikTok [Official]:** auto keywords per post, editable (Apr 2026) [S11]. Creator Search Insights shows what people search, including "content gap" topics that are "searched for often, but aren't featured in a large number of videos" [S12b]. Auto captions exist and can be edited [Secondary, S13b].
- **Facebook:** no official detail found.
- **YouTube [Official]:** title, tags, description and "video content" against the query, plus watch time per query and topic authority [S20]. Uploading accurate captions (or correcting auto captions) and setting the video language is cheap insurance. **[Best guess]**

### What owners actually search (YouTube autocomplete, pulled Oct 28, 2026)
YouTube autocomplete reflects real searches. Results I pulled [S32]:
- "plumbing business" → plumbing business **owner**, **tips**, **marketing**, podcast, coach, start up
- "hvac business" → hvac business **owner**, **advice**, **growth**, **pricing**, **marketing**, coaching
- "roofing business" → start up, profits, partner, **owner**, **tips**, podcast
- "cleaning business" → **tips**, **pricing**, supplies, podcast, **marketing strategy**, contracts
- "landscaping business" → startup, **tips**, **pricing**, equipment
- "contractor business" → **tips**, podcast, model, ideas
- "contractor tips" → contracting tips, general contractor tips, **contractor business tips**, roofing contractor tips
- "home service business" → ideas, coach, **marketing**, podcast, facebook ads, website
- "how to price a job" → in construction, **painting job**, **roofing job**, **cleaning job**, **plumbing job**, **landscape job**
- "how to get more leads for" → my business, **service business**, **your cleaning company**, **my construction business**, **my painting business**
- "how to get more jobs as a" → **contractor**; "how to get more work as a contractor"
- "how to close more jobs" → **as a contractor**
- "how to respond to a lead" → how to respond to leads

Warning signs (terms that pull the wrong audience):
- "plumbing tips" → tips and tricks, for beginners, **for apprentices**, for new house. That is DIY homeowners and apprentices, not owners.
- "speed to lead" → **ai, ghl, n8n, hubspot, automation, system**. That is marketers and automation builders.
- "small business tips for" → students, women, kids, college students. Too broad.
- "contractor marketing" → "contractor marketing pros" and similar agency names. Agency-heavy; use only paired with a trade word.

### Keyword research practice (repeatable, 10 minutes a week)
1. Start from the lesson's job to be done (reply to leads, follow up, price, quote, reviews, numbers).
2. Check YouTube autocomplete for "[job] + [trade]" and "[trade] business + [job]". Keep phrases that contain business, owner, contractor, company or a trade plus a money word (price, leads, jobs, close).
3. Check TikTok Creator Search Insights for the same phrase and its "content gap" filter [S12b].
4. Pick one **primary phrase** for the first caption line and the spoken hook, and two **secondary phrases** for the rest of the caption, YouTube description and tags.
5. After posting, check TikTok's auto keywords on the post and block any that point at homeowners or marketers [S11].

## 5. AI disclosure for an animated video with a cloned voice of the account owner

Our facts: pixel-art animation (not photorealistic), bearded host character, narration is an AI clone of the owner's own voice, used with the owner's consent.

### Instagram and Facebook (Meta)
- **[Official] Rule:** Meta requires people to use its disclosure tool for organic content "with a photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered" and "may apply penalties if they fail to do so" [S33]. Instagram Help: tap "Label as made with AI" when creating a post or reel; "There may be penalties" for not labeling when required [S34].
- **Applies to us?** The video is not photorealistic, but the voice is realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created. Meta lists no own-voice exception. **Verdict: turn the AI label ON** on both Instagram and Facebook.
- **[Official] How it shows:** AI-generated content shows an "AI info" label on the post; content only edited with AI has the label moved to the menu (Meta, Sep 2024 update) [S35].
- **Reach:** Meta has not said the per-post label lowers distribution [S35]. **[Best guess]** No meaningful penalty for labeling; the stated penalty risk is for *not* labeling.
- **Profile-level "AI-generated profile" label (Aug 31, 2026) [Official]:** for profiles that feature an AI-generated person instead of a human. Unlabeled ones "may see limits to their profile's reach"; those that add it "will not see a change to their profile's reach"; "Creators who simply use AI tools as part of their creative process don't need to add the label." [S36] **[Best guess]** Not required for us: the host is a cartoon stand-in for a real owner, not a fake person presented as real. Watch Account Status for any notice; if one comes, add the label (reach is unaffected by adding it).

### TikTok
- **[Official] Rule:** TikTok "requires people to label AI-generated content that contains realistic images, audio or video" (TikTok Newsroom) [S37]. Community Guidelines: "Synthetic media showing realistic scenes that are not prominently disclosed or labeled" is not allowed; disclosure can be "a sticker or caption, such as 'synthetic', 'fake', 'not real', or 'altered'" [S38]. The current guidelines (Aug 25, 2026 version, per a secondary summary) require disclosure for "AI audio that mimics a real voice," do not require it for anime-style art or generic text-to-speech, and prohibit a private person's likeness *without consent* [S39].
- **Watch out:** the guidelines page I could read says "We do not allow synthetic media that contains the likeness of any real private figure" with no consent wording shown [S38]. The secondary summary of the 2026 version says the ban is on use *without consent* [S39]. **[Best guess]** Using your own voice with your own consent is the intended allowed case, but the official text I could read does not say so. Labeling plus a plain caption line removes any doubt that we are hiding it.
- **Verdict: turn ON "AI-generated content"** (in the post settings under More options; the post then shows "Creator labeled as AI-generated") [S40][S41].
- **Reach:** TikTok's help text (as quoted by a secondary source) says the setting "does not affect distribution as long as the post does not violate Community Guidelines" [S41]; the 2026 guideline summary says labeled AI content stays For You eligible [S39]. **But:** since Nov 2025 TikTok lets viewers use an "AIGC setting" slider in Manage Topics to see less AI content [S42][S43]. **[Best guess]** A small number of viewers who turned AI down may see us less. No public number exists. Not labeling is the bigger risk.
- **Separate setting, "Disclose commercial content":** turn on "Your brand" only on videos that promote our own paid product or service; TikTok defines it as for videos that promote "your own brand, business, product, or service" [S44]. A purely educational lesson with no product pitch does not need it. **[Secondary]** In the 2026 guidelines, undisclosed commercial content may get reduced visibility [S39].

### YouTube
- **[Official] Rule:** disclose content that "makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do," alters real events or places, or "generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur." **Not required:** "Using an AI-generated or altered animation ... in a fully animated video" and "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs." Setting is in YouTube Studio upload, **Attributes > "AI use": Yes or No**. "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." For non-photorealistic content, labels "may appear in the expanded description." [S23]
- **Verdict: set "AI use" to No.** Both of our AI elements are named exemptions. (Setting Yes would also be safe for reach per YouTube's statement; No is the accurate answer.)
- If the voice or animation tool embeds C2PA content credentials, YouTube or TikTok may label automatically, and the creator cannot remove a C2PA-based label on YouTube (Postora summary, not checked on a YouTube page) [S28].

### One caption line for all four (honest and short)
"Narrated with an AI version of the host's own voice." It costs nothing, matches the brand name, and satisfies TikTok's caption-based disclosure option on top of the toggle.

## 6. Other upload settings that affect audience

### Instagram
- **Trial reels [Official]:** "Trial reels will be shown to non-followers first." You can auto-share to followers if it performs well "judged by its views in the first 72 hours." Metrics appear about 24 hours after sharing. Eligibility is on the Help Center (not stated on the blog) [S45]. Use: test two hooks for the same lesson on non-followers before committing. Trial reels must still be original files.
- **Topics:** use if the "Add topics" option appears [S8].
- **Location:** skip (national audience). **[Best guess]**
- **Share to Facebook toggle:** **[Best guess]** Post natively on the Facebook Page instead so you can set Facebook's own caption, AI label and hashtags. Not verified whether cross-posted Reels perform differently.
- **Upload a clean master** with no TikTok or CapCut watermark: watermarked reels are made less visible [S3].

### TikTok
- AI-generated content: ON. Disclose commercial content: only when selling. Language and country: lower-weight inputs [S5]; keep English and US. After posting, open the post's keyword management and block off-target auto keywords (homeowner or marketer terms) and suggest owner terms [S11][S12].
- No official "topics" picker at upload that I could verify.

### Facebook
- Post as a Reel from the Page (all videos are Reels now) [S17]. Audience: Public. AI label: ON. Caption: no links, 2 to 3 hashtags [S16]. Check the audience prompt Meta shows after the Reels change [S17].

### YouTube Shorts
- **Made for kids: No.** YouTube's factors for "made for kids" include "characters, celebrities, or toys that appeal to children" ... "including animated characters or cartoon figures," and "it is your legal responsibility to comply with COPPA" [S46]. A pixel-game cartoon could trip automated review, so set No explicitly and make the business subject obvious in title and first line. Videos set as made for kids are "more likely to be recommended alongside other kids' videos" [S46], which would be the wrong audience.
- **AI use:** No (see section 5).
- **Category:** YouTube does not publish any ranking effect for category. **[Best guess]** "Education" fits; set it once in upload defaults.
- **Language:** set video language to English and caption certification as appropriate [S24]. Helps captions and auto-dubbing (auto-dubbing opened to all creators Feb 2026, per OutlierKit [S26]).
- **Related video [Official]:** "A related video allows you to showcase your Videos, Shorts, and Live in the Shorts player" as a clickable link under the channel handle. Studio > Content > the Short > choose Related Video > Save. Requires "advanced feature access"; the video must be public or unlisted [S47]. Use it to chain lessons (lead reply → follow-up → quote).
- **Custom thumbnails for Shorts** arrived in Jul 2026 (Creator Insider, via OutlierKit) [S26]; they matter for search and channel page, not the swipe feed.
- **Series:** Shorts can be grouped into series since Sep 23, 2026 (Google blog, via OutlierKit) [S26]. Put each lesson theme in a series.

## 7. Practical templates

### 7a. Ranked core keyword list (all platforms)

Rank reflects (1) evidence it is a real search, (2) how well it filters to owners who can buy, (3) how often it fits our lessons.

| 2 | **[trade] business tips** | Autocomplete for plumbing, roofing, cleaning, landscaping, contractor [S32]. The word "business" excludes DIY homeowners, unlike "plumbing tips". |
