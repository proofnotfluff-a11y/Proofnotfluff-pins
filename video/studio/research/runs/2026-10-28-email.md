# Emails that win work: research brief for ProofNotFluff trades videos

Prepared Oct 28, 2026 (America/Chicago). Every claim carries a URL. Tags used:

- [PRIMARY] official rule, regulator, or the original study
- [VENDOR] data published by a company that sells email software or lead gen; self-selected users, no outside audit, treat as directional
- [WEAK] no sample size, opinion, or secondhand
- [UNVERIFIED] could not confirm from a primary page in this session

Quotes are kept under 15 words. Everything else is our own wording.

---

## 0. The short version (safe to say in a script)

1. Reply to new leads fast. Within an hour beats later by a wide margin (HBR audit of 2,241 firms), and Thumbtack says pros replying within 30 minutes get hired 25% more often than those replying within three hours.
2. Keep cold emails short: roughly 50 to 125 words. No study shows a magic number; several large datasets show shorter first emails do a bit better, and length matters less than relevance.
3. One ask, phrased as a low-pressure question ("Worth a quick call?") beats a hard meeting demand on cold email (Gong, 304,174 emails).
4. Follow up. Two or three follow-ups add real replies; returns shrink with each step. Space them a few days apart.
5. Plain text, no links, no tracking in the first email. Evidence is mostly practitioner and vendor, but it all points the same way.
6. Send from your own domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up. Google, Yahoo and Microsoft all now require this of anyone who sends in volume, and it costs nothing for a small sender to do.
7. US law (CAN-SPAM) covers business-to-business sales email: honest subject line, your postal address, a way to opt out, honor it within 10 business days.
8. GCs and property managers vet on insurance, licensing, safety record, references, capacity and reliability. Lead with those facts, not adjectives.

Big caution for scripts: the "average reply rate" swings from 0.74% (Smartlead typical sender) to 3.43% (Instantly) to 4.5% (Hunter) depending on how each vendor counts. Do not quote one of these as "the" industry average. Safer line: "Most cold emails get no reply; a few percent is normal."

### 1.2 Length

- Smartlead: first emails under 50 words got 1.47% replies; it took 68 contacts per reply for those versus 97 for 100 to 149 words. https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks [VENDOR]
- Hunter (34M): the 20 to 39 word band scored best at 4.5%, but the spread across lengths was small (about 3.4% to 4.5%) and the author says word count alone is not a reliable lever. https://hunter.io/blog/cold-email-word-count [VENDOR]
- Hunter 2026: bodies of 61 to 80 words did comparatively well (no exact figure published). https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email [VENDOR]
- Instantly: top-10% senders average under 80 words on first touch. https://instantly.ai/report-page [VENDOR]
- Boomerang (warm email): 50 to 125 words was the best range, all above a 50% response rate. https://blog.boomerangapp.com/?p=3933 [VENDOR]
- Counterpoint: Gong 2021 found 30 to 150 word emails booked more meetings than very short ones (effect size only in a chart). https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-email-stats [VENDOR] [WEAK]

Script-safe takeaway: aim for about 50 to 100 words. Short enough to read on a phone, long enough to say who you are, why them, and one ask.

### 1.3 Subject lines

- Boomerang: 3 to 4 word subjects got the most replies; emails with no subject did badly. https://blog.boomerangapp.com/?p=3933 [VENDOR]
- Hunter 2026: 5 to 6 word subjects had the best average reply rates; "Quick question" subjects underperformed (2.5% reply vs 2.9%). https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email [VENDOR]
- Hunter 2026: two personal details in the subject lifted opens to 40.2% from 35.4% with one. https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email [VENDOR] (Open rates are inflated by Apple Mail preloading, per Smartlead, so treat open-rate findings as soft.)
- Legal floor: subject lines must not mislead about the content. FTC https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business [PRIMARY]

Script-safe takeaway: short, specific, and honest. Name the job, the property, or the trade. Skip clickbait like "Quick question."

### 1.4 Personalization

- Hunter 2026: bodies with two custom details got 5.6% replies vs 3.6% with none; manually edited emails got 5.2% vs 4.4% fully automated. https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email [VENDOR]
- Hunter 2026: smaller sends win. Sequences to 21 to 50 people got 6.2% vs 2.4% for 500+. Emailing 1 to 2 people per company got 5.1% vs 3.5% for 3+. https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email [VENDOR]
- Woodpecker's "18% vs 9%" personalization figure has no visible method. [WEAK] Do not use.

Script-safe takeaway: a real reason you picked them (a project you saw, a permit, a building they manage) beats a mail merge. Send fewer, better emails.

### 1.5 One ask, and how to phrase it

- Gong (304,174 emails): on cold email, asking about interest beat asking for a specific meeting time. The specific-time ask hit 15% success; the interest ask did better (its exact figure is only in a chart). On emails inside an active deal, the specific time ask won (37% vs 25%). https://www.gong.io/blog/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings [VENDOR]
- Gong 2021: "I never heard back" raised replies but cut meetings booked by 14%; asking for "thoughts" raised replies but cut meetings by 20%. https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-email-stats [VENDOR] [WEAK: no method shown]
- Boomerang: emails with 1 to 3 questions were about 50% more likely to get a reply than emails with none. https://blog.boomerangapp.com/?p=3933 [VENDOR]
- Instantly FAQ: best campaigns are focused on a single ask. https://instantly.ai/report-page [VENDOR] [WEAK: no data shown for it]

Script-safe takeaway: cold message, soft question ("Open to adding a backup sub for this?"). Warm lead or live job, specific time ("Can I come Thursday at 8?").

### 1.6 Plain text, links, tracking

- HubSpot A/B tests: plain text beat image-heavy HTML on clicks; even one image lowered click rate in its customer data. Marketing email, not cold sales, and sample sizes not published. https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/plain-text-vs-html-emails-data [VENDOR]
- Hunter 2026: sequences without open and click tracking got 7.4% replies vs 4.4% with tracking. https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email [VENDOR] (Correlation; senders who skip tracking may differ in other ways.)
- lemlist coaching note (Mar 5, 2026): recommends zero links in a first cold email and no click tracking, because filters treat tracked links like phishing. No data shown. https://help.lemlist.com/en/articles/13941647-lemcoach-should-you-track-clicks-and-opens-in-cold-email-campaigns [WEAK]
- [UNVERIFIED] No primary study isolates "link vs no link" on cold reply rate. Present "no links" as a common best practice, not a proven number.

Script-safe takeaway: write it like a normal email to a person. No logos, no images, no links in the first message. Offer photos or a COI in a reply once they say yes.

### 1.7 Timing

- Smartlead: replies peaked around 7am recipient time (1.34%); day of week barely mattered (0.82% to 0.91%). https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks [VENDOR]
- Hunter 2026: day of week had no meaningful effect. https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email [VENDOR]
- Instantly: says Wednesday is highest but gives no figures. https://instantly.ai/report-page [VENDOR] [WEAK]
- Smartlead: 64% of replies arrive within an hour and 88.5% within 24 hours of the send. https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks [VENDOR]

Script-safe takeaway: day of week is a wash. Early morning on a weekday is a fine default, which suits trades (GCs and supers start early). Do not promise a "best day."

### 1.8 Follow-up count and spacing

- Smartlead (850M): reply rate falls each step, from 1.18% on email 1 to 0.73% (2), 0.56% (3), 0.49% (4), down to 0.33% (7). Replies per 1,000 contacts grow from 12 (one email) to 25 (three) to 30 (four). Cannot see unsubscribes or complaints. https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks/do-cold-email-follow-ups-work [VENDOR]
- Hunter 2026: three messages got 6.8% total replies vs 3.3% for one; sequences with more than three follow-ups did worse on average. https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email [VENDOR]
- Belkins (7.53M): follow-ups (steps 2 to 6) produced 58.6% of replies, and step 3 alone produced 35.6% of booked meetings. https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics?frame=0 [VENDOR]
- Instantly: 42% of replies came from follow-ups; recommends 3 to 4 days between touches; says step 2 that reads like a reply beats a formal reminder by about 30% (baseline undefined). https://instantly.ai/report-page [VENDOR] [WEAK on the 30%]
- Spacing in days: no dataset above measures it directly. Instantly's 3 to 4 days is a recommendation. [WEAK]

Script-safe takeaway: plan on the first email plus two or three follow-ups, a few days apart, then stop. Each follow-up should add something new (a date, a photo offer, capacity), not "just checking in."

## 2. Deliverability rules for a small business (official pages)

### 2.1 Google (Gmail)

Source: Email sender guidelines https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en and FAQ https://support.google.com/mail/answer/14229414?hl=en-GB [PRIMARY]

All senders (since Feb 1, 2024):
- Set up SPF or DKIM for your sending domain.
- Valid forward and reverse DNS (PTR) for sending IPs; use TLS.
- Keep the spam rate in Postmaster Tools below 0.3%; Google advises staying under 0.10%.
- Follow message format standard RFC 5322; do not fake a gmail.com From address.

Bulk senders (close to 5,000+ messages a day to personal Gmail accounts; status is permanent once reached):
- Both SPF and DKIM.
- A DMARC record (policy can be p=none), with the From domain aligned to SPF or DKIM.
- One-click unsubscribe (List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers, RFC 8058) on marketing and subscribed mail, plus a visible unsubscribe link. Google recommends honoring requests within 48 hours.
- Transactional mail (password resets, confirmations) is exempt from one-click unsubscribe.
- From November 2025, Google said non-compliant mail faces temporary and permanent rejections. (FAQ above; reported Nov 3, 2025 at https://www.spamresource.com/2025/11/google-warns-sender-requirements.html)

Google's guidance on list practice (same page): get opt-in, do not buy lists, do not mail people who did not sign up, and ramp volume slowly. Note for scripts: Google's general advice is against unsolicited mail. Cold B2B outreach is legal in the US (see CAN-SPAM) but Gmail filters judge it by recipient reaction. That is why small, relevant, low-volume sends matter.

### 2.2 Yahoo (and AOL)

Source: https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices [PRIMARY]
- All senders: SPF or DKIM at minimum; spam rate below 0.3%.
- Bulk senders: SPF and DKIM; DMARC at p=none or stricter that passes, From domain aligned; one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 POST preferred, mailto acceptable) for marketing mail; honor unsubscribes within 2 days.

### 2.3 Microsoft (Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live)

Source: Microsoft support page for bounce code 550 5.7.515 https://support.microsoft.com/en-au/topic/fix-ndr-error-550-5-7-515-in-outlook-com-34cfe8f8-6fbf-457e-9e8b-9e4dbaf4e0ef (page dated May 2, 2025) [PRIMARY]
- Applies to domains sending 5,000+ messages to Microsoft consumer mailboxes.
- Requires SPF and DKIM passing, a DMARC record (p=none is fine), and alignment with the From domain.
- Failing mail is rejected with "550 5.7.515 Access denied" (Microsoft's wording).
- Original announcement (Apr 2, 2025, updated Apr 29/30, 2025): https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%E2%80%99s-new-requirements-for-high%E2%80%90volume-senders/4399730 . [UNVERIFIED] We could not load the post body. Secondary sources report enforcement began May 5, 2025 and that the April update moved from junk-foldering to outright rejection (https://docs.contactlab.com/blog/2025/05/09/new-microsoft-rules , https://www.suped.com/learn/email-deliverability/what-are-the-new-microsoft-email-sender-requirements-and-observed-enforcement-bounces). Say "since May 2025" in scripts, not a specific day, unless verified.
- Microsoft has not published a complaint-rate threshold like Google and Yahoo's 0.3% (per contactlab above). [WEAK, secondary]

### 2.4 What this means for a tradesperson

A one-person shop sending 20 emails a week is nowhere near 5,000 a day, so the "bulk" rules do not legally bind them. But:
- Free addresses (gmail.com, yahoo.com) cannot carry your own DMARC, and Hunter's 2026 data shows custom-domain senders got 5.2% replies vs 2.5% from freemail. https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email [VENDOR]
- Setting up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your own domain is a one-time job in your email host's settings and is what all three mailbox providers check first.
- Keep volume low: Hunter 2026 saw 20 to 49 emails a day per account reply at 5.7% vs a 4.5% average. https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email [VENDOR]

### 2.5 CAN-SPAM (US law)

Source: FTC compliance guide https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business [PRIMARY]
- Applies to commercial email, with no exception for business-to-business.
- Rules: accurate From/To/Reply-To and routing; no deceptive subject line; identify the message as an ad; include a valid physical postal address; give a clear way to opt out; honor opt-outs within 10 business days; keep the opt-out working for at least 30 days after sending; do not charge or ask for more than an email address to opt out; you stay liable if someone else sends for you.
- A street address, a USPS P.O. box, or a private mailbox at a registered commercial mail receiving agency all count as the postal address.
- Penalty: up to $53,088 per violating email (FTC figure on that page; adjusted for inflation each year).
- Transactional or relationship messages (confirming a deal the person already agreed to, delivery, warranty, safety info) only need accurate routing info. The FTC reads these categories narrowly.
- [UNVERIFIED, legal judgment] A follow-up on a quote the customer asked for is probably not "transactional" under the FTC's narrow reading, because no deal is agreed yet. Safe practice: put your postal address and a one-line opt-out in every sales or follow-up email. The "identify as an ad" rule has no fixed wording; a plain statement of who you are and what you are offering is the common approach, but a script should not claim that alone satisfies the law. Recommend viewers check with counsel.

## 3. What GCs and property managers want from subs and vendors

Honest finding: there is no recent large US survey from AGC, NAHB, IREM or BOMA ranking "what I want in a sub's email." What exists is (a) the standard prequalification forms GCs use, (b) small academic surveys, and (c) insurance and risk guidance for property managers. Build scripts from these, not from invented "top 5" lists.

### 3.1 General contractors

- ConsensusDocs 721, the standard subcontractor prequalification statement, asks for: company structure, key personnel, financial information, relevant experience, safety program and safety record, bonding capacity, insurance coverage, licensing, and references or past performance. https://www.consensusdocs.org/contract/721-statement-of-qualifications/ and AGC summary https://agc.org/news/2011/02/09/summary-2011-updated-consensusdocs-constructor-and-subcontractor-statements [PRIMARY for what the form asks; it is a form, not a survey]
- Clemson study (Gajjar and Anderson, 2021), survey of 65 large GC firms in the Southeast: beyond price, GCs weighed past performance, experience, financial standing, available workforce, familiarity with the scope, and current workload. https://open.clemson.edu/aic-journal/vol46/iss1/7 [PRIMARY, small sample, regional]
- ProcurePro 2024 (appears Australian, sample size not given): 93.7% of builders said they would pick a high performer over a cheaper sub; top criteria were quality, safety, communication and cooperation, cost, and schedule. https://procurepro.co/news/performance-trumps-price-in-construction [VENDOR] [WEAK: no sample, not US]
- Why GCs want capacity: AGC's 2025 workforce survey (1,342 respondents, Jul to Aug 2025) found 45% of firms had a project delayed by worker shortages, and 20% said subs lost workers to immigration enforcement. https://www.agc.org/sites/default/files/users/user21902/2025%20Workforce%20Survey%20Analysis_V2.pdf [PRIMARY]
- Residential builders: NAHB's September 2026 survey found shortages of framing crews (57%) and finished carpenters (58%), plumbers (45%), HVAC (43%), roofers (36%); builders also report subcontractor shortages separately, with framers, carpenters, bricklayers and concrete workers over 50% on both. Sample size not stated in the post. https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/10/labor-shortages-a-growing-problem/ [PRIMARY, trade association]

Script-safe takeaway: when emailing a GC, state trade, area, license, insurance, crew size or open capacity, and one comparable job. Those map directly to what their prequal form asks.

### 3.2 Property managers

- National Apartment Association (May 25, 2019): a certificate of insurance only shows coverage exists; managers should require the owner and manager named as additional insured by endorsement, with coverage that fits the work. https://naahq.org/node/3256 [PRIMARY, industry association, legal author]
- IREM runs a course on auditing vendor COIs and endorsements, calling insurance compliance a constant headache for managers. https://irem.org/online-course/id-odcmmcs-0323hq02/how_to_audit_cois_and_endorsements_for_property_managers [PRIMARY that the issue matters; no data]
- CondoExec / Travers Hartnett Insurance (Aug 28, 2026): managers look for general liability, workers' comp, auto and umbrella, additional insured endorsement, waiver of subrogation, and current certificates at renewal. https://www.condoexec.com/?p=196 [VENDOR, insurance agency]
- Property Meld 2025 benchmarks ("thousands" of companies, no exact sample): sites where vendors accepted work orders in under 1.2 hours averaged 4.6 resident satisfaction vs 4.1 for over 4 hours; target acceptance time 0.5 to 1.2 hours. https://propertymeld.com/blog/what-we-learned-in-vendor-management-in-2025/ [VENDOR]
- [UNVERIFIED] BOMA: no public BOMA page found stating vendor selection criteria. Do not attribute claims to BOMA.

Script-safe takeaway: property managers care about paperwork (COI with additional insured, W-9, license) and speed of response on work orders. Say you can send a COI naming them, and state your response time honestly.

## 4. Replying to inbound leads

Evidence:
- HBR, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" (Oldroyd, McElheran, Elkington, March 2011): audit of 2,241 US companies; 37% responded within an hour, 23% never responded, average response 42 hours. Firms contacting within an hour were nearly 7 times as likely to qualify the lead as those waiting an hour or more, and over 60 times as likely as those waiting 24 hours. https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads (paywalled; figures confirmed via https://www.smartcompany.com.au/marketing/20110704-are-you-letting-online-leads-go-cold/) [PRIMARY study, 2011, B2B and B2C web leads, not trades-specific]
- Thumbtack (updated Nov 6, 2025): pros replying within 30 minutes are 25% more likely to be hired than those replying within three hours. https://www.thumbtack.com/guide/content/3-takeaways-from-the-2025-pro-summit [VENDOR, but it is marketplace first-party data on trades hiring]
- Thumbtack Pro Center (undated): 75% of hires go to pros who respond within three hours; customers may be comparing you with four other pros; top pros use the customer's name, are clear on price, explain next steps, and end with a question. https://pro-center.thumbtack.com/?p=434 [VENDOR] [WEAK: undated, no method]
- Boomerang: 1 to 3 questions raise reply odds about 50%. https://blog.boomerangapp.com/?p=3933 [VENDOR]
- Gong: in active deals, a specific time ask books more meetings than an open question (37% vs 25%). https://www.gong.io/blog/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings [VENDOR]
- Smartlead: most replies come within an hour of the send, so a lead who just wrote you is likely still at their screen. https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks [VENDOR, indirect]

What a strong first reply contains (built from the above):
1. Speed: same hour if at all possible. [HBR, Thumbtack]
2. Their name and their job in your words, so they know you read it. [Thumbtack Pro Center, WEAK]
3. One or two questions you actually need answered (photos, size, timeline). [Boomerang]
4. A concrete next step with a time window ("Tuesday or Wednesday morning"). [Gong deal-stage finding]
5. Price clarity: a range or how you price, if you can give it honestly. [Thumbtack Pro Center, WEAK]

## 5. Six templates (our own words)

Rules applied: plain text, no links, one ask, under 90 words in the body. The signature block is not counted. For any sales email, the signature carries a postal address and an opt-out line (CAN-SPAM; FTC page above). Bracketed items get filled in.

Standard signature (not counted):
```
[Name], [Company]
[License no.] | Insured
[Phone]
[Street address, P.O. box or registered mailbox]
Not interested? Reply "stop" and I won't email again.
```

### T1. Inbound lead reply (send within the hour)

```
Subject: Your [deck repair] on [Street]

Hi [Name],

Thanks for reaching out about the [deck repair]. I do this kind of work around [Town] most weeks.

Two quick questions so I can price it right:
1. Can you send 2 or 3 photos of the damaged boards?
2. Is there a date you need it done by?

I can come look Tuesday or Wednesday morning. Which works better?

[Name]
```

Line by line:
- Subject names their job and street: specific beats generic; short subjects get more replies (Boomerang 3 to 4 words; Hunter 5 to 6 words).
- "Thanks for reaching out about the [job]": shows you read it; Thumbtack top pros use the customer's name and their specifics (WEAK source).
- "I do this kind of work around [Town]": answers "can you do it here", the local-capability point GCs and owners vet for (Clemson: experience, familiarity with scope).
- Two numbered questions: 1 to 3 questions raised reply odds about 50% (Boomerang).
- Two time options: a specific time works better once someone is already engaged (Gong deal-stage, 37% vs 25%).
- Send within an hour: HBR 7x; Thumbtack 25%.

### T2. Quote follow-up 1 (2 to 3 days after sending the quote)

```
Subject: Re: Quote for [deck repair]

Hi [Name],

Following up on the quote I sent [Monday] for the [deck repair]. Happy to walk through any line on it.

One thing worth knowing: I have an opening the week of [date]. If you want that slot, I can hold it until [Friday].

Want me to pencil you in?

[Name]
```

Line by line:
- "Re:" on the same thread: Instantly reports step 2 that reads like a reply beats a formal reminder (about 30%, baseline undefined, WEAK). Keeping the thread also keeps context.
- No "just checking in": Gong found "I never heard back" lifts replies but cuts meetings 14% (WEAK method); add new information instead.
- Opening and hold date: gives a real reason to reply now. Truthful only; never invent scarcity (CAN-SPAM bans deception in subject lines, and invented claims break our own rules).
- One yes/no ask.
- Timing: Instantly recommends 3 to 4 days between touches (recommendation, not measured).

### T3. Quote follow-up 2 (about 4 to 5 days after T2)

```
Subject: Re: Quote for [deck repair]

Hi [Name],

Sometimes a quote lands higher than expected. If that's the case here, I can price it two ways: replace only the worst boards now, or do the full job in spring at the same rate.

If you've already picked someone, no problem at all. Just let me know so I can release your slot.

Which way are you leaning?

[Name]
```

Line by line:
- Names the most common silent objection (price) and offers options: adds new value, which is what Instantly says separates useful follow-ups (WEAK); avoids the "thoughts?" ask Gong tied to fewer meetings.
- Gives them an easy out: a clear no is useful data and lowers spam-button risk; Google tells senders to keep spam reports under 0.10%.
- One question.
- Follow-up count: Hunter 2026 saw three messages total perform best; Smartlead shows each extra step adds fewer replies.

### T4. Breakup message (about a week after T3, last touch)

```
Subject: Closing out your [deck repair] quote

Hi [Name],

I haven't heard back, so I'll assume the timing isn't right and close this out on my end.

If anything changes, reply here and I'll pick it back up. The quote is good through [date].

Thanks for considering us.

[Name]
```

Line by line:
- Clear subject: honest about what the email is (FTC: subject must match content).
- "I'll close this out": signals the last message. Hunter found sequences beyond three follow-ups did worse on average; stopping protects your reputation.
- Quote expiry date: a real, factual deadline.
- No question here on purpose: the ask is implicit (reply to reopen). [UNVERIFIED] No dataset we found measures "breakup" emails specifically; claims that they get unusually high reply rates come from vendor blogs without method. Do not say breakup emails "work best" in a script.

### T5. Cold email to a general contractor

```
Subject: Drywall sub for [Project name]

Hi [Name],

I saw [Company] pulled the permit for [Project name] on [Street]. I run a licensed, insured drywall crew of [6] based in [Town], and we have room for one more commercial job starting [month].

Recent work: [similar job, size, GC name if allowed].

I can send our COI, license and references today.

Are you still taking drywall numbers on this one?

[Name]
```

Line by line:
- Subject names their project and your trade: specific and honest.
- Permit reference: a real, verifiable reason you chose them. Personalized bodies replied at 5.6% vs 3.6% (Hunter 2026, VENDOR). Only use facts you checked.
- Licensed, insured, crew size, location, open capacity: the exact categories in the ConsensusDocs 721 prequal form, and the workforce and workload factors in the Clemson GC survey. AGC 2025 shows 45% of firms had shortage delays, so capacity is the hook.
- One comparable job: past performance and experience rank high with GCs (Clemson).
- Offer documents instead of attaching or linking: keeps the first email plain text with no links or attachments (HubSpot, Hunter tracking data, lemlist; mostly WEAK to VENDOR).
- Interest question, not a meeting demand: Gong found interest asks beat specific-time asks on cold email.
- About 60 words: inside the ranges that did well across Smartlead, Hunter and Instantly.

### T6. Cold email to a property manager

```
Subject: Plumbing backup for [Property name]

Hi [Name],

I'm a licensed plumber in [Town] and I already handle calls for [two] apartment buildings nearby. We answer work orders within [2 hours] on weekdays and take after-hours emergencies.

I can send a COI naming [Management company] and the owner as additional insured, plus a W-9.

Would it help to have a backup plumber on file for [Property name]?

[Name]
```

Line by line:
- Subject: their property and your trade.
- Local, licensed, nearby buildings: proof of experience with similar properties (Clemson experience factor; adjust if you have none).
- Response time stated as a number: Property Meld data ties fast vendor acceptance (under 1.2 hours) to higher resident satisfaction (VENDOR). Only state a time you can keep.
- COI with additional insured: matches what NAA and insurance advisers tell managers to require; it shows you know their process.
- "Backup plumber on file": low-commitment interest ask (Gong); most managers already have a vendor, so asking to be second is easier to say yes to (reasoning, not data).

## 6. Gaps and flags for the script writer

- No public large-scale study measures cold email to GCs or property managers specifically. All reply-rate numbers come from software-vendor datasets across many industries. Say "across millions of sales emails", not "contractors who do X get Y".
- Vendor "average reply rates" disagree by up to 6x; do not quote a single average.
- HBR 2011 is the best response-time study but is old and not trades-specific. Pair it with Thumbtack's 2025 figure.
- Microsoft enforcement date and the junk-to-reject change rest on secondary sources (official post body would not load). Microsoft's support page confirms the requirements and the reject code.
- CAN-SPAM application to quote follow-ups and how to "identify as an ad" in a one-to-one email are legal judgment calls; add a "not legal advice" line in any video that covers them.
- Not used: Gong 85M guide (gated), Woodpecker roundup numbers (no method), Lavender word count data (not visible as text), BOMA (no source found).
