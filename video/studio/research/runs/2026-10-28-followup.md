## 1. Speed to lead

### 1a. What the famous studies actually say

**The "5 minutes vs 30 minutes, 21x" claim (Lead Response Management study)**
- Source: "Lead Response Management" study, Dr. James Oldroyd (then MIT Sloan) with InsideSales.com (Dave Elkington, CEO). Presented Oct 16, 2007 at MarketingSherpa's B2B Demand Generation Summit. PDF copy dated 2008: https://www.mortech.com/hs-fs/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf
- Data: 3 years of data from 6 companies, about 15,000 web leads and about 100,000 call attempts, all from the InsideSales.com system. Funder not stated; the data and a co-author came from a company that sells call software.
- Finding: odds of contacting a lead were 100 times higher calling in 5 minutes vs 30 minutes; odds of qualifying were 21 times higher. 5 vs 10 minutes: contact odds fell 5x, qualify odds fell 4x.
- Big caveats: six B2B-style companies, 2004 to 2007 era, not home services; measured "contact" and "qualify", NOT closed jobs (the study says it did not address close rates); odds ratios, not a "21x more sales" figure. Vendor co-authored, not peer reviewed.
- Verdict: REAL but OLD and widely MISQUOTED. Never say "21 times more likely to close" or "MIT proved". Confidence LOW for use as a headline number.

**HBR "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" (2011)**
- Source: Oldroyd, McElheran, Elkington, Harvard Business Review, March 2011 (Vol 89, issue 3, p. 28). Record: https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9711 ; HBR page (paywalled): https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads
- Numbers (via a 2011 summary of the article, since HBR is paywalled: https://www.smartcompany.com.au/marketing/20110704-are-you-letting-online-leads-go-cold/):
  - Audit of 2,241 US companies sent a test web lead: 37% replied within an hour, 16% in 1 to 24 hours, 24% took longer than 24 hours, 23% never replied. Average reply among those who replied within 30 days: 42 hours.
  - Separate analysis of 1.25 million leads from 29 B2C and 13 B2B companies: firms that reached a lead within an hour were nearly 7 times as likely to qualify it as firms that waited one more hour, and more than 60 times as likely as firms that waited 24 hours or longer.
- Caveats: same author team and InsideSales data; "qualify" not "win the job"; 15+ years old; general business, not trades.
- Verdict: credible, published, but old. Confidence MEDIUM for "most businesses are slow" and "the first hour matters a lot." Do not say "60x more sales."

**Velocify "391% in the first minute" claim**
- Source: Velocify white paper "The Ultimate Contact Strategy" (deck on SlideShare): https://www.slideshare.net/Velocify/the-ultimate-contact-strategy ; 2016 press release: https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/velocify-research-shows-time-of-day-has-minimal-impact-on-sales-effectiveness-consider-quick-and-strategic-follow-up-instead-300275320.html
- Data: about 3.5 million leads from 400+ Velocify clients, leads from first half of 2012. Vendor that sells lead software.
- Findings: calling within 1 minute linked to a 391% conversion lift (baseline not clearly defined); lift falls to 17% at 24 hours. Reaching leads by the 6th call attempt captured 93% of converted leads. Leads sent emails were 16% more likely to be reached by phone.
- Verdict: vendor data, 2012, baseline unclear. Confidence LOW. Use only as "one large software study found the effect was strongest in the first minute," if at all.

**Drift secret-shopper test (2017)**
- Source: Drift, "We tested the response times of 433 sales teams", Feb 27, 2017: https://medium.com/marketing-102/we-tested-the-response-times-of-433-sales-teams-here-are-the-results-ef2fd005072e
- Finding: only 7% of 433 B2B SaaS companies replied within 5 minutes; 55% did not reply within 5 business days.
- Verdict: B2B software firms, not trades, vendor test. Confidence LOW for our audience.

### 1b. Newest and strongest evidence: peer-reviewed marketplace study (2026)

**"Speed Is a Signal: When Faster Replies Increase Hiring Likelihood"**
- Authors: On Amir (UC San Diego), Einav Hart (George Mason), Eric VanEpps (Vanderbilt), Ovul Sezer (Cornell). Journal: Management Science (INFORMS), DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2024.06185. Press coverage June to July 2026.
- Journal page: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06185
- University summaries: https://today.ucsd.edu/story/looking-for-work-dont-worry-about-seeming-too-eager and https://www.newswise.com/articles/fast-replies-boost-hiring-chances-study-shows
- Data: about 11.6 million interactions on Fiverr (a freelance services marketplace), plus 3 main experiments (3,600+ people) and 5 supplemental studies (about 5,000 people). Academic, not vendor-funded as far as stated.
- Findings: on Fiverr, a one-hour reply delay was linked to about a 46% drop in the chance of being hired; a full day was linked to about a 90% drop. Even 5 to 10 minute delays cut hiring noticeably. In experiments, faster repliers were seen as more responsive and were picked more, even when quality and message content were held equal. Speed lost its edge when people thought the reply was automated or AI-written.
- Caveats: the Fiverr part is correlational; freelance services, not home services.
- Verdict: best current evidence that reply speed itself changes who gets hired in a service marketplace. Confidence HIGH (for the finding), MEDIUM (for applying it to trades).

### 1c. Platform data for home services

**Thumbtack**
- Source: Thumbtack, "3 takeaways from the 2025 Pro Summit": https://www.thumbtack.com/guide/content/3-takeaways-from-the-2025-pro-summit
- Claim: pros who respond within 30 minutes are 25% more likely to get hired than pros who respond within three hours. Thumbtack also says its Front Desk tool replies in about two minutes on average.
- Caveats: platform's own data, no sample or method shown; Thumbtack sells the auto-reply tool.
- Confidence: MEDIUM. Also: a Thumbtack moderator said in a 2022 community post that customers are much more likely to hire when they hear back in the first hour, without a number (https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/comment/1513/). Do not quote a number from that.

**Angi**
- Source: Angi Pro help, "Why it's important to call quickly": https://intercom.help/angi/en/articles/6213728-why-it-s-important-to-call-quickly
- Claims: calling leads within 15 minutes "more than doubles" your chance of reaching the homeowner; a second call the next day raises your chance of reaching them by 45%; "the majority of closed leads" go to the first pro to make contact (no percentage given).
- Caveats: undated help page, no method shown, platform's own data. "Connect" is reaching the homeowner, not winning the job.
- Confidence: MEDIUM for the 15-minute and second-call claims; LOW for "first pro wins" (no number).

**Google Local Services Ads (LSA)**
- Ranking: https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305 . Google lists responsiveness among ranking inputs and says "Missed calls may negatively affect your responsiveness." Profile quality includes rating, review count and average response time. Google also says turning on message leads helps when you cannot answer the phone.
- Billing (new in 2026): https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435 . A missed call during your business hours is now a charged lead if the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds. Voicemail or a greeting during business hours counts as connected after 20 seconds. Repeat contacts from the same person within 15 days are not charged again. Effective Oct 1, 2026 per Google's emails to advertisers, reported Aug 25 to 26, 2026: https://seroundtable.com/google-lsa-missed-subsequent-calls-41940.html and https://ppc.land/invoca-packages-ai-agents-as-google-bills-missed-lsa-calls-over-20-seconds/
- Confidence: HIGH (Google's own pages). This is a strong, current, trade-specific hook: on LSA, you can now pay for calls you never answered.

**Yelp**
- No Yelp-published number on response time and hiring was found. Yelp shows a business's response time and response rate on its page (per Yelp's business guidance, seen only in a mirrored copy; no date). Treat as UNVERIFIED for any number.

### 1d. Homeowner surveys on speed
- Housecall Pro homeowner survey: 1,040 US homeowners, fielded Oct 23, 2025 via SurveyMonkey Audience, published Dec 10, 2025: https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/ . Chart says 97% value fast response and pricing; 59% expect text updates; 73% would refer a pro after an excellent experience; 68% would hire the same company again. Vendor-run survey, stated preference. Text and charts disagree in places, so use chart numbers. Confidence MEDIUM.

## 2. Missed calls

**Invoca Home Services Lead Conversion Benchmarks Report 2026 (best current source)**
- Source: https://invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026 (July 2026)
- Data: anonymized calls on Invoca's platform (part of 70M+ calls across 10 industries); home services includes carpeting, construction, doors and windows, home security, HVAC, lawn and trees, pest control, plumbing, restoration. Averages across Invoca customers, who tend to be larger, marketing-heavy firms. Home-services sample size not stated.
- Findings: 52% of all inbound home-services calls reach a person. Removing very short calls: 65% answered for calls over 15 seconds, 73% for calls over 30 seconds. So roughly 1 in 4 real calls (over 30 seconds) still go unanswered. Sub-industry answer rates range 32% to 74% (reported by PPC Land as plumbing 74%, HVAC 34%, construction 32%: https://ppc.land/invoca-packages-ai-agents-as-google-bills-missed-lsa-calls-over-20-seconds/). 45% of phone leads convert on the call; 55% of home-services businesses don't ask the caller to book.
- Caveats: vendor data; the 52% figure includes misdials and hang-ups; which calls count as "answered" depends on setup.
- Confidence: MEDIUM to HIGH (large real call data, recent, method stated briefly).

**The older "27% of home-services calls are missed" figure**
- Source: Invoca blog, Apr 6, 2023: https://www.invoca.com/blog/how-many-phone-calls-are-you-missing . "Thousands of calls" across 12 industries; home services 27% missed. No definition of "missed", no time period. Repeated by Housecall Pro: https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/missed-calls/
- Confidence: LOW to MEDIUM. Prefer the 2026 report.

**What callers do next**
- CallRail survey of 1,000 US consumers, blog dated Sep 25, 2025: https://www.callrail.com/blog/missed-calls-cost-businesses-more-than-ever . 82% say they'll call a competitor if a business doesn't answer; 78% say they have abandoned a business after an unanswered call; after a missed call 42% leave a voicemail and 21% immediately call another business. Vendor survey of stated behavior, fieldwork date not given. Confidence MEDIUM.
- Hiya "State of the Call 2026" (12,000+ consumers in 6 countries, Apr 14, 2026): https://blog.hiya.com/eroding-trust-is-crippling-the-voice-channel . 86% of calls from unidentified numbers go unanswered (41% wait for voicemail, 28% reject, 14% answer). 73% say businesses should identify themselves on caller ID. Not US-only. Relevance: when you call a lead back from an unknown number, most won't pick up, so text first or use branded caller ID. Confidence MEDIUM to HIGH.

**Famous missed-call stats we could NOT trace (do not use)**
- "85% of people whose call isn't answered won't call back": no primary source found; one review traces it to a distorted Vonage auto-attendant stat. UNVERIFIED.
- "80% of callers sent to voicemail don't leave a message": a 2014 trade article attributes it only to "Forbes" with no study, sample or year (https://www.destinationcrm.com/Articles/CRM-Insights/Insight/Business-Voicemail-Goes-Unanswered-100080.aspx). And CallRail 2025 found 42% do leave a voicemail. UNVERIFIED and likely wrong.
- "62% of calls to small businesses go unanswered" (411 Locals): only seen second-hand; 85 small businesses over 30 days; original not located. UNVERIFIED.
- "$1,200 lost per missed call": attributed to Invoca by Housecall Pro but not on the Invoca page cited. UNVERIFIED.

## 3. Quote and estimate follow-up

**Hard data is thin. Most "5 follow-ups" stats are fake.**
- The "80% of sales happen on the 5th to 12th contact" and "48% never follow up" stats are attributed to a "National Sales Executive Association" that VentureBeat could not find any record of (Stewart Rogers, Aug 15, 2014): https://venturebeat.com/business/these-incredible-sales-stats-everyone-cites-are-actually-completely-false . Do NOT use.
- ServiceTitan blog recap of a Jan 2026 webinar (Apr 17, 2026): https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/webinar-recap-follow-ups-101-back-2-basics . Says only 37% of estimates close on the first visit "according to ServiceTitan data"; recommends at least four follow-ups. The same post repeats generic sales stats (64% say no four times, 87% stop after one try) with no source. Use only the 37% figure, labeled as ServiceTitan's own number with no method shown. Confidence LOW to MEDIUM.

**Cadence guidance from field-service vendors (advice, not proven data)**
- Jobber Academy, "Quote follow-up email templates" (updated May 28, 2026): https://getjobber.com/academy/quote-follow-up-email-templates/ . First follow-up 1 to 2 days after the quote, second about a week later, last one 24 to 48 hours before the quote expires, then stop. Its automation example uses day 2, day 6, day 14.
- Jobber Academy, "How to follow up on a quote": https://getjobber.com/academy/how-to-follow-up-on-quote/ . First follow-up 2 to 3 days after the quote; keep it short with one clear next step; a contractor quoted calls text the most useful channel.
- Velocify (2012, vendor): reaching leads by the 6th call attempt captured 93% of converted leads; sending emails alongside calls was linked to 16% higher phone contact. Confidence LOW (old, vendor, not trades), but it supports "don't stop after one try."
- Angi help page: a second call the next day raised connect rates by 45% (see section 1c). Confidence MEDIUM.

**Text vs call vs email**
- No independent study comparing channels for contractor quotes was found.
- Housecall Pro homeowner survey (2025): 59% expect text updates. Confidence MEDIUM.
- Hiya 2026: most people ignore unknown callers, which supports a text before or after a callback. Confidence MEDIUM.
- Housecall Pro's "15% higher close rate with SMS" case study is about Housecall Pro's own software sales team, not contractors: https://www.casestudies.com/company/close-crm/case-study/how-housecall-pro-increased-their-close-rate-by-15-using-sms . Do not use for trades.
- Note: under the TCPA, texts count as calls (see section 4), so follow-up texts need the same consent care.

**What to say (evidence-based framing)**
- 2026 Management Science study: speed lost its advantage when the reply felt automated or AI-written; personal touches mattered. Supports: fast AND human-sounding.
- Podium/Momentive 2021 survey (1,004 US and Australian respondents): 74% more likely to text a local business if a real person reads and replies; 50% more likely to reply when the sender introduces themselves. https://www.cxtoday.com/loyalty-management/podium-report-shows-smb-conversational-messaging-desire/ . Vendor, 2021. Confidence MEDIUM to LOW.
- Invoca 2026: 55% of home-services businesses don't ask a phone lead to book. Supports: always ask for the next step.

## 4. Texting rules for US contractors in 2026 (plain-language level; not legal advice)

**Texts are calls under federal law.** The Supreme Court said a text to a cell phone counts as a "call" under the TCPA (Campbell-Ewald v. Gomez, Jan 20, 2016): https://library.nclc.org/article/supreme-court-applies-tcpa-text-messages-affirms-fccs-vicarious-liability-principles . Confidence HIGH.

**Consent**
- Marketing texts sent by automated systems need "prior express written consent": a signed written agreement (e-signatures, checkboxes count) that names the seller and the phone number. 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(2) and (f)(9). Read via a full-text copy of the rule: https://compliancepoint.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/47-CFR-64.1200.pdf (official: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200, blocked here).
- The rule's definition of "telephone solicitation" leaves out messages sent with the person's prior invitation or permission, or to someone you have an established business relationship with. In plain words: replying to someone who asked you for a quote is very different from texting a cold list. Confidence HIGH (rule text); how it applies to a specific campaign is a legal question.
- The FCC's 2023 "one-to-one consent" rule was struck down by the 11th Circuit in Jan 2025 (Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC): https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule . Confidence HIGH.

**Opt-outs**
- People can revoke consent any reasonable way. Replying "stop," "quit," "end," "revoke," "opt out," "cancel" or "unsubscribe" counts automatically. You must honor it within 10 business days. You may send one confirmation text, no marketing, ideally within 5 minutes. FCC order FCC 24-24, Federal Register Mar 5, 2024: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-03-05/html/2024-04587.htm . Main revocation rules took effect Apr 11, 2025: https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/fcc-sets-effective-date-for-tcpa-reasonable-consent-revocation-rules . Confidence HIGH.
- The extra "revoke all" piece (one opt-out covers all your message types) is delayed to Jan 31, 2027 by FCC order DA-26-12 (Jan 6, 2026): https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-01-16/fcc-extends-robocall-consent-revocation-rule-waiver-to-january-2027/ (FCC PDF blocked here: https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-12A1.pdf). Confidence HIGH (law firm report of an FCC order). Recheck before Jan 2027.

**Quiet hours**
- Federal: no telephone solicitations before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m., recipient's local time. 47 CFR 64.1200(c)(1) (same sources as above). Confidence HIGH.
- Some states are stricter, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m.: Florida (also max 3 messages per 24 hours on the same subject), Oklahoma, Washington, Maryland (SB 90, effective Jan 1, 2024, max 3 calls or texts per 24 hours). Sources: https://goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2023/02/02_28-mini-tcpa-laws-you-should-know , https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2023/05/florida-legislature-narrows-litigation-spawning-telephone-solicitation-act/ , https://kleinmoynihan.com/marylands-new-telemarketing-law-sb90/ . Confidence MEDIUM (law firm summaries, 2023; state laws change). Safe script line: "Keep marketing texts between 8 a.m. and 8 p.m. their time. That covers the strictest states."

**A2P 10DLC registration (carrier rule, not a law)**
- Any business texting US customers from a regular 10-digit number through software (not your personal phone) must register a Brand and a Campaign with The Campaign Registry. Unregistered traffic is blocked: Twilio says fully blocked after Aug 31, 2023 (https://pages.twilio.com/10DLC-HelpArticles-WW); Telnyx says blocked from Feb 3, 2025 (https://support.telnyx.com/en/articles/3679260-frequently-asked-questions-about-10dlc). Dates differ by provider; both agree unregistered business texting gets blocked now.
- Businesses without an EIN can use a "Sole Proprietor" brand with limits (Twilio: 1 campaign, 1 number, 1,000 messages a day).
- Confidence HIGH that registration is required (two major carrier-connected providers). Carrier pages themselves not read.

**CTIA Messaging Principles (industry best practice)**
- Latest version May 2023 (PDF, blocked here): https://api.ctia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/230523-CTIA-Messaging-Principles-and-Best-Practices-FINAL.pdf . Per a summary (https://bird.com/explained/compliance/what-are-the-ctia-messaging-principles): get opt-in before messaging, say who you are and how often you'll text, include opt-out info, send one confirmation after an opt-out then stop, and never buy, sell or share opt-in lists. Confidence MEDIUM (could not open the PDF directly).

## 5. Reviews and referrals

**Reviews: asking works**
- BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026: 1,002 US adults via SurveyMonkey panel, published Feb 11, 2026: https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/local-consumer-review-survey/
  - 78% were asked to write a review in the last 12 months; 83% of those asked went on to leave one (65% of all respondents).
  - 28% say they will "always" write a review if asked (up from 16% in 2025).
  - 97% read reviews for local businesses.
  - 68% will only use a business with 4+ stars; 31% only 4.5+.
  - 74% look for reviews from the last three months.
  - 89% expect owners to respond; 80% more likely to use a business that responds to all reviews; 50% put off by templated replies.
  - BrightLocal sells review software. Confidence MEDIUM to HIGH (clear method, recent, n about 1,000).
- Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking ("prominence"): https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091 . On LSA, rating and review count are profile-quality ranking inputs: https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305 . Confidence HIGH.
- How to ask on Google: share a Google review link or QR code: https://support.google.com/business/answer/7035772 and https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815

**Reviews: what you cannot do**
- Google: no discounts, freebies or rewards for reviews; no asking only happy customers ("selectively solicit positive reviews"); no discouraging negative reviews. https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114 and https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122 . Confidence HIGH.
- FTC rule on fake reviews (announced Aug 14, 2024; took effect 60 days after Federal Register publication on Aug 22, 2024): bans fake reviews, incentives tied to positive or negative sentiment, review suppression by threats, and undisclosed insider reviews. Civil penalties possible. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials . Confidence HIGH.

**Timing of the ask**
- No credible study found that pins the best hour or day to ask for a review in home services. UNVERIFIED. Safe advice: ask while the job is fresh (same day), make it one tap (link or QR), ask every customer.

**Referrals**
- Clear Seas Research (BNP Media) with The ACHR News, Aug 20, 2024: 67% of homeowners found their HVAC contractor by word of mouth or recommendation; 73% chose by contacting a company they'd used before. Sample size not stated. https://achrnews.com/articles/155046-survey-shows-how-homeowners-choose-their-hvac-contractor . Confidence MEDIUM.
- Housecall Pro 2025 homeowner survey: 73% would refer a pro after an excellent experience; 68% would hire the same company again. Confidence MEDIUM.
- Academic: referred bank customers were at least 16% more valuable over time and stayed longer (Schmitt, Skiera, Van den Bulte, Journal of Marketing, Jan 2011; about 10,000 customers of a German bank): https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Schmitt-Skiera-vandenBulte-2011-Referral-Programs-Customer-Value.pdf . Peer reviewed, but banking. Confidence HIGH for the finding, LOW for applying it to trades.
- Nielsen Trust in Advertising 2021: 89% trust recommendations from people they know (global figure). https://develop.nielsen.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2022/02/2021-Nielsen-Trust-In-Advertising-U.S.-sell-sheet.pdf . Confidence MEDIUM.
- "83% of satisfied customers are willing to refer but only 29% do (Texas Tech)": cited everywhere with no link, year or study. UNVERIFIED. Do not use.
