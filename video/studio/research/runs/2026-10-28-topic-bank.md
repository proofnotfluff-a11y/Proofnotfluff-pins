## How to read this

- **Opened and read**: "yes" means the desk fetched the page and read the number on it. "Secondary" means the number was read on a page that reports the study, not on the original paper. Secondary anchors are ranked lower and flagged.
- **Scores** are the desk's judgment, 1 to 5 each: D = owner demand, P = strength of proof, C = how copyable the takeaway is, S = how sendable it is. Rank follows the total, with ties broken by proof.
- **Demand signals**: forum threads were not collected. The web search budget for this run ran out before the forum pass. Demand signals are surveys the desk opened, or plain search phrases owners type (search volume not checked). A follow-up run can add Reddit or trade-forum threads.
- **Known items** (Speed Is a Signal, Thumbtack 30 min, HBR 2011, ServiceTitan good-better-best, ServiceTitan after-hours, BrightLocal 2026, JLC markup) are not pitched as new. Two appear as anchors because they were re-verified: JLC markup (#11) and ServiceTitan good-better-best (support in #13).

## The 40, in full

### 1. Raise prices with a reason customers accept
- **One idea**: Customers judge a price increase by the reason, so tie it to your costs, never to their bad luck.
- **Anchor fact**: In survey questions, raising lettuce prices after a wholesale cost increase was rated acceptable by 79% (N=101); a landlord raising rent after costs rose, 75% acceptable (N=151). A hardware store raising snow shovel prices the morning after a snowstorm: 82% unfair (N=107). Raising prices on stock already on the shelf after a wholesale increase: 79% unfair (N=147).
- **Source**: Kahneman, Knetsch, Thaler, "Fairness as a Constraint on Profit Seeking: Entitlements in the Market," American Economic Review 76(4), 1986. https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/course131/Kahneman-FairnessConstraintProfit-1986.pdf
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: Customers accept price increases. Just not this kind.
- **Takeaway (letter template)**: "Starting [date], our rates go up [X]%. Our labor and material costs went up this year, and we won't cut corners to hide it. Anything you've already approved stays at the price we quoted. Thanks for trusting us with your home."
- **Trades**: all
- **Demand signal**: Fed SBCS 2026 found rising costs the most common financial challenge for employer firms. Search phrase: "how to tell customers about a price increase" (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D5 P5 C5 S5

### 2. Close it before you leave the driveway
- **One idea**: The best shops ask for the decision on the visit, so there's less to chase later.
- **Anchor fact**: "Top performers close 77% of their estimates during the same visit." The rest: 62%. Top performers are the 90th percentile of revenue in their trade and segment. ServiceTitan notes this is correlation, not cause.
- **Source**: ServiceTitan, "The Profile of a Top Performer: Four Behaviors That Set the Top 10% of Contractors Apart," May 19, 2026. https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/top-performer-profile
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: The best shops don't chase quotes. They close them on site.
- **Takeaway (script)**: "Here are your options. If you want to go ahead today, I can get you on the schedule for [day]. What questions do you have before you pick one?"
- **Trades**: HVAC, plumbers, electricians, roofers, pest control, handymen
- **Demand signal**: Search phrase: "customer ghosted after estimate" (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D5 P4 C5 S5

### 3. Reply to every review
- **One idea**: Owners who answer reviews get more reviews and slightly better ratings.
- **Anchor fact**: "when hotels begin to respond, they receive 12 percent more reviews" and "their ratings increase by an average of 0.12 stars."
- **Source**: Proserpio and Zervas, "Online Reputation Management: Estimating the Impact of Management Responses on Consumer Reviews," Marketing Science; INFORMS press release, Sept 8, 2017. https://www.informs.org/News-Room/INFORMS-Releases/News-Releases/Want-to-improve-your-business-s-online-ratings-Make-sure-to-respond-to-reviews
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: Answering reviews got businesses 12% more of them.
- **Takeaway (two reply templates)**: Good: "Thanks, [first name]. Glad the [job] went smooth. We're here when you need us. [Your name]." Bad: "Sorry we missed on [specific thing]. I want to make it right. Call me directly at [number]. [Your name]."
- **Trades**: all
- **Demand signal**: Jobber 2026 owner survey: reviews are the #2 thing customers weigh (51%). Search phrase: "should I respond to Google reviews" (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D4 P5 C5 S5

### 4. Know your close rate, then beat the median
- **One idea**: Your close rate is a number you can compare, so track it monthly.
- **Anchor fact**: "Regional median close rates range from 31% in the Northeast to 38% in the West." Among the top 25% by close rate in each region, medians range from 70% to 73%. Data: May 2025 to July 2026 ServiceTitan platform sample; not representative of the whole industry; the page does not define the formula.
- **Source**: ServiceTitan, "Trades Industry Trends: Call Booking, Close Rates & Off-Hours Calls," Oct 22, 2026. https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/quarterly-industry-trends
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: If you close 1 in 3 quotes, you're median.
- **Takeaway (formula)**: Close rate = jobs sold ÷ estimates given, same month. Write both numbers on a whiteboard every Friday.
- **Trades**: all that quote (bids)
- **Demand signal**: Search phrase: "what is a good closing rate for contractors" (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D5 P4 C5 S4

### 5. Put a monthly payment next to the price
- **One idea**: Offer financing on big tickets because many customers can't pay all at once.
- **Anchor fact**: "financed jobs have a median ticket 64% higher than comparable jobs" that were not financed (H2 2025). Plumbing: largest lift, 75%. 51% of ServiceTitan contractors used financing in 2025.
- **Source**: ServiceTitan, "Financed ticket sizes and adoption rates in the trades," July 8, 2026. https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/financing-in-the-trades
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: Financed jobs ran 64% bigger. Here's why that's not magic.
- **Takeaway (script)**: "Most folks split a job this size into monthly payments. Want me to show you that next to the pay-in-full price?"
- **Trades**: HVAC, plumbers, roofers, electricians, GCs
- **Demand signal**: ServiceTitan, Synchrony and Visa press release (Sept 30, 2025): "41% of consumers actively seek financing options." Search phrase: "should I offer financing to customers contractor" (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D4 P4 C5 S4

### 6. Book the call, not just answer it
- **One idea**: Answering the phone is half the job; turning the call into a booked appointment is the other half.
- **Anchor fact**: "Top performers book 62% of their inbound lead calls." "The rest average 39%." Separately, regional median booking rates range from 28% (Northeast) to 38% (Midwest) (Oct 22, 2026 page).
- **Source**: ServiceTitan, Profile of a Top Performer, May 19, 2026. https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/top-performer-profile
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: Most calls that come in never get booked.
- **Takeaway (script)**: "I can get someone out [today at 2 or tomorrow at 9]. Which works better?" Always offer two specific times, never "when works for you?"
- **Trades**: HVAC, plumbers, electricians, pest control, cleaners, handymen
- **Demand signal**: Search phrase: "how to book more calls HVAC CSR script" (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D4 P4 C5 S4

### 7. 1% on price beats 1% more jobs
- **One idea**: A small price increase moves profit more than chasing more volume.
- **Anchor fact**: "a 1% improvement in price, assuming no loss of volume, increases operating profit by 11.1%." Volume: "improving unit volume by 1% yields a 3.3% increase in operating profit." Based on average economics of 2,463 Compustat companies.
- **Source**: Marn and Rosiello, "Managing Price, Gaining Profit," Harvard Business Review, Sept to Oct 1992. https://pages.charlotte.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/868/2014/12/ManagingPrice.pdf
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: Raising prices 1% did more than 1% more customers.
- **Takeaway (formula)**: Profit gain from a price raise ≈ price raise % ÷ your net margin %. At a 6.3% net margin (NAHB remodeler average), +1% price ≈ +16% net profit, if you lose no jobs.
- **Trades**: all
- **Demand signal**: Search phrase: "should I raise my prices" small business (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D5 P4 C4 S5

### 8. Ask everyone for a review. Never cherry-pick
- **One idea**: Asking only happy customers breaks Google's rules, and paying for good reviews breaks the FTC's.
- **Anchor fact**: Google prohibits businesses that "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers." FTC final rule bars incentives "conditioned on the writing of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment, either positive or negative," and lets the FTC seek civil penalties.
- **Source**: Google Maps User Contributed Content Policy, Prohibited and restricted content. https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en ; FTC press release, Aug 14, 2024. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials
- **Opened and read**: yes (both)
- **Hook**: Only asking happy customers for reviews? Google bans that.
- **Takeaway (text template)**: "Hi [name], thanks for having us out today. If you have 30 seconds, an honest review helps neighbors find us: [link]. Good or bad, we read every one."
- **Trades**: all
- **Demand signal**: Search phrase: "how to get more Google reviews" (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D5 P5 C4 S4

### 9. Every job sells the street
- **One idea**: Neighbors copy neighbors, so market hardest right around the job you just finished.
- **Anchor fact**: At the average number of owner-occupied homes in a zip code, an additional solar installation "increases the probability of an adoption in the zip code by 0.78 percentage points."
- **Source**: Bollinger and Gillingham, "Peer Effects in the Diffusion of Solar Photovoltaic Panels," Marketing Science 31(6), 2012. https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormksc:v:31:y:2012:i:6:p:900-912
- **Opened and read**: yes (abstract)
- **Hook**: Your best ad is the job you just finished.
- **Takeaway (checklist)**: After every job: ask for a yard sign for 7 days; hang door hangers on the 5 houses each side and across; say on the hanger "We just finished [job] at [street name]."
- **Trades**: roofers, landscapers, painters, HVAC, GCs, pest control
- **Demand signal**: Search phrase: "do door hangers work for contractors" (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D4 P5 C5 S4

### 10. Count your cash buffer days
- **One idea**: Know how many days your business could pay its bills with no money coming in.
- **Anchor fact**: "The median small business holds 27 cash buffer days in reserve." Industry medians: Construction 20, Repair and Maintenance 18. Sample: 597,000 Chase business accounts, Feb to Oct 2015.
- **Source**: JPMorgan Chase Institute, "Cash is King: Flows, Balances, and Buffer Days," Sept 1, 2016. https://www.jpmorganchase.com/institute/all-topics/business-growth-and-entrepreneurship/report-cash-flows-balances-and-buffer-days
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: The typical contractor has about 20 days of cash.
- **Takeaway (formula)**: Cash buffer days = cash in the bank ÷ (last 90 days of spending ÷ 90).
- **Trades**: all
- **Demand signal**: Billd 2026 subcontractor report (June 23, 2026): "83% of subcontractor owners now worried about cash flow." Fed SBCS 2025: 51% cite uneven cash flows.
- **Scores**: D5 P4 C5 S4

### 11. Markup is not margin
- **One idea**: If you add 50% to cost, you are not making 50%.
- **Anchor fact**: "Therefore, a 50% markup is equivalent to a 33% margin." "So, a 100% markup gives a 50% margin."
- **Source**: Sal Alfano, "Markup vs. Margin," Journal of Light Construction (no date on page). https://www.jlconline.com/business/markup-vs-margin_o/
- **Opened and read**: yes (known anchor, re-verified)
- **Hook**: 50% markup is not 50% profit. It's 33%.
- **Takeaway (formula)**: Price = cost ÷ (1 - target margin). Want 40% margin on $1,000 cost: $1,000 ÷ 0.60 = $1,667.
- **Trades**: all
- **Demand signal**: Search phrase: "markup vs margin contractor" (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D5 P3 C5 S4
- **Note**: The desk found the page's second formula ("Markup = Margin / Cost") does not hold in general. Use only the worked examples.

### 12. Charge for the trip
- **One idea**: A service call fee is normal in the market, so stop driving out for free.
- **Anchor fact**: Angi lists a typical plumber service call fee of $100 to $250. "Generally, plumbing companies will charge an additional $50 to $300 in trip fees" for customers beyond the service area (more than 10 miles). Average plumber visit: about $338.
- **Source**: Angi, "How Much Does Plumbing Repair Cost? [2025 Data]," no date on page. https://www.angi.com/articles/plumber-cost.htm
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: Free estimates cost you gas, time, and a job.
- **Takeaway (script)**: "Our service call is $[X]. It covers the trip and the diagnosis, and it comes off the bill if you go ahead with the repair today."
- **Trades**: plumbers, HVAC, electricians, handymen, pest control
- **Demand signal**: Search phrase: "should I charge for estimates" (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D5 P3 C5 S4

### 13. Three options, and the middle one sells
- **One idea**: When there's a cheaper and a pricier choice, people lean to the middle.
- **Anchor fact**: In Simonson's Study 1, the same apartment option had a 50% share when it was not the middle choice and 66% when it was the compromise option (significant at 0.05).
- **Source**: Simonson, "Choice Based on Reasons: The Case of Attraction and Compromise Effects," Journal of Consumer Research 16(2), 1989. https://cognition.aau.at/bg/BA/Simonson%20%281989%29.pdf
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: Want them to pick the good option? Make it the middle.
- **Takeaway (template)**: Option A: fix it (just the repair). Option B: fix it right (repair + the part that fails next + 1-year warranty). Option C: replace it. Build B as the one you'd pick for your own house.
- **Trades**: all
- **Demand signal**: Search phrase: "good better best pricing contractors" (volume not checked). Support: ServiceTitan top performers present good-better-best on 57% of residential jobs vs 41% (known item, re-verified).
- **Scores**: D4 P4 C5 S4

### 14. One star is real money
- **One idea**: A better rating moves revenue for small independent businesses.
- **Anchor fact**: "a one-star increase in Yelp rating leads to a 5-9 percent increase in revenue," driven by independent restaurants; chain ratings had no effect.
- **Source**: Luca, "Reviews, Reputation, and Revenue: The Case of Yelp.com," HBS Working Paper 12-016, revised March 2016. https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:hbs:wpaper:12-016
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: One more star meant up to 9% more revenue.
- **Takeaway (checklist)**: Ask every customer. Reply to every review. Fix the thing the 3-star reviews mention twice.
- **Trades**: all
- **Demand signal**: Jobber 2026 owner survey: reviews weigh with 51% of customers.
- **Scores**: D4 P5 C3 S4

### 15. Finish your Google Business Profile
- **One idea**: An incomplete profile costs you trust before anyone calls.
- **Anchor fact**: "Customers are 2.7 times more likely to consider a business reputable if they find a complete Business Profile" and "70% more likely to visit and 50% more likely to consider purchasing."
- **Source**: Google Business Profile Help, "Complete your Business Profile on Google." https://support.google.com/business/answer/10515606?hl=en
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: Google says a full profile looks 2.7x more reputable.
- **Takeaway (checklist)**: Hours, service area, every service listed, primary category, 10 real job photos, phone, website, description. 20 minutes.
- **Trades**: all
- **Demand signal**: Search phrase: "how to set up Google Business Profile for contractors" (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D4 P3 C5 S4
- **Note**: Google gives no method or date for these figures. Say "Google says."

### 16. What actually ranks you in Google Maps
- **One idea**: Your category and business name matter more than posting every day.
- **Anchor fact**: 47 experts ranked map factors: #1 "Primary GBP Category," #2 proximity to searcher, #3 "Keywords in GBP Business Title," #6 high ratings, #9 review count with text, #11 "Recency of Reviews." "Keywords in Owner Responses to Reviews" ranked 182.
- **Source**: Whitespark, 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors, Nov 6, 2025. https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: The #1 Maps ranking factor takes 10 seconds to fix.
- **Takeaway (checklist)**: Pick the primary category that matches your money job. Add secondary categories. Get reviews steadily, not in bursts. Never stuff keywords into your name (Google can suspend for it).
- **Trades**: all
- **Demand signal**: Search phrase: "how to rank higher on Google Maps" (volume not checked).
- **Scores**: D5 P3 C4 S3
- **Note**: This is an expert opinion survey, not Google data.

### 17. Show the receipt for your price increase
- **One idea**: Government data shows trade wages rising, which is a fair reason customers accept.
- **Anchor fact**: ECI wages and salaries for "Construction, extraction, farming, fishing, and forestry occupations" rose 3.9% in the 12 months to June 2026 (civilian workers, not seasonally adjusted). Private construction industry: 3.5%.
- **Source**: BLS, Employment Cost Index News Release, 2026 Q2, July 31, 2026. https://www.bls.gov/news.release/eci.htm
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: Your crew costs went up 3.9%. Did your prices?
- **Takeaway (line for the letter)**: "Federal data shows trade wages rose about 4% in the last year. Our rates are adjusting to match."
- **Trades**: all
- **Demand signal**: Fed SBCS 2026: rising costs of goods, services and wages was the most common financial challenge.
- **Scores**: D4 P5 C4 S3

### 18. Everyone is passing costs on
- **One idea**: Most small firms facing higher costs passed some of it to customers, so you're not alone.
- **Anchor fact**: "Seventy-seven percent of firms reported one or both of these challenges" (rising costs; tariff costs). Among firms sourcing inputs from outside the US that saw higher input costs, 76% passed at least some on to customers.
- **Source**: Federal Reserve Banks, 2026 Report on Employer Firms (2025 Small Business Credit Survey, fielded Sept 3 to Nov 14, 2025), March 2026. https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/fsbsite/reports/2026/2026-report-on-employer-firms/2026-report-on-employer-firms.pdf
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: 3 in 4 small firms say costs are squeezing them.
- **Takeaway (checklist)**: List your 5 biggest costs. Price each vs. last year. If total cost is up more than your prices, the gap is your raise.
- **Trades**: all
- **Demand signal**: the same survey (77%).
- **Scores**: D4 P5 C3 S3
- **Note**: the 76% applies only to importing firms with higher input costs, not all firms.

### 19. Know what a normal margin looks like
- **One idea**: Compare your gross and net margin to a real benchmark, not a guess.
- **Anchor fact**: Remodelers' 2024 results: "an average gross profit margin of 29.9%" and "an average net profit margin of 6.3%." Operating expenses: 23.6% of revenue.
- **Source**: NAHB Eye on Housing, "Remodelers Saw Profit Margin Gains in 2024," Rose Quint, April 8, 2026. https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/04/remodelers-saw-profit-margin-gains-in-2024/
- **Opened and read**: yes
- **Hook**: The average remodeler keeps about 6 cents per dollar.
- **Takeaway (formula)**: Gross margin = (revenue - job costs) ÷ revenue. Net margin = profit after overhead ÷ revenue. Check both on last year's P&L.
- **Trades**: GCs, remodelers, painters, roofers, handymen
