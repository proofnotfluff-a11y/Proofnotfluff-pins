# Trades-first insights bank: what trade creators teach, backed by trade data

Research desk run, Oct 28, 2026, evening (America/Chicago). Todd's brief, same evening: lessons must come from the trades,
built around what successful trade creators teach, with credible sources. Every number below was read on its own page
by this desk on Oct 28, 2026 (marked "re-read Oct 28, 2026"). Quotes are 15 words or fewer, one per source.

## How to read this

- **Trade evidence only.** Every main number is about trade or home-service businesses or their customers: trade data
  (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, Angi, Thumbtack, Invoca, Billd), trade associations (ACCA, ABC, NAHB), trade press
  (ACHR News, JLC), or government data on construction (BLS). Where a non-trade study existed, it was dropped.
- **Vendor data is vendor data.** ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, Angi, Thumbtack, Invoca and Billd sell to
  contractors. Their numbers describe their own customers or their own surveys, are mostly correlation, and are said as
  "ServiceTitan's data on its customers", never as "the industry".
- **Creator claims are leads, not sources.** A number a coach says on a podcast (for example Joe Crisara's 60%, 80% and
  92%) is not repeated as fact. We teach the method and back it with the trade data.
- **Engagement.** Platform pages (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube) could not be read as text from here. Views come from
  third-party stats pages and are marked as such.

("how I charge"), a single number to track ("the KPI that is not revenue"), and a blunt consequence line ("busy but

## 2. The bank: 21 lessons, ranked

Rank = how many creators teach it, times how strong the trade evidence is, times how copyable the takeaway is.

### 1. Book the call, don't just answer it
- **Idea:** A ringing phone is a lead; a booked time is a job. Ask for the booking on every call.
- **Creators:** ServiceTitan's own content ([Oct 25, 2022](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/data-call-booking-rates)); Ellen Rohr and Tommy Mello (lead close rate as a core number, [Site Shed ep. 436](https://feeds.buzzsprout.com/2198439/episodes/16722035-the-few-numbers-that-make-all-the-difference-ft-ellen-rohr-ep-436?t=0)).
- **Evidence:** (a) Top performers (90th revenue percentile in trade and market) book 62% of inbound lead calls; the rest 39%. Participating ServiceTitan customers, trailing 12 months to January 2026, correlation. [ServiceTitan, May 19, 2026](https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/top-performer-profile). (b) Regional median booking rates 28% (Northeast) to 38% (Midwest); top 25% medians 76% to 82%; May to July 2026; ServiceTitan sample, "not representative" of the whole industry. [ServiceTitan, Oct 22, 2026](https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/quarterly-industry-trends). (c) 55% of home services businesses don't ask leads to book; 52% of all inbound calls reach a person. [Invoca, Jul 2026](https://invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026). All re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "On ServiceTitan's data, the top shops book about 6 in 10 lead calls. Everyone else, about 4 in 10."
- **Hook:** "Plumbers, HVAC guys: the top shops book 62% of their calls. Do you know yours?"
- **Takeaway (call close):** "I can get someone out [today at 2] or [tomorrow at 9]. Which works better?" Then: "Can I get the address and the best number to text you the tech's photo?" Track it: booked calls / lead calls, every Friday.
- **Visual world:** an old telephone switchboard. Lead calls light up; the operator plugs each into BOOKED or a dead jack; the board's counter reads 62 vs 39.

### 2. Lead with the monthly payment
- **Idea:** On a big job, show the monthly number first, next to the full price. Contractors who do sell more financed, better-equipped jobs.
- **Creators:** Joe Crisara and Tommy Mello (financing as the primary offer, [May 26, 2026](https://www.audioscrape.com/podcast/the-home-service-expert-podcast/episode/the-sales-reframe-that-changes-how-every-technician-thinks-about-their-job-18580)); ServiceTitan's financing content.
- **Evidence:** (a) ACCA and Farmington Consulting Group "Contractor of the Future", more than 1,000 HVAC contractors: leading with a monthly payment, 42% of new or replacement sales financed vs 21% when leading with the overall price; entry-level equipment mix 34% vs 42%; average close rate 38% without financing, 49% with it; 72% of contractors lead with the overall price. [ACHR News, Feb 10, 2026](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/165799-survey-reveals-strategies-to-boost-hvac-close-rates-by-double-digits). (b) 62% of homeowners say they are more likely to go ahead when financing is offered (1,100+ US homeowners). [Housecall Pro, May 13, 2026](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-spending-report-2026-release/). (c) Financed jobs: median ticket 64% higher than comparable non-financed jobs (plumbing 75%, HVAC 66%, electrical 66%), H2 2025, correlation. [ServiceTitan, Jul 8, 2026](https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/financing-in-the-trades). All re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "In an ACCA survey of over 1,000 HVAC contractors, leading with the monthly payment doubled the share of financed sales, 21% to 42%." Correlational survey: say "contractors who", never "this makes".
- **Hook:** "HVAC owners: 72% of you lead with the big number. The ones who lead with the monthly one financed twice as many jobs."
- **Takeaway (quote line):** "This system is [$X] a month for [term], or [$Y] paid in full. Which way works better for you?" Put the monthly line above the total on every replacement quote.
- **Visual world:** a vending machine. One giant price coin jams the slot; the tech flips a lever and it breaks into small monthly coins that drop in and the machine lights up.

### 3. Raise your prices; most trades already did
- **Idea:** Rising costs are the normal reason to raise prices, and most trade owners did it last year. Say the reason, keep approved jobs at the quoted price.
- **Creators:** Danielle Putnam (reset prices from real billable hours, [Feb 25, 2026](https://hvachats.podbean.com/e/the-hvac-pricing-mindset-and-strategies-to-drive-growth-in-2026/)); Keith Kalfas (job minimum, [Jun 24, 2025](https://thelandscapingemployeetrap.libsyn.com/why-we-have-a-700-minimum-to-do-any-landscaping-job)); Michael Stone (price to cover overhead and profit).
- **Evidence:** (a) 65% of home service businesses raised prices last year; HVAC 83%, plumbing 74%, electrical 74%, roofing 74%, cleaning 55%; reasons: inflation and material costs 72%, labor costs 50%. 1,050 US home service owners, December 2025, Conjointly. [Jobber 2026 Home Service Trends Report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/) (modified Mar 25, 2026). (b) Wages and salaries for construction and extraction occupations (civilian) up 3.9% in the 12 months to June 2026; all civilian workers 3.2%. [BLS ECI, Jul 31, 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/eci.htm). Both re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "In Jobber's survey of about a thousand home service owners, 83% of HVAC companies raised prices last year." "Federal data: construction trade wages rose 3.9% in the year to June."
- **Hook:** "83% of HVAC companies raised prices last year. Here's how to tell your customers."
- **Takeaway (letter):** "Starting [date], our rates go up [X]%. Our labor and material costs rose this year: trade wages alone are up about 4% (federal data). Anything you've already approved stays at the price we quoted."
- **Visual world:** a tool-shop counter with a split-flap price board. Cost receipts (wages, copper, fuel) slide onto the counter one by one, and each flips the board a notch.

### 4. Four options, best one first
- **Idea:** Don't hand over one price. Lay out options, start at the top, and let the customer choose.
- **Creators:** Joe Crisara (premium, mid-range, economy, in that order; [Nov 3, 2020](https://servicetitan.com/podcasts/toolbox-for-the-trades/joe-crisara-interview)); Tommy Mello ("options, not ultimatums", [May 26, 2026](https://www.audioscrape.com/podcast/the-home-service-expert-podcast/episode/the-sales-reframe-that-changes-how-every-technician-thinks-about-their-job-18580)); Danielle Putnam (menu pricing).
- **Evidence:** (a) Contractors offering four or more options close 52%; one, two or three options 42%; only 10% offer four or more. ACCA and Farmington, 1,000+ contractors. [ACHR News, Feb 10, 2026](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/165799-survey-reveals-strategies-to-boost-hvac-close-rates-by-double-digits). (b) Top performers present good-better-best on 57% of residential jobs; the rest 41%; trailing 12 months to January 2026. [ServiceTitan, May 19, 2026](https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/top-performer-profile). Both re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "In ACCA's survey, contractors who offered four or more options closed 52% of the time; with three or fewer, 42%." Never "three beats one": the survey groups 1 to 3.
- **Hook:** "Only 1 in 10 HVAC contractors gives four options. They close 52%."
- **Takeaway (option sheet, top first):** Best: replace with the top system and the longest warranty. Better: replace, standard warranty. Good: repair plus the part that fails next. Basic: the repair only. Close with: "Which of these should we do?"
- **Visual world:** an RPG item shop. Four items on the shelf, priciest on the top shelf with a glow; the customer sprite walks the shelf top down and picks.

### 5. Answer the lead inside 30 minutes, and call twice
- **Idea:** Speed decides platform leads. Reply within half an hour, and if you miss them, call again the next day.
- **Creators:** Thumbtack's Pro Summit takeaways; Angi Pro help; Ellen Rohr (leads and lead close rate).
- **Evidence:** (a) Thumbtack: pros who respond within 30 minutes are 25% more likely to be hired than pros who take three hours (Thumbtack's own data, its pros). [Updated Nov 6, 2025](https://www.thumbtack.com/guide/content/3-takeaways-from-the-2025-pro-summit). (b) Angi: calling within 15 minutes "more than doubles" the chance of reaching the homeowner; a second call the next day raises it 45%. [Angi Pro help, Aug 14, 2026](https://intercom.help/angi/en/articles/6213728-why-it-s-important-to-call-quickly). (c) Owners report what customers expect: immediate 28%, within an hour 28%. [Jobber 2026 report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/). All re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "Thumbtack says its pros who answer within 30 minutes get hired 25% more than pros who take 3 hours." "Angi says a second call the next day raises your odds of reaching them by 45%."
- **Hook:** "Answered that lead in 3 hours? Thumbtack says the 30-minute pro was 25% more likely to get it."
- **Takeaway (two-touch rule):** Inside 30 minutes: text first ("Hi [name], [you] from [company]. Got your request about [job]. Can I call you in 5 minutes?"), then call. No answer: call again the next day at a different time.
- **Visual world:** a top-down racing game. The lead is a checkered flag; your truck and three rival trucks race; the lap timer shows 0:30 vs 3:00.

### 6. Know what a normal margin looks like
- **Idea:** Busy is not profitable. Compare your gross and net margin to a real trade benchmark.
- **Creators:** Michael Stone ("why roofers stay busy but still go broke"); Shawn Van Dyke ([ep. 187](https://contractor-evolution.cohostpodcasting.com/episodes/187-how-to-be-a-profit-first-contractor-shawn-van-dyke)); Ellen Rohr (sales, profit, cash).
- **Evidence:** (a) Remodelers, fiscal 2024: average gross margin 29.9%, net margin 6.3% (highest since 1996's 6.8%); operating expenses 23.6% of revenue; average revenue $2.7 million. NAHB Remodelers' Cost of Doing Business Study, 2026 edition. [NAHB Eye on Housing, Apr 8, 2026](https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/04/remodelers-saw-profit-margin-gains-in-2024/). (b) Subcontractors' average net margin fell to 13.3% (600+ subs, suppliers and GCs). [Billd, Jun 23, 2026](https://billd.com/blog/billds-sixth-annual-national-subcontractor-market-report-reveals-the-playbook-behind-the-industrys-fastest-growing-subcontractors/). Both re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "NAHB's study: the average remodeler kept 6.3 cents of profit on each dollar in 2024."
- **Hook:** "Remodelers: the average one kept 6.3 cents on the dollar. Are you above it?"
- **Takeaway (formulas):** Gross margin = (revenue minus job costs) / revenue. Net margin = profit after overhead and your own pay / revenue. Pull last year's P&L; write both next to 29.9% and 6.3%.
- **Visual world:** an arcade high-score board. Initials and margins scroll; the NAHB line sits at 6.3; the owner types their initials and their number lands above or below it.

### 7. Your next job is in your old invoices
- **Idea:** Most homeowners hire someone they've used before or someone a friend sent. Stay in touch with past customers before you buy more leads.
- **Creators:** Tommy Mello ("own 15% of your market before a new one", [CreatorDB, Oct 6, 2026](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/officialtommymello/)).
- **Evidence:** (a) 73% of homeowners chose the same HVAC contractor they had used before; 67% found their contractor by word of mouth or recommendation. Clear Seas Research (BNP Media) with ACHR News, sample size not stated. [ACHR News, Aug 20, 2024](https://achrnews.com/articles/155046-survey-shows-how-homeowners-choose-their-hvac-contractor). (b) Homeowners who hired a pro, Q1 2026: 31% had worked with them before, 31% came by a friend or family referral. HIRI tracker, 1,198 homeowners, March to April 2026. [Angi Pro help, Jul 17, 2026](https://intercom.help/angi/en/articles/15972206-the-backlog-boom-73-of-homeowners-have-projects-waiting). Both re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "In one HVAC survey, 73% of homeowners went back to a contractor they'd used before."
- **Hook:** "73% of homeowners went back to the HVAC company they already knew. Is that you?"
- **Takeaway (past-customer calendar):** 1 week after the job: "Everything still working?" 6 months: seasonal check reminder. 12 months: "It's been a year since we [job]. Want us to look at it before [season]?" Pull the list from your invoices once a month.
- **Visual world:** a dungeon treasure room. Dusty chests labeled with old invoice dates; each one opened with a text spills coins.

### 8. Half your competitors ignore their reviews
- **Idea:** Homeowners read reviews to pick a trade company, and many contractors never answer them. Answering is an easy edge.
- **Creators:** practice advice across trade marketing coaches (no single creator page read with a number).
- **Evidence:** 91% of homeowners rate online reviews as important in choosing an HVAC contractor (400 homeowners); 56% would look on Google; 45% of contractors answer reviews on third-party sites, 43% don't, 13% don't know. Clear Seas Research with ACHR News. [ACHR News, Sept 17, 2024](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/155206-91-of-homeowners-rely-on-online-reviews-before-picking-contractor). Re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "In a 2024 HVAC survey, 9 in 10 homeowners said reviews matter when they pick a contractor, and 43% of contractors said they don't answer reviews."
- **Hook:** "43% of HVAC contractors don't answer their reviews. 91% of homeowners read them."
- **Takeaway (two replies):** Good: "Thanks, [first name]. Glad the [job] went well. We're here when you need us. [Your name]." Bad: "Sorry we fell short on [issue]. I want to make it right. Call me directly at [number]. [Your name]." Block 10 minutes every Monday.
- **Visual world:** a boss fight. A one-star review is the boss; the owner's calm reply is the attack that knocks its health bar down; the crowd of homeowner sprites watches.

### 9. Stop driving out for free
- **Idea:** A service call fee is normal. Charge for the trip and the diagnosis, and credit it if they go ahead.
- **Creators:** Tom Reber ("No More Free Estimates", [Feb 23, 2026](https://stt.ai/podcasts/the-contractor-fight-with-tom-reber/tcf1102-no-more-free-estimates-how-to-protect-your/?lang=pt)); Keith Kalfas ($700 job minimum, [Jun 24, 2025](https://thelandscapingemployeetrap.libsyn.com/why-we-have-a-700-minimum-to-do-any-landscaping-job)).
- **Evidence:** Angi cost guide: most plumbers charge a $100 to $250 service call fee to visit and give an estimate; $50 to $300 extra trip fee beyond about 10 miles; average plumber visit about $338. [Angi, "2025 Data", no date on page](https://www.angi.com/articles/plumber-cost.htm). Re-read Oct 28, 2026. Context: 68% of homeowners seek more quotes when pricing comes in higher than expected ([Housecall Pro, May 13, 2026](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-spending-report-2026-release/)): free trips feed price shoppers.
- **Safe say:** "Angi's cost guide says most plumbers charge $100 to $250 just to come out and quote." Market norm, plumbing only; not a study.
- **Hook:** "Plumbers: Angi says most of you charge $100 to $250 to come out. Still doing it free?"
- **Takeaway (phone script):** "Our service call is $[X]. It covers the trip and the diagnosis, and it comes off the bill if you go ahead today."
- **Visual world:** a side-scrolling job route. The truck drives right; the fuel gauge and a clock drain with every free stop; a toll booth labeled SERVICE CALL refills both.

### 10. Sell the membership, then make it renew itself
- **Idea:** Recurring maintenance plans are common at the top shops, and renewals fall apart when nobody automates them.
- **Creators:** ServiceTitan's content (Follow-Ups 101 lists expiring memberships as a follow-up category); Mello and Nexstar are known for it, not re-read this run.
- **Evidence:** (a) 87.5% of top performers run an active paid membership or service agreement program; the rest 68.7%. [ServiceTitan, May 19, 2026](https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/top-performer-profile). (b) Commercial agreements reaching end of term, as of January 2026: manual renewal 29.8%, auto-renew 42.4%, auto-renew plus email 49.1%; correlation. [ServiceTitan, May 6, 2026](https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/commercial-service-agreement-renewal-automation). Both re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "On ServiceTitan's data, manual renewals kept about 3 in 10 commercial agreements; auto-renew with reminders kept about half."
- **Hook:** "Renewing memberships by hand? On ServiceTitan's data that kept 3 in 10."
- **Takeaway (renewal email):** Subject: "Your plan renews on [date]." Body: "Nothing to do: it renews automatically. Your next visit is [month]. Want to change anything? Reply here."
- **Visual world:** a factory conveyor belt. Renewal cards ride the belt; a tired hand grabs 3 of 10; a switch flips to AUTO and a claw catches half.

### 11. Subs: price in the wait
- **Idea:** If you sub to GCs, you wait weeks to get paid. Count the days and put the cost of that money in your price.
- **Creators:** Shawn Van Dyke (cash first); Michael Stone (payment schedules and cash).
- **Evidence:** Subcontractors wait an average of 51 days after a pay application; GCs estimate 35; 83% of sub owners worried about cash flow (up from 71%); 54% price in the cost of capital, and 62% of those grew vs 46% of those who don't. 600+ subcontractors, suppliers and GCs; field dates not stated. [Billd, Jun 23, 2026](https://billd.com/blog/billds-sixth-annual-national-subcontractor-market-report-reveals-the-playbook-behind-the-industrys-fastest-growing-subcontractors/). Re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "Billd's 2026 survey: subs wait 51 days on average after a pay app. GCs think it's 35." Lender's own survey; correlation.
- **Hook:** "Subs: your GC thinks you get paid in 35 days. Billd says it's 51."
- **Takeaway (formula):** Cost of waiting = job price x your borrowing rate x (days to pay / 365). Add it to the bid, or ask for the payment terms in writing before you sign.
- **Visual world:** a mail-route game. The sub's mailbox sits at the end of a long road; a mail truck crawls 51 squares while a speech bubble from the GC says 35.

### 12. Price the emergency
- **Idea:** People pay more to have it fixed today. Put a same-day option on the menu at a same-day price.
- **Creators:** Danielle Putnam (menu pricing); Crisara (options).
- **Evidence:** 72% of homeowners would pay more to resolve an emergency within 24 hours; 58% contact a pro after a sudden problem or failure. 1,100+ US homeowners. [Housecall Pro, May 13, 2026](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-spending-report-2026-release/). Re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "In Housecall Pro's 2026 homeowner survey, 72% said they'd pay more to get an emergency fixed within a day." Stated willingness, not purchases.
- **Hook:** "72% of homeowners say they'd pay more to get it fixed today. Is same-day on your price sheet?"
- **Takeaway (two lines on the menu):** "Standard: next open slot, [$X]. Same-day: tech there today, [$X plus Y]." Say: "Do you want it today, or is [day] OK?"
- **Visual world:** a weather map. A storm cell (a burst pipe icon) rolls over the town; the forecaster's board shows two lanes: TODAY in red with a price tag, LATER in blue.

### 13. Send the after photo
- **Idea:** Homeowners expect proof of the work. A before and after photo with the invoice is a habit, not a nicety.
- **Creators:** no creator page read with this lesson; included for the strength of the homeowner data.
- **Evidence:** 68% of homeowners expect photo or video proof of completed work; 28% say it would be nice; 92% say visuals of past work influence them. 1,040 US homeowners, Oct 23, 2025. [Housecall Pro, Dec 10, 2025](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/). Re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "In a Housecall Pro survey of about a thousand homeowners, 68% said they expect photo or video proof of the finished work."
- **Hook:** "68% of homeowners expect a photo of the finished job. Did you send one today?"
- **Takeaway (end-of-job checklist):** Before photo on arrival. After photo from the same spot. Send both with the invoice: "Here's the before and after on your [job]. Any questions, call me." Save the best pair for your Google profile.
- **Visual world:** a retro photo booth. Two frames, BEFORE and AFTER, develop on a strip; the strip slides into an envelope with the invoice.

### 14. The 5 to 9 pm calls
- **Idea:** Off-hours calls spike in busy season and cluster in the evening. Have a plan for them before summer.
- **Creators:** ServiceTitan's content.
- **Evidence:** (a) Residential HVAC: 14.1% of inbound calls arrived outside business hours in June 2025, 9.8% in October; biggest cluster 5 p.m. to 9 p.m. local; same-store cohort, 2025, not nationally representative. [ServiceTitan, Jun 3, 2026](https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/hvac-summer-after-hours-call-spike). (b) Off-hours share 12.1% in May to July 2026; 11.8% for shops with 1 to 10 techs. [ServiceTitan, Oct 22, 2026](https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/quarterly-industry-trends). Both re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "On ServiceTitan's data, about 1 in 7 calls to home HVAC shops came after hours in June 2025."
- **Hook:** "1 in 7 HVAC calls in June came after hours. Who answers yours?"
- **Takeaway (after-hours text-back):** "Hi, it's [company]. We're off the phones but saw your call. Is this an emergency (no heat, no cooling, water leak)? Reply YES and the on-call tech calls you; otherwise we'll call at 8 am."
- **Visual world:** a 24-hour clock tower at night. The dial lights brightest from 5 to 9; a moon crosses; phone icons ring in the dark windows below.

HIRI tracker via [Angi, Jul 17, 2026](https://intercom.help/angi/en/articles/15972206-the-backlog-boom-73-of-homeowners-have-projects-waiting). Both re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "On ServiceTitan's records, water heaters get replaced at a median age of about 11 years."
- **Hook:** "Water heater 12 years old? On ServiceTitan's records, most get replaced around 11."
- **Takeaway (script):** "Your [unit] is [X] years old. The typical one gets replaced around [median]. Here's the repair price and the replace price side by side. Which makes more sense for you?"

### 16. Hire before you need to
- **Idea:** The labor gap is real and specialized trade workers are the hardest hire. Recruit all year, not when you're drowning.
- **Creators:** Mike Andes (pay for performance, profit share, open books, [MDL ep. 327](https://es.player.fm/series/million-dollar-landscaper-podcast/winning-the-hiring-game-mike-andes-proven-approach-for-landscapers-mdl-episode-327)); Tommy Mello (open books, visible metrics).
- **Evidence:** (a) Construction needs 349,000 net new workers in 2026 and 456,000 in 2027 (ABC model on Census and BLS data). [ABC, Jan 15, 2026](https://www.abc.org/wfshortage). (b) Hardest roles to fill: specialized trade workers 30%, crew leaders 19%; 23% name workforce shortages a growth challenge. [Jobber 2026 report](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/). Both re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "ABC estimates construction needs 349,000 new workers this year."
- **Hook:** "Construction needs 349,000 more workers this year. How do you get yours?"
- **Takeaway (always-hiring checklist, our advice):** A "we're hiring" line on every truck and invoice. A referral bonus paid after 90 days. Pay tied to a number they can see (Andes' approach). One interview slot held open every week.
- **Visual world:** an RPG tavern recruit screen. Empty party slots blink; a counter above the door reads 349,000; one recruit card fills a slot when the bonus is posted.

### 17. Count your cash buffer days
- **Idea:** Know how many days the business could pay its bills with nothing coming in. Trades run thinner than most.
- **Creators:** Shawn Van Dyke (Profit First); Ellen Rohr (cash is one of three numbers).
- **Evidence:** Median small business 27 cash buffer days; construction 20; repair and maintenance 18. 597,000 Chase business accounts, Feb to Oct 2015. [JPMorgan Chase Institute, Sept 1, 2016](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/institute/all-topics/business-growth-and-entrepreneurship/report-cash-flows-balances-and-buffer-days). Re-read Oct 28, 2026. Old data: say the year.
- **Safe say:** "A JPMorgan Chase study of 2015 bank data found the typical construction business had about 20 days of cash."
- **Hook:** "The typical construction business had 20 days of cash. How many do you have?"
- **Takeaway (formula):** Buffer days = cash in the bank / (last 90 days of spending / 90).
- **Visual world:** a survival game hunger meter. A 20-segment bar drains one block per day; each paid invoice refills a few blocks.

### 18. Stock up for your busy month
- **Idea:** Demand has a calendar. Find your peak month and staff and stock before it.
- **Creators:** none read with this lesson.
- **Evidence:** Water heater replacements indexed to each year's monthly average: January 118 in 2024, 124 in 2025; October about 2% to 6% above; ServiceTitan, mainly residential plumbers, not nationally representative. [ServiceTitan, Aug 18, 2026](https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/residential-water-heater-replacement-seasonal-trends). Re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "On ServiceTitan's data, water heater installs ran 18% to 24% above a normal month in January."
- **Hook:** "Plumbers: January ran 24% above a normal month for water heaters last year. Stocked?"
- **Takeaway (how-to):** Pull two years of jobs by month. Divide each month by the monthly average x 100. Anything over 110 is your stock-up and hiring month.
- **Visual world:** a calendar grid as a heat map. Twelve tiles warm up month by month and January glows red; crates stack beside it.

### 19. Know your average ticket
- **Idea:** Average ticket shows whether your techs are offering options. Track it next to close rate.
- **Creators:** Ellen Rohr and Tommy Mello (average ticket, lead close rate, service close rate, [Site Shed ep. 436](https://feeds.buzzsprout.com/2198439/episodes/16722035-the-few-numbers-that-make-all-the-difference-ft-ellen-rohr-ep-436?t=0)).
- **Evidence:** ACCA and Farmington benchmarks, 1,000+ contractors: average service ticket $422 ($390 residential, $516 commercial); install close rate 43% (45% residential, 38% commercial). [ACHR News, Feb 10, 2026](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/165799-survey-reveals-strategies-to-boost-hvac-close-rates-by-double-digits). Re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "ACCA's 2026 survey put the average HVAC service ticket at $422."
- **Hook:** "The average HVAC service ticket is $422. What's yours?"
- **Takeaway (formula):** Average ticket = total sold / jobs run, by tech, by month. Install close rate = installs sold / install quotes.
- **Visual world:** a retro truck dashboard. Two gauges, TICKET and CLOSE, with redlines at $422 and 43%; the needle moves as jobs drop in.

### 20. No surprises: say the range on the first call
- **Idea:** Sticker shock sends people shopping. Give a range early and list what's not included.
- **Creators:** Tom Reber (screen before you drive out).
- **Evidence:** 77% of homeowners frustrated by hidden costs ([Housecall Pro, Dec 10, 2025](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/)); 68% seek additional quotes when pricing comes in higher than expected ([Housecall Pro, May 13, 2026](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-spending-report-2026-release/)); among barriers to finding a pro, too expensive 25%, trust and transparency 21% ([HIRI via Angi, Jul 17, 2026](https://intercom.help/angi/en/articles/15972206-the-backlog-boom-73-of-homeowners-have-projects-waiting)). All re-read Oct 28, 2026.
- **Safe say:** "In Housecall Pro's 2026 survey, 68% of homeowners said they get more quotes when the price is higher than they expected."
- **Hook:** "68% of homeowners go shopping when your price surprises them. Here's the fix."
- **Takeaway (first-call line):** "Most [job type] jobs like yours run [$low] to [$high]. If that works, I'll come out and give you an exact price."
- **Visual world:** a game-show price wheel. The wheel spins to a shock number and the contestant runs off stage; replay, a range appears first and the contestant stays.

### 21. Every job sells the street
- **Idea:** Neighbors copy neighbors. Market hardest right around the job you just finished.
- **Creators:** Tommy Mello (own your local market first).
- **Evidence:** Solar panels in California: at the average number of owner-occupied homes in a zip code, one more installation raises the probability of another adoption by 0.78 percentage points. Peer-reviewed. [Bollinger and Gillingham, Marketing Science 2012 (abstract)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormksc:v:31:y:2012:i:6:p:900-912). Re-read Oct 28, 2026. Solar installation is a trade; say "solar" and "percentage points", never "78%".
- **Safe say:** "A study of solar in California found each new install made the next one nearby a little more likely."
- **Hook:** "Your best ad is the job you just finished. A solar study shows why."
- **Takeaway (after-job checklist):** Yard sign for 7 days (ask first). Door hangers on the 5 houses each side and across: "We just finished [job] on [street]." Post the after photo tagged with the neighborhood.
- **Visual world:** a city-builder street. Houses light up one by one outward from the finished job, like tiles spreading.
