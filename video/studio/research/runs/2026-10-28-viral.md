## 2. Hooks for educational business shorts

### What the evidence says
- Instagram added a "skip rate" metric in 2025: the share of viewers who leave in the first 3 seconds. Instagram's own wording: "A high Skip Rate indicates that people did not find the beginning of the reel engaging" (Marketing4eCommerce, Aug 25, 2025).
- Average skip rate is high. Socialinsider's 2026 study of about 9.77 million brand Reels (Jan to June 2026) found 65.5% of plays skipped in the first 3 seconds for 1K to 5K follower accounts and 60.5% for 100K to 1M accounts. So most people leave before second 3. The hook is the biggest lever.
- TikTok's own research: about 90% of ad recall impact happens in the first 6 seconds (TikTok Creative Codes; TikTok's own studies, 2020 to 2022).
- Meta (Dec 2025) named three hook types for Reels: a value promise (lead with the viewer's benefit), a statement of intent (say up front what they will learn), and a question or invitation. Meta also said Reels with music or voiceover deliver up to 13% higher incremental conversions (ads context).
- Question hooks: in 14,424 TikTok and Instagram videos from 355 accounts, spoken question hooks averaged 10.08x the account's typical views versus 7.04x for statements. The share that doubled typical views was about the same (30.4% vs 29.9%), so questions raise the ceiling, not the floor. Vendor data, observational (The Content Labs, Aug 2026).
- Hook length: same dataset, 9 to 12 spoken words did best (9.03x typical views); 1 to 8 words did worse (7.15x) (The Content Labs, Aug 20, 2026). A separate study of 4,148 million-view YouTube openings found a median of 12 words (11 for Shorts) (OverseerOS).
- Shorts openings lean on "you" (36.9%), questions (23.7%) and "this/these" (23.8%), meaning direct address and pointing at something on screen. Most common opening phrases: "This is", "What if", "If you", "Have you / Do you" (OverseerOS, 4,148 hooks). No causation shown.
- Paddy Galloway's study of 5,400 YouTube Shorts (3.3 billion views, April 2023): Shorts under 60% "viewed vs swiped away" rarely did well; the best were 70% to 90%. Advice: make the first second punchy and treat the intro "like a thumbnail."
- Caution on hook timing: a vendor study of 1,111 winning TikToks (Riffkit, through Aug 2026) found the median "hook" beat lasted 4.1 seconds, as a short first act that sets a question, and 84% were single takes. Hooks do not have to be one frantic second, but the question must be set fast.
- Phil Risher writes hooks from real customer questions pulled from call recordings (Jobber podcast). Hunter Patrick opens on the payoff moment (getting paid) and then shows how he got there (Jobber podcast).

### On-screen text hooks
- Length: about 5 to 8 words; if it needs more than one glance, it is too long (Skeepers creator guide, Jan 29, 2026).
- Timing: show it in the first second (Skeepers). Hold time: about 1.5 to 2.5 seconds for 3 to 5 words and 2.5 to 4 seconds for 6 to 8 words; reading floor about 0.3 seconds per word, from BBC subtitle guidance of about 160 to 180 words per minute (OverseerOS summary of BBC, UC Davis and Section508.gov guidance). Test: can you read it twice before it leaves.
- Placement: center for the main hook, top for list content; keep clear of the bottom caption area (Skeepers). Meta style safe zone for 9:16: keep text out of the top 14% and bottom 35%, with about 6% side margins (Billo, March 2026; check in Ads Manager's safe zone overlay). Section508.gov suggests key text in the upper two thirds.
- Spoken line and text line should say the same idea, not two different ideas. The text is for the roughly half of viewers watching with sound off (Section 3).

## 3. Retention and structure

### Length
- Instagram's cap is 3 minutes (Mosseri, Jan 2025). In May 2024 Instagram said it would not recommend Reels over 90 seconds to non-followers; in March 2025 it updated the tip to "don't post Reels longer than three minutes" (Social Media Today, Jun 5, 2024 and Mar 5, 2025).
- Socialinsider 2026 (about 9.77M Reels): 45 to 60 seconds had the highest median views (10,374) and engagement (0.35%); under 30 seconds had 4,700 median views; over 3 minutes drops to 4,428 and is not placed in the Reels tab. Reach rate was highest for 30 to 60 seconds (5.60%) and fell to 3.50% over 2 minutes.
- Academic: a 2025 survey experiment (155 people, 10 to 60 second clips) found an inverted U with the best length about 35 seconds, rising to about 44 seconds with music (Marketing Trends Congress 2025). Stated intent, small sample.
- Vendor data points both ways (OpusClip viral tier median 41 seconds; Revid 61 to 90 seconds highest median views). Takeaway for PNF's 45 to 90 second plan: aim for 45 to 60 seconds, go to 90 only when the payoff earns it.

### How people actually watch
- In 9.2 million donated TikTok views (CHI 2024 study, 347 users), only 45% of views were watched to the end; 24% were skipped before 20% of the video played (Zannettou et al., 2024).
- Paddy Galloway: average view duration matters more than length; Shorts with AVD over 50 seconds averaged 4.1M views (2023 study).

### Pacing (cuts and visual changes)
- There is no solid public study giving a "cuts per second" number for educational Reels. TikTok says faster scene changes draw viewers in early but gives no number (TikTok Creative Codes). OpusClip's "one cut every 2 to 4 seconds in high performers" has no named dataset. Riffkit found 84% of its 1,111 winners were single takes.
- What is supported: speaking pace. In 6.9 million edX video sessions, faster speakers held viewers up to 2x longer within the same length band; the advice was to cut filler and pauses, not to force speed (Guo, Kim, Rubin, 2014).
- Working rule (not proven): a new visual beat every 2 to 4 seconds, checked against Instagram's retention graph. Instagram says "the flatter the line, the more engaged your audience is" (Marketing4eCommerce, Aug 2025).

### Open loops and payoffs
- Curiosity comes from a felt gap in knowledge (Loewenstein, 1994 information gap theory, as summarized by PBS NOVA). Name the gap in the hook ("why your quotes go quiet"), and close it only near the end.
- Riffkit's winners mostly use two beats: attention, then payoff (39%). Keep it simple: hook, the problem with proof, the fix, the payoff.
- Phil Risher's working structure: hook, educate without selling, agitate the problem, call to action (Jobber podcast).

### Captions on screen
- Mosseri said about half of Instagram videos are watched without sound (Colin and Samir interview, via Buffer).
- Verizon Media and Publicis Media (2019, 5,616 US adults): 69% watch with sound off in public; 25% even in private.
- Mosseri's Reels checklist includes captions turned on, a strong hook, audio, original non-watermarked content and 3 minutes or less (Instagram post DQrPklCAXb1, via Hootsuite).
- Vendor data: 80.2% of OpusClip's viral-tier clips burn in captions and 78.6% animate them; 96.8% of captioned Revid exports use animated captions.

### The end (CTA and loop)
- Meta's Explore system treats finishing a video (95%+ watched) as a strong positive signal (Meta transparency page, via Hootsuite). Instagram's 2023 Reels explainer lists "watch a reel all the way through" as a top prediction.
- Replays count toward watch time per secondhand sources (Kompozy, Sept 2026); no direct Instagram quote found. A loop end (last frame matches first frame, or the last line leads back into the first) is a reasonable test, not a proven win.
- TikTok research: CTA cards placed instead of interrupting content were linked to a 45% lift in recall and 19% more likability (TikTok Creative Codes, ads).
- Keep the CTA short and after the payoff. Never put the CTA where the payoff should be.

### What Instagram has said about ranking, 2025 to 2026
- Jan 22, 2025, Mosseri: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends." "Pay close attention to average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach." "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." (Social Media Today)
- 2023 explainer (Instagram, "Shedding More Light on How Instagram Works"): Reels predictions are likelihood to watch to the end, like, say it was entertaining or funny, and visit the audio page. Instagram avoids recommending low resolution, watermarked, muted, bordered, "mostly text," or political Reels.
- Trial Reels: launched Dec 2024; shown to non-followers first; about 24 hours later you see views, likes, comments and shares; wider access for public accounts with 1,000+ followers (Social Samosa, July 2025). Mosseri called it "a great way to test" (Instagram post DDZ15g9vs0v, via Hootsuite). Scheduling from Feb 2026 is reported by a secondary source only (Dataslayer).
- Originality: April 30, 2026, Instagram extended its aggregator rule from Reels to photos and carousels: accounts that mostly repost others' content "will no longer be eligible for recommendations." Speed changes, watermarks and screenshots do not count as material edits (Digital Music News, May 1, 2026; Instagram post DXwiWwVm9n7).
- Dec 31, 2025, Mosseri: the polished feed "is dead"; value is in "the ability to be real, to connect, to have a voice that couldn't be faked" (Engadget).
- Other dated changes (KeywordsEverywhere tracker): hashtags capped at 5 per post (Dec 18, 2025); "Your Algorithm" topic controls on Reels (Dec 10, 2025); public professional content indexed by Google by default (July 10, 2025); Meta Q3 2026 call: nearly 45% of recommended Reels are under a day old (Oct 21, 2026).
- Mosseri also said posting again "in the next day or two" after a viral Reel helps keep reach, and creators who post Stories see fewer unfollows (Instagram posts via Hootsuite, undated).

## 4. Motion that holds attention (practical for a code-drawn pixel video)

Evidence first:
- Signaling works: a meta-analysis of 29 experiments (2,726 people) found highlighting key information improves learning, overall d = 0.38 (Alpizar, Adesope, Wong, 2020). Use motion to point at the one thing being said, not to decorate.
- Kinetic text speed: in a 2024 study, viewers rated about 90 words per minute the most attractive text speed, and recall did not drop at faster speeds. Smooth custom easing curves beat simple presets for recall: 75 vs 55 correct answers out of 100 (Dolić et al., 2024, small sample aged 18 to 30).
- Drawing builds: "Khan style" drawing tutorials held students 1.5x to 2x longer than slide tutorials, and short cuts to the instructor's face raised engagement (46% vs 33% problem attempts) (Guo et al., 2014). For PNF: build charts and numbers on screen stroke by stroke, and cut back to the bearded host often.
- Game "juice": tweening and easing, squash and stretch, screen shake on impacts, particles, flashes and sound make simple game scenes feel alive (Jonasson and Purho, "Juice It or Lose It"; Vlambeer's Nijman showed screen shake in the opposite direction of a hit, Game Developer, Jan 2014). Counterpoint from Folmer Kelly: every effect should fit the scene or it breaks immersion.
- Instagram avoids recommending Reels that are "mostly text" (2023 explainer). So keep the host and a scene on screen; text supports, it does not carry the video.
- Safety: never flash more than 3 times in any one second (WCAG 2.3.1).
- Weak evidence: "a transition every N seconds" has no public study behind a specific N (Section 3). Use 2 to 4 seconds as a test setting and read the retention graph.

Practical motion rules for a pixel-art engine:
1. Every spoken key number gets a count-up counter (ease out, land with a 1 to 2 frame squash and a small coin or spark burst). This is signaling plus juice.
2. Punch-in: 8% to 15% zoom on the host or the key object on emphasis words, eased in over about 6 to 8 frames and held; never constant zoom drift.
3. Screen shake only on a "hit": a bad number, a myth busted, a lost job. 2 to 4 pixels, 6 to 10 frames, decaying. At most once every 10 seconds so it keeps meaning something.
4. Kinetic captions: words pop in with the voice at roughly speech pace, current word highlighted; keep inside the safe zone (out of the top 14% and bottom 35%).
5. A new visual beat every 2 to 4 seconds: a scene change, a zoom, an object entering, a chart bar growing. Inside-the-shot motion counts; you do not need hard cuts.
6. Draw-on charts: bars, lines and pixel boxes build in sync with narration (Khan style), then the host reacts.
7. Use smooth custom easing (cubic or back-out), not linear moves; pixel art can snap to the grid on the last frame for crispness.
8. Open with motion in frame 1: the host mid-gesture or an object already moving. No logo intro, no fade from black.
9. End frame matches the opening frame for a clean loop, then a short CTA card that does not cover the payoff.
10. Flash and shake limits: no more than 3 flashes per second; offer at least one calm beat (about 1 second) before the payoff so the payoff lands.

## 6. Trust and the site: moving viewers without looking salesy

What Instagram allows and penalizes:
- "Comment a word for the template" is allowed when it is earnest. In June 2024 Instagram clarified that single word CTAs were not the target, that tools like ManyChat are fine, and that "Earnestly engaging with your audience in the comments and DMs" to generate leads is not engagement bait. The problem is when "the end goal is to simply maximize the quantity of engagements." Instagram will not say which terms trigger penalties (Social Media Today, Jun 5, 2024).
- Links: URLs typed into a caption are plain text. Since Sept 15, 2026, Meta One lets accounts add clickable links to posts and Reels, starting at the $49.99 a month Advanced plan with up to 4 linked Reels a month (Blotato, citing Meta's help page; rolling out gradually). Otherwise the link lives in the bio.
- Originality: accounts that mostly repost others' content lose recommendations (April 30, 2026). Do not use other creators' clips. Do not leave other app watermarks; Instagram avoids recommending watermarked Reels (2023 explainer).
- Hashtags: max 5 per post (Dec 2025). Instagram's search guidance says put keywords in captions, not comments (KeywordsEverywhere tracker).
- Sends matter most for reaching new people (Mosseri, Jan 2025). Content a trade owner would send to a partner, office manager or crew lead is the growth engine.

How educational creators move viewers without selling:
1. Teach the whole lesson in the video. Phil Risher's rule is "educate without selling," then a short CTA (Jobber podcast).
2. Offer a free tool that finishes the lesson: "The margin calculator is free on the site, link in bio." The free tool is the CTA, not a sales page.
3. Comment-for-template via DM automation, kept earnest: one keyword, and the DM delivers exactly what was promised.
4. Put the proof on screen: show the source name and year on a pixel "receipt" card for every number. That is the brand promise and it builds trust.
5. Keep the CTA after the payoff and short. TikTok found CTA cards that do not interrupt content lifted recall 45% (ads data).
6. Make the bio and profile match the promise: one line on who it is for, one link to the free tools.
7. Use Trial Reels to test hooks on non-followers before showing followers (Mosseri: "a great way to test").
8. Post again within a day or two after a hit, and use Stories to keep followers (Mosseri, via Hootsuite).

What to avoid:
- "Like if you agree," "comment YES," or tag bait meant only to pump numbers.
- Reels that are mostly text slides (not recommended per Instagram 2023).
- Fake or untraceable stats (Section 5, idea 17). One bad number breaks the brand.
- Over 3 minute Reels (not placed in the Reels tab, per Socialinsider) and other app watermarks.
