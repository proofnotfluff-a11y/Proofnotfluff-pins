# Viral short formats: what 1M+ view shorts do, and a new Lead Clinic format

Research date: Thu Oct 29, 2026 (America/Chicago). Asked by Todd, Oct 29, 11:10 am: "go out there and find what is working right now, what's getting millions of views, and then we replicate that on our side ... keep the motion graphics style ... the format and the script and the structure and the hooks."

Status: research and a proposal only. Nothing in `_shared/`, `anim.py`, the gates or any lesson changes until Todd says yes. It builds on `research/runs/2026-10-28-viral.md` and `research/runs/2026-10-28-script-playbook.md`, which had no specific videos because the platforms refused automated reads. This run adds 18 specific videos read on the platform's own pages.

## How it was read, and what could not be read

- 18 YouTube Shorts, each read on its own watch page in a cloud browser (not signed in) between 16:40 and 17:45 UTC on Oct 29, 2026. Views and post dates are copied from the page's description panel. Spoken words and timings come from YouTube's own transcript panel. Words per minute is transcript words divided by length.
- Window: posted Nov 2, 2025 to Oct 27, 2026. Every one has 1M+ views (smallest 1,022,343).
- Could not read: YouTube video playback was blocked in the browser, so what is on screen frame by frame, picture changes and caption styles are NOT measured here. TikTok profile grids errored and TikTok search needs a login; Instagram reel pages did not load. So no TikTok or Reel is verified. A separate web search found 33 more in-window 1M+ candidates on third-party trackers (CreatorDB, Modash, OutlierKit, HomePros News); they are listed at the end, labeled third party, and are not counted in the pattern numbers.
- Five of the 18 are spoken in Hindi (V1, V15 to V18). Their words per minute count Hindi words and are left out of the pace comparison.

## The short answer

The 1M+ business and money shorts drop you into a moment (a person, an object, a price), make you wonder how it ends, act the lesson out instead of explaining it, and stop on the answer. Ours open on a survey percentage, cite the source out loud in the first 12 seconds, explain instead of show, and keep talking for 15 to 25 seconds after the answer. Ours run 72 to 87 seconds and 183 to 250 words; the spoken winners have a median of 44 seconds, and the three animated explainers run 69 to 124 words.

Other counts:
- All 18: 14 of 18 run under 50 s; median 35 s. The four long ones are street interviews or podcast clips (V2, V6, V7 at 155 to 160 s; V12 at 67 s), formats we do not make.
- Pace (8 English videos): 136 to 224 words a minute, median 178. Our lessons run about 165 to 173. Pace is not our gap.
- Comments come from a choice or a debatable claim: viewers pick a side (V15, V18), fix the mistake (V4), or argue with the lesson (V12, V13). Top comments on V8 quote the script back ("A WHOLE 37 cents", 8.1K likes).
- Captions and hashtags do not separate winners: 8 of 18 have no description at all and 7 of 18 use zero hashtags, while others use 18 to 36. 9 of 18 put an emoji in the title.
- Trade content (niche A) that gets millions is almost all no-voice: 5 of the 6 trade videos are 4 to 19 s clips of a satisfying job, a tool trick or a fail, set to music, often reposted by small channels (V10: 31.6M views on a 59K subscriber channel, likes about 0.1% of views). That is reach from replays, not business education, and it matches the Oct 28 finding that views are not leads.
- Only one creator style is both animated and in our lane: Zack D. Films (V8, V9, V13). Calm narration, 28 to 44 s, 69 to 124 words, no intro, no CTA, ends on the punchline or the lesson line. V8 ("How Banks Make Money With Your Savings", 6.3M) and V9 ("The $23 Michael Jordan Signed Basketball", 8.3M) are money lessons told as a tiny story.

Third-party data that points the same way (vendor data, directional only):
- FluxNote (Oct 4, 2026), 500 videos with 1M+ views, Jan to Sep 2026: "direct claim with specific number" was the top hook type (14%); setups over 4 s underperformed.
- Revid (Sep 15, 2026), 2.38M TikToks: faceless videos passed 1M views 30% of the time vs 22% for person on camera; faceless tutorials had a 156K median vs 52K on camera.
- Faceless.so (Sep 25, 2026), 4,604 TikToks: stick figure and meme explainers scored a views index of 212, the top faceless format (small sample: 127 posts).
- Revid and Faceless.so both found views barely change with length. So shorter alone is not proven to win. What the winners share is one idea and nothing after the answer; the shorter runtime follows from that.

## What we do differently (our 10 lessons vs the 13 spoken winners)

| Gap | Ours | Winners | Likely cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook built on a survey percentage | 7 of 10 hooks (62%, 77%, 83%, 43%, 31 to 38%) | 0 of 13 | High: a stat about other shops is abstract; the winners open on a moment |

| Length and words | 72 to 87 s, 183 to 250 words | spoken median 44 s; animated 69 to 124 words | Medium |
| "Trade owners," opener | 3 of 10 | 0 of 13 | Low to medium |

Not gaps: our pace, our captions and hashtags, the motion graphics look (faceless animated is the strongest faceless format in two vendor sets).

## Proposed format: Lead Clinic v2 (motion graphics stay)

Target 40 to 50 s with the end card, 100 to 135 spoken words, 5 takes. The 75 s cap stays as the hard limit. The other thread's hook motion rules stay as they are (picture changes within 1 s and twice in the first 3 s, 9 to 12 word spoken hook).

| Beat | Time | Words | What it does | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Drop-in | 0 to 3 s | 9 to 12 | A moment on a real job, with the customer's own words or one object and a number. Sets the question the video answers. No source, no percent, no "trade owners". The trade word sits inside the scene (homeowner, estimate, water heater) so the keyword rule still holds. | The scene, the line in a speech bubble |
| 2. Play it out | 3 to 12 s | 20 to 25 | The usual way, acted by the cast: what most owners say, and what happens next. | Cast acts it; the cost shows (phone rings for a competitor, quote goes in a drawer) |
| 3. The fix | 12 to 26 s | 30 to 40 | Rewind to the moment; the right line, text or math, word for word. | The artifact typed out, held 3 s or more |
| 4. Proof | 26 to 34 s | 18 to 25 | One sourced number in one or two sentences. | Number bars and the source tag, including "pattern, not proof" where the source is correlational |
| 5. Button | 34 to 42 s | 15 to 22 | One line rule that echoes the hook's words. Last spoken word. No spoken CTA. | Back to the scene, the win |
| End card | 3 s | 0 | Lockup, proofnotfluff.com, the send line as text | as built Oct 29 |

What it keeps: every number from facts.json and checked by the facts gate; the source on screen whenever its number is; one idea per lesson; the copyable artifact; the voice rules on tags, stress and spoken numbers.

## Example rewrite: lesson 5, "Close it before you leave the driveway" (unposted)

Picked because it has not posted and no other run is rebuilding it (lesson 3 is in the lab). Same facts.json: ServiceTitan top performer profile (77% vs 62%, the momentum quote) and our three-line ask.

Current: 213 words, last word at 74.9 s, 7 takes, hook "Still no yes when you leave the driveway? Top shops close seventy-seven percent of their estimates on the same visit. Here's how to ask." (the source beat follows at 5 s).

New: 113 words, about 40 to 44 s spoken at our pace, about 47 s with the end card.

```
[curious] The estimate's done. The homeowner says, let me think about it.

[matter-of-fact] You say, sure, no rush, and drive off. Then the waiting starts. They call a competitor, or talk themselves out of it.

[warmly] Try this before you open the truck door. Here are your options. If you want to go ahead today, I can get you on the schedule for Thursday. What questions do you have before you pick one?

[matter-of-fact] ServiceTitan's top shops close seventy-seven percent of estimates on the same visit. Everyone else, sixty-two. It says the gap comes down to momentum.

[confident] That last question pulls the worry out while you're still in the driveway. So don't let them think about it alone.
```

Beat sheet (motion graphics, same cast and looks):
- 0.0 s: driveway at dusk, homeowner on the porch holding the estimate. 0.8 s: cut in on the speech bubble "Let me think about it." 2.0 s: Snow's face reacts. (Two picture changes in 3 s.)
- 3 to 11 s: truck pulls away, a clock spins, a phone lights up with "Competitor", the estimate slides into a drawer.
- 11 to 25 s: rewind effect back to the porch; the three lines type into the dialog box and hold 3 s; "Thursday" snaps onto a calendar.
- 25 to 33 s: two bars, 77 and 62; source tag "ServiceTitan customers, 12 months to Jan 2026, top 10% by revenue. Pattern, not proof."
- 33 to 41 s: back to the porch, the homeowner nods and signs; last line lands; end card with "Send this to whoever runs your estimates."

Facts check on the rewrite: 77, 62 and "comes down to momentum" are in facts.json (`servicetitan.quote`); "call a competitor, or talk themselves out of it" paraphrases `servicetitan.whyQuote`; the three ask lines are `ask.line1` to `ask.line3` (ours, advice); "Thursday" is the example day already in facts.json; "pulls the worry out" is our advice, as in the current script. The caveat moves from a spoken take to the on-screen source tag and the caption.

## Rule changes this needs (for Todd's yes)

These conflict with today's rules in `_shared/voice.md` and the 12-point script check, so they wait for Todd:
1. Length band: 45 to 75 s becomes a 40 to 50 s target (100 to 135 words); 75 s stays the hard cap.
2. Stakes: "a sourced number by 12 s" becomes "a sourced number in the proof beat, source on screen".
3. The turn line ("Here's the part people skip") and the spoken "one honest note" go; the caveat lives in the source tag and the caption.
4. Spoken send line goes; the send line moves to the end card and the caption.
5. Hook bans: no percentage, no spoken source and no "trade owners" call-out in the first 3 s.
6. The lesson is acted by the cast (the usual way, then the fix) instead of explained to camera.
