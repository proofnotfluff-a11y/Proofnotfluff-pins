# Script: The first reply wins the job (v2, more depth; Todd, Oct 28, 12:56 pm)

[calm] A homeowner just texted you for a quote. [short pause] You're up on a ladder, so you figure you'll get back to them tonight. [matter-of-fact] Here's why tonight is too late, and what to send instead.

[leaning in] A business journal, Management Science, published a study this year. The researchers looked at eleven point six million requests on Fiverr. When a reply came an hour late, that person was about half as likely to get hired. A day late? About ninety percent less.

[thoughtful] Now, Fiverr isn't the trades. But the reason carries over. People read your reply speed as a sign of how you'll treat them later. And it helped on its own, even when the work and the message were just as good.

[matter-of-fact] Thumbtack's own data shows the same thing. Pros who answer inside thirty minutes are twenty-five percent more likely to get hired than pros who take three hours.

[short pause] Here's the part people skip. Speed stopped helping when the reply looked automated. So that canned thank you for your inquiry text? [quickly, playful] Fast, sure. It just doesn't count.

[warmly] So here's what I'd send. Start with their name, so it's clearly you. Answer exactly what they asked: yes, we replace water heaters. Ask one question that moves the job, like gas or electric? Easy to answer, and you know what to bring. Then a real time. I can come by tomorrow between two and four.

[confident] Fast, human, one question, a time. Send this to whoever answers your phone. The exact text is in the caption.
