# Brief: The text that revives a quote that went quiet

Lesson 2, segment The Text. Planned from the script playbook (research/runs/2026-10-28-script-playbook.md, example 1), Oct 28, 2026; rebuilt the same evening on trade evidence only (Todd: evidence from the trades, new scenery and motion in every video). Every source re-opened and re-read Oct 28, 2026.

- **Where:** Instagram Reel (and TikTok, Facebook, YouTube Shorts), 9:16, 75 s or less end card included (Oct 29 rebuild: 9 word hook said by 3.6 s, 75 s cap; the cadence beat is cut and stays in the caption).
- **Audience:** a trades or home-service owner (plumber, HVAC, electrician, roofer) who left a quote at the kitchen table and hasn't heard back.
- **The one idea (12 words):** When a quote goes quiet, text one yes-or-no question about the job.
- **Shape:** hook (quote went quiet? one text can bring it back) -> stakes (ServiceTitan: only 37% close on the first visit) -> why "just checking in" fails (it hands them homework; Jobber's tips: their name, the job, a question) -> the text, shown big on a phone -> the turn (text it, don't email it: ServiceTitan's webinar on open rates, Housecall Pro's 59% of homeowners who expect text updates) -> close (one name, one job, one question; send line).
- **Hook pattern:** problem-question
- **Treatment:** copy-this
- **Example:** quiet-quote-text
- **Metaphor:** homework (a reply they have to write vs a yes or no). Drawn once as a sticky note on the homeowner's fridge that gets buried under the week's other notes.
- **Voice:** Todd's clone in `_shared/voice.md` (eleven_v4). Oct 29: takes 2, 3 and 4 kept; take 1 (new hook), take 5 (the turn, tightened) and take 6 (the close, was take 7) re-voiced; the cadence take cut.
- **Worlds (each section its own, `_shared/sets.md`; no stock room):** hook and stakes = the street in three shots (the cover: the van parked at the job with a QUOTE sign, the hook in the sky; a cut in on "quiet" to our own close-up of your phone in the dash, the quote Seen with no reply, Snow big; back out on "One"; then the van pulls away on "driveway"); homework = the homeowner's fridge (our own piece: magnets, sticky notes that pile on, Jobber's three tips on a card); text = the chat set (the phone in the truck, the text typed as it is said, YES and NO buttons); turn = the mail desk (our own piece: an email tray stacking up unopened while the phone lights up with a read text, then the 59% count); close = the level-up screen (the bar fills, LEVEL UP, three perks: one name, one job, one question).
- **Real screens:** your quote on a phone, Seen, no reply; the "just checking in" text on a phone; the revive text typed on a phone and held; the sources on screen as they are said.
- **Artifact (on screen):** `Hi [name], it's [you]. Still want that water heater swapped before winter?` Placeholders, no invented names.
- **Close:** "One name, one job, one question." Send line: "Send this to whoever follows up on your quotes." Exact text in the caption.
- **Trust notes:** Trade sources only; no other-industry study and no "this isn't the trades" caveat. ServiceTitan's 37% is vendor data, method not shown: voice and screen say it is ServiceTitan's own data; never the same page's 64% / 72% / 87% (do-not-use list). The texts-vs-email line is a ServiceTitan presenter's point on the same page, with no number: said as "ServiceTitan's follow-up webinar says", no figure on screen. Housecall Pro's 59% is a vendor survey of 1,040 U.S. homeowners (Oct 23, 2025) about text updates on a job: voice and screen say "expect text updates", never "want follow-up texts"; the sample rides on screen. Jobber is practice advice written for email follow-ups: said as "Jobber says" and "Jobber's follow-up tips", on screen as Jobber's timing.
