# Script: The text that revives a quote that went quiet (The Text, lesson 2; from script playbook example 1, facts re-read Oct 28, 2026)

[leaning in] Your quote's gone quiet? Skip "just checking in." Send this.

[matter-of-fact] Software maker ServiceTitan says its data shows only thirty-seven percent of estimates close on the first visit. So most of your quotes aren't decided when you pull out of the driveway. The rest ride on your follow-up.

[curious] Here's the problem with "just checking in." It hands them homework. They have to write you an answer, and a busy homeowner puts homework off. The fix comes from marketer Dean Jackson: one short question about what they wanted, and no pitch.

[warmly] So here's the text. Their name, then yours, so they know it's you. Then one line. "Still want that water heater swapped before winter?" That's it. One question they can answer with a yes or a no.

[short pause] Here's the part people skip. Keep it in your own words. In a Management Science study this year, fast replies stopped helping once people thought a bot wrote them.

[matter-of-fact] When do you send it? Jobber says within two days of the quote. Then a week after you sent it. Then once more, a day or two before it expires. Still nothing? It's okay to close the file.

[confident] One name, one job, one question. Send this to whoever follows up on your quotes. Next time a quote goes quiet, you'll know what to send.
