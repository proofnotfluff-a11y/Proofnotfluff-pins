# Script: Markup vs margin (Lesson 3, Run the Numbers; playbook example 2, adjusted to the sources)

[leaning in] Mark up a job fifty percent. Is that your margin? [short pause] Nope. It's a third. I'll show you the math, and the fix.

[matter-of-fact] Let's say your cost is a thousand dollars. A fifty percent markup adds five hundred, so the quote says fifteen hundred. That five hundred is one third of the price. So your margin is about thirty-three percent. Not the fifty you pictured.

[curious] Why the gap? Markup is measured on your cost. Margin is measured on the price, the money the customer actually pays you. And margin isn't take-home pay. It's what's left to cover your overhead and pay yourself.

[a little surprised] Want a real fifty percent margin? You'd have to DOUBLE your cost. That's a hundred percent markup. A thousand in cost, two thousand on the quote.

[short pause] Here's the part people skip. [warmly] Screenshot this one. Price equals your cost, divided by one minus the margin you want. Want forty percent? Divide your cost by point six. A thousand becomes about sixteen hundred sixty-seven dollars.

[confident] Markup is on cost. Margin is on price. Send this to whoever sets your prices, even if that's you. And your last quote? Was the margin half, or a third?
