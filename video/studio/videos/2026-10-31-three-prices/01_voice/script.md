# Script: Three prices on every quote (Lesson 4, Quote Clinic; playbook example 3, facts re-read Oct 28)

[leaning in] One price on your quotes? The top shops usually quote three. Here's how you build them.

[matter-of-fact] ServiceTitan pulled a year of home jobs, up to this January. The top ten percent offered good, better, best on fifty-seven percent of them. Everyone else? Forty-one. That's a habit you can copy.

[curious] Why does that work? Researchers tracked seven years of orders at a German restaurant. When the menu changed, a dish that landed in the middle took about five more points of all orders, on average. It worked even better when the new dish was the pricey one. So put a price above the one you want them to pick.

[warmly] Build your three like this. Good is the job done right, nothing extra. Better is the one you'd pick for your own house. Best adds upgrades, like a longer warranty or a faster start date.

[short pause] One more thing from the data. Three doesn't have to be the ceiling. A heating and cooling trade group surveyed over a thousand contractors. Those offering four or more options closed fifty-two percent of the time. One to three options? Forty-two.

[calm] Now, that survey lumps one to three options together, so it can't tell you three beats one. Shops that gave more choices just heard yes more often. Start with three.

[confident] One price is a yes or a no. Three prices? That's a choice. Send this to whoever writes your quotes, so the next one has three prices.
