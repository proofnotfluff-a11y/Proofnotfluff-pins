# Brief: Reply to every review

Lesson 7, segment Reviews Clinic. Planned Oct 28, 2026 from the topic bank's T3 (research/runs/2026-10-28-topic-bank.md), every number re-read on its source page the same day.

- **Where:** Instagram Reel, 9:16, about 70 s, then TikTok, Facebook, YouTube Shorts.
- **Audience:** a trades or home-service owner (plumber, HVAC, electrician, roofer) with a Google profile who leaves most reviews unanswered.
- **The one idea:** answer every review, good or bad, in your own words. (10 words)
- **Shape:** hook (hotels that started replying got 12% more reviews) -> stakes (the study: Marketing Science, TripAdvisor hotel reviews; ratings +0.12 stars on average; small, but ratings barely move) -> why (it shows you care; unhappy customers think twice before a complaint they can't back up) -> artifact 1 (the good-review reply, typed on a phone under a 5-star example review) -> artifact 2 (the bad-review reply under a 2-star example review; name the real problem, don't argue in public) -> the turn (bad reviews got rarer but longer: detail you can act on) -> the honest note (hotels, not trades; a guide) -> close (good or bad, answer every review; send line).
- **Metaphor:** none. The phone with a real-looking review and the reply being typed is the lesson.
- **Voice:** Todd's clone in `_shared/voice.md`.
- **Real screens:** a phone showing an example 5-star review and the good reply typing; the same phone with an example 2-star review and the bad reply; the study as a stat card with the source on it; a "fewer but longer" card for bad reviews; the caveat card.
- **Close:** "Send this to whoever runs your Google profile." Brand plate, send line.
- **Trust notes:** the study is hotels on TripAdvisor (the abstract also uses other review platforms), not trades: the hook says "hotels", the caveat is spoken (take 6) and shown. The 12% is review volume ("12 percent more reviews", "12% increase in review volume"): the hook says "twelve percent more reviews", never more jobs, leads or revenue. The 0.12 is stars on average: said "point one two stars on average". "Ratings barely move" is the release's "ratings tend to vary little over time". The why lines are the release's ("managers care about their feedback"; "unsatisfied consumers become more hesitant to leave unsubstantiated complaints"). "Bad reviews got rarer, but longer" is the abstract's "fewer but longer negative reviews". The two reply templates are our advice, with placeholders, no real names; the example reviews on screen are labeled example.
