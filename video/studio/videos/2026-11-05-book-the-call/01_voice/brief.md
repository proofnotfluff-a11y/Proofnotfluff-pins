# Brief: Book the call, don't just answer it

Lesson 9, segment Lead Clinic. Planned Oct 28, 2026 (evening) from the trades-first bank's lesson 1
(research/runs/2026-10-28-trades-insights.md), every source page re-read with WebFetch the same evening. Trade evidence only.

- **Where:** Instagram Reel, 9:16, about 70 s (195 words; expected 68 to 75 s on Todd's voice), then TikTok, Facebook, YouTube Shorts.
- **Audience:** a plumbing, HVAC or electrical owner whose phone rings with leads, answered by the owner, a CSR or whoever is at the desk, and who has never measured how many of those calls end with a booked time.
- **The one idea:** ask every lead caller to book, with two times to pick from. (13 words)
- **Shape:** hook (top shops book 62% of lead calls, everyone else 39%; the line that books yours) -> stakes (ServiceTitan's customers, 12 months to Jan 2026, top 10% by revenue; ServiceTitan: near 39, the problem probably isn't your marketing, it's often the first minute after the phone rings) -> why (Invoca: 55% of home service businesses don't ask leads to buy or book the job) -> the artifact (the three-line booking script ending in two times, held big) -> the turn (two times, not "when works for you?"; ServiceTitan's own sample script asks "mornings or afternoons?") -> the honest note (ServiceTitan's customers, a pattern, not proof; track your own every Friday) -> close (answering isn't booking; send line; "book the next call").
- **Voice:** Todd's clone in `_shared/voice.md`.
- **Real screens:** the ServiceTitan 62 vs 39 with its window and source; the "first 60 seconds" line as ServiceTitan's words; the Invoca 55% with its source; the booking script as a card (marked example); ServiceTitan's "Are mornings or afternoons better for you?" as a quoted line with its source; the Friday formula.
- **Close:** "Send this to whoever answers your phone." Brand plate, send line.

## Worlds (one per section; none of the stock framing, blackboard, workshop or garage rooms; none used in lessons 6 to 8)
1. **hook, close: the versus screen.** A fighting-game select screen in the arcade look: TOP SHOPS on the left, EVERYONE ELSE on the right, a big VS between them; their booking scores count up to 62 and 39 as the bars fill; the source plate small under it. The close returns to it as a rematch: a third slot, YOUR SHOP, blinks "?" (the hook's loop).
2. **stakes, why: the switchboard.** An old telephone switchboard. Lead calls light up as lamps; an operator plugs cords into BOOKED jacks. A 60-second sweep clock sits over the board for "the first minute after the phone rings". For Invoca, a lit call is answered, talks, and its cord drops out with no booking (a NO ASK tag) while "55% of home service businesses don't ask leads to book" lands on the board's brass plate (no lamp counts: the 55% is businesses, not calls).
3. **ask: the calendar lock.** A week grid. The three script lines type onto a headset card at its side; two slots glow as said (TODAY 2 PM, TOMORROW 9 AM, tagged example); on "Which works better?" one slot clicks shut with a padlock and turns into a booked job.
4. **skip: the boss fight.** The open question "When works for you?" spawns a boss named "Let me check and call you back", with a health bar. The player's two-button attack (MORNINGS / AFTERNOONS, ServiceTitan's sample script line) knocks the bar to zero.
5. **caveat: the dispatch board.** A magnetic dispatch board with columns Mon to Fri; booked-job magnets snap into slots as calls book; the Friday column holds the formula card "booked calls / lead calls"; a corner stamp "A pattern, not proof".

## Trust notes
- ServiceTitan's top performers are the 90th revenue percentile in their trade and market segment: said "top ten percent by revenue", shown "Top 10% by revenue". The page says "book 62% of their inbound lead calls" and "The rest average 39%": we say "lead calls" and "everyone else", and the 39 is shown as the rest's average (the page's word). Data: participating ServiceTitan customers, trailing 12 months through January 2026, no sample size; correlation (the page says so): the script says "a pattern, not proof", never "this made them top".
- "If your booking rate is closer to 39% than 62%, the problem probably isn't your marketing. It's often what happens in the first 60 seconds after the phone rings." The voice keeps "probably" and "often" and says "first minute" (60 seconds, shown as the page has it).
- Invoca: "55% of home services businesses don't ask leads to buy or book the job", a highlight tile with no paragraph behind it; data is calls tracked by the Invoca platform across nine home services sub-industries (HVAC and plumbing among them), averages across its customer base; the page states no time period and no business count. Said with "found", never "never ask" or "most shops". The page's own advice is talk tracks, not a specific line, so the two-times script is ours.
- ServiceTitan's sample line "Are mornings or afternoons better for you?" is in its Expired Memberships script (step 6, optional), not a lead-call script: said "One of ServiceTitan's sample scripts asks it this way", nothing more. "An open question is homework" is our advice, not a finding.
- The booking script, the leaking water heater, the times (today at 2, tomorrow at 9) and Friday are examples, shown "example".
