- **Where:** Instagram Reel, 9:16, then TikTok, Facebook, YouTube Shorts.
- **Audience:** the owner of a residential cleaning company (5 to 25 staff) being pitched an AI receptionist or voice bot.
- **The one idea:** fix follow-up first with drafts you approve, before paying for a voice bot.
- **Shape:** drop-in (the demo says an AI receptionist answers every call) -> play it out (you pay for the voice bot, the phone gets answered, but the quotes you already sent just sit, nobody follows up; acted on a calendar of quiet days) -> the fix (fix follow-up first: paste the quote into an AI chat app with a two-sentence prompt, on screen as said; the drafted check-in waits in a tray; nothing goes out until you read it and hit send) -> proof (ServiceTitan's survey of over 1,000 contractors: only 11% place high or complete trust in their AI tools, 58% little or none) -> button (your hand stays on the send button; don't buy an AI receptionist before you fix the follow-up).
- **Hook pattern:** myth
- **Treatment:** myth-fact
- **Example:** ai-receptionist-myth
- **Metaphor:** the myth as a boss you beat with two moves (follow-up first, you hit send). No damage numbers shown.
- **Voice:** Todd's clone in `_shared/voice.md`.
- **Real screens:** a voice bot sales demo (generic, no product, no logo); a month calendar where sent quotes sit with no follow-up; an AI chat app window (unbranded, marked example) with the prompt and the drafted check-in in an approval tray; the ServiceTitan trust numbers on a score table with the source tag.
- **End card send line:** "Send this to whoever handles your follow-ups." (SCENE.sendLine)

Trust notes: the demo's promise is a story element (a generic pitch), not a claim about any product; no vendor or product is named, shown or called bad.
