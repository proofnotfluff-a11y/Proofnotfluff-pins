# Script: The robot is a better voicemail (story; 00_story/story.md; r5 after read-aloud r4, facts r4 PASS)

An AI receptionist can earn its keep. Just not at two in the afternoon. For years it's been the same: you're up on a roof, the phone rings, it goes to voicemail, and you call back that night.

So AI companies pitched a robot to answer instead, and owners tried it. On the forum for Jobber, the job software, one owner who runs it after hours said it plainly. Hardly anyone stays once they hear it's AI.

That's not just forum grumbling, either. This spring, when a trade magazine's publisher asked homeowners about AI, only thirty-six percent were comfortable with AI booking appointments or answering basic questions.

So at two in the afternoon, when the customer expects you, a robot is a gamble. They can hang up and call the next shop.

[matter-of-fact] But that forum owner keeps his on anyway, at night. He calls it a better voicemail. Owner after owner says the same. The AI takes the night, and they take the day.

If you get one, have your carrier send it calls only after hours. But don't hide that it's AI, even if some callers hang up. Even Jobber, which sells one, encourages telling them. So write a greeting that says it's AI, then call it tonight like a customer with no heat.

[confident] Two in the afternoon, they want you. At night, the robot only has to beat a beep.
