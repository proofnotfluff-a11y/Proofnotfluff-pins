- **Story test:** new 2 (Google's help page on the move, re-opened Nov 6, 2026; service-area shops are in the late 2026

## ABT line
You set the most you'll pay for a Local Services Ads lead, AND your reports show what each lead really cost, BUT Google is
moving those ads into Google Ads, where a max cost per lead isn't supported and your old reports don't come along,
THEREFORE screenshot your reports, and note each trade's max, before your move.

## Message line
Google is moving Local Services Ads and your old reports won't come along, so screenshot them before your move.

## Who
Example: a shop that runs plumbing and HVAC on Local Services Ads, the little "Google Guaranteed" ads at the top of a
search. It's a service-area shop, no storefront, so it's in the later wave.

## The story (told the way I'd tell it to an owner)
Two scenes. Today: you open Local Services Ads and there's a box. You type in the most you'll pay for a plumbing lead. After the move: you open the new screen, and that box isn't there.

That's not a glitch. Google is moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads, the main ad system. It started in August with
some home service shops. If you work out of your truck instead of a storefront, your turn comes later this year.

Every day it's worked like this: you pick your ceiling, you run the ads, and once a week you look at the report to see
what a lead actually cost you. That report is how you know if it's worth it.

But on Google's own help page for the move, two things change. Manual bidding, like setting a maximum cost per lead, is
no longer supported. So the box is gone for good. And your Local Services Ads performance reports
don't move over. Your leads do. Your spend history and weekly reports don't.

Because of that, the month after your move, you can't line up what a lead costs now against what it cost before. You'd be
judging the new system with no record of the old one.

The turn: Google says you don't have to do a thing for the move, except save your old reports first. But this week, Google's help page still says the download is coming soon. If you need
your reports now, it says, take screenshots before you move. And the heads-up comes as an email, 14 days before your move,
to whoever is listed as the account admin, plus banners in the dashboard.

What to do this week, in under thirty minutes:
1. Screenshot your Local Services Ads reports for each trade: spend, leads, cost per lead, as far back as it shows.
2. Write down your max per lead and your weekly budget for each trade.
3. Check the admin email on the account is one you actually read. The 14-day notice goes there.

Kicker: The box goes away. Your numbers don't have to.

## Receipts
1. Google Ads Help, "Local Services Ads moving into Google Ads" (no date on page; opened Nov 6, 2026):
   https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585
   - phases: August 2026, select US home and storefront service advertisers (plumbing, HVAC, electrical, appliance repair,
     house cleaning, lawn care, roofing, pest control, moving); late 2026, "service-area businesses without physical
     storefronts and accounts with custom bidding or booking configurations"; 2027 non-US and the rest
   - "Manual bidding (such as setting a maximum cost-per-lead) is no longer supported in Google Ads."
   - no vertical-level Target CPA: "a unified campaign-level Target CPA will be calculated and applied to all vertical
     categories"; separate bidding rules by "setting up separate campaigns for each vertical" (plumbing and HVAC example)
   - "Your Local Services Ads performance reports will not transition to Google Ads." Download "soon"; "If you need your
     reports immediately, please take screenshots of them before migrating."
   - lead history transfers to the Leads page; still pay only for valid leads; same Search and Maps positions
   - the account administrator gets an email 14 days before the migration date (plus warning banners in the current
     dashboard), a final reminder 7 days later, one after the move
   - weekly budget / 7 = daily average; monthly spend capped at daily average x 30.4
2. PPC Land, Jul 20, 2026 (opened Nov 6, 2026), quoting Google's Ads Product Liaison: "no action will be needed by
   advertisers" beyond downloading historical reports; Google "strongly recommend[s]" downloading ahead of the move:
   https://ppc.land/google-folds-local-services-ads-into-google-ads-cuts-historical-reports/

## The other side
Google says you still pay only for valid leads, the ads show in the same spots on Search and Maps, and your lead history
(contacts, messages, call recordings) moves to the new Leads page. We never say cost per lead will rise: no source measured
it. We don't name the later wave's date: Google hasn't.
