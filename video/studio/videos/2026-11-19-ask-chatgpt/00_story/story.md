## ABT line

## Message line
Ask ChatGPT who it would hire in your town; if it isn't you, fix your Google profile first.

## The story (told the way I'd tell it to an owner)
This week, on Jobber's owner forum, one owner said something that stopped me. At least four people in the last month
told them the same thing: ChatGPT sent them. And the owner said they didn't think they'd done anything there to promote themselves.

Here's what normal looks like. Before a homeowner calls you, they check you. Google first, then your website, maybe Yelp.
You've known that for years, so you ask for reviews and you keep your Google profile half alive.

But the checking has a new stop. This spring, the publisher of a heating and cooling trade magazine asked 782 homeowners
where they'd go for reviews of a heating and cooling contractor. Thirty-seven percent said AI tools like ChatGPT. That's almost the same as
Yelp, at thirty-eight. And in the same survey, half of the contractors said they don't use AI at all.

Because of that, most owners have no idea what the robot says about them. When someone asks it for the best furnace guy
in town, it names a few shops. If you're not one of them, that call never rings, and you never find out it happened.

Now, the honest part. Today, this is a small slice. A marketing firm looked at one data company's numbers and wrote it up
in that same magazine. They say ChatGPT brings most contractors about one percent of revenue, against two thirds from Google's
business profiles. But they say the jobs that do come from ChatGPT are big: about eleven and a half thousand dollars a
ticket, against about thirty-four hundred. Their numbers, not checked by anyone else.

The turn: you don't need an AI agency to fix this. The same column says these tools rebuild your business from data
that already exists: your name, address and phone, your website, your reviews, your Google posts. The same stuff
your Google profile is made of. So the owner who got four ChatGPT customers without trying probably did the trying years
ago, on Google.

What to do this week:
1. Tonight, open ChatGPT and type "who's the best <your trade> in <your town>?" Do the same in Google's AI Mode.
2. Write down whether you're named, and anything it gets wrong about you: hours, area, services.
3. Fix those in your Google Business Profile and on your website's contact page. Ask your last three happy customers for
   a review.
4. Ask the same question again in two weeks.

Kicker: Some of your customers are already asking the robot who to hire. Ask it first.

## The other side
The survey asks where homeowners would look, not where they did, and it asked about HVAC contractors only. The 1% and the
$11,500 ticket are one firm's reading of one data company's numbers, method not published: said as theirs. One owner's
four customers is one owner. We never say how often ChatGPT gets a business wrong: no source measured it, so the card asks
the viewer to check their own.
