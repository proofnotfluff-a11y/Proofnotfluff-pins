# Brief: Twenty minutes on a furnace, two checks, and a judge who said no (v4 told)

Planned Nov 18, 2026 in Todd's live chat, the first `told` lesson (BUILD-2026-11-18.md Part A step 9), from the top row of the told bank (research/STORIES.md). Rules: `_shared/formats.md` "v4", "Case" and "Told"; coo/constitution/video.md.

- **Test:** the told format itself: a story told whole as one person talking, 45 to 90 s, a held result, cards only for numbers, names and the label. Bars: coo/DECISIONS-2026-11-18.md "Bars" (first 3 told lessons at 72 h).
- **Format:** told
- **Where:** Instagram Reel, 9:16, then TikTok, Facebook, YouTube Shorts.
- **Audience:** anyone who has let a contractor into their house, and every honest tech who has walked into a home after one like this.
- **The one idea:** A contractor pulled a couple's furnace apart, would not leave until they wrote a second check, and never did the work; the judge refused his probation deal and gave him the maximum, but they may never see the money, so a contractor who will not leave until you pay is your answer.
- **Pull:** conflict yes (the couple and the contractor, then the judge); dollars yes ($7,250 and $3,650, over $8,000 to fix it, $750 a month); send yes (a parent or neighbor about to replace a furnace). Breadth: a homeowner stops for the two checks; a tech stops because this is who they get compared to.
- **Send:** a tech sends it to a homeowner friend getting furnace quotes, because the second check is the tell; caption only, no spoken send line.
- **Story test:** each answer is a line from the source (rows in the claims table below); a retelling site alone is never the source.
  1. An act a stranger can picture: he took the system apart, then would not leave until they wrote a second check (rows 3 and 6).
  2. Worse at least twice: the work was never done (row 7); then a plea deal of one year of probation (row 10); then the sentence cannot start until November 2027 (row 16).
  3. Two people the viewer can tell apart: the couple at home and the contractor with the furnace in pieces (rows 2 and 3).
  4. A dollar figure: $7,250, then $3,650 (rows 4 and 6).
  5. An ending: the judge rejected the deal, then gave the maximum 2.5 years and $10,900 at $750 a month, which cannot start until November 2027 (rows 11 to 15; WRTV, Oct 6, 2025 and Feb 9, 2026).
  6. Claims: 18 rows in the table below.
- **Ending:** result. The `[payoff]` cue is the maximum sentence, at 60% or later; the catch and the question follow it.
- **Shape:** the furnace in pieces and the two checks on frame 0; the friend's referral; the second check and our take on it; the work never done; the record they did not know; the deal and the judge's no; the maximum (the `[payoff]` cue on "the most the law allows", about 63% in); the catch; the question.
