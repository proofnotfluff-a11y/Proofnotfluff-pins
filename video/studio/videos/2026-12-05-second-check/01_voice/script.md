# Script: Twenty minutes on a furnace, two checks, and a judge who said no (v4 told, Nov 18, 2026, fix round 1 after facts, read-aloud and pick r0; WRTV Investigates, three reports, Feb 2025 to Feb 2026)

[conversational] A couple says a contractor spent twenty minutes on their furnace and took almost eleven thousand dollars. Their heat had started leaking, and a family friend recommended him.

They say he pulled the furnace apart, said they needed a new system, and they wrote a check for seventy-two fifty. A day or so later, he said an electric one cost more, and wouldn't leave until they wrote a second check, for thirty-six fifty. If you ask me, that second check was the moment to call someone else.

The new system never came. They didn't know he had about six old convictions, and was out on bond in another county.

In court, he took a deal: one year of probation. But the judge read his record and threw the deal out.

He pleaded guilty, and the judge gave him the most the law allows: two and a half years, and every dollar back.

Here's the catch. None of it starts until late twenty twenty-seven, when another sentence he's serving ends. He can do it at home on a monitor, paying them seven fifty a month, or face jail. Will they ever see the money? The wife says she'll pray about it.

As for him: was the max enough, or did he get off easy?
