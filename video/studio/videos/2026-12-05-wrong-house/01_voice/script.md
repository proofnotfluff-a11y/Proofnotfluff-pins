# Script: They drove out to mow the lawn, and the house was gone (v4 told, Nov 18, 2026, fix round 1 after facts, read-aloud and pick r0; KDFW, NBC DFW, CBS Texas, Fort Worth Star-Telegram, ABC News, 2013 and 2014)

[conversational] One Saturday, a couple drove out to mow the lawn at their family's old house, and the house was gone. All that was left was a bare slab and their mailbox.

The house had been his grandmother's, three bedrooms in Fort Worth, Texas, and they were planning to move in.

The city had ordered the condemned house next door torn down. That one sat back in the trees, behind a mailbox you could barely read, and a city worker marked their house instead. So a demolition company's crew leveled it. A neighbor says they tried to warn the city that Friday morning, as the machines started.

Here's the part that gets me. The condemned house was still standing, and the city still got a bill for over six thousand dollars for the job. When the city looked closer, it found the same company had knocked down another building the day before, one that nobody had ordered torn down. That's when the city stopped every demolition.

None of that brought the house back. In the end, the couple got a hundred and two thousand, five hundred dollars, and the city paid most of it.

So who should have paid the most: the city, whose worker marked it, or the company, whose crew knocked it down?
