# Script: They drove out to mow the lawn, and the house was gone (v4 told, Nov 18, 2026; KDFW, NBC DFW, AP, CBS Texas, Fort Worth Star-Telegram, ABC News, 2013 and 2014)

[conversational] One Saturday, a couple drove out to mow the lawn at their family's old house, and the house was gone. All that was left was a bare slab and their mailbox.

It was a three-bedroom ranch in Fort Worth, Texas. It had been his grandmother's, and they were planning to move in.

The city had ordered the empty house next door torn down. That one sat back in the trees, behind an old mailbox you could barely read. But a city worker marked the wrong house, and the demolition crew leveled it. A neighbor says they tried to warn the city that morning, as the machines started.

It gets worse. The condemned house was still standing, and the city got a bill for over six thousand dollars. And the city found the same company had torn down a building the day before that wasn't on its order. The city stopped every demolition.

In the end, the couple got a hundred and two thousand, five hundred dollars. The city paid sixty-two five, and the demolition company and its insurance paid the other forty thousand.

If you run a crew, you check the address with your own eyes before anything swings. So who should have paid the most: the city worker who marked it, or the crew that knocked it down?
