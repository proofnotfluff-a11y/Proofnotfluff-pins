# Brief: One angry video about his builder (v4 told)

Planned Nov 26, 2026 in Todd's live chat ("find one true, public trade dispute on a site this session can open, fact-checked today"), after 2026-12-06-void-the-deal and 2026-12-06-one-wall were shelved (the blind pick lost three rounds each: no second side, the ending known early). Rules: `_shared/formats.md` "v4", "Case" and "Told"; coo/constitution/video.md.

- **Test:** the told format on a two-sided fight with a viral act and a turn (the directors lose, appeal and win). Bars: coo/DECISIONS-2026-11-18.md "Bars".
- **Format:** told
- **Where:** TikTok and YouTube Shorts (Instagram and Facebook held since Nov 19).
- **Audience:** anyone who has ever posted a bad review of a contractor, and every builder who has been on the other end of one.
- **The one idea:** A homeowner's angry TikTok about his builder, left up for well over a year, ended in $883,000 in damages, so review the job all you want but don't say what you can't prove.
- **Pull:** conflict yes (the homeowner and the builder and its two directors); dollars yes ($673,000, $105,000 each, $883,000, past a million with costs); send yes (anyone about to post about their builder). Breadth: everyone who posts reviews stops; every builder stops. Split: venting customer versus a business losing jobs to claims a jury found untrue.
- **Send:** a builder sends it to the friend drafting an angry post; caption only, no spoken send line.
- **Story test:** each answer is a line from the source (rows in the claims table below); a retelling site alone is never the source.
  1. An act a stranger can picture: he posts a TikTok in Punjabi naming the company and its two directors (row 4).
  2. Worse at least twice: about 220,000 views, shared on WhatsApp, comments saying the company is going under (rows 5 and 6); then about twelve would-be customers walk away and the video stays up for well over a year despite repeated requests (rows 7 to 9).
  3. Two people the viewer can tell apart: the homeowner and the builder's directors (rows 1 and 8).
  4. A dollar figure: $673,000, then $883,000 (rows 11 and 14).
  5. An ending: a County Court jury rejected his truth defences and the company got $673,000; the directors won on appeal (Victorian Court of Appeal, August 2026, [2026] VSCA 191) and got $105,000 each on Oct 28, 2026; $883,000 in all, costs expected to pass $1 million (rows 10 to 15; SmartCompany, Nov 5, 2026; The Good Builder, Nov 9, 2026).
  6. Claims: 16 rows in the table below.
- **Ending:** result. The `[payoff]` cue is the jury and the award ("A jury rejected"), about 61% in; then the appeal, the total and the closing question.
- **Shape:** you post, they sue on frame 0; Melbourne and the ended contract; the Facebook post and the TikTok; the views and the comments; the customers, the calls and the video left up; court; the jury and $673,000; the directors' appeal; $883,000; was he venting.
- **Music:** bounce.
- **Voice:** Todd's clone (`_shared/voice.md`).
- **Source:** SmartCompany, "Viral TikTok complaint leaves homeowner facing $1 million-plus bill" (Nov 5, 2026) and The Good Builder, "A homeowner's posts about his builder cost him $883,000" (Nov 9, 2026), both on Singh v Singh Homes Pty Ltd [2026] VSCA 191 and the County Court trial. Amounts are Australian dollars (a Victorian court). The jury's findings are the court record; the twelve customers and the calls are the builder's and a director's account and are said so.
