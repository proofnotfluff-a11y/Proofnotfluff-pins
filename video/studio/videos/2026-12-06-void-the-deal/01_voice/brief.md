# Brief: She sued her contractor to void the deal (v4 told)

Planned Nov 26, 2026 in Todd's live chat ("find one true, public trade dispute on a site this session can open, fact-checked today"), after the told bank ran dry. Rules: `_shared/formats.md` "v4", "Case" and "Told"; coo/constitution/video.md.

- **Test:** the told format on a story where the tradesperson wins, with the owner's rule (what a contractor may say to an insurer) as the road the story turns on. Bars: coo/DECISIONS-2026-11-18.md "Bars".
- **Format:** told
- **Where:** Instagram Reel, 9:16, then TikTok, Facebook, YouTube Shorts (Instagram and Facebook held since Nov 19).
- **Audience:** anyone who has filed a home insurance claim, and every roofer or restoration crew that talks to adjusters.
- **The one idea:** A homeowner tried to void her flood repair contract by saying the crew acted as an unlicensed public adjuster; the court said a contractor may talk damage, repair and cost with the insurer but never argue the claim or coverage, so she owes over $60,000.
- **Pull:** conflict yes (the homeowner and the restoration crew); dollars yes ($166,300 estimate, over $72,000 paid, almost $74,000 billed, over $60,000 ordered, almost $196,000 in fees lost); send yes (a restoration owner sends it to the estimator who talks to adjusters). Breadth: a homeowner with a storm claim stops for the bill; a tradesperson stops because it is the line they walk every job.
- **Send:** a roofing or restoration owner sends it to the estimator who calls the insurer; caption only, no spoken send line.
- **Story test:** each answer is a line from the source (rows in the claims table below); a retelling site alone is never the source.
  1. An act a stranger can picture: a winter storm bursts the pipes in her house while she is visiting her daughter in California, and the next day a restoration crew starts pulling the water out (rows 2 and 3).
  2. Worse at least twice: the estimate grows to $166,300 and the insurer turns down many line items (rows 6 and 7); then she has paid over $72,000, tells the crew to stand down and is billed almost $74,000 more, so she sues to void the contract (rows 8 to 11).
  3. Two people the viewer can tell apart: the homeowner and the restoration crew (rows 1 and 3).
  4. A dollar figure: almost $74,000 owed, over $60,000 ordered (rows 1 and 14).
  5. An ending: the trial court awarded the contractor $60,709.69 and the Texas Sixth Court of Appeals affirmed on Aug 20, 2026; the fee bid of $195,966.13 failed (rows 14 to 16; the opinion, Aug 20, 2026, and Insurance Business, Sep 1, 2026).
  6. Claims: 16 rows in the table below.
- **Ending:** result. The `[payoff]` cue is the ruling ("This crew stayed inside that line"), about 72% in; then the fee twist and the closing question.
- **Shape:** the bill and the lawsuit on frame 0; the flood; the agreement and the insurer form; the estimate and the denials; the stand down and the last invoice; the lawsuit and its theory; the line for contractors; the ruling; the fees lost; was she wrong to sue.
- **Music:** classic.
- **Voice:** Todd's clone (`_shared/voice.md`).
- **Source:** Insurance Business, "Texas court rules contractor did not act as unlicensed adjuster", Sep 1, 2026, and the court's own memorandum opinion, Texas Sixth Court of Appeals No. 06-25-00098-CV, Aug 20, 2026 (public domain text, opened on law.co; trial court: 162nd District Court, Dallas County). The facts are the court's findings and the record as the opinion states it. The opinion does not name the legal theory for the $60,709.69 award, so the lesson says only that the court ordered her to pay. The fee motion failed for delay and because the fees were not split by claim; the lesson says "partly" for the delay. No person or business is named in the video, the captions or this folder; the insurer is "her insurer". The scenes are acted from the court's account; no acted lines.
