# Script: She sued her contractor to void the deal (v4 told, Nov 26, 2026, Todd's live chat, fix round 2 after pick r1; Insurance Business, Sep 1, 2026; Texas Sixth Court of Appeals opinion, Aug 20, 2026)

[conversational] Can a contractor lose a seventy-four thousand dollar bill just for talking to your insurance company? One homeowner in Texas said yes.

In February 2021, a winter storm burst her pipes while she was out in California seeing her daughter. The next day, a restoration crew was pumping water out of her house.

She signed their deal: she'd pay whatever insurance didn't, and they could ask her insurer for updates. But their estimate kept climbing, and her insurer kept turning down pieces of it.

By September she'd paid the crew over seventy-two thousand, and she told them to stand down. Their final bill asked for almost seventy-four thousand more.

So she sued. She said only a licensed public adjuster can fight your insurer for you, and the crew had done it without a license, so the whole contract was void.

The court looked at what the crew actually said to her insurer: the damage, the repair, the cost. In Texas, that's allowed. Fighting over her claim or her coverage would not have been, and the crew never did.

So she was ordered to pay them over sixty thousand dollars, and an appeals court agreed.

But the crew didn't win everything. They wanted almost a hundred and ninety-six thousand more for their lawyers, and lost it, partly because they asked thirty-one days after the judgment.

So was she wrong to sue?
